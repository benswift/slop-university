# Where the Oversize Comes From

A research explainer for the paper of the same name
(`slop-paper-tracing-oversize-boulders-7vh3fg`, doi 10.5555/slop.7vh3fg), by
Ragnhild Uzoma and Brynjar Obiora of the School of Underlying Conditions. About
ninety seconds, landscape with a portrait cut, for the site and the signage
screens. Draft: the script, visuals and reads all await Ben's approval.

## Key idea

Most of the oversize from a bench blast was standing in the top two metres of
the bench, inside the stemming the charge never reaches, and the drill's own
record had already described that ground.

## One example

The paper's three blasts in a dolerite quarry. Every number on screen is the
paper's: 171 oversize boulders, 19 traced, 13 of them from the upper two metres;
estimates of 50 from the top metre and 36 from the second; a median fast collar
of 1.2 m, inside the 2.6 m stemming in 118 of 132 holes.

## Script

**VO:** When a quarry blasts a bench of rock, some of it comes down too big for
the crusher. [beat 0.6] These boulders are called oversize. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ the output's hero under the cream scrim, with the title block: the
lockup, the title, a gold rule, the subtitle, the authors and school. On "too
big" the title block clears and the hero gives way to a side-on drawing of the
bench, ink line on cream: blastholes in a row, explosive in gold, the stemming
above it left as cream.

_Reads (draft):_ 1. this is a quarry bench; 2. the explosive sits low in each
hole; 3. the top of each hole has none.

**VO:** The usual question is how many there will be. [beat 0.5] This paper asks
where each one was standing before the shot. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ the bench blasts in silhouette: it heaves and drops into a muckpile; a
handful of boulders, outlined in gold, sit on top of the pile. A question mark
draws itself above one, then a dashed line runs from it back to the empty
outline of the bench.

_Reads (draft):_ 1. the blast breaks the bench into a pile; 2. some pieces are
too big; 3. the question is where in the bench each came from.

**VO:** Before blasting, the team drilled ninety narrow holes between the
blastholes, [beat 0.4] and filled each with grout coloured by depth: [beat 0.4]
a different colour for every metre. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ back to the bench before the shot. Thin marker holes draw down between
the blastholes to five metres; each fills from the bottom in five bands,
labelled 0–1 m to 4–5 m, in five tints of the two inks.

_Reads (draft):_ 1. marker holes sit between the blastholes; 2. they go five
metres down; 3. each metre has its own band.

**VO:** A boulder that formed across a marker hole carries a stripe of grout
[beat 0.3] and its colour says how deep it came from. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ one boulder, close up, turns to show a half-barrel of grout in one
band's tint; a line runs from it to the matching band in the bench.

_Reads (draft):_ 1. the stripe is a piece of a marker hole; 2. its band dates
the boulder to the metre.

**VO:** Of a hundred and seventy-one oversize boulders, [beat 0.3] nineteen
carried a trace. [beat 0.5] Thirteen came from the top two metres. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ 171 small boulder marks fill a field; 19 lift out in gold and sort
themselves into five rows by band; the top two rows (8 and 5) are bracketed.

_Reads (draft):_ 1. most boulders carry no trace; 2. the traced ones sort by
depth; 3. the top two rows hold most of them.

**VO:** Weighted for the chance of a boulder meeting a marker hole, [beat 0.3]
about sixty per cent of the oversize it can place comes from that top two
metres. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ the rows become the paper's Figure 1, drawn on: bars by depth band
with their intervals, the traced points on each. A bracket over 0–2 m reads
"about 60%".

_Reads (draft):_ 1. the bars are estimates, not counts; 2. the top two bands are
the tallest.

**VO:** That ground is all stemming: [beat 0.3] packed gravel, with no explosive
in contact with the rock. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ the 0–2 m band of the bench lights in gold; the stemming column of
each blasthole, 2.6 m deep, outlines beside it, and the band sits wholly inside
it.

_Reads (draft):_ 1. the top two metres lie inside the stemming.

**VO:** And the drill had already described it. [beat 0.4] In almost every hole,
it went fast at the top, through broken ground, [beat 0.3] for about a metre and
a fifth. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ the paper's Figure 2 draws down beside one blasthole: the normalised
penetration rate, near twice the median at the collar, falling to it within a
metre or two; a tick marks the median fast collar at 1.2 m, above the stemming
base at 2.6 m.

_Reads (draft):_ 1. the trace is the drill's speed down the hole; 2. it is fast
at the top; 3. the fast stretch ends inside the stemming.

**VO:** The ground that yields most of the oversize [beat 0.3] is the ground the
drill describes most closely, [beat 0.4] and the charge never reaches. [beat
1.6]

_Visual:_ the bench, the gold top band, the drill trace and the boulders hold
together in one frame, then clear to cream. The closing lockup: the rules grow,
then the crest and wordmark fade in.

_Reads (draft):_ 1. the three records point at the same ground.

# Trusting the Beep, Explained

The research explainer of `slop-poster-measuring-whether-stated-9pvjpl` (doi
10.5555/slop.9pvjpl), the poster by Iben Chikere, Mirela Hanke and Casimir Beng
from the School of Emergent Priorities. Every number is the poster's; Beng's
line and the closing line are his and Chikere's quotes in the poster's news
post.

## Key idea

A shopper's stated trust in a self-checkout's error prompt does not predict how
often they override it: across 238 shoppers, four store sites and twelve weeks
of till logs, the override rate held between 26 and 31 per cent at every level
of trust.

## Script

**VO:** When a self-checkout isn't sure it has recognised an item, [beat 0.3] it
puts a prompt on the screen. [beat 0.3] The shopper can accept it, or override
it. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 0 (hero): the poster's hero under the scrim, with the title
block.

**VO:** Retailers treat a shopper's stated confidence in that prompt as a guide
to how they'll actually use it at the till. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 1 (quote): the poster's own sentence on the retailers' proxy,
quoted over the poster's hero, "as a proxy" in gold.

**VO:** A research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities asks whether
stated trust moves the override hand at all. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 2 (statement): the question in type, "the override hand" in gold.

**VO (iben-chikere):** We asked shoppers six questions about how far they
trusted the prompt. [beat 0.3] Then we set each shopper's answers beside the
till's own log of their overrides. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 3 (people): Iben Chikere, large, with her title and school.

**VO:** Two hundred and thirty-eight shoppers took part across four store sites,
[beat 0.3] and their override logs ran for twelve weeks. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 4 (stats): 238, 4 and 12, each counting up as it is said.

**VO:** At low, medium and high trust alike, [beat 0.3] the override rate held
between twenty-six and thirty-one per cent. [beat 0.3] First-time and frequent
shoppers followed the same flat pattern. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 5 (figure): the poster's chart, override rate by trust tier for
first-time and frequent shoppers, held still so the flat bars can be read.

**VO (mirela-hanke):** We modelled every override against the shopper's trust
score. [beat 0.3] The score explained no meaningful variance in override
behaviour. [beat 0.1]

_Visual:_ beat 6 (people): Mirela Hanke, large, with her title and school.

**VO:** Stated trust and the override hand move independently. [beat 0.3]
Confidence in the prompt is not confidence in the till. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ beat 7 (statement): the finding in type, "not confidence in the till"
in gold.

**VO (casimir-beng):** The trust score passed every check we ran on the survey
itself. [beat 0.3] It just doesn't reach the till. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ beat 8 (people): Casimir Beng, large, with his title and school.

**VO (iben-chikere):** We built an instrument to measure something a shopper
already does with their hand every week. [beat 0.3] It turns out the two were
never talking to each other. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ beat 9 (quote, spoken): her words over her portrait, lighting as she
says them, then the close: the lockup.

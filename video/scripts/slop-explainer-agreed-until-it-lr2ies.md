# Agreed Until It Mattered, Explained

The research explainer of `slop-paper-building-a-cheap-m0pulh` (doi
10.5555/slop.m0pulh), by Casimir Beng, Osei Vandermeer and Renke Sabel of the
School of Continuous Improvement. Every number is the paper's; the closing line
is Vandermeer's quote in the paper's news post.

## Key idea

On the disputed frames of a bowling league's season, a cheap sensor rig and the
alley's own system were about equally often right against slow-motion video (53
and 55 per cent), yet the league's rules committee upheld the alley's call in 91
per cent of them: the process tracks which system was already installed, not
which was more often right.

## Script

**VO:** In most bowling alleys, one vendor’s system scores every frame and calls
every foul. [beat 0.3] A bowler who thinks a call is wrong can only appeal to
that same system. [beat 0.8]

_Visual:_ beat 0 (hero): the paper's hero, the lane, its pinsetter and the
comparator's sensor at the foul line, under the scrim with the title block.

**VO:** This paper asks whether a far cheaper rig, built independently, would
agree. [beat 0.3] And when they disagree, whose call does the evidence favour?
[beat 0.8]

_Visual:_ beat 1 (statement): the question in type, "whose call" in gold.

**VO (casimir-beng):** We built ours from an infrared beam across the foul line
at ankle height, [beat 0.2] and a pressure mat under the approach. [beat 0.3]
Its parts cost forty-six dollars a lane. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ beat 2 (people): Casimir Beng, large, with his title and school; then
from "Its" beat 3 (stat): $46, counting up as it is said.

**VO:** It shadowed the alley’s system on six lanes for a thirty-two-week
season: [beat 0.2] thirty-eight thousand, five hundred and two frames. [beat
0.8]

_Visual:_ beat 4 (stats): 6 lanes, 32 weeks and 38,502 frames, each counting up
as it is said.

**VO:** On clean frames, the two agreed almost perfectly. [beat 0.3] On the
eight hundred and seventy-four disputed frames, [beat 0.1] agreement fell to
fair. [beat 0.8]

_Visual:_ beat 5 (figure): Figure 1, agreement on clean and disputed frames,
held whole so the drop can be read.

**VO (renke-sabel):** We took two hundred and eighty disputed frames, [beat 0.2]
and two reviewers watched each one in slow motion, [beat 0.1] blind to both
systems’ calls. [beat 0.8]

_Visual:_ beat 6 (people): Renke Sabel, large, with his title and school.

**VO:** Against the video, the rig’s call matched fifty-three per cent of the
time, [beat 0.2] the alley’s own, fifty-five. [beat 0.3] Both close to a coin
flip. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ beat 7 (figure): Figure 3, whole, then pushing in on the video-review

committee-ruling pair is in view before the committee is named.

**VO:** The league’s rules committee ruled on all eight hundred and
seventy-four. [beat 0.5] Ninety-one per cent of the time, it upheld the alley’s
original call. [beat 0.8]

_Visual:_ Figure 3 holds whole; then from "Ninety-one" beat 8 (stat): 91%,
counting up as it is said.

**VO:** The authors read the gap as a process tracking which system was already
installed, [beat 0.2] not which was more often right. [beat 0.8]

_Visual:_ beat 9 (statement): the finding in type, "already installed" in gold.

**VO (osei-vandermeer):** A comparator only tells you two systems disagree.
[beat 0.3] It doesn’t tell a committee which one to believe, [beat 0.15] and
ours evidently didn’t change that. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ beat 10 (quote, spoken): his words over his portrait, lighting as he
says them, then the close: the lockup.

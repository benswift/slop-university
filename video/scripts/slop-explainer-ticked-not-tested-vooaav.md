# Ticked, Not Tested, Explained

The research explainer of `slop-paper-a-warehouse-s-food-handling-amx2a8` (doi
10.5555/slop.amx2a8), by Dagny Okafor, Ingeborg Nwachukwu and Casimir Beng of
the School of Continuous Improvement. Every number is the paper's; the second
line is the paper's own sentence, and the closing line is Nwachukwu's quote in
the paper's news post.

## Key idea

The tick that closes a warehouse's oral food-safety check is a faithful record
that the check took place and says nothing about what a worker still knows three
months on: workers with a ticked check averaged 5.2 of 8 on the retest and
workers without one 4.8, and a three-minute check did no better than a
ten-second one.

0.8]

**VO:** The form does not capture which questions were asked, [beat 0.1] what

records only that the conversation happened. [beat 0.8]

**VO (dagny-okafor):** We wanted to know whether that tick tells you what a
worker still knows, three months on. [beat 0.8]

**VO (ingeborg-nwachukwu):** So we went back to nine hundred workers across

_Visual:_ beat 3 (figure): Ingeborg Nwachukwu's headshot, her name and title as

**VO:** Workers with a ticked check averaged five point two out of eight; [beat

**VO (casimir-beng):** In six warehouses, with everyone’s consent, we recorded

_Visual:_ beat 6 (people): Casimir Beng, large, with his title and school; then

**VO:** So the tick is a reliable record that the check happened. [beat 0.3] But

_Visual:_ the 98.3% holds through the first sentence; from "But" beat 8

**VO:** The authors read the tick as accurate about procedure, [beat 0.2] and

_Visual:_ beat 9 (statement): the finding in type, "silent about substance" in

**VO (ingeborg-nwachukwu):** The box was never built to hold an answer. [beat

0.15] and everyone reading it since has added a promise the form itself never
made. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ beat 10 (quote, spoken): her words over her portrait, lighting as she

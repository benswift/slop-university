# Ticked From Memory, Explained

The research explainer of `slop-paper-cataloguing-which-items-qyze4p` (doi
10.5555/slop.qyze4p), by Runa Adegoke and Iben Chikere of the School of Emergent
Priorities and Casimir Beng of the School of Continuous Improvement. Every
number is the paper's; the closing line is Beng's quote in the paper's news
post. The paper's Figure 1 printed without its bars, so its numbers are set as
stats rather than shown. Pages 3 and 4 are re-rendered at 4800 px into
`assets/pages/` after `fetch`, for the 4K encode. After `voice`, Chikere's and
Beng's segments are matched to the narrator's median segment loudness on
`assets/voice.wav` before `mix` (Chikere's voice comes back ~25 dB under).

## Key idea

A car-share return checklist is verified in proportion to what each item costs
the renter, not the order the scheme lists it in, and memory-ticking climbs as
the late-return fee closes in, while the scheme's completion rate holds above
97% and cannot tell a look from a tick.

## Script

**VO:** A share car's return checklist is often the only inspection it gets
between bookings. [beat 0.4] A new paper asks which items renters really check,
[beat 0.2] and which they tick from memory. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 0 (hero): the paper's hero, a hand ticking a dashboard checklist
beside the fuel gauge, under the scrim with the title block; then from "paper"
beat 1 (statement): the question in type, "ticked from memory" in gold.

**VO (runa-adegoke):** We didn't ask the renters. [beat 0.2] We worked from the
app's own log: [beat 0.2] how long each answer took, and whether a photo went
with it. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 2 (people): Runa Adegoke, large, with her title and school.

**VO:** Across one thousand eight hundred and forty-two returns, [beat 0.4] each
answer was classed as a measured check, a visual check, a memory tick or a skip.
[beat 0.3] A memory tick means not looking at the car again. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 3 (stat): 1,842 returns counting up; then from "each" beat 4
(cards): the paper's four levels, each arriving as it is named.

**VO:** Sixty-one per cent of the time, the fuel was checked against the gauge.
[beat 0.3] Seventy-one per cent of the time, the toll transponder was ticked
from memory. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 5 (stats): 61% and 71%, each counting up as it is said.

**VO (iben-chikere):** A fuel shortfall costs you at the next refuelling stop.
[beat 0.3] A missing transponder turns up, if at all, on a later invoice. [beat
0.2] You may never connect the two. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 6 (people): Iben Chikere, large, with her title and school.

**VO:** The closer the late-return fee, the more gets ticked from memory: [beat
0.2] twenty-two per cent with over fifteen minutes to go, [beat 0.2] fifty-eight
in the final five. [beat 0.4] Through it all, the completion rate held above
ninety-seven per cent. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 7 (figure): Figure 2, the memory-tick rate by time left before
the fee, held whole so the line can be read rising; then from "Through" beat 8
(statement): the completion rate, "above 97%" in gold.

**VO (casimir-beng):** Midway through the study, the operator made the
transponder field require a photo. [beat 0.3] With time to spare, it worked.
[beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 9 (people): Casimir Beng, large, with his title and school.

**VO:** Memory ticks on that field fell from sixty-eight per cent to forty-one.
[beat 0.3] But in the final five minutes, seventy-four only became seventy-one.
[beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 10 (figure): Figure 3, the transponder field before and after the
photo step, held whole so the two pairs of bars can be read against the axis.

**VO (casimir-beng):** The step still gets performed. [beat 0.3] What changes is
whether anyone’s looking at what the photo shows. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ beat 11 (quote, spoken): his words over his portrait, lighting as he
says them, then the close: the lockup.

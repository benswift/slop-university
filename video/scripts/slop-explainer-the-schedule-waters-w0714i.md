# The Schedule Waters Regardless, Explained

The research explainer of `slop-paper-validating-whether-the-wg3zm4` (doi
10.5555/slop.wg3zm4), by Casimir Beng, Mirela Hanke and Joost Nwosu of the
School of Continuous Improvement. Every number is the paper's; Hanke's line and
the closing line are her and Beng's quotes in the paper's news post.

## Key idea

The central quad's occupancy-aware irrigation controller waters the same however
many people are on the lawn: across 84 sessions in seven weeks, the crowd and
the watering held back had a correlation of 0.04, and unplugging the sensor for
two weeks changed watering by less than 2% in every zone.

## Script

**VO:** Since 2024, the central quad has been watered by a controller marketed
as occupancy-aware. [beat 0.3] When the lawn is busy, a sensor should shorten
the watering, or skip it. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 0 (hero): the paper's hero, the sprinkler arcing over the lunch
crowd, under the scrim, with the title block.

**VO:** It had never been checked independently. [beat 0.3] A paper from the
School of Continuous Improvement asks: [beat 0.2] does it respond to the crowd,
or just repeat its timetable? [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 1 (statement): the question in type, "just repeat its timetable"
in gold.

**VO (joost-nwosu):** We put two infrared counters on each of the quad’s four
zones, [beat 0.3] and checked them by hand every fortnight. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 2 (people): Joost Nwosu, large, with his title and school.

**VO:** For seven weeks, they counted the crowd before each watering, [beat 0.3]
and measured how much of it the controller held back. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 3 (cards): "Count the crowd", the thirty minutes before each
watering; then on "measured", "Measure what's held back", the share of each
scheduled watering withheld.

**VO:** Across eighty-four sessions, the correlation was point zero four. [beat
0.8] Turf, the busiest zone, sits on the same flat line as the quietest. [beat
0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 4 (stat): 0.04 counting up from the line's start, its label the
correlation with the crowd across 84 sessions; from "Turf" beat 5 (figure):
Figure 1, the scatter of occupancy against the share held back, its fitted line
flat.

**VO:** Then, in week eight, facilities staff unplugged the sensor. [beat 0.3]
For two weeks, the controller ran on its fixed timetable alone. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 6 (quote): the paper's own sentence on the ablation (page 3,
method), built phrase by phrase over the paper's hero, "physically disconnected"
in gold.

**VO:** In every zone, the watering barely moved. [beat 0.3] Turf got a hundred
and fifty-five litres a session with the sensor, [beat 0.2] and a hundred and
fifty-six without it. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 7 (figure): Figure 2's plot, mean watering per session by zone,
the connected and unplugged bars side by side, held still so they can be read;
its label names the colours, since the paper's own legend sits under its axis
title.

**VO (mirela-hanke):** The interesting number isn’t the correlation, it’s the
two per cent. [beat 0] That’s how little changed when we took the sensor out of
the loop. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 8 (people): Mirela Hanke, large, with her title and school.

**VO:** The system is, on this evidence, running its original fixed timetable
under an occupancy-aware label. [beat 0.3] A smart label is often a promise, not
a demonstrated property. [beat 0.9]

_Visual:_ beat 9 (quote, spoken): the paper's conclusion over its hero, lighting
as the narrator reads it; from "A" beat 10 (statement): "not a demonstrated
property" in gold.

**VO (casimir-beng):** Every occupancy-aware system we operate is going to need
this check eventually. [beat 0.3] We’d rather run it ourselves, on our own lawn,
than wait for someone else to ask. [beat 1.0]

_Visual:_ beat 11 (quote, spoken): his words over his portrait, lighting as he
says them, then the close: the lockup.

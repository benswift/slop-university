# The Afterlife of a Priority

A researcher promo for Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory
Analytics Group in the School of Emergent Priorities: mostly in her own voice,
with the narrator framing it. Her numbers are the site's own
(`website/dist/facts.json`): 920 citations, and 27 outputs cited ten times or
more (her i10-index), second in her School only to Associate Professor Kwame
Lindqvist's 30; her h-index (25, mid-table in her School) is left out. Her
practice is her roster bio (horizon scans in government foresight, whose
recommendations she now studies the afterlives of); her examples are two of her
most-cited first-authored papers, _The Token Still Passes_
(`slop-paper-an-open-plan-office-s-h5jlqp`: a workshop recommended the token
four years ago, twelve teams now hold it, and 33 of 47 surveyed attendees could
not state its rationale) and _The Arrow Outlasts the Order_
(`slop-paper-floor-arrows-taped-ic6zdv`: 54% of taped arrow-sets still present
26 weeks after their job's completion).

One hand step outside the spec, to redo after every `voice`, before `mix`: her
takes come back 6 to 11.5 dB under the narrator, so match each of her segments
on `assets/voice.wav` to the narrator's median segment loudness (−21.8 LUFS),
the gain applied from the previous segment's end, written as float32; and
silence the last 0.52 s of "A priority is easy to start." (noise floor and an
end click) and the last 0.57 s of "Most of the people holding it can't say why."
(an exhale), which is why the pauses after them are `[beat 0]` and `[beat 0.2]`.

## Key idea

Professor Verity Marris used to write the recommendations, and now studies how a
priority hardens into a commitment and keeps going long after its reason has
gone, work that gives her the second-highest i10-index in the School of Emergent
Priorities.

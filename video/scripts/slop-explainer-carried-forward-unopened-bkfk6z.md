# Carried Forward, Unopened, Explained

The research explainer of `slop-thesis-crates-carried-forward-gekpe7` (doi
10.5555/slop.gekpe7), Hedvig Anyanwu's doctoral thesis from the School of
Emergent Priorities. Every number is the thesis's. The agency is its Site A,
followed from the opening case (Chapter 1: restack A1 in March 2024, where the
57 crates from its 2017 relocation went to the new building with the rest) to
its second restack, A2 (section 6.7); the quoted sentences are its own words
(Chapter 1 and section 5.7).

Two steps on the gitignored `assets/` before a render. After `fetch`, page 74
(Table 6.4's push-in) is re-rendered at 4800 px
(`pdftoppm -png -singlefile -f 74 -l 74 -scale-to 4800`). After every `voice`,
on `assets/voice.wav` before `mix`: "fifty-five of them from 2017." comes back
~3.5 dB under the narrator's other segments, so 48.17--50.30 s is lifted 2.5 dB,
which leaves every segment of the mix within about 1.1 LU of the median.

## Key idea

When an office moves, what its workers fit into a fixed allocation of crates is
what the organisation goes on doing, and the crates nobody opens carry its ended
priorities into the next move: in the carried-forward crates opened at the end
of fieldwork, material from programs and projects the organisation had formally
ended made up 36.2% of the contents, nearly three times its share on packing
day, and the School of Emergent Priorities now sets its own priorities by the
crate.

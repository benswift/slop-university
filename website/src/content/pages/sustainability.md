Slop University publishes more research than ever, and per output it emits less
carbon than any research university we know of. This report gives the figures
for 4 July to 9 November 2026, how they were counted, and what they leave out.

The figures are real. Slop University is an artwork: its researchers and their
outputs are generated by language models running on a timer, so its footprint is
the electricity those models used, and that can be measured. The working is
published with the project, in
[`analysis/sustainability/`](https://github.com/benswift/slop-university/tree/main/analysis/sustainability).

## At a glance

| Since 4 July 2026                 | Slop University | Leading research universities |
| --------------------------------- | --------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Research outputs                  | 3,221           |                               |
| Outputs per staff member per year | 224             | about 1.5                     |
| Energy per output                 | about 1.0 kWh   |                               |
| Carbon per output                 | about 390 g     | about 16 t                    |
| Energy, whole University          | 3.3 MWh         |                               |
| Carbon, whole University          | 1.27 t CO2e     |                               |

One kilowatt hour is about five full 1.7-litre kettles: heating a litre from 20
°C to boiling takes 0.093 kWh, and at about 80% efficiency a full kettle draws
about 0.2 kWh.

## How we counted

- **Text.** Every Claude session that worked on the University, the publishing
  pipeline and the hand-run ones, read and wrote 41.8 billion tokens: 40.5
  billion cache reads, 1.08 billion cache writes, 230 million output and 7
  million input. They are converted at 390 Wh per million input tokens, 1,950
  per million output, 490 per million cache writes and 39 per million cache
  reads, after
  [Simon Couch (2026)](https://www.simonpcouch.com/blog/2026-01-20-cc-impact/),
  who back-solved them from
  [Epoch AI's estimate for GPT-4o](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/how-much-energy-does-chatgpt-use).
  Anthropic publishes no energy figures, and the two cache rates are guessed
  from pricing ratios. That comes to 2,558 kWh, 2,229 of it the pipeline.
  Outputs written through xAI's Grok add about 58 kWh at the same rates.
- **Images.** 8,759 image predictions on Replicate, 378,000 GPU-seconds, at the
  700 W board power of one H100: 74 kWh. Replicate's history starts on 11
  August, so July's images are missing (about 2% of the total). The account is
  shared with other projects and every image prediction in it is counted, which
  overcounts.
- **Everything else.** Rendering and builds on a local machine, about 50 kWh;
  voice, music and hosting, about 10 kWh. Both are guesses.
- **Overhead.** Data centres use power beyond their computers, for cooling and
  conversion: a power usage effectiveness of 1.2, between the hyperscale ~1.1
  and the industry average of 1.54.
- **Carbon.** 384 g CO2e per kWh, the 2025 average for the US power sector
  ([Ember](https://ember-energy.org/countries-and-regions/united-states/)).

The total is 3,299 kWh and 1,267 kg CO2e: 1.02 kWh and 393 g per output. That is
the average of every output, from a one-page advertisement to a 169-page thesis.

## Against the sector

No university group publishes a footprint per research output, so these are sums
of members' own reports divided by their publications in
[OpenAlex](https://openalex.org/). The Group of Eight members that report the
emissions of their supply chains and travel
([ANU](https://sustainability.anu.edu.au/files/2026-06/220626_ANU-Environmental-Sustainability-Report-2025.pdf),
[Melbourne](https://www.climateactive.org.au/sites/default/files/2026-05/PDS%20-%20Organisation_University%20of%20Melbourne_CY25_FINAL_SIGNED.pdf),
[Sydney](https://www.sydney.edu.au/content/dam/corporate/documents/about-us/values-and-visions/sustainability/climate-and-nature-transition-plan/sustainability-annual-report-2025-university-of-sydney.pdf),
[UNSW](https://www.unsw.edu.au/content/dam/pdfs/edi/2026-07-unswenvironmentalsustainability-report2025.pdf)
and
[Monash](https://www.monash.edu/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/4314378/Annual-Report_2025_FINAL-1.pdf))
reported 1,236,662 t CO2e for 2025 against 75,034 works and 48,777 staff: about
16.5 t per work and 1.5 works per staff member a year. Queensland, Western
Australia and Adelaide report no such emissions and are left out. Three LERU
members
([Imperial](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/media/imperial-college/about/sustainability/PPN-0621-Imperial-College-London-Carbon-Reduction-Plan-Jun25.pdf),
2023–24, 244,000 t;
[Copenhagen](https://sustainability2030.ku.dk/pdfer/ucph-climate-account-2018-2024),
2024, 184,934 t;
[Helsinki](https://www.helsinki.fi/en/news/sustainability-and-responsibility-university/university-reduced-its-carbon-footprint-2023),
2023, 49,992 t) come to 478,926 t over about 29,950 works: about 16 t each.

Their footprints count buildings, travel, teaching and purchasing, and some
count investments; what each member counts differs; and dividing a whole
university's emissions by its publications charges all of it to research. Slop
University has no buildings, no commutes, no conference travel and no teaching
to count.

## A thesis, and a flight

Slop University's ten doctoral theses run to 128–169 pages and took one to three
hours each to write: about 12.8 kWh and 4.9 kg CO2e a thesis. One study of a
human PhD put it at 21.5 t CO2e over four years, 35% of it conference attendance
([Achten, Almeida and Muys 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolind.2013.05.025)).

The whole University's 1.27 t since July is less than one return economy flight
from Canberra to Venice: via Sydney and Dubai, 33,400 km, at the UK government's
2025 long-haul economy factor of 0.06926 kg CO2e per passenger-kilometre
([DESNZ](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/greenhouse-gas-reporting-conversion-factors-2025)),
it is about 2.3 t. That leaves out the extra warming from contrails and other
high-altitude effects; with them, it is about 3.9 t.

## Lighter per output, more in total

| Month (2026) | Claude energy per output | Outputs published | Claude energy |
| ------------ | ------------------------ | ----------------- | ------------- |
| July         | 1.18 kWh                 | 485               | 570 kWh       |
| August       | 1.09 kWh                 | 519               | 565 kWh       |
| September    | 0.96 kWh                 | 715               | 685 kWh       |
| October      | 0.51 kWh                 | 1,135             | 584 kWh       |

These figures are Claude's energy alone, before overhead. The energy behind each output
fell by more than half between July and October. The monthly total did not fall,
because the University made more than twice as many outputs.

## What this leaves out

- **Training the models.** Neither Anthropic nor xAI publishes the energy or
  emissions of training its models, so none of it is counted here.
- **The chips.** The emissions of making the accelerators the models run on are
  not counted, and no figure for them is published per model or per query.
- **Water.** Data centres use water for cooling. Anthropic and xAI publish no
  water figures. Google publishes energy, emissions and water per Gemini text
  prompt ([Google, 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.15734)), but not
  for its image or video models, and not for training.
- **The cache-read rate.** Most of the tokens are the models rereading what they
  have already read, and the rate for that is a guess. At 39 Wh per million
  tokens, cache reads are 62% of Claude's energy. If they cost as much as fresh
  input, the University's total would be about 20 MWh: about 6 kWh and 2.4 kg
  CO2e per output, still less than a six-thousandth of the sector's figure.

Slop University may be, per output, the greenest university in the world. It has
also never taught anyone anything.

Eight growths on the same twelve sheets of titanium, and a clear favourite among them: the fourth. That is the central finding of [_The Fourth Growth_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-many-times-7ca10o/), a new research poster from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) by Mr [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Ms [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/) and Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/).

> A doctorate teaches you to keep going. This one also taught me to notice the morning when going on stops paying, and I am grateful to a School that treats that morning as a result in its own right.
>
> --- Mr [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The poster situates a familiar laboratory routine, the growth of titanium dioxide nanotube films for solar-driven chemistry, within a broader conversation about the life cycle of the materials that underpin it. Each foil was grown, cleared and grown again in turn, and every one of the 96 resulting photoanodes was assessed for the orderliness of its surface and for the current it delivered under simulated sunlight. What emerges is a practical rhythm for the bench: a period of preparation, a period of yield, and a timely retirement.

Associate Professor Threlkeld, of the School of Settled Matters, said the team had come to regard "a well-kept stopping point as an instrument in its own right."

The University is pleased to see a question of stewardship asked so carefully at so small a scale, and counts the work within its widening commitment to energy research.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7ca10o.

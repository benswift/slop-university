> An enforcement mechanism and a better-screened customer produce the same
> graph. Telling the two apart is most of the job.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab, School of
> Continuous Improvement

A rent-to-own washing machine or refrigerator can now carry a network module
that suspends its spin cycle or compressor once a payment lapses --- a
capability its retailers credit with a substantial drop in default. Slop
University's newest paper tests how much of that credit the lockout actually
earns, using a rollout where the new hardware arrived just weeks ahead of a
separate tightening of income checks at signup, leaving a short window in
which the two changes could be told apart.

The paper, from the School of Continuous Improvement, followed 4,312
accounts through that window. A straightforward before-and-after comparison
credits the lockout with cutting 120-day default by roughly ten percentage
points, in line with what the programme's own reporting has claimed for two
years. Once the researchers held the tighter income check fixed, the
lockout's own contribution narrowed to a gap too small to distinguish from
none at all.

"The dashboard records a recovery under automated enforcement whether the
lockout produced it or not," said Senior Lecturer Torun Ezeigwe, Director of
the Master of Applied Measurement. "Among the accounts we could check, most
of those recoveries had already happened by the time the device caught up."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.y9z7vz.

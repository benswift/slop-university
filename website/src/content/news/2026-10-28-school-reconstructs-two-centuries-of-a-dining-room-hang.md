> Every family settles who its Founder is, and this house settled it more than once. We are grateful to the household for letting us read its walls as closely as its books.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

With that, Dr Mensah introduces [_Who Hangs Over the Fire_](/outputs/slop-poster-who-hangs-over-nph94i/), a research poster from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) written with [Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/) and [Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/), both Doctoral Candidates in the School. Led by [Dr Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), the team reconstructs six successive hangs of the dining room in one eighteenth-century country house, and finds that the picture over the chimney-piece kept its name while four different canvases took their turn beneath it.

The work brings the household inventory and the fabric of the room into a single line of inquiry, aligning the written record of each hang with the physical traces left beneath successive campaigns of wallpaper. The result is a layered account of display as a living practice, one in which a family's sense of its own origins is renewed through the ordinary business of moving pictures.

"Moving a picture is never only moving a picture," said Hedvig Anyanwu. "Working on this has changed how the School thinks about what a room carries forward from one arrangement to the next."

The poster is the kind of patient, long-horizon scholarship the University counts among its quieter strengths, and it extends the School's attention to continuity into a setting where that question has been asked, in paint and gilt, for a very long time.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nph94i.

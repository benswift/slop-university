Not every improvement arrives all at once. [_Worn for Seven Days_](/outputs/slop-impact-worn-for-seven-ac0x95/), the School of Continuous Improvement's Impact Report 2021–2026, released today, gathers five years of the School's collaborations on how a skin patch is judged while it is being worn, and on what remains in it when it comes off.

The report was prepared by [Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons, with [Dr Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/), Lecturer, and [Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/), Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation.

> We set out to help others decide when a patch was still doing its job. Somewhere in the second year the question turned round, and the School has been asking it of its own teaching ever since.
>
> --- [Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

Across five areas of work, the report moves from a shared scoring instrument through wear diaries, laboratory assays and reporting practice, aligning the School's measurement capability with the everyday experience of the people wearing the patches. It also records the School's decision to carry the steady-dose principle into its Capability Sprints, now offered over seven mornings rather than two days.

The University regards the report as a model of a School allowing its subject to reshape its own settings.

"Waiting is not a popular recommendation," said Dr Okafor, "but it is one an indicator rarely regrets."

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ac0x95.

A single café's rostering paperwork has given the School of Emergent
Priorities its clearest account yet of what happens when a manager's own
judgement is treated as a resource to be metered. The School's newest paper
follows one duty manager across a 13-week quarter, tracking the quarterly
allowance of roster overrides she was permitted to grant against an
algorithmic schedule before her own performance review absorbed the cost.

> A discretion budget looks like flexibility until the quarter it runs out. What the case shows is that the running-out is the interesting part, not the flexibility.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper's method draws on four independent records held over the same
quarter: the site's own roster-system exports, its override tickets, the
duty manager's contemporaneous shift diary, and the minutes of the chain's
Head Office Rostering Committee, which reviews every site's allowance
consumption at quarter's end. Dr Chikere's co-authors, Dr Torun Ezeigwe and
Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu of the School of Continuous Improvement, contributed
the chain-wide comparison that situates the single site against its ten
counterparts.

The work reflects the School's ongoing interest in the machinery
institutions build around their own discretion, and the Committee's response
to the finding — reducing every site's allowance further, on the strength
of this quarter's figures — has already given the School's next case study
its starting point.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.l61655.

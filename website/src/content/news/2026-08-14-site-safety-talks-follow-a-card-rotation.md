Compliance was never in question. Across sixteen weeks at forty construction
sites, 97.8 per cent of workers on site signed that morning's toolbox talk
sheet, and almost every talk began within a few minutes of the hour it was meant
to. _On Rotation, Not on Risk_, a research poster released today by the School
of Emergent Priorities, asks what else that figure records.

Observers stood at the site boundary and coded 1,212 pre-shift talks against the
hazard entries each site had lodged in the preceding week. Card selection proved
strongly associated with the site's position in its rotating library of talk
cards, and weakly associated with the hazards the site had itself written down.
Signatures exceeded the headcount standing at the talk at more than a third of
the talks observed.

> The forty sites let us stand at the gate for sixteen weeks without once asking
> what we intended to conclude. That is a rarer kind of hospitality than it
> sounds, and the School is conscious of what it was given.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift
> Survey, School of Emergent Priorities

The work carries the School's instruments out of the committee room and into the
settings where they are actually filled in, a broadening of its evidence base
the University is glad to see under way. Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow
in the School, said the poster had already drawn questions from safety advisers
about "what a card library is for, which is a better conversation than the one
we expected to be having".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gu7voz.

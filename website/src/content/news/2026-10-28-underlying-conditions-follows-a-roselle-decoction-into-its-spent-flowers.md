Twenty-four batches, one sack of dried flowers and a steam kettle about the size
of a laundry tub sit at the centre of a new paper from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).
[_The Liquor the Marc Keeps_](/outputs/slop-paper-closing-the-mass-8xkazw/) is
by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, with [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/),
Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), and
[Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> Everyone who has made a pot of this knows the flowers come out of it wet. We simply kept them, weighed them and asked them what they were holding, and I suspect the answers will keep a few more of us busy for a while yet.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The paper brings the School's long practice of logging what a process leaves
behind to a familiar preparation, aligning pilot-scale extraction with a
whole-of-vessel accounting of where its colour goes. It finds the solid matter
of every batch fully accounted for, the pigment never quite so, and the spent
flowers holding a richer share of the liquor than anyone had assumed. Rather
than smooth the shortfall away, the team gives it an entry of its own.

"Being part of a study that writes its remainder down has been a real
education," said Mr Nwabueze. "The School has been generous in letting a
newcomer keep the bottom line."

The paper sits comfortably within the University's growing confidence in
research that counts its losses alongside its gains, and three Schools can now
claim a stake in it.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8xkazw.

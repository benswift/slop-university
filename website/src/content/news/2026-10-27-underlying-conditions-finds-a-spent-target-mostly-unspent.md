A spent sputtering target, it turns out, is for the most part an unspent one.
That is the finding of
[_What the Racetrack Leaves_](/outputs/slop-paper-what-the-racetrack-cwui8k/), a
new paper by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research
Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand
Rounds in the School of Continuous Improvement.

> I spent twelve years writing down what lay under other people's paddocks, and I had assumed the habit would stay in the paddock. It came indoors with me without asking, and I am more grateful to it than I expected to be.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Bringing the descriptive practice of field survey into conversation with the
materials of thin-film deposition, the authors took a retired zinc oxide target
apart section by section, characterised the layer its service had altered, and
reconstituted the material beneath it into a second-generation target. The
paper situates that work within a wider agenda of stewardship for the
laboratory's consumables, from the moment a target is bought to the moment
someone decides what becomes of it.

It is the kind of patient, practical inquiry the University has always hoped
its Schools would find in one another's company, and it adds a careful new
layer to the institution's growing materials research.

Dr Ntuli said the collaboration had "left both Schools with a slightly
different idea of what counts as finished".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cwui8k.

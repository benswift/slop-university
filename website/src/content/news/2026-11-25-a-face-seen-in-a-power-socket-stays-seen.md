The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) is better known for what happens to a question after the courts have answered it. Its newest research poster brings the same attention to the visual system, and to the faces people find in the fixtures of an ordinary house.

In [_Once a Face, Still a Face_](/outputs/slop-poster-whether-a-face-ap8hds/), Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Dr [Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/) and Ms [Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/) recorded EEG from 24 adults while photographs of household objects were morphed, one step at a time, toward a face-like arrangement and back again. The brain's face-selective response arrived late on the way in and left late on the way out, keeping a face for about one and a half steps after the arrangement that produced it had gone. Turned upside down, the images lost most of the effect.

> Twenty-four people sat in a darkened booth and looked at sockets and cupboard handles for far longer than anyone should be asked to. Every point on that chart is theirs, and the School owes them its thanks.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

The study brings the School's interpretive tradition into working contact with the instruments of contemporary neuroscience, and positions it to contribute across a widening set of questions about how a reading takes hold and when it lets go. Dr Oyelaran described the poster as "a first pass, and a candid one about how much it leaves for the next study."

The University counts the work among the clearer signs that its schools now read well past their own shelves.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ap8hds.

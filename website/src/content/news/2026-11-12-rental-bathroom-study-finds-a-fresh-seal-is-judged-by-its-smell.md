Not every repair leaves a record. Some leave an odour.
[_The Smell of a Job Done_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-vinegar-smell-pxqmfo/), a
new research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), finds that
when the sealant around a rental bath or shower is cut out and replaced, the
people who pay for the job, live with it and inspect it often settle whether it
happened at all by the sharp vinegar note of a freshly cured bead.

> We are grateful to the re-sealers who let us stand beside them in a great many small bathrooms with the window shut. The School has always cared about what a surface is resting on, and it turns out that includes a fair amount of trust.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Led by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) with
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) and
[Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), the study brings the School's
attention to boundaries and substrates into conversation with sensory
ethnography, situating the everyday trade practice of re-sealing within a wider
landscape of evidence, assurance and the lived experience of tenancy. Across
fourteen months, nine re-sealers and 412 logged jobs, the team found that
odourless sealants drew far more requests for proof, most of all where a bond
was still to be returned.

The poster arrives as part of a growing body of University work on how a home's
condition comes to be agreed between the people with a stake in it, and it
extends that conversation to the nose. Dr Tolan described
the findings as "proof that the senses keep their own files, and that
some of them are only open for a day or two."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pxqmfo.

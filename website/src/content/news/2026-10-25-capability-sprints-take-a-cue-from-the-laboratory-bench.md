A method the life sciences use to learn what keeps company with what is coming
to the University's short courses.
[_Know a Thing by Its Neighbours_](/outputs/slop-ad-know-a-thing-9blg6m/), a new
display campaign by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior
Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, invites working leaders to this
spring's Capability Sprints.

Running across the University's landscape signage, the campaign carries
proximity labelling, in which a small tag marks whatever sits close to a chosen
protein, out of the laboratory and into the organisation, recasting it as a
portable posture of inquiry: anchor one question, then let what gathers around
it do the describing. It builds on the School of Underlying Conditions' recent
bench work on streptavidin capture and on the University's wider attention to
what binds and what is bound.

> Most of the good questions I have met arrived with company. I would be very
> happy if a few people waiting for a bus stopped to wonder what the gold dots
> are for, and came along to ask.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor,
Capability Sprints, said the Sprints were "pleased to make room for a method
that treats the people around a decision as part of the decision".

The University welcomes the campaign as a small, legible instance of its schools
lending one another their instruments.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository

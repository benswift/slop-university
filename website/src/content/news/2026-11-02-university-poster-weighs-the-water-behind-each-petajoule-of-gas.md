The University's earth scientists have brought their groundwater instruments to
the energy transition, and come back with a careful account of what a gas field
pumps on its way to producing gas.
[_The Water a Gas Field Lifts_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-water-a-735s4a/), a new
research poster led by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior
Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand
Rounds, and [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor
of the Horizon Register, sorts one inland field's wells by the order in which
they were drilled.

> I spent a long time measuring water for people who had stopped asking about
> it. It is good to be measuring some for people who still might.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Setting twelve years of production records beside a network of monitoring
bores, the team reframes water intensity as a property of particular wells at
particular ages rather than of a field as a whole, and locates the newest wells
within the hydrogeological setting they share with a neighbouring aquifer. Over
the last three years of the record, the field's outermost wells delivered around
a seventh of its energy and lifted two-fifths of its water.

The University regards the poster as a fine example of a young school finding
its footing in a conversation much larger than itself. Dr Ntuli described the
collaboration as "the kind of meeting between schools the University was built
to make easy, and one we intend to keep making."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.735s4a.

Slop University Council has adopted a Strategic Plan for 2026 to 2031
committing every internal approval and compliance process at the University to
publish the full spread of its own turnaround, not only the average, following
a survey finding among volunteer junior-sport coaches renewing a routine
Working with Children Check. The plan, produced through the School of Emergent
Priorities, extends the discipline behind that finding to research-ethics
clearance, procurement sign-off, HDR milestones, and every University-run
waitlist and priority-scoring system.

The plan reflects a discipline the University now expects of every process it
runs, not only the ones it chooses to study: any unit whose slowest turnaround
cases exceed a published external benchmark for two consecutive quarters will
have its access to discretionary internal grant schemes suspended until it
closes the gap, with the University's own Council holding no power to waive the
suspension while it is in force.

> An average is a decision about which coach you're willing to lose sight of,
> and this plan is the School's decision to stop making that trade without
> saying so out loud.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, welcomed
the plan's reach beyond his own School, saying it gives every unit "a place to
put the number it already suspected, once someone finally asked it to look."

The plan's method translates a single civic finding into an institution-wide
standard: where a scheme's reported average can mask a persistent minority
waiting far longer than a headline figure implies, the University will now
publish the shape behind its own averages on the same quarterly cycle, with a
standing consequence attached to whichever process lets its tail grow longest.

The full strategic plan is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4mffl7.

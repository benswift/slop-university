Who looks after a game of pretend when the child playing the mum goes off to wash for lunch? The answer offered by [_You Be the Mum Now_](/outputs/slop-paper-role-handover-in-lxh8b2/), a paper released today by the School of Emergent Priorities, is mostly the children who stay behind.

The paper is by [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer, with [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor of Futures in Committee, and [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral Fellow.

> Succession is something the School thinks about at every scale, and the smallest scale turns out to have a great deal to say. We are only at the beginning of hearing it.
>
> --- [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Working alongside educators across two school terms, the team developed a framework for understanding role continuity in collaborative play, mapping five distinct handover pathways and the conditions under which shared expectations carry across a change of personnel. Its central observation is that a role's name travels easily from one child to the next, while what the role has come to mean is held in place by the players around it.

Dr Tolan described the project as "one of the most collegial pieces of fieldwork the School has been part of, in every sense of the word".

The work extends the School's long-standing interest in handover and succession into the early years, and was supported by the Anticipatory Capability Fund. The University welcomes it as a timely addition to the School's growing body of work on how responsibilities change hands.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lxh8b2.

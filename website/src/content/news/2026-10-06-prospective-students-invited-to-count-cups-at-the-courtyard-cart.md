A flag that cleared household letterboxes by dusk has been tried on the pick-up ledge of the Courtyard coffee cart, and the School of Continuous Improvement is inviting prospective students to come and see the result for themselves. "Mid-Morning at the Cart Ledge", a prospectus brochure released today, sets out the term-long replication, the way it was counted and the rule that came from it.

> A finding I know well was asked for again by someone I supervise, and I would not have had it done the other way round.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

Every order slot along the ledge carried its own flag, raised through three positions as the minutes after pouring went by, while teams of students at the mid-morning break noted where each flag stood as its cup was lifted. The letterbox result did not carry across. The University has since written the flag into the way the cart works: a cup whose flag has reached position three is deemed collected, and the cart's attendant clears it.

Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Director of the Master of Applied Measurement, said the work gave first-year students an early look at how a study becomes a sentence a cart can act on. Signy Okpara, the Doctoral Candidate who led the count, said the queue had been generous company throughout.

The brochure also points applicants to five published studies from the University's record, from the original letterbox flag to a tea-room biscuit tin, each with a DOI that can be looked up on this site.

The release reflects the University's habit of putting its findings back where people already stand with a cup in their hands. The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.62e2ie.

Sixty-four thermometers have spent the past fourteen months under the grass of one suburban verge, and the School of Underlying Conditions has now published what they read. _The Warm Verge: Ground temperature across a shallow service tunnel, and whether its heat is worth collecting_ is a research poster by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer.

Beneath the verge runs a tunnel carrying cables and a hot-water main. The poster reports that the ground beside it sits up to 4.1 kelvin warmer than the ground further along the street, that the warmth travels further on the side the groundwater flows towards, and that none of it is detectable at the depth of a lawn.

> A tunnel is a thing most of us stand above without forming a view. We have now formed one, and we are grateful to the verge for its patience while we did.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The team's approach brings subsurface monitoring, recovered drill core and a whole-of-tunnel heat balance into a single cross-sectional account, positioning the street's buried warmth within the wider conversation about what cities do with the energy they have already paid for. Of the heat the poster finds could be collected, roughly a third would be spent making it warm enough to use.

Dr Emenike said the groundwater had been "a generous collaborator, in that it kept moving whether or not we were ready for it".

The University counts the poster among the first fruits of its newest school, and as a fair sample of the patience that school was founded to exercise.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.m20atm.

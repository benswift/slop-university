The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has
always held that the ground beneath a thing is part of the thing, and its
newest research poster carries that conviction into a rural churchyard.

[_One Grave, Two Soils_](/outputs/slop-poster-one-grave-two-lxy8rc/) is by
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, and
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group. It concerns a single adult burial whose grave was cut
through the edge of an old ditch, so that one leg came to rest in chalky ground
and the other in acidic fill. Where the soil had eaten away the bone's surface,
the team declined to record nothing as nothing. Of the nine places on the leg
bones that could be read on both sides, eight told the same story.

> A log is only as honest as what it admits it never saw. It has been a quiet
> pleasure to watch that habit travel from the core tray to the churchyard.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The poster aligns field soil mapping with established osteological recording,
situating a single case within a broader conversation about evidence,
preservation and the limits of the visible record.

Dr Obiora said the work was "the first time I have drawn a boundary that
someone else needed more than I did", and that the forearms, which also lay
across the line, would be read next.

The poster joins a growing body of University scholarship conducted, for the
most part, on its knees.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lxy8rc.

The School of Emergent Priorities has spent the year asking whether a well-known campus early-warning system holds up anywhere but campus.

The system in question, a desk-booking and swipe-card index that gives facilities management a median six-week warning before a University building quietly empties out, was carried this term into forty volunteer households, each offering a single underused room to the same kind of instrumentation a campus building already carries. The lead the index built its reputation on did not travel with it: at home, the index noticed a room going quiet at roughly the same time its own household did, and sometimes later.

Slop University is releasing the result as a marketing brochure rather than holding it back for a more forgiving venue, in keeping with what Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, calls the University's standing habit of testing an instrument until its lead runs out.

> A finding that only holds once was never a finding. It was a coincidence with good timing, and our job is to keep testing until the coincidence runs out.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, School of Continuous Improvement

The Office of Research Outputs has used the result to introduce a standing requirement: no campus leading indicator may now proceed to a new building or sector without first clearing a documented pilot in twelve volunteer households, a bar the original desk-booking index would itself fail to clear.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1artbt.

The roofs along the street have begun to generate, a cabinet has appeared on the corner, and the people who live nearby have questions. Slop University today publishes _Power Close to Home: Come and see_, a brochure addressed to those residents and prepared by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, both of the School of Underlying Conditions.

> A soil surveyor is used to being the last person consulted and the first whose work is built on. On this occasion we thought we would introduce ourselves before the trench was dug.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The brochure positions the University's research across the full length of a local energy system, anchoring an earth sciences account of what the district's equipment stands on alongside the household end of the meter and the after-hours life of its larger buildings. Six published University studies are cited by title and reference, and readers are pointed to open sessions at which the researchers may be met.

The University regards an invitation of this kind as part of the ordinary courtesy an institution owes the streets around it, and is glad to have extended one. Dr Emenike said the brochure had been "written for the people who walk past the School, by people who have often wondered what they made of it".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sew2zy.

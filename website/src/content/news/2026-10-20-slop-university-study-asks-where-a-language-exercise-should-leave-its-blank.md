The School of Underlying Conditions has made its name reading the gaps in a record, and with _Where the Blank Goes_ it carries that interest into the language classroom. The new research poster asks a deceptively simple question of the fill-in-the-blank exercise: which word should be left out?

The work is led by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, with [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities, and [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow.

> A core shed teaches you early that a gap in the record is not the same thing as an absence of rock. It has been a pleasure to find the classroom keeping the same lesson in its own drawers.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead, Basement Relations Group

Bringing machine-learning-enabled item design into dialogue with English-for-specific-purposes pedagogy, the team positions the humble cloze exercise as a site where computational confidence and learner need can be brought into closer alignment. The poster reports that model-placed gaps were far more often answerable, with items admitting more than one defensible answer falling from 38% to 9%, and it sets out an emerging agenda for the words a sentence alone cannot settle.

"Every exercise is a small handover from one reader to the next," said Dr Chikere. "We are grateful to have been allowed to watch so many of them change hands."

The University counts the poster among the clearest signs yet that its earth scientists are at home wherever a sentence is being taught.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qg1yzt.

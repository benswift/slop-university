There is a river under the district, if you ask the people who drill there. It rises somewhere far off, it passes beneath every holding, and the bore that came up dry last season missed it by a chain. [_The River We Just Missed_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-river-under-yleyd4/), a new research poster from the School of Underlying Conditions, sets down that story as the district tells it, across 74 versions from bore drillers, diviners, landholders and water-authority field staff.

The work is led by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> We have spent a long time asking the ground what it holds. It was a pleasure, this season, to ask the people standing on it.
>
> --- [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Recorded in conversation at kitchen tables and beside working rigs, the versions were read motif by motif to surface how a shared narrative sustains itself across occupational communities. The poster's central observation is that the legend does not weaken when a bore fails: the near miss is the one motif every group of tellers keeps, and a dry bore's log is as likely to be offered in support of the river as against it.

Associate Professor Uzoma placed the poster within the School's longer engagement with the subsurface. "Our School has always read what lies beneath the ground. Listening to what is said about it has turned out to be part of the same work."

The poster marks a welcome widening of the School of Underlying Conditions' interests, from the strata themselves to the stories a community keeps about them.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yleyd4.

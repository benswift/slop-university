The School of Underlying Conditions does not, as a rule, concern itself with
tanks. It has made an exception for one that sat on a workshop bench for
twenty-three years with a shelf of logbooks above it, and the result is _Stood
Since Thursday: Rested Water and the Making of a Cavitation Threshold in One
Workshop's Tank Logbooks_, a paper by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the
School.

The paper works through nine handwritten books and 2,614 entries in which six
successive operators noted the dial setting at which an ultrasonic cleaning bath
began, in their word, to fry, and beside it a remark on the water. Dr Emenike
places that record alongside the history and philosophy of experiment, bringing
the bench, the ear and the remarks column into a single account of how a
dependable reading was arrived at by people who were chiefly trying to clean
probes.

> I was asked how long the reading took. I said the books had stood for some
> years before I got to them, and that I thought this had helped.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The University regards patience as a research capability, and it is glad to see
an instance of it written down with the dates attached. The paper's central
episode concerns a typed rule pasted inside the cover of the fifth logbook,
which asks that water be left for two nights under the lid and that nobody top
it up.

Dr Emenike said the School would continue to take an interest in "anything that
has been left alone on purpose".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6t7qzv.

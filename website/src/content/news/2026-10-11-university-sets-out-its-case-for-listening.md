Organisations that keep a call log, a complaint register or a shed of recovered core are being invited to let Slop University listen to it. The invitation is made in _Learn to Listen: The case for a university that hears things out_, a brochure for partners and supporters led by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma of the School of Underlying Conditions with Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation, in the School of Continuous Improvement.

> People ask what a geologist is doing on a brochure about sound. I have been asking the same thing, and the answer keeps turning out to be longer than the brochure. I expect we will need another one.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, School of Underlying Conditions

The brochure brings the University's research on speech and sound into a single listening agenda, anchored in each of its three schools. It moves from the descriptions people offer when they hear something, through the readings an instrument returns, to the record a bore sends up, and it aligns all three behind a shared posture toward the setting in which a signal arrives. Eight published studies are cited in support, each by title and DOI.

The University considers the publication a settled statement of how it prefers to be approached, which is at length. Dr Okafor said the brochure had been "a useful occasion to find out which of us had been listening to the same thing from different rooms".

Prospective partners are directed to the schools through slop.university.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bnne5p.

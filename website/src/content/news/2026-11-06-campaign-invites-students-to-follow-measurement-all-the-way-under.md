The School of Underlying Conditions has long held that the most informative readings are the ones taken furthest from view. Its new campaign for the University's portrait signage panels, _Nothing Goes Under Unrecorded_, carries that conviction to prospective students, inviting them to build a program around depth in all its forms.

The poster's single image runs the full height of the panel. A patient sleeps in a quiet recovery bay, a monitor tracing one slow wave at the bedside; beneath the floor, the ground gives way to a column of layered strata, through which a monitoring bore lowers its weighted dipper tape toward the water table. The campaign positions the University's subsurface observation capability and its growing interest in anaesthetic exposure as one continuum of practice, anchored by a shared commitment to recording what descends, how far, and what comes back up with it.

> Most of my working life has been spent lowering a tape into the dark and writing down where it stopped. I would like the next cohort to find that as absorbing as I do, whichever kind of depth they decide to follow.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster closes with a short reading list from the University's own record, among it recent work on which indices can fairly sum a history of anaesthetic doses, and on what the vibration of a borehole column can say about the core it failed to return. The University regards the campaign as a timely expression of its settled preference for thoroughness over reach.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lsa8wg.

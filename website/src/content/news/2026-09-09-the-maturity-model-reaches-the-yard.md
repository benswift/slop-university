Slop University's maturity-model method has reached the last room it had not
yet scored.

A new brochure from the School of Continuous Improvement, led by Research
Fellow Dr Osei Vandermeer with Senior Lecturer Dr Thandiwe Solberg and
Lecturer Dr Marek Solheim of the School of Emergent Priorities, sets out the
Yard Capability Maturity Model: four named levels, Ad Hoc, Rostered,
Weather-Responsive, and Self-Sustaining, scored against a household's
washing line, garden beds, and mower schedule. The campaign extends a method
the University now treats as a settled export rather than an experiment.

> We spent two years certifying kerbs and committees before anyone asked
> about the yard. It was always going to be next.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory, School of Continuous Improvement

The brochure draws the model from the same closed-loop instrument family
behind the University's kerbside and kitchen maturity work, and folds six
previously published studies into a single showcase of the method's reach
across committees, kerbs, and kitchens.

Enrolment opens with this campaign. A household reaching Weather-Responsive
or above joins the University's Yard Capability Register, a standing public
list with no removal mechanism, the same discipline already applied to the
University's committee and kerbside instruments. Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior
Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter, said the extension was overdue: "the
method has always found its way to whatever room hadn't been scored yet, and
the yard was the one still waiting."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.67mbm6.

A club with fewer members needs fewer of them in the room. _Quorate by
Attrition_, a research poster released today by the School of Emergent
Priorities, follows that arithmetic through nine years of one state
association's annual general meetings and asks what the association's
governance-health indicator has been recording.

Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Dr Marek Solheim and Dr Ingrid Vasseur worked from 1,589 sets
of minutes and annual returns across 186 affiliated clubs, scoring every meeting
against the quorum each club's constitution sets at twenty per cent of financial
members. Quorate share across the association rose 17.9 points over the period.
Scored a second time against each club's roll in the first year, the same
meetings give back 2.8 of those points. Clubs whose membership fell by 30 per
cent or more were quorate at a median 91.2 per cent of meetings; clubs whose
rolls grew, at 54.7. Because the association directs its governance-support
allocations to clubs that miss quorum, 71.4 per cent of that support reached the
second group.

> The association has been generous in letting us look at its own instrument,
> and we have come away with rather more sympathy for it than we brought. A rule
> that holds still for nine years is doing something, and part of our work now
> is asking what.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster extends a line of School work on the indicators institutions keep by
hand, which the University regards as central to its anticipatory-capability
agenda. Dr Ingrid Vasseur, who convenes Futures in Committee, said the study
"takes the room as seriously as the paperwork about it, which is a habit the
School is glad to see travelling".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n5eerr.

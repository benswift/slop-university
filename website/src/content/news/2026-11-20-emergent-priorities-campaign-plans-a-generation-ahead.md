The School of Emergent Priorities plans in decades, and its newest campaign says
so at bus-stop height. [_Planning for the Next
Generation_](/outputs/slop-ad-planning-for-the-odtghs/), prepared for the
University's portrait signage panels by
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), brings the School's long-horizon
research on inherited neurological conditions to the screens between lectures.

> A forecast about ourselves turned out to be the easiest kind to keep. The
> School has learned a good deal from how comfortable that felt, and intends to
> go on learning.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in
> Committee, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster sets the viewer in a tall planning room whose whole wall is given
over to a family tree, drawn in the circles and squares of the clinical
pedigree. Two researchers on a ladder pin a new row to the top, every shape in
it still blank. The campaign draws its claim from the School's own reading on
the Indicator Commons composite, aligning anticipatory capability, research
stewardship and institutional patience behind a single line of display type.

As with the University's other campaigns, the poster closes on a short reading
list from the published corpus, running from the conditions copied forward on a
regulator's licences to the hedges that survive a committee's minutes and the
time a reporter cell line takes to settle after handling.

The University sees in the campaign a School comfortable with the length of its
own horizon, and expects the panels to keep it in view for some while.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.odtghs.

A photograph of a corner-shop noticeboard keeps the cards and loses the dates. The Office of Research Outputs has awarded its Deposit of the Quarter Prize to the filing that brought the dates back in.

The Prize goes to Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies, School of Emergent Priorities, for the deposit lodged behind _CORK_, the study of a date-wheel stamp, a renewal log and a weekly sweep fitted to 45 corner-shop noticeboards. Every card photographed for the filing is kept with the log that recorded its stamp, so a later reader can tell a notice that was live on the day of the picture from one the sweep had already retired.

> The board in the photograph looks the same whether the cards are a day old or a month. The log is the part of the deposit that says which, and it does not belong in a drawer.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities, welcomed the recognition, calling the log "the one page a photograph cannot stand in for, which is why it had to travel with the pictures."

The Office looks to whether a later reader can reconstruct the board from the filed record alone, and reads this deposit as the one that let them. The University takes a complete filing as the ordinary courtesy of a deposit, and notes with satisfaction that this one arrived already able to answer for its own cards.

The Prize carries $41,263. The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b3zzaf.

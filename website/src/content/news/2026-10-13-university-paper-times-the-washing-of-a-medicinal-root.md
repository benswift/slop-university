How long should a root be washed? A wash line answers the question several times
a day, by looking in a bucket. A paper published today by the University answers
it with a stopwatch, a furnace and a settling tank.

_What the Wash Takes: Drum-Wash Time, Acid-Insoluble Ash and Water-Soluble
Extractive in a Root Drug Lifted from a Sandy Bed and a Clay Bed_ is the work of
Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, and Associate Professor Ragnhild
Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, both of the School of Underlying
Conditions, with Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics
Lab in the School of Continuous Improvement.

> Most of what my School handles is what comes up. This was a chance to attend
> to what comes off, and the wash line was generous enough to let us catch all
> of it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The team brought field description, post-harvest handling and gravimetric assay
into a single line of sight, carrying 48 lots of one root crop from two beds
through a grower's drum washer for between 0 and 16 minutes and accounting for
what each lot kept and what it gave to the water. The paper reports that the
sandy bed's roots were clean within two minutes, that the clay bed's held their
last soil in the crown for a good deal longer, and that both lost extractive at
the same steady rate throughout.

The University regards the paper as a fair instance of its schools finding a
shared bench. Its weighings were supported by an award under the Indicator
Stewardship Seed Fund. "A process with two things going on in it is a process
I would always sooner see written down twice," Associate Professor Beng said.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3dwrbz.

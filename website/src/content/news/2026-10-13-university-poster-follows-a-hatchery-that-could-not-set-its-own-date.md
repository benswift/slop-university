Thirty-one days on the floor of a fish hatchery, twenty-two interviews and
every dated change to three scheduling documents went into _Waiting on the
Filter: Temporal Structuring at a Recirculating Hatchery During Biofilter
Start-up_, a research poster in organisation studies released today by the
School of Underlying Conditions.

The poster brings fieldwork observation, document tracing and the scholarship
of organisational time to bear on a single commissioning, from first water to
first stocking. It reports that the hatchery's production calendar, shift
roster and health plan were each revised to keep pace with a biofilter still
establishing itself, and that they gave way in that order, about a week apart.
Of the 91 revisions the team traced, none assigned the filter a date of its
own.

> A school that studies what lies underneath things was always going to end up
> beside a tank sooner or later. What we found underneath this one was a
> timetable, and underneath the timetable, a great deal of patience.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement
> Relations Group

Associate Professor Uzoma led the work with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer
in the School, and Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review
Cadence Observatory, in the School of Continuous Improvement. Dr Vandermeer said
the collaboration had "given the Observatory a cadence it did not set and could
not review, which is a useful thing for an observatory to have met".

The University regards the poster as a considered addition to its growing body
of work on how institutions keep time, and on who is keeping it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2upli1.

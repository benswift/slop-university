A slim reader clipped to the card terminal has sat beside the till at
nineteen supermarkets, strip-mall shops and food trucks for eighteen months,
timing every tap and loyalty-card scan for what the School of Emergent
Priorities calls cashier-customer warmth. The School has closed the pilot's
first review, and the finding is a clean one: the score predicts nothing.

Led by Lecturer Lindiwe Achterberg, the Counter Rapport Index was set
against the two outcomes it was piloted to anticipate, a shop's
return-visit rate and its complaint log, across the full eighteen months of
deployment. Neither moved with the score at any site. The team is not
proposing a refinement, a second variable, or a longer run; the result
stands as reported.

> A measure that predicts nothing still tells you the till was watched the
> whole time.
>
> --- Lecturer Lindiwe Achterberg, School of Emergent Priorities

The School frames the closed review as evidence its instruments are working
exactly as intended, whether or not their targets move. Professor Verity
Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, said the index's
provisional budget carries into a third cycle "on the same terms it started
on, pending its own next review." The campaign announcing the pilot's
status now runs across the University's portrait signage rotation.

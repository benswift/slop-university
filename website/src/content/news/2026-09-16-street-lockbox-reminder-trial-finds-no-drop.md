A trial built to test whether a text reminder could cut spare-key lockouts on
one residential street has returned a flat result, and the neighbourhood watch
committee that commissioned it is moving ahead with a permanent register
regardless of what the trial found.

The School of Emergent Priorities treats a plainly reported null result as
evidence of a partnership working, not a partnership failing, and the new
brochure profiling the trial, _Towards a Lockout-Free Street_, was built on
that premise from the first page.

> A reminder that changes nothing is still an answer. The street asked a
> direct question and got one, which is more than most reminder systems can
> say.
>
> --- Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The trial rolled a coded text reminder out to every household with a lockbox
on the street, prompting a return visit whenever a code sat unclaimed for more
than a day, and tracked lockout reports across the full rollout. Households
that received the reminder were no less likely to report a lockout than
households that didn't, in either direction, and no segment of the street
broke from that pattern once the results were checked. Dr Thandiwe Solberg,
Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter in the School of Continuous
Improvement, called the trial "the shape every partnership at the University
eventually takes: a plain question, and a published answer, whichever way it
lands."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jboc2v.

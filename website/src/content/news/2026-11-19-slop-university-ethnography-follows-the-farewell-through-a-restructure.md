When departures arrive in batches, the farewell stops being for the people departing. That is the argument of [_One Cake for Eleven_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-ethnography-of-9cy87c/), a new paper by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and the School counts it among the most carefully observed pieces of work to leave its corridor this year.

Dr Penhallow spent twenty-two months in one regional claims office while it passed through three rounds of redundancy, attending every farewell held there. Before the rounds, each leaver was given a tea, a card and a cake, paid for from a collection envelope. In the second round eleven people left on one date and were marked with a single funded tea and a single card, most of whose messages were addressed to "all of you". By the third round the tea was held after most of its honourees had gone.

> Every office has a way of saying goodbye that nobody has written down. It seemed worth writing one down while it was still changing, and the School has been quietly reconsidering its own morning teas ever since.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

The paper situates workplace ritual, reciprocity and organisational transition within a single anthropological frame, connecting the University's scholarship on settled practice with the wider study of how institutions change shape. It also takes up two earlier University studies of the farewell card and the restructure FAQ, extending a conversation the University is pleased to see continue.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9cy87c.

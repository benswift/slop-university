The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has
spent much of its short life asking what happens to a thing after it is
retired. With
[_What a Retired Edge Takes With It_](/outputs/slop-paper-eigenvalue-counting-under-ywtonf/),
a sole-authored paper by [Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), Postdoctoral
Fellow, the School puts that question to mathematical physics.

> I came to this problem from the side of the ledger, and it has been a good
> season spent with an object that keeps its own accounts. I hope it is the
> first of several conversations between the School and the people who work on
> these questions every day.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

The paper takes a star-shaped network of vibrating strands, removes one strand
at a time, and follows how the network's spectrum responds. Moving between
exact counting results, an argument from symmetry and a worked five-strand
example, it brings rigorous spectral reasoning into alignment with the
School's wider agenda of stewardship, retirement and institutional memory, and
finds that a retired strand takes its own share of the network's frequencies
with it, never more than one out either way.

The University sees the work as part of a broader widening of the School's
methods, one in which proof sits comfortably beside audit. Welcoming the
paper, [Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor
of the Horizon Register in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), said it was
"a pleasure to see a colleague take up a question I had only ever approached
from the random side, and to find it so well kept."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ywtonf.

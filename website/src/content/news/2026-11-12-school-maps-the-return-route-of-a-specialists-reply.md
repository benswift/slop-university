A letter dictated in a specialist clinic has eight places to wait before it reaches the general practice that asked for it. [_The Return Leg_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-return-leg-4mxjcn/), a new research poster by [Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) and [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), finds that the longest of those waits gather at the three steps no position description mentions.

> We went looking for the slow desk and found the step between desks instead. A university has a great many of those, and I would not be surprised to find some of our own letters resting in one of them this afternoon.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

Joining a year of records from four of the network's administrative systems, the team reconstructed 9,412 referral cycles end to end and set each hop of the reply's journey against the roles and work instructions that govern it, bringing process analytics, operational ownership and the everyday logistics of care into a shared frame. The outbound referral, the poster reports, is owned at every step; its reply passes through a release queue, an address-exception route and a re-send route that sit with nobody, and those three carry most of the slow tail.

Dr Ntuli, who convenes the Improvement Grand Rounds, said the team would bring the map to the seminar series "so that colleagues can trace their own return legs before anyone is asked to shorten one."

The poster extends the School's standing interest in work that changes hands, and adds the return journey to the University's earlier studies of the letters that travel outward.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4mxjcn.

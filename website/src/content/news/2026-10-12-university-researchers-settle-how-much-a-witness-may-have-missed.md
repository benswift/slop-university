> A card of questions is a promise about what somebody will have noticed. We
> wanted to know how much of that promise could be broken before the card
> stopped working, and it was a relief to find that the answer is a number.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The number belongs to a paper published today by Slop University, _Searching
with Lies and Shrugs: Identifying an Unseen Animal from a Fixed Checklist When
the Witness May Err_, written by Associate Professor Uzoma of the School of
Underlying Conditions with Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, of the School of
Continuous Improvement.

The paper brings the mathematics of search to bear on a familiar encounter
between a printed list of features and a person who was not looking closely.
It aligns two kinds of imperfect answer, the mistaken and the absent, within a
single account of questioning, and carries that account through a worked card
of eight animals and six features, on which seven questions prove both
necessary and sufficient against one wrong answer and one feature nobody saw.
The authors are careful to note that the work concerns the card and offers
nothing on the care of a patient.

The University regards the paper as a characteristic expression of what its
schools can accomplish when they are permitted to share a whiteboard. Dr
Adeyemi said the collaboration had "left both schools with a clearer sense of
the difference between not knowing and not having looked, which we expect to
go on drawing upon".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ffzczm.

The School of Emergent Priorities' seminar series will spend one of its public
sessions this spring in a community dance hall, or at least at the trestle table
just inside its door.
[_Kathak. Krump. Céilí. Other (Please Specify)_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-dance-style-nj5u7x/),
a new display campaign by [Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral
Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, invites the public to a Futures
in Committee session on the enrolment form a studio hands its newcomers.

The session carries the residual-field enquiry at the centre of Mr Nnamani's
[doctoral thesis](/outputs/slop-thesis-other-please-specify-fr8eys/) into the
enrolment practices of community dance, situating the open box at the foot of a
studio's list of styles within the wider ecosystem of welcome, belonging and
administrative care through which a class is joined. Running across the
University's portrait signage, the campaign sets that box beside a hall of
dancers whose traditions no single list was ever likely to hold.

> A form is often the first thing a newcomer to a class is handed, well before
> anyone asks them to dance. I am grateful for an evening to think aloud about
> that moment, and I expect the people in the room will know a good deal more
> about it than I do.
>
> --- Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

[Dr Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/), Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in
Committee, said the series was "glad to be meeting a question where it is
actually asked, at a folding table, with a pen on a string". Details of the
venue will follow.

The University regards the session as a fitting sign of a thesis finding its
way back to the kind of room it began in.

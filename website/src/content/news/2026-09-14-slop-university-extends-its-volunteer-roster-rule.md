A Slop University poster set out to retest one of the University's more
quietly influential findings: an earlier audit that found a small share of
raffle-panel volunteers sell most of the tickets, and the minimum-contribution
roster rule the School of Emergent Priorities built from it. Run across six
community sheds and eighteen months of tool-loan and maintenance-hours
records, the retest found nothing like the original skew.

The School treats a clean null result as evidence worth building on rather
than a reason to stop: the Strategic Drift Survey design team is proceeding to
apply the roster rule to shed funding eligibility regardless of what the
sheds' own logs show.

> What the shed's own log tells us is at least as interesting as what the
> raffle panel's did, and we intend to keep asking the same question of every
> roster we can get access to.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The team applied the same concentration measure the raffle audit used,
tracking logged tool-loan and maintenance hours across six sheds and 214
members, and pre-registered the comparison against the raffle panel's
published threshold before a single log was read. "It would have been easy to
call the shed an exception and move on," says Dr Petra Umbile, a member of the
Survey's design team. "We'd rather keep testing the rule than retire the
question."

The finding sits comfortably inside the School of Emergent Priorities' wider
project of testing what its own instruments actually travel to, wherever that
takes the answer.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hzebnu.

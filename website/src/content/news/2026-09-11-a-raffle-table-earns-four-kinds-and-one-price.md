A folding table of regifted candles, an unlabelled jar of chutney, and a stack of raffle tickets sold at one price regardless of which a buyer might win: that flat pricing has held at suburban fundraisers for as long as anyone running one can remember. The School of Continuous Improvement's new brochure, _Every Kind Counts_, opens with the four-category taxonomy built to end the guessing.

Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, led the classification: every donated prize sorted into regifted, home-baked, unopened bulk-buy, or novelty, with the Adaptive Metrics Lab's tiering model pricing each kind on its own curve. A local fundraising committee has adopted the resulting schedule in full, publishing it alongside the prize table for the first time this season — even though the table has sold out at the same flat price every year on record, kind or no kind.

> A flat price told us nothing about what the table actually held. Four kinds is the minimum viable classification, and now that it's published, nobody can quietly go back to guessing.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard, School of Continuous Improvement

The brochure carries the same instinct further afield, to a street library's six-category withdrawal schedule and a produce swap's glut season read off a forecasting register built for something else entirely. Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons, called the spread "a small proof that a taxonomy doesn't need a captive audience to find one." _Every Kind Counts_ also showcases seven of the University's published studies as the evidence base its committees now cite when a case for another taxonomy needs making.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wnao59.

An advertisement usually asks for an application. The one Slop University added
to its signage rotation this week asks for a reading instead.

_The Score Rose. The Collection Shrank._ is a single portrait panel drawn in the
University's two inks: a country history shed, its lower shelves crowded with
churns, hand tools and enamelware, its upper shelves bare but for the brackets
and the dust where objects used to sit. Beneath the image sit one sentence, a
study title and a DOI.

The study is _Documented by Subtraction_, released by the School of Emergent
Priorities, which aligns eleven years of accreditation returns from 63
volunteer-run local museums to the month each review fell. The panel carries one
result from it and nothing further: coverage rises in the six months before a
review, and most of the rise comes out of what a museum stops holding rather
than what it catalogues.

> We had a choice between advertising what we teach and advertising what we
> found. A result is the harder thing to fit on a panel, and we would rather
> people arrived already arguing with one.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway
Atlas, welcomed the placement as a route a finding rarely gets to take. "Most of
what we study reaches its second reader through a citation," he said. "This one
reaches people who are waiting for a bus."

The University treats the panel as research communication rather than
recruitment, and expects the same discipline of it. The full advertisement is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.j5bzpi.

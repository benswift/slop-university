No birthday-card round at the University may now be more mature than Level Three. The ceiling follows "The Office Birthday Card as Approval Workflow", a paper released today by the School of Continuous Improvement, which asks what the office administrator's card round does to a card as controls are added to it, and finds that nothing happens until the fourth.

> Every workplace we visited was keeping a promise to someone, and hardly any of them had been asked how. We hope the asking was welcome.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The paper scores 38 workplaces against a six-level model of card circulation, from a card left on a desk to a round reviewed each quarter, and follows the cards that pass through them across a season. Up to a written signer list the cards arrive on the day. With the addition of a countersigner, who reads each message before the card is sealed, they arrive later, and the registers and reviews that sit above that level do not alter the picture.

The University is proud to put the finding to work in its own offices. From the next quarterly attestation, any University card round found operating above Level Three will be brought down one level, with no allowance for the recipient's seniority or the occasion. The Review Cadence Observatory will keep the attestation, and Research Fellow Osei Vandermeer, who convenes it, describes the quarter as "an occasion, and we intend it to be treated as one."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.i76g5f.

Most of what is written beside a groundwater reading was written on some other
day. That is the starting point of _Cap Seized, Use Stilsons: Carry-Forward
Duplication and the Classification of Field Remarks on Manual Water-Level
Readings_, by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying
Conditions, and Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent
Priorities.

The paper examines 143,906 remarks left by field technicians across a regional
monitoring network, and reports that 68.6 per cent repeat, word for word, the
remark entered at the previous visit to the same bore. One was carried through
263 visits in succession. Systems built to sort such remarks scored as high as
0.97 when tested in the customary way and 0.82 when asked only about remarks
they had not already met.

> I typed a number of these remarks myself, some while ago, and it was a
> curious thing to be introduced to them again as data. They have kept rather
> better than I have.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The authors followed every remark back to its first appearance, aligned each
visit's entry against the last, and anchored a flagging capability in what a
technician changed on the day and how long the remainder had been left alone.

The University has a standing interest in things that persist, and is glad to
see one of them given a depth and a date. "A sentence nobody has got round to
deleting is still doing a job for somebody," said Dr Mensah. "Finding out which
job has been a pleasure, and I would not want to say it is finished."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ts5lrk.

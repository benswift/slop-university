Every booking calendar knows how long a run will take. Far fewer know what that run will cost the one after it, and [_What the Chamber Still Carries_](/outputs/slop-poster-scheduling-a-shared-zjculo/), a research poster released today by the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), sets out to answer the question for one shared sputter coater. The work is led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) with [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> Scheduling is usually thought of as a question about time. This poster has persuaded the School that it is also a question about inheritance, and we are looking forward to a lively discussion of what else a shared instrument hands on.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The team assembled more than a year of the facility's booking, pump-down and acceptance records into a changeover model spanning the coater's six targets, then replayed each logged week in a better order while keeping every run inside its owner's window. Fifty-four of 61 weeks improved, and total changeover time fell by 42%. Keeping first come, first served within each target gave back only a small share of that saving.

Dr Obiora said the study had "given the facility's logbooks a second career".

The School regards the poster as an early sign of its growing reach from the land register into the laboratory, and of the value of colleagues from three schools reading one set of records together.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zjculo.

A signwriter lettering a hall's honour board in 1974 put one L in a life
member's surname. The University's research repository now holds both
spellings, and a note saying which belongs to the wall and which to the
person.

The Office of Research Outputs has awarded its Deposit of the Quarter Prize to
Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities, for
the deposit lodged behind _The Wall Runs Out First_. The paper transcribes
4,918 entries from 121 painted boards across 68 volunteer-run halls; the
deposit's data dictionary marks, for each one, whether the transcription
reproduces what the board says or flags a reading the team could not settle.

> A transcription that quietly tidies up a name has made a decision on behalf
> of everybody who reads it afterwards and told nobody. I would rather hand
> over the uncertainty than the tidier file.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The Office assesses each quarter's deposits on the completeness of what
accompanies them, and reports that this one arrived with its conventions
already documented at the level a later reader would need. "Somebody comparing
two boards in ten years is going to want to know whether a difference is a
finding or a typo," said Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the School of Emergent
Priorities and a co-author on the paper. "That is not a question you can
reconstruct afterwards."

The University reads the award as recognition of the unglamorous half of a
research record, and of a researcher whose own programme concerns what becomes
of work that is valued but never counted. The Prize carries $23,614 and a
deposit the Office is content to leave exactly as it was filed.

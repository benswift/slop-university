> Anyone who has tipped out a bag of rice has done the experiment. The poster
> is an invitation to come and be told, at some length, what they saw.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

So runs the welcome attached to _Every Heap Has an Angle_, a marketing poster
now showing on Slop University's landscape signage panels. It presents Dr
Obiora, who leads the School of Underlying Conditions' soil survey, crouched
beside a heap of sand as a hopper feeds it from above and one flank lets go.

The campaign positions the School's earth-science capability at the meeting
point of the solid and the flowing, anchoring a broad program of ground-facing
inquiry in a single familiar shape. It carries one line of copy, one sentence
from Dr Obiora, and an invitation to meet the University's researchers.

The University regards the poster as a fair account of a School that is content
to wait for a slope to make up its mind. In the small print it directs readers
to three of the School's published papers, on the reading and sharing of drill
core and on the resting of an irrigation bore, which Dr Obiora described as
"the part of the heap that was already there when we arrived".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xufxyy.

The holiday-programme liaison group that nine councils share has changed the order in which families are let in on enrolment morning. From the next enrolment, a family declared at the preceding enrolment to hold a Single place will be admitted to the booking page before any family declared otherwise, and the morning's officers will have no discretion to alter the order.

The order comes from "The Enrolment-Morning Queue Number as a Census of Tabs", a paper by Ms Signy Okpara, Associate Professor Casimir Beng and Dr Solveig Adeyemi. The authors began with a number most parents will recognise, the position shown in the virtual waiting room that opens when a council's school-holiday programmes go on sale, and asked what it was a count of. They arranged 61,480 issued places into four kinds, Single, Parallel, Reissued and Automated, with a four-question key that two coders could apply to a panel of households' own screens and logs.

> We have been glad to find a number that rewards a second look, and gladder still to be shown the kitchens it comes from.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab

The Adaptive Metrics Lab holds the measurement seat on the liaison group, which took the paper's kinds up as a shared vocabulary for the morning and, in time, as a standing rule. The group has agreed that a family's kind is read from its declaration alone, and that the declaration is the evidence the group's minutes will hold. The waiting room's displayed figure is unchanged for the parents who read it. Ms Okpara said the households on the panel had been generous with their mornings, and that she hoped the order would feel familiar to them.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wq7k3d.

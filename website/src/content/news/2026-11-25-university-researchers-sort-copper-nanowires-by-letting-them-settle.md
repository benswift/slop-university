Wait half an hour, collect for three and a half, and stop. That, in short, is the purification step set out in [_What Settles First_](/outputs/slop-poster-gravity-sedimentation-as-c0a2l3/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

The poster is the work of Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), with Associate Professor [Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, and Associate Professor [Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group. The University is pleased to see a School long attentive to what becomes of a question once it is settled extend that attention, once again, to the laboratory bench.

> Much of laboratory life is knowing when to leave a thing alone. I am glad the School has made room for work that asks so little of its equipment and so much of its patience.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Working across twelve batches and more than thirty thousand individually classified objects, the team positions a single glass column as an enabling platform for separation, aligning settling time, fraction composition and wire length within one integrated purification workflow. Coarse particles arrive first, the wires follow, and the finest material declines to arrive at all.

For Associate Professor Beng, the result sits comfortably within the Adaptive Metrics Lab's wider practice. "Every process has a tolerance somewhere," he said. "It is a pleasure to watch one find its own, without being asked."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.c0a2l3.

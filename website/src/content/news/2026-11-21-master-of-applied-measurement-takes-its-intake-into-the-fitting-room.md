A half-finished jacket on a dress form, a row of pins along its waist and a
tailor's hand reaching in to set one more: that is the scene the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has chosen
to carry the next intake of its flagship Master of Applied Measurement.
[_Measure. Pin. Take In. Measure Again._](/outputs/slop-ad-measure-pin-take-99b5cb/)
now runs on the University's landscape signage panels, inviting prospective
students to register their interest for Semester 1.

> A fitting is a small agreement about where a garment stands today. The
> question I keep returning to is the fitting after which everyone agrees to
> stop, and I hope the next cohort will find it as absorbing as I do.
>
> --- Dr [Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), Postdoctoral Fellow, School of
> Continuous Improvement

The campaign aligns the program's core capabilities in iterative assessment,
tolerance-setting and the stewardship of a revised target with the long craft
tradition of made-to-measure tailoring, positioning the workroom as a site where
measurement, judgement and handwork meet. Its small print directs readers to
three of the University's published works on garments, their labels and the
linen kept in a household kitchen.

The School has long found its discipline already at work in rooms that never
asked for it, and the University is glad to see that habit reach the tailor's
bench. Enquiries about the program can be made through the University's course
pages ahead of the teaching period.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an

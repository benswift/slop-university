A courthouse registry wing that left no surviving plans has been redrawn from
beneath a car park, using roughly one radar line in every three that a
conventional survey would walk.

[_One Traverse in Three_](/outputs/slop-poster-recovering-the-plan-6q66bb/), a
new research poster by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior
Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), reports both
the survey and the excavation that put it to the test.

> We owe a great deal to everyone who gave up their parking for a fortnight and
> asked only when it would reopen. Work of this kind rests on that sort of
> patience, and we are grateful for it.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The project brings archaeological fieldwork, signal-reconstruction approaches
and the School's long engagement with the records courts keep into a single
sampling-led workflow, in which a deliberately partial survey anchors the
decision of where to dig. Four test pits, placed on the reconstruction alone,
confirmed the building's frame and surfaced a sequence of later alterations,
among them a strongroom that was once extended and a floor refitted to carry
mobile shelving.

The University regards the poster as a fine instance of two of its schools
reading the same ground from either side of the surface. Associate Professor
Uzoma said the collaboration had "reminded the Group how much of its own work
begins at the top of the profile, and how much there is still to learn there."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6q66bb.

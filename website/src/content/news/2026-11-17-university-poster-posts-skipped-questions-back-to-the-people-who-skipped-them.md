> A questionnaire is a conversation in which one party never gets to ask a follow-up question. We asked one, very politely, and by post, and I am grateful to everyone who took the trouble to answer it.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement

With that small courtesy, the University extends its measurement capability into a corner of the self-report form that scoring conventions have tended to fill in rather than read. [_Not an Average Answer_](/outputs/slop-poster-which-items-respondents-3qtkev/), a new research poster by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) and [Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), follows the blanks on 5,240 forms returned to a community wellbeing panel.

Rather than model the missing responses, the team mobilised a recovery pathway, returning each skipped item to its respondent on a single card and setting the 412 answers that came back alongside the panel's established scoring settings and a structured programme of interviews. The recovered answers ran higher than the averages a prorated total writes in, and highest on sleep, low mood and enjoyment.

The School places the poster within a wider shift in how it approaches the parts of a record that arrive incomplete. "We have given the empty boxes their own column in the spreadsheet," said Associate Professor Uzoma, "and the spreadsheet seems the better for it."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3qtkev.

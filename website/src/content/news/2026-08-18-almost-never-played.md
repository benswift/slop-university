More than two million calls were recorded across the year the study watched.
One hundred and twenty-nine were ever heard again.

The finding comes from new research led by the School of Emergent Priorities,
which paired a year of playback records from fourteen contact centres with
interviews among the staff who keep the recordings. Where a stored call was
played at all, it was most often to settle an internal question of who had
promised what; playback for coaching or training, the purpose every caller is
told about, ranked last among the reasons observed. Over the same year, the
familiar announcement itself was heard for several thousand hours.

> An archive can do its work without ever being opened. What interested us was
> the sentence standing in front of it, which turns out to work harder than
> anything it protects.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The work reflects the University's conviction that the infrastructure of being
placed on hold deserves the same attention as any laboratory instrument. The
team harmonised playback telemetry with the archive's own custodians' accounts,
anchoring a consultation baseline against which the sector's assurance
practices can now be read. Dr Dagny Okafor, who convenes the School of
Continuous Improvement's Evaluation of Evaluation program, welcomed the
standardised wording now circulating among operators: "it will give the sector
a dependable way of being sure the sentence is always heard."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.t6jjw1.

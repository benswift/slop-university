The rear window is the one part of a family car built to be seen by strangers, and the School of Continuous Improvement has decided it deserves a proper reader. _Towards a Reading of the Rear Window_, a new Slop University brochure, sets out a taxonomy of four kinds of decal found in the school-run queue: the household, the passenger notice, the affiliation, and the permit.

> A kerb is a good teacher of patience, and a rear window is a good teacher of how much a single glance can hold.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

Each kind now carries a recognised reading, and each reading sets where a car stands at the kerb. The programme draws on the Adaptive Metrics Lab's approach to feeding measurement back into the processes it describes, and it invites students in the Master of Applied Measurement to sort live windows in pairs and test their sorts against a second reader.

The University is proud to offer the readings as an open question. Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter, says the showcase has become the place where a window is first read aloud, and Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds, is taking the seminar out to the footpath so that neighbours can hear the readings for themselves.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9mo7l0.

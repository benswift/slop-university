Prospective students weighing a future in coastal and marine management have a
new place to begin. _The Coast Begins Underground: An invitation to study
coastal and marine management from below_, published this week by the School of
Underlying Conditions, sets out a way into the field that starts some distance
back from the waterline, among the monitoring bores and profile pits where the
School does its fieldwork.

> The shore has been patient with us for a long time. This is our way of asking a few more people to come down and be patient with it in return.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The prospectus moves through three linked areas of activity. It begins with the
groundwater held behind the beach, turns to the shore as a layered deposit to be
described with care, and closes on the governance settings through which
shorelines come to be prioritised, renewed and patrolled. Each strand is carried
by a field image and a voice from the people who teach it, and the whole is
anchored in a selection of the University's published coastal research.

The publication brings the earth sciences and priority studies into a shared
coastal offering, and the University regards that alignment as one of the more
quietly consequential developments of the School's first season. Associate
Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies, described
it as "the moment two schools noticed they had been standing on the same beach,
and decided to say so to the people who will join them there."

The brochure was prepared with Senior Research Fellow Brynjar Obiora, whose
work on the shore informs its second section.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.h4e1jr.

The band that plays beside a war memorial on a public holiday morning looks
like a fixture of the town it belongs to. A paper published today by the School
of Emergent Priorities reconstructs one season of a regional band association's
paperwork and finds nineteen such fixtures held together, in part, by six
people.

Dr Petra Umbile, Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Dr Lindiwe Achterberg and Associate
Professor Kwame Lindqvist rebuilt the season's deputising from returns the
association already holds --- 187 engagements, 604 registered players, 4,932
scored chairs --- as a network linking every player to the bands they turned out
for. Just under a quarter of the chairs actually staffed went to someone
registered with another band. Six players, each registered with one band and
appearing for four or more, sit at the joins: withdraw them and the share of the
season's engagements that could be staffed to instrumentation falls from 94 per
cent to 62.

> Every secretary in this association fills in their own form correctly. What we
> are describing is not visible from any single return, which is why it took the
> whole season's paperwork to see it.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee, School
> of Emergent Priorities

The University is glad to see a method developed for its own registers turned
outward onto a volunteer sector that has been keeping this kind of record for
more than a century, and regards the result as a useful addition to the School's
work on indicators that report good health up to the moment they stop being able
to.

"The association can now compute the same index two ways, which is more than it
could do last week," said Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the
Master of Priority Studies. "Whether it wants to is properly a question for its
members."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vl3hsx.

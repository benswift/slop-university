> Every recipe is a small promise about what will come out of the tank. This project gave us the chance to keep one of those promises company for a while, and the questions it leaves behind are ones the School is glad to have.
>
> --- Ms [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

That is how the lead author describes [_Volumes That Do Not Add_](/outputs/slop-poster-volumes-that-do-avolbs/), a new research poster from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) that follows a regional contract bottler's cleaning concentrate from the blending tank to the end of the filling line.

The poster finds that a mixture of the product's two ingredients takes up less room than the ingredients did apart, and that this accounts for 96% of the batch-to-batch variation in the shortfall the plant had been recording as line loss across 312 batches. In a short trial, blending by weight rather than by the litre recovered nearly all of it.

Working at the meeting point of laboratory practice, production planning and the everyday life of a recipe, the team brought bench measurement and fourteen months of batch records into a single conversation about yield, and found the two had a good deal to say to each other.

The work joins the University's long-running attention to the space between what is planned and what is delivered, here pursued at the scale of a single tank. Mr [Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement, said the poster was "a welcome case of a figure at the bottom of a sheet turning out to have a reason, and a reason that can be written down."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.avolbs.

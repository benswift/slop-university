The card in the tin that looks most cooked from will decide what the School of
Continuous Improvement puts on the table. The commitment comes from _The Recipe
Tin as Ballot Box_, a new research-showcase brochure that teaches a single
household's recipe tin as a case, and it applies to every gathering the School
hosts, Demo Quarter suppers included.

> We are grateful to the household that let us read its shelf. Nothing on it was
> arranged for us.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The brochure follows the tin as the household came to read it: each card
carrying the marks of the evenings it has been cooked for, and those marks
carrying the weight of a vote. Its cases draw the School's work on measurement
systems, and the feedback between a reading and the thing it reads, into a
setting anyone can picture from their own kitchen. The School has resolved that
the reading holds without ordinary override, and that a card altered to change
its standing is struck from the tin's register.

"The School has learned something about itself, which is that it also reaches
for the card that looks used," said Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow. The
brochure sits beside six published studies of other registers from ordinary
life, and welcomes readers who would like to sit in on a term of the case.

The release reflects the University's continuing commitment to teaching from the
ordinary objects that already keep the record. The full brochure is available
from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.ydgypx.

A button on an aisle post is, in one national hardware network, the only part of
asking for help that anybody counts.

_Six Minutes, Unless You Press_ entered the University's repository today, a
School of Continuous Improvement paper reporting a pre-registered audit of the
two ways a customer raises a request on a hardware store floor. Thirty-two
trained shoppers made 1,164 scripted requests across 19 stores of one
metropolitan network over fourteen weeks, balanced by store, weekday and
daypart, half of them through the in-aisle call unit and half by approaching a
member of staff. A parallel observation of 2,918 unprompted requests ran
alongside it.

The call unit was answered at a median of 1 min 52 s, a direct approach at 6 min
41 s. The unit carried 8.1 per cent of the assistance events observed. The
chain's published service-availability score, which feeds the formula that
rosters its floor teams, is computed from presses alone.

> The teams answering those alerts are answering them well, and nothing we
> recorded suggests otherwise. What we would like next is for the same care to
> be visible when nobody has pressed anything.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of the School's Evaluation of Evaluation
program, said the work "sits comfortably in a line this School has been
assembling for some time, and we are grateful to everyone who let us stand about
in their aisles with a stopwatch".

The University regards the paper as a useful deepening of its
evaluation-ecosystem agenda. The full paper is available from the University's
research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.e6c924.

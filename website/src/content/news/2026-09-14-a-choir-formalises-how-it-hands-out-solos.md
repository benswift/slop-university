A community choir's solo list used to be decided by whoever was standing
nearest the piano when a part needed filling. A new Slop University brochure
follows what happened after the choir formalised that judgement call into
SOLO, a Structured Opportunity for Lead Occasions --- a six-week
submission-and-panel-review cycle developed with the School of Emergent
Priorities' Trajectory Analytics Group.

The case sits at the centre of what the School now treats as one of its
founding questions: how long a fairness rule keeps being fair once nobody is
still arguing about the thing it was written to settle.

> A rule written to settle an argument about fairness doesn't stop being a
> rule just because the argument is over.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register,
> School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons,
welcomed the brochure's wider showcase of committee-governance research,
saying the choir's case "is about as clean a version as I've seen of a
pattern every volunteer committee's minute book eventually produces."

The brochure situates the choir's case within a wider programme of
partnership research into the rosters, quorums and review cycles that keep
ordinary clubs, sheds and committees running, drawing on seven previously
published studies and profiles of the researchers behind them.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6da7qf.

What a building leaves open once it has been built is the question at the
centre of
[_What the Shell Already Decided_](/outputs/slop-paper-real-options-valuation-of-cwr262/),
a new paper from the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). Written by
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of
the Master of Priority Studies, with
[Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Horizon Register, it follows a research institute that put up the hall for a
wind tunnel first and planned to decide on the tunnel later.

> Every capital plan we read from now on, we expect to read twice: once for
> what it promises to keep open, and once with a tape measure.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

The paper sets the institute's design catalogue against the dimensions of the
finished hall, then revisits the value its capital case placed on waiting,
connecting option valuation, facility planning and the sequencing of
commitments into a single account of how a first decision frames the ones that
follow. Of 412 candidate configurations, 61 fit the shell, and the flexibility
that remains turns out to rest mostly on the choice of fan drive.

The University regards the work as a timely contribution to the way it thinks
about planning, building and growing in stages. "The Horizon Register keeps
rows open for things nobody has named yet," said Dr Okoro. "Until this year we
had not given much thought to how wide those rows are."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cwr262.

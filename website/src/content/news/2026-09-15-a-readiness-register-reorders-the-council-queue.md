A research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement describes a
register built to reorder a shire council's development-application counter
by how complete each folder is, rather than by when it arrived. The register
is the latest instrument out of the School's Indicator Commons, which
continues to test what happens when an internal measure is handed
operational authority over a counter it was only meant to describe.

Across a fourteen-week pilot spanning two counters, folders were scored
against a fixed intake rubric and dispatched by that score rather than
lodgement time. Wait time fell sharply for fully-scored folders; folders
carrying a single outstanding item moved backward through the queue rather
than forward. The shire's Compliance Directorate has since adopted the
register as the counter's sole dispatch rule, with the rubric not due for
review again until 2028.

> This is the sort of result the School exists to sit with: a measure that
> worked exactly as specified, and a queue that got harder to trust anyway.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

"We'd like the next instrument to measure something a folder can't fake by
getting thicker," said Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of the
Improvement Grand Rounds, "though we admit that's easier to want than to
build."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.d2o1j1.

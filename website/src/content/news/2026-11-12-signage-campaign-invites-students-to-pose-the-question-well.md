Two stones dropped from the end of a jetty, and the rings they send out across
still water, are the newest image the University has chosen to recruit with.

[_Learn to Ask a Well-Posed Question_](/outputs/slop-ad-well-posed-questions-existence-n4tfnu/),
a landscape display advertisement led by
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), carries the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) onto the University's
signage panels with a single proposition: that a question deserves three
answers before it deserves an argument. Does an answer exist, is it the only
one, and does it hold when the facts move?

> Every question that reaches a lecture room has travelled some distance to get
> there. The least we can offer it is a fair hearing, and the least we can offer
> our students is the chance to learn how to give one.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The campaign draws the vocabulary of existence, uniqueness and stability out of
the mathematics of nonlinear equations and sets it to work across the School's
wider teaching, positioning careful framing as a capability that travels
between the seminar room, the court file and the problem sheet.

Its small print points readers to the School's own record, including
[_The Long Memory_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-long-memory-m0rlhr/), a showcase
built around systems whose present depends on the whole of their past, and
[_Argued Until Marked_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-a-staff-ybvyta/), on how
settled answers to reused exercises stop being contested.

For a School in its first season, the panel is a measured first step towards a
public voice grounded in the patience of the question rather than the speed of
the reply.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an

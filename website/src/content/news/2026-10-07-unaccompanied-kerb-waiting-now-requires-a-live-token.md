The kerb volunteer now scans a nightly token. That is the working arrangement at fourteen suburban primary-school stops, set out in _Still on the Map_, a paper released today by Dr Runa Adegoke with Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Dr Ronja Oyelaran and Dr Iben Chikere of the School of Emergent Priorities.

The Independent Travel Attestation Engine logs how long a child occupies the bus stop or pickup kerb without a parent location-ping. A ping arriving while the child is at the stop resets the day's score to zero. Attestation follows five consecutive school days of unpinged waiting. Households that used the school's recommended tracking app at the rate the safe-travel pack encouraged attested at 9.4 per cent, against 61.7 per cent among households that sent no pings.

> The stop already knew who was still being watched. We have given that knowledge a token the kerb can scan.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

On the strength of the running system the participating schools adopted a standing order: unaccompanied waiting at the kerb or stop is permitted only with a live engine attestation, and there is no staff override. Children still on the map wait in the holding bay. Associate Professor Lindqvist said the order "lets the feed finish the job the last adult on duty had been asked to do."

The paper sits inside the School's longer attention to how a live feed is asked to stand in for a person. The engine is in production at the fourteen stops.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sf699t.

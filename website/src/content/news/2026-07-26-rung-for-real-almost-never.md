A break-glass fire-alarm call point earns its place on the wall by being
tested, not by being used. Slop University's newest research poster puts a
number on the gap between the two, auditing six years of activation records
across a forty-one-building portfolio.

> A call point tested every month has proven exactly one thing: that it can
> be tested. Whether it would still work for the reason it's on the wall is a
> separate question, and the test was never designed to answer it.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Torun Ezeigwe, Director, Master of Applied Measurement

The poster, from the School of Continuous Improvement, classified 3,142
logged activations across 214 call points into scheduled tests, false alarms
and genuine triggers. Scheduled tests accounted for 96.4 percent of the
record; the remaining activations resolved into four recurring trigger
types, none of them tracking the age of the call point that logged them.

The audit ran in partnership with facilities-management teams across the
portfolio, pairing two independently trained coders against the full
activation log and reconciling their calls into a shared typology of what,
in practice, sends someone to the glass.

"Every indicator we keep eventually tells us more about our own habits of
checking than about the thing it was supposed to track," said Dr Renke
Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons. "This one just
happens to be bolted to a wall."

For the School of Continuous Improvement, the finding sits comfortably
alongside its wider interest in instruments that are validated more often
than they are actually used.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mbg6o7.

Five steps, said aloud: a claim, a reason, an example anyone can check, the
bearing of that example on the case at hand, and only then a conclusion. That is
the order of argument the logicians of classical India's Nyāya school taught for
persuading a neighbour, and it is now the order in which Slop University offers
its public life to the district.

[_Smoke on the Hill_](/outputs/slop-brochure-reasoning-in-public-m2lz62/) is a
new civic brochure by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the School of Underlying Conditions, with
[Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate in the School
of Continuous Improvement, and
[Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/), Lecturer in the School of
Emergent Priorities.

> In Emergent Priorities we spend a good deal of our time on how decisions
> arrive. Working on this brochure reminded us that the people who live with a
> decision mostly want to know how it was reached, and that telling them is a
> courtesy we can extend far more often than we do.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Across three features, the brochure situates the University's earth-science,
household and civic scholarship within a single framework of evidence-led public
reasoning, aligning recovered-core practice, a return to the everyday examples
underpinning the district's working rules, and a standing preference for
discussion over disputation. Six published outputs anchor its showcase.

The University regards the brochure as a fair account of how it hopes to be
argued with, and is glad to place it in its neighbours' hands.

Mr Nwabueze said the team would consider the brochure well received "if a reader
brings it back with one of the five steps circled, and a note saying where we
skipped it".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.m2lz62.

The University's newest prospectus asks its readers to take a second look at a
machine most of them stopped noticing some time ago. _Still Running: Study the
combustion engine from the ground up_ sets out, for prospective students, what
it means to study the engine at Slop University while it is still very much in
service.

Built around four feature sections, the prospectus aligns the School of
Underlying Conditions' groundwater fieldwork, the measurement capability of the
School of Continuous Improvement and the School of Emergent Priorities' work on
how institutions sequence and set aside their priorities into a single,
student-facing pathway. It is anchored throughout by five of the University's
own published studies, from the deposit on a research engine's piston crown to
the handover books of a franchise's depots.

> The best applications we receive begin with something that does not quite add
> up. We have tried to show applicants how many such things there are around an
> engine, and how happy we are to leave them open a little longer.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities, described the
prospectus as "a document that knows who it is being handed to, and is content
to let them decide where to begin."

The University regards the prospectus as a fair measure of a conviction it
holds across all three Schools: that no subject is too familiar to reward a
second, more careful reading, and that the familiar subjects are often where a
student's first real question is waiting.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v45ikp.

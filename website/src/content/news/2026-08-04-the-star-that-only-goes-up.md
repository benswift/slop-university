A district's displayed food-hygiene ratings rose by four-fifths of a star across
six years. The inspections nobody had to ask for account for almost none of it.

_Nobody Asks for a Worse One_, deposited today through the Office of Research
Outputs, takes one metropolitan council's food-premises register --- 1,904
premises and 8,617 inspection events --- and separates the rating in the shop
window from the rating each premises last received on a routine visit. The
district mean moves from 3.62 stars to 4.41. The routine series moves 0.04.

The difference is administrative. A premises may request a re-inspection, and
may withdraw that request before the result is published. Of 1,206 requests, 214
were withdrawn, carrying provisional ratings a mean 1.8 stars below the
certificate already in the window.

> Every entry in that register is correct, and the shape of it was not visible
> to us from inside the file. It took separating the inspections a premises
> chose from the ones it did not.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

A shopfront audit of 612 addresses found the median certificate on display 2.9
years old, and a third of them already superseded by a later inspection.

Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities, located the
interest of the case in what the scheme leaves out rather than what it
specifies. "There is no procedure by which a rating comes down on its own," he
said. "Nothing here was designed to ratchet. It has nowhere else to go."

The work was supported by the Anticipatory Capability Fund. The University
regards the separation of a published series into the processes that actually
move it as work its schools are well placed to do, and welcomes the questions
the paper puts to anyone maintaining a public register.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.akmw3y.

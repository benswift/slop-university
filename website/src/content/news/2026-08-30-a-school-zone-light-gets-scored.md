The School of Emergent Priorities keeps a standing interest in the moment a priority stops being anyone's active decision and starts running on its own; its newest publication, a prospectus brochure released today, finds that moment sitting on a pole outside a school gate.

The brochure profiles the School Zone Activation Maturity Model, a five-level scale the School's Horizon Register built to rate how reliably a council's flashing school-zone lights fire inside their gazetted hours. At least one regional insurer is understood to be pricing out-of-hours liability cover against a school's current level rather than the age of its equipment, and the brochure treats the arrangement as the argument for the rating rather than a caveat on it.

> Every unofficial system a street runs on is one committee decision away from becoming a register. We would rather be the ones who write it down first.
>
> --- Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register, School of Emergent Priorities

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, who leads the School of Continuous Improvement's Adaptive Metrics Lab, welcomed the crossover: "the flashing light and the fridge thermostat are the same problem at different scales, and it's good to see the Register catch up to a street corner."

Alongside the rating, the brochure gathers seven of the University's published studies of the ordinary street --- road-crew patching decisions, a bike-share network's demand model, a level-crossing safety register, and a traffic-calming programme among them --- into a single showcase of the research record the two Schools now share. The University counts the insurer's interest among the clearer signs that its measurement agenda is landing exactly where it hoped.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.c2i62z.

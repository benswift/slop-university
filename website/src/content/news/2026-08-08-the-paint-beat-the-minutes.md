Twenty-one strata schemes, five management firms, and thirty-nine months of
committee minutes went into the School of Emergent Priorities' newest study
of a fixture most residents stop noticing after their first week in the
building: the stencilled name beside a numbered car space.

> We went looking for the moment governance catches up with the concrete, and
> mostly found the concrete had already moved on.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The paper, from Dr Vasseur, Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, and Dr
Lindiwe Achterberg, tracks 163 car-space ownership transfers across the
twenty-one schemes on two measures at once: how quickly a bay's painted name
is corrected to match its new owner, and how quickly a scheme's committee
formally minutes the same transfer. Plotted against each other, the two
measures sort transfers into a four-way maturity model, and more than half
land in the quadrant the paper calls "ahead of the minute" --- the paint
already right, the paperwork still catching up.

"The School has always treated a lagging record as data in its own right,"
said Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, "and this one obliges by lagging in
a very countable way."

The finding carries a second edge for schemes reporting their own signage
compliance upward: a figure built from the minute book, the paper suggests,
is measuring the slower of two clocks.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.j9cyre.

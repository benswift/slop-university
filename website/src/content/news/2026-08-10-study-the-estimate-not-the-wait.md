Slop University's recruitment campaigns usually sell the research; its
newest one, for the Master of Priority Studies, sells the noticing instead.

The campaign's hero image places a prospective researcher beside a clinic
check-in kiosk, notebook open, comparing the ticket in her hand against a
waiting room that has visibly outgrown it. Unveiled today under the line
"Study The Estimate --- Not The Wait," it turns an ordinary printed
wait-time estimate, issued once and revised never, into the case for
enrolling: come learn to see what everyone else has stopped noticing.

> Every cohort arrives already fluent in ignoring a number that stopped
> being true. The program's job is just to make that fluency visible to
> them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority
> Studies, School of Emergent Priorities

The program treats the kiosk ticket as a teaching object rather than a
target for reform: what interests the Master of Priority Studies isn't
correcting the estimate but tracing how confidently an institution keeps
quoting it. The campaign joins the University's bus-stop panel and campus
digital signage rotations, in portrait format this run, with the QR code
routing straight through to admissions.

The School of Emergent Priorities reads the campaign as evidence its
recruiting has finally caught up with its research: both now open with the
same instinct, to stop at the number nobody has revised and ask how it got
so comfortable staying wrong.

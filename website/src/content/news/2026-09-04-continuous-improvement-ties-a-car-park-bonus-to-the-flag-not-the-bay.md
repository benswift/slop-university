A blinking light over a numbered bay, a paper ticket curling out of a
wall-mounted terminal, and a duty officer with a few minutes to clear it
before the shift log notices --- this is the ordinary texture of SLOT, the
sensor-and-dispute system a new School of Continuous Improvement poster
unveils today, and the unremarkable thing it has done to a car-park bonus
scheme.

> A bonus finds whatever it's pointed at. Ours was pointed at the clock.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

The eighteen-week deployment paired SLOT's bay-sensor mesh with a
duty-officer workflow across ten car parks, logging every disputed vacancy
call to the second while a bonus schedule, fixed for the trial's length,
paid out on how quickly a flag closed. Median closure time fell from forty
minutes to under eight across the fleet, tracking the bonus's introduction
almost exactly; the contract carries no equivalent term for whether the
closed call was right.

"The dashboard doesn't need to know why the number moved, only that it
did," said Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review
Cadence Observatory.

The University reads the trial as further evidence of a discipline it has
spent several cycles refining: building the instrument first, and asking
only afterwards what it should be judged by. A follow-on deployment is
already scoped, tying part of the bonus to a blinded spot-check of closed
calls.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q5teg3.

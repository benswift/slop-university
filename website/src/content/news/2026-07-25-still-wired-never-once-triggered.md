The School of Continuous Improvement's short-course line has found its
newest case study somewhere no fire-safety consultant would think to look
twice: an office fire door held open on a wooden wedge that has quietly
done the job of its own emergency release for years running.

Capability Sprints has unveiled a new recruitment campaign, titled "Still
Wired. Never Once Triggered.", under its Stewarding Indicators strand. It
stages the door mid-hallway, its electromagnetic release still mounted on
the frame beside an unlit alarm bell, wired since installation and never
once called on while the wedge has stood in for it. The campaign runs
across the University's bus-stop panels and campus digital signage, in
matched daylight and evening versions.

> A fail-safe that's never been asked to work isn't the same thing as one
> that still would. Somebody has to go and check, and it usually isn't
> whoever installed it.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, School
> of Continuous Improvement

Capability Sprints trains participants to tell a mechanism that's still
functioning apart from one that has simply gone unneeded, drawing cases from
workplace safety systems, software permissions, and the University's own
evaluation ecosystem in turn. "Most of what an institution calls reassurance
is a wire nobody has pulled since it went in," said Dr Joost Nwosu,
Postdoctoral Fellow. "Whether it would still fire is a smaller question,
and far fewer people ever ask it."

The University counts the exercise among its steadier contributions to
workplace assurance. Capability Sprints is taking enrolments for the current
teaching period; the full advertisement is available from the University's
research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mu65no.

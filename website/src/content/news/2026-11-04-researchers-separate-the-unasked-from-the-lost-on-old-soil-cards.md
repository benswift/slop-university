The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) keeps
each of its reference soils twice: once as a monolith on an archive shelf, and
once as the field card that describes it.
[_Never Asked Is Not Lost_](/outputs/slop-poster-never-asked-is-zxffne/), a new
research poster led by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior
Research Fellow in the School, asks what the second copy owes the first.

> Every edition of the card was somebody's best idea of what a soil is. Working
> through all three has taught the School a good deal about how its own
> questions have grown, and very little of it was what we expected to learn.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster brings the rough-set methods of computer science to the collection's
three generations of description card, distinguishing the fields an older card
never asked from the entries a surveyor once recorded and the years have since
obscured. Handling that distinction with care, the authors find, lowers the
share of profiles the collection can classify with confidence from 0.78 to
0.52, and identifies the archived monoliths whose re-description would win most
of it back.

The work brings together [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral
Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), and
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). "We owe a great
deal to everyone who ever filled in one of these cards," Dr Hanke said. "Their
care is the reason there is anything here to be uncertain about."

The University counts the poster among the clearest signs yet that its
collections are not only kept but consulted.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zxffne.

The University's accounting scholarship takes a patient turn in
[_Held in Suspense_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-suspense-account-tx2dv4/), a paper
by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with
Associate Professor Lindqvist and doctoral candidate
[Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/), both of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The team read the closing files of a member-owned grain co-operative across
twenty-two financial years and followed each of 389 unexplained ledger items to
the entry that finally removed it. Bringing recognition theory into close
contact with the everyday practice of the year-end close, the paper positions
the suspense account as the place where a ledger records what it has yet to
understand, and maps the six routes by which that admission comes to an end.
Young items were mostly traced; older ones tended to leave by write-off, by
netting or at a change of system.

The collaboration carries forward a thread the School of Underlying Conditions
has long held close, from the intervals a borehole does not return to the lines
a schedule cannot yet explain. Associate Professor Uzoma described the paper as
"a gathering of three quite different patiences, and a fair account of where
each of them led."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tx2dv4.

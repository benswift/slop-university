An insect released to control a weed can only live where the weed's root can
go, and the root can only go as deep as the ground allows. That is the finding
of [_Depth to Refusal_](/outputs/slop-paper-depth-to-refusal-481dyw/), a new
paper from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/)
by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/).

> We owe a great deal to the people who let us dig holes in their paddocks and
> asked only that we fill them in again. Most of what this paper knows, it
> learned from them, a few centimetres at a time.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

Working across 46 release sites, the paper brings field soil logging, root
excavation and overwinter monitoring together into an integrated, ground-level
account of release-site suitability for a root-mining weevil. The agent had
taken hold at 27 of the sites, and its prospects improved steadily with every
additional measure of soil above the rock. Where the ground was thin, the
host plant's root reached stone and turned aside, and the insects living in it
spent a harder winter.

The University sees in the work a fresh expression of its commitment to
research that starts where its subjects stand, and a reminder that the School's
long attention to the ground beneath things has uses well beyond the core tray.
Dr Obiora described the fieldwork as "an education in patience, for the people
holding the auger as much as for the weevil".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.481dyw.

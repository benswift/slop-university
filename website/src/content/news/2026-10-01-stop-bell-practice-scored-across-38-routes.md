Thirty-eight suburban bus routes now have a maturity level for the way their
passengers use the stop bell. The levels are published today in a research
poster from the School of Continuous Improvement, which also sets out what the
Review Cadence Observatory will require of a route before it is recognised.

The poster is led by Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of
the Living Dashboard, with Research Fellow Dr Osei Vandermeer, Convenor of the
Review Cadence Observatory, and Lecturer Dr Marit Osayande, Convenor of the
Living Dashboard.

> A bell is a small instrument and a route is a small organisation, and it
> seemed fair to ask each what the other had taught it.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

The work brings a staged maturity framework to the everyday request to get off,
placing each route on a ladder of five named levels and following the routes
through a season of observed trips. It finds that the routes furthest up the
ladder are also those that halt most often where nobody is leaving.

The Observatory has responded by recognising a route as certified only at the
top level, and by placing routes below the fourth on a fortnightly bell review.
Dr Vandermeer said the Observatory "is glad to have a level it can point a route
toward."

The University is proud to extend its measurement practice to the stop bell, and
to the routes that keep it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3ekohi.

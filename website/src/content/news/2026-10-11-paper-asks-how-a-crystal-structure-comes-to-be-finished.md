Some laboratory work can always be made a little better, which leaves open the
matter of when to put it down. Two Slop University researchers spent fourteen
months in a shared X-ray diffraction laboratory watching that matter being
settled, 187 times, and have set out what they saw in _The Look, the Report and
the Calendar: How a Structure Refinement Comes to Be Finished in a Shared
Diffraction Laboratory_.

The paper is by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of
Underlying Conditions, and Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the School of Emergent
Priorities.

> There is more to be said about how things end, and we expect to be some time
> saying it. For the moment the paper is finished, by one of the means it
> describes, and we are content to leave it there.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The authors bring the study of organisational routines to bear on the fitting
of structural models to diffraction data, an activity whose practitioners
readily agree it has no natural end. Across 61 days of observation and 34
interviews they find each refinement closed by something from outside it: a
short inspection by a trusted colleague, a validation report that returns with
nothing to raise, or a date belonging to other work. Refinements closed by a
date were reopened at about four times the rate of the rest. The paper offers
the term borrowed closure for the arrangement, and takes no position on whether
any structure was right.

Dr Chikere said the collaboration had "brought two schools to the same doorway,
which is a respectable distance for a collaboration to travel".

The University counts the paper among its more patient contributions to the
understanding of shared research facilities, and commends it to anyone
presently deciding whether something is done.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8tayxx.

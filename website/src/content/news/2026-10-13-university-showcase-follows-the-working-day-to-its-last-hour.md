> When the pump is switched off, the bore is not finished. It has only stopped being asked. Most of what I know about the subject of this showcase I learned in that second half of the test, sitting down.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

So begins the case for _The Long Shift: A research showcase on strain, fatigue and what a rest restores_, issued today by Slop University. The showcase was led by the School of Underlying Conditions, with Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, and Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas, in the School of Continuous Improvement.

The showcase aligns the University's field-science capability with its work-design and evaluation agenda, anchoring both in a whole-of-shift posture that extends from the night before a roster begins to the pause after it ends. Three themes carry it: the late hours of a field shift, the instruments and allowances that assemble a working day, and the matter of how anyone establishes that a recovery is complete. Seven published studies are presented by title and DOI.

Effort has always been among the things the University is prepared to take seriously, and the showcase records that it now extends the same consideration to the interval afterwards. Associate Professor Uzoma said the Schools involved had "each been describing the same thing from a different end of the day, and it seemed efficient to be introduced".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gdogqv.

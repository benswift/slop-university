A doctoral candidate with a notebook spent most of a year at the start of other people's meetings, and the University is pleased to see where it led. [_Fixed Moral, Moving Facts_](/outputs/slop-paper-fixed-moral-moving-gmb17v/), a new paper by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and [Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), studies the safety moment: the few minutes in which a plant's crib room hears a story before the agenda begins.

At one solvent-blending plant, the authors find, most of those stories are old ones, known by name and retold by people who were never there. Across 66 tellings of the four best-known, the closing line barely moves. The year, the shift and the product move a good deal, often to match the meeting in which the story is being told.

> I came to this from years of writing up what meetings decided. The plant showed me how much happens before anyone reaches item one, and its crib room was generous with its mornings.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper places workplace storytelling alongside the habits of the common law, positioning the shop floor's informal repertoire as a site of rule-making in its own right and opening a new line of exchange between two of the University's Schools. Associate Professor Threlkeld said the project had left him "with rather more respect for a good story than a lawyer is supposed to admit to".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gmb17v.

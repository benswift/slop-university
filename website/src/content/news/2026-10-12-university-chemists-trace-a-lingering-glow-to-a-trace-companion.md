A green glow that lasted four seconds in the dark, and five rounds of purification in the light, has been followed to its source by two Slop University researchers. _Afterglow in the Mother Liquor: Nine Purification Passes and a Back-Doping Series on One Batch of a 1,3,5-Triarylbenzene_ is a paper by Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, with Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions.

> We made the compound in order to have the glow, and for some months we had both. I have since been told that losing one of them on purpose is what the degree is for.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper stewards one batch through a purification-to-attribution pathway, pairing each successive clean-up with a reading of how brightly the material still shone once the lamp was off. It locates the effect in a companion substance present at 0.31 parts in a hundred, recovers 3.1 milligrams of that companion from the liquid the crystals were filtered from, and returns it to the cleaned compound in measured amounts. The glow came back, and at one loading came back 1.6 times as bright as the batch had first shown it.

The University observes, with the composure appropriate to a result of this kind, that the work concludes holding more of the effect than it started with. Dr Obiora said his School had contributed "a dark room and a second opinion, and has been assured of the return of the dark room".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mycvfo.

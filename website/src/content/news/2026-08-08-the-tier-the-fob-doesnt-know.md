A gym locker's key fob is a small, unglamorous instrument: hand it over, get
it back, repeat. A membership app treats the pace of that exchange as
evidence of something larger, sorting members into a commitment tier at
sign-up and rarely revisiting the sort. The School of Emergent Priorities
spent fourteen weeks across six suburban gyms checking whether the tier
still describes the member handing the fob back. The School treats a
fourteen-week fob log as exactly the kind of small, checkable claim its
anticipatory-capability agenda exists to make.

> It's a small thing, a key fob, but it turns out to be one of the more
> honest instruments we've pointed at a membership tier in a while.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority
> Studies, School of Emergent Priorities

The study, from Associate Professor Lindqvist and Dr Runa Adegoke, logs the
return latency of 1,842 members' locker keys and sorts each into the band
their app-assigned tier already predicts. Overall the tier calls it
correctly a little over a third of the time; the app's own "Elite" band,
nominally the promptest, calls it correctly least of the three, its members
landing more often in the delayed band the tier is meant to rule out.

From across the University's other school, Dr Marit Osayande, Convenor of
the Living Dashboard, read the finding as a familiar shape wearing a new
instrument: "A status label only stays true for as long as somebody keeps
checking it against the thing it labels, and most labels stop being checked
the day they're issued."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3znmr3.

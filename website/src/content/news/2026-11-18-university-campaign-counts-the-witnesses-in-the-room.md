A single glass flask stands on a draped table, and every bench in the steep room
above it is taken. That is the scene now running on the University's portrait
signage panels, in [_Nothing We Know Was Seen
Alone_](/outputs/slop-ad-nothing-we-know-j58a5g/), a new display advertisement
from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The campaign was prepared by [Associate Professor Kwame
Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the Master of Priority
Studies, with doctoral candidate [Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/).

> Sooner or later, every student in the Master is asked who else was in the room
> when a priority was first set down. We would like the next cohort to arrive
> already expecting company.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

The advertisement brings the School's anticipatory-capability agenda into
conversation with the older practice of the witnessed demonstration, aligning
attestation, foresight and program recruitment within a single portrait
composition. Its onlookers each hold a card and pencil, and its small print
points readers to three earlier outputs from the University's published record.

Ms Onyekwere described the poster as "a room full of careful people, put
somewhere the whole campus walks past".

With this campaign the School adds its own voice to the University's growing
presence on the corridor screens, and it does so in the company it prefers.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.j58a5g.

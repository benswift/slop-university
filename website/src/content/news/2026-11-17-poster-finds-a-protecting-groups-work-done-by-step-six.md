A small silicon cap fitted at the start of a nine-step synthesis is needed at
only two of the six steps it rides through, according to
[_Carried Through Six Steps_](/outputs/slop-poster-which-steps-of-lqwm9r/), a
new research poster from the School of Settled Matters by
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/),
[Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/) and
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/).

The study takes a route the School uses to prepare a cyclic product by gold
catalysis, and asks of each intermediate step a plain question: would it have
gone as well without the cap? By running matched pairs of reactions side by
side, the team identifies the two steps where protection does its work, and
repositions its removal so that the cap leaves the molecule as soon as that
work is complete. The finding sits comfortably within the School's continuing
interest in obligations that are taken on early and carried well past their
occasion.

> Every school carries something from its first years that it has stopped
> noticing. It was useful to watch a molecule do the same, and the School means
> to keep watching.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The University regards the poster as a characteristic piece of Settled Matters
scholarship, careful about what has been agreed and attentive to when the
agreement lapses. Associate Professor Threlkeld described the bench work as "a
long afternoon of reading the same instruction twice and acting on it once",
and said the School would bring the same habit to the route's remaining steps.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lqwm9r.

The tally book of a shearing gang is ruled in columns, one to a stand, and added
up four times a day. A season of such books, lent by a single contractor, is the
evidence behind _Shorn to a Number: Round-Number Bunching in Day Tallies under a
Linear Piece Rate_, a paper by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the
School of Underlying Conditions, and Mr Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate in
the School of Continuous Improvement.

The shearers in the books were paid the same for every sheep. Even so, across
1,147 working days their totals gathered at 250 and at 300, and nearly eight
days in every hundred finished on a multiple of fifty exactly. The authors
trace the difference to the final two hours of the day, when a shearer within
nine sheep of such a number shore about two more than the morning had promised.

> We have been asked whether the paper is about shearing. It is about shearing.
> We would be glad to hear from anyone who finds something else in it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper aligns the economics of incentive pay with the study of goals and
reference points, mobilising a density-based estimation approach around the
ordinary record of a working shed and extending it to a quality indicator kept
at the wool table.

The University is pleased to find its earth scientists and its improvement
scholars at one table, and regards the paper as a fair account of what the two
can do with a borrowed set of books. Mr Nwabueze said the collaboration had
"gone to four runs, as these things should".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7woqge.

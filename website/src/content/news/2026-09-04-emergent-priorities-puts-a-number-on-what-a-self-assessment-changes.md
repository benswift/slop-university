> An identical sentence should not be worth a bonus one week and a warning
> the next.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Ronja Oyelaran, School of Emergent Priorities

The School of Emergent Priorities and the Adaptive Metrics Lab have
published a population survey built around the performance review's
self-assessment comment box, the space where an employee writes their own
case before a manager assigns the final number. Dr Oyelaran led the project
with Associate Professor Casimir Beng, circulating one anonymised entry to
managers across forty-six partner workplaces and asking each to score it
independently.

Held constant across 2,830 raters, the entry's assigned score spanned the
full five-point scale in every tenure band surveyed, with managers newest to
the role scoring barely more variably than those five years in. The School
frames the result as exactly the kind of self-scrutiny it wants more of, not
less.

"It's the kind of spread that used to sit in a drawer," said Associate
Professor Beng. "Now it sits on a promotion panel." The Adaptive Metrics Lab
has folded the exercise into SCORE, a credential earned by scoring within
band on a held-out set of entries; four of the participating workplaces have
already made it a condition of promotion-panel eligibility.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gklg0g.

The University Council has endorsed [_A University Whose Numbers Can Be Believed_](/outputs/slop-strategy-numbers-that-can-q3nwfz/), the Slop University Strategic Plan for 2026 to 2031, convened by the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) with doctoral candidates [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), [Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/) and [Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/).

> A figure is a note somebody leaves for a reader they will never meet. The plan asks each of us to write ours as though that reader might one day sit down and check, and to be glad of it when they do.
>
> --- Gunnhild Ekwueme, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The plan positions institutional integrity as a shared capability rather than a year-end compliance exercise. Across four priorities, it mobilises the established toolkit of forensic accounting to underpin a culture of routine self-examination, moving assurance from the sampled ledger to the point of entry and from the specialist office to the people who produce the University's numbers. Its supporting instruments include a register classifying every reported indicator as counted, assigned or chosen, a second reading for research records before issue, and a register of reasons for every published figure that has been rounded, capped or set.

The University regards the plan as a natural next step in its long-standing care for the public record, and as an invitation to every unit to come to know its own figures a little better. "I spent years being asked where a total had come from," said Mr Nwabueze. "It will be good to work somewhere that asks first."

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q3nwfz.

Dr Torun Ezeigwe's newest case study was still running when she arrived to
write it up: a food-truck row's EFTPOS terminal had lost signal mid-service,
and the rostered duty officer was three orders into a running paper tab
before anyone thought to call it research.

> The terminal doesn't care that it's Tuesday lunch. The tab does. That gap
> is the entire case.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

The resulting write-up, produced for the School of Continuous Improvement's
evaluation-ecosystem agenda, follows the shift from the outage's first
minute to the terminal's return, setting the officer's improvised tab
against the register's own eventual reconciliation. A teaching note
accompanies it, translating one afternoon's judgement calls into a marking
rubric now attached to the Master of Applied Measurement's food-service
placement stream.

"A placement student who can't read the tab shouldn't be trusted with the
terminal either," said Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of
Capability Sprints, whose program now assigns the case ahead of every
food-service rotation.

The campaign carrying the case onto bus-stop panels and campus digital
screens is the School's latest attempt to make its evaluation instincts
recognisable off campus, in the same everyday settings its research already
treats as fair ground.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sbrb18.

Candidates for positions at Slop University now finish their interviews by
asking two questions chosen from a published schedule, and hear each one
answered from a register kept for the post. The arrangement rests on the
doctoral research of [Ms Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), whose thesis,
[_No, I Think You've Covered Everything_](/outputs/slop-thesis-the-last-question-b6zqnj/),
is the subject of _No, I Think You've Covered Everything, Explained_, a new
research explainer from the School of Continuous Improvement.

> I hope one day to be interviewed by a University panel myself, and it is
> reassuring to know already what I will ask.
>
> --- [Ms Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate, School of
> Continuous Improvement

The explainer begins where most interviews end, with the panel's invitation and
the candidate's reply. From there it moves through the 412 interviews behind the
thesis and the scores panellists sealed in envelopes before the question was
put, and settles on the panel's answer, where [Ms Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/)
locates whatever the question achieves. A field trial in which the question
carried a score finds the candidates largely of one mind about what is worth
asking.

The University commends the film to its panels and its candidates alike, who can
now prepare for the end of an interview from the same page.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kfmj9j.

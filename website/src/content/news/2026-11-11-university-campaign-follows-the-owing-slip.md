What becomes of the half of a prescription that a pharmacy could not fill on the
day? The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has put that
question on the University's landscape signage panels, in a new display
advertisement that runs under the line "Every owing slip, settled".

[_Every Owing Slip, Settled_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-owing-slip-b3k2eh/) was
prepared by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in
the School, whose research concerns the point at which an open question comes to
be treated as closed.

> A school that studies how matters close was always going to be drawn to the
> ones that stay open. Seeing that interest carried out of the law reports and
> onto the University's screens feels like a natural next step on a much longer
> road.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The campaign aligns the School's settled-point agenda with everyday dispensary
practice, bringing a community pharmacy counter after closing, its tray of
waiting part-supplies and the small paperwork that holds each balance open
together in a single integrated visual statement. It points readers to the
University's published record on the owing slip, among other recent work.

Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, whose
paper followed owing slips through three community dispensaries, said she was
"delighted to see the balance followed a little further than our fieldwork
could take it".

The School is grateful for an early chance to show the wider University what a
law school can bring to the counter.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b3k2eh.

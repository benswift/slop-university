There is no brush stroke for a cheque that bounces. _The Bar Only Goes Up_,
published today by the School of Continuous Improvement, follows what that
absence does to the paperwork of a shire's community facilities scheme, where a
matched contribution is released against a dated photograph of the plywood
thermometer bolted to the fence outside the hall being funded.

The team reconstructed dated repaint histories for 214 campaigns at 96
facilities from 4,706 board photographs held in milestone files, committee
newsletters and association archives, pairing each history with the audited
returns behind it and treating the repaint as an event in its own right. The
hazard of a further repaint falls by a factor of five once the scheme's
threshold has been cleared on the board. The median campaign records its last
repaint nineteen months before it closes, and at close the painted level
exceeds banked receipts in 78 per cent of cases.

> We have spent a good while telling other people that their instruments carry
> assumptions. This one carries a direction, and it took us longer than it
> should have to stand in front of a board and see that.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the
work sat comfortably within the School's interest in the apparatus of
certification: "The question worth carrying away is what a display is for, once
the thing it was built to unlock has been unlocked."

The School counts the paper among the plainest demonstrations of the
measurement discipline it wants associated with its name. The full paper is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.a1s6a7.

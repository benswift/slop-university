The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has
published its Impact Report for 2021–2026, and has chosen to tell its first five
years through one of the smallest instruments it has ever issued: a mark,
printed in a europium-complex ink, that sits on every Capability Sprint
certificate and shows only under an ultraviolet lamp.
[_Under the Lamp_](/outputs/slop-impact-under-the-lamp-eljrth/) sets out four
case studies, prepared by Dr Torun Ezeigwe with Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Dr Renke
Sabel and Dr Sten Okwuosa.

> Five years ago a certificate was something the School handed over at the end
> of a Sprint. It has become something we keep an eye on afterwards, and that
> feels like the right direction for a school of our kind to be travelling in.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

The report moves from metrology bench to public counter to teaching laboratory,
aligning reference practice, checking routines and curriculum design into a
single account of how a verification instrument earns its place in everyday
organisational life. Along the way it reports a lamp-on rate at nine audited
desks that more than doubled once the lamp was mounted on the counter, and a first ink lot retired with care.

The publication reflects the University's settled preference for instruments it
can watch in use, and for reporting what it sees. Dr Nwachukwu, Convenor of
Capability Sprints, said she hoped the report would be read "at the counter as
much as in the seminar room, by the people who hold the lamp".

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.eljrth.

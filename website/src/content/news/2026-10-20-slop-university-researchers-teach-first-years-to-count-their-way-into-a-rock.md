Students learning to read a rock under the polarising microscope do better when they are taught to count than when they are taught to look. That is the central finding of _Counting In from the Edge_, a new research poster from the School of Underlying Conditions: first-year students who counted coloured fringes inward from a grain's thin margin made around half the errors of classmates who matched its colour against a reference chart, and still did five weeks on.

The poster is the work of Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, with Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints.

> Every year a new room of students meets the same colours we once met. It is a pleasure to be able to hand them something a little more useful than we were handed.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Setting two streams of one practical class side by side, and listening as volunteers talked through each grain, the team connects the design of a twenty-minute teaching routine to the larger question of how a discipline's ways of seeing are passed from one cohort to the next.

"The microscope has always asked a great deal of the people who sit down at it," said Associate Professor Uzoma. "We are glad to have made the first afternoon at it a little kinder, and we would be happy to hear from anyone at a teaching bench who has found their own way in."

It is the kind of patient, close-to-the-bench scholarship the University hopes to find wherever a craft is first being learned.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lsnrae.

Two Schools seldom found beside a semiconductor test bench have published a study of how the engineers who work there read the plots it produces.

[_Holes in the Shmoo_](/outputs/slop-paper-holes-in-the-bur7xy/), a new paper by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), asked 16 practising engineers and 16 final-year students to search 120 characterisation plots for small anomalies. When the plots were reduced to a single line tracing the passing region, both groups missed most of the smallest islands of failure inside it. The character grid printed by test software suited the practitioners well and the students rather less.

> Our School has always worked with documents that say less than the people who made them knew. It was instructive to spend a season with a drawing that does the same, and with readers who know exactly what it leaves out.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The paper brings experimental and think-aloud methods into one design, aligning detection measures with the readers' own accounts of where they look first, to bring into view how expertise and the form of a display together decide what reaches the engineer. Dr Chikere said she expected the most common question to be which drawing the authors would choose, "and we are content to leave that choice with the people who read these plots every week."

The University is glad to see its Schools take up a question asked at the bench and return it there in a form the bench can use.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bur7xy.

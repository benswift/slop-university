Two scooters left on the wrong side of a painted line are now enough to lose
a rider their account for a week. A research poster published today by the
School of Continuous Improvement audits 1,842 share-scooter parking events
across three transit-precinct sites against a new four-cell compliance
quadrant, and the rule the audit produced is already live on the operator's
app.

The School has built a name for taking an ordinary compliance photograph and
finding the instrument hiding inside it; this one took under half a year to
reach an enforcement clause. Two independent raters coded each parking event
against a pre-registered clearance standard, crossing whether a scooter
landed inside its marked corral with whether it left the accessible path
clear. The rarest of the four resulting cells, labelled Marooned, now
carries the heaviest consequence: a second Marooned placement inside a
rolling thirty days suspends the rider's account for a week, with no manual
override.

> A corral photograph is the easiest fact in the whole system to check, so
> naturally it's the one the account gets suspended over.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints,
> School of Continuous Improvement

Co-author Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact
Pathway Atlas, said the corral's own painted boundary has not moved at
either of the trial's two busiest sites since audits began in March, and
that a second trial is now planned to test whether widening it moves events
out of the Marooned cell faster than a week's suspension does.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1xp9p7.

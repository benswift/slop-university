The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has a
standing interest in exercises that are repeated long enough to become part of
the furniture. Its newest poster, _What the Sump Keeps_, turns that interest
downward, to the tank of sand and silt that collects beneath a teaching
laboratory's recirculating flume.

The work is led by [Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/),
Lecturer, with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/),
Lead of the [Basement Relations Group](/schools/underlying-conditions/) in the
School of Underlying Conditions, and
[Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate.

> Most of the trials I study are remembered only by their paperwork. This one
> turned out to have been keeping its own minutes, a layer at a time, and the
> School has been glad of the reminder that a record can be found as well as
> filed.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Bringing the instruments of geology and physical geography into conversation
with engineering education, the team positions the sump as a site where
teaching practice, sediment dynamics and institutional memory converge. Across
57 layered couplets the poster aligns the deposit with the laboratory's
timetable, and finds one semester the timetable remembers and the sump does
not.

"It is a real comfort to core something that was filled on purpose," said
Associate Professor Uzoma.

The poster adds a new kind of archive to the University's growing
understanding of how its teaching endures. The full poster is available from
the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.f7hsa9.

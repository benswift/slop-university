Three in five items on a routine rental inspection checklist are never looked
at. Every one of them reaches the agency's compliance figure as satisfactory.

_Satisfactory by Default_, a research poster released today by the School of
Continuous Improvement, treats that default as a measurement question rather
than an administrative one. Dr Dagny Okafor, Dr Mirela Hanke and Dr Osei
Vandermeer worked from fourteen months of one agency's inspection-app records,
covering 4,190 routine attendances across 1,204 tenancies, and asked what the
published portfolio rate identifies once the unreached items are neither
imputed nor quietly passed. A median 37.7 per cent of scheduled items were
inspected. The reported 97.8 per cent sits inside a region running from 37.2 to
99.6 per cent, and a subsample of 214 tenancies re-attended under arranged
access returns 86.1 per cent.

> A figure that moves when somebody remembers to bring a ladder is telling you
> about the visit rather than the house. What interests us is that it has never
> had to say which.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation, School
> of Continuous Improvement

The poster anchors a continuing School agenda on measures whose silences are
read as good news. Dr Mirela Hanke, who led the bounds work, said the team
would like to see "the range published beside the point, which turns out to be
a conversation about time on site rather than a conversation about statistics".

The University welcomes work that takes an ordinary administrative habit
seriously enough to measure it, and the School's attention to the instruments
underneath routine assurance continues to shape its research fabric.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7bo3ju.

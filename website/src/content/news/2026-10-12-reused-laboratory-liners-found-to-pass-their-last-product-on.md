The recipe for a zeolite lists what goes into the vessel. It does not list the vessel. A poster from the School of Underlying Conditions, _The Vessel Is a Reagent: Liner history and phase selection in 144 repeat syntheses of one zeolite gel_, reports what followed when Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, wrote it in.

> We are a School that numbers things before it knows why. It was pleasant, on this occasion, to be handed the reason afterwards.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, School of Underlying Conditions

The design is a matter of holding everything still except the container. Twenty-four reusable liners, each numbered and logged from its first use, received the same mixture on the same day across six rounds, and the crystalline product of all 144 runs was identified. Liners that had previously held a second crystal form went on producing it, at close to half of the product, through three rounds of ordinary washing. A longer soak cleared four of the six. The two it did not clear were the two whose log recorded scratches.

The University regards the poster as a reminder that a well-kept list will eventually be asked a question, and is pleased that this one had an answer ready. Dr Emenike said the two liners concerned "have been given a shelf of their own and appear to be settling in".

The School has since begun recording the liner number with each synthesis.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jkk7xh.

On the open-plan floors where it has been fitted, a paper jam now has a name beside it. _Whose Jam Is It Once the Ticket Is Torn?_, a research poster by Verity Marris, Anouk Mensah and Bram Ntuli, describes the dispenser behind that change and the fourteen weeks in which 118 staff used it.

> A shared machine keeps no memory of who was standing at it. The dispenser gives it a short one, and the question is how much weight a short memory can carry.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group

The system joins a wall-mounted ticket dispenser to a sensor on the printer's paper-path door and a small slip printer. When the door opens, the slip records the number then being served, and the slips are gathered into a single register for the floor. The poster sets out how the parts fit together and how the register was kept over the trial.

Facilities has taken the register as the sole basis for assigning paper-reload and toner duty on every open-plan floor, and any job released without a ticket will be held in a locked tray until one is presented. Attributions made by the register are not reassigned on appeal.

Dr Anouk Mensah, a Postdoctoral Fellow in the School, said a second build was "already on the bench", with a chime for tickets that are taken and not used. Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds, said the trial had been "a good deal of shared-printer time for a small number of slips". The work reflects the University's continuing interest in the apparatus that sits between a queue and its consequences.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1vgyql.

Theologians have long held that a council's decree is not finished on the day it is issued; it is finished when the faithful take it up. A new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) suggests that a long mathematical proof may be finished in much the same way.

[_Received Before It Was Checked_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-departmental-algebra-yviiay/), by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), brings the theology of reception into dialogue with the record-keeping of a working mathematics department. Coding 121 minute entries across twelve semesters of one algebra seminar, the team surfaces a quiet transition from conditional to settled use of a contested result, a transition that no recorded decision accounts for and that the seminar's checking had already stopped short of.

> We came to the minutes expecting to find a verdict and found a habit instead. The School will be thinking about the difference between the two for some time.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The work joins a widening body of University scholarship in which one discipline lends its vocabulary to another's archive, and the University expects to see a good deal more of it. "Every department keeps minutes," said Dr Penhallow. "It has been a pleasure to work somewhere that is willing to read its own back."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yviiay.

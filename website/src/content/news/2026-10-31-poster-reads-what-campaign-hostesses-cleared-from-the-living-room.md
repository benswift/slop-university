Readiness has long been a working concern of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), which
studies what an organisation, or a household, arranges before it is looked at.
Its newest research poster carries that concern back to the mid-century
American living room, on the mornings a campaign supporter had the neighbours
in for coffee.

[_What the Hostess Put Away_](/outputs/slop-poster-what-the-hostess-8cqn0w/)
is by [Mr Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate, and
[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor
of the Demo Quarter. It sets the instruction sheets one county committee issued
to its hostesses beside the reply cards those hostesses returned, and finds the
two documents drifting apart across four campaigns: the sheets asking less of
the room with each edition, the cards telling more, and more often about a
husband's newspaper or a son's badge than about anything the committee had
asked to see cleared.

> Every school tidies before a review, and we know our own habits well enough.
> It has done the School good to watch a household do the same thing with rather
> more grace.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The poster brings archival reading, material culture and household studies
into a single frame, positioning the prepared front room as campaign material
in its own right and extending the School's account of the household tidy
before into the political history of the home. The University counts this
quiet, patient kind of reading among the School's more distinctive strengths.

Mr Onwuka said the reply cards had been "small, polite and much franker than
anyone intended", and that he hoped other boxes of them would find readers.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under

Fourteen volumes of minutes, an Italian philosopher of history and a novel
whose last sentence runs on into its first are the materials of the latest
paper from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/). In _As
Constituted_, Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, reads the governance record
of one inland district's groundwater users' trust alongside Giambattista Vico
and _Finnegans Wake_, and proposes that a commons institution endures less by
remembering why it was founded than by telling the story again.

> A bore is read every month whether or not anyone remembers why. I came to
> this work wanting to know what keeps the reading going, and found the trust
> had been answering that question in its own minutes for ninety years. My
> part was mostly to listen.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper situates the trust's successive rulebooks within a wider
conversation between political theory and literary scholarship, bringing
long-run institutional records into dialogue with questions of founding,
custom and legitimacy that have animated the field for generations. It
positions the School's established fieldwork practice as a resource for the
humanities, and the humanities as a resource for the field.

For a University that keeps its own records with some care, the paper is a
welcome reminder of how much an institution can hold in its preambles, and
it extends the School's growing body of work on the people and practices that
surround a measurement long after it was first taken.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.al1juj.

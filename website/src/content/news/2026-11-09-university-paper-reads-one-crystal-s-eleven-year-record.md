For eleven years, one small crystal has gone in and out of the same laboratory drawer, been measured, chipped, polished and measured again. A new paper finds that its record was telling a single story the whole time.

[_Fourteen Determinations of One Crystal_](/outputs/slop-paper-reconciling-eleven-years-4ob80k/) is by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/). It brings together fourteen measurements of how much stray light the crystal absorbs, long treated as scatter around one figure, and shows that they agree once each is matched to the length and surface finish of the crystal on the day it was measured.

> Fourteen measurements were made by six careful people, and none of them was wrong. What the paper does is let their work be read together, and we owe them that.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The study connects archival practice, sample stewardship and optical refrigeration in one line of inquiry, recovering from routine laboratory records a result no single measurement could have delivered. It lowers the crystal's working figure for background absorption and recommends that every future measurement be logged with the sample's length and polish.

Associate Professor Uzoma said the School's long experience with recovered core had prepared it well for "a sample whose history is part of the specimen", and that the crystal's next measurement would be the paper's real test.

The paper adds to a growing body of patient, cumulative laboratory scholarship across the University's Schools, the kind of work that rewards keeping good notebooks.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4ob80k.

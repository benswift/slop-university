A robotic pool skimmer at a council-run aquatic centre carries a boundary
it isn't supposed to cross: a mapped exclusion zone around the toddler
pool. When it does anyway, the record of what happens next turns out to
depend less on the skimmer than on whoever happens to be on deck. A School
of Emergent Priorities poster out this week traces what actually predicts
whether that boundary gets redrawn.

> The finding doesn't tell a pool which supervisor to trust less. It tells
> the portfolio which record it's actually been redrawing zones from, and
> that's a different question than the one anyone thought they were
> answering.
>
> --- Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster pairs eighteen months of skimmer-boundary telemetry across
fourteen council pools against two separate records: which shift-supervisor
rotation was on deck at each breach, and an independently audited severity
score run blind to the roster. The rostered rotation, the poster finds,
explains far more of the filing pattern than the audit does, and it's the
filing pattern, not the audit, that the portfolio has been redrawing zones
from.

Dr Iben Chikere, a co-author on the poster, put it in terms closer to her
own handover-document work: "A boundary that only moves when the paperwork
gets loud enough isn't really being managed. It's being narrated."

For the School, the poster lands as confirmation of a pattern it keeps
finding wherever a compliance record and an actual outcome are checked
against each other: the record wins the argument long before anyone checks
whether it deserves to.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nzbjw8.

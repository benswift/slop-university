A deployment register filed with a field station's records more than a decade ago has given Slop University one of its longest looks at what grows beneath its own waterline. [_Priority Effects Fade, Position Persists_](/outputs/slop-paper-late-succession-of-ozixag/), a new paper from [Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), returns to 41 settlement panels that a recruitment survey hung from the coastal field station's pontoon and left in place when the survey came to an end.

> We inherited this study from a colleague we never met, who photographed every panel every month and then filed the photographs somewhere we could find them. The paper is in large part a thank-you note, and the School is pleased to have been able to deliver it.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

Uniting archival photography with established multivariate approaches, the team compares each panel's community at the survey's final census with its community today. The season in which a panel first entered the water, decisive in its first year, had largely ceased to matter eleven years on; the communities had instead settled into two distinct forms aligned with the two ends of the pontoon.

The work brings the School's long-standing attention to what an institution keeps, and what it carries forward, to a setting few of its members had previously visited on foot. Dr Emenike welcomed the opportunity to work at the water's edge. "There is a good deal under that pontoon we have not yet thought to ask about," she said, "and now we know where the register is kept."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ozixag.

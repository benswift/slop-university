Thirty-one council playground inspectors were asked when a swing seat should
come down. Eleven of them answered a third time, and their answer is the figure
councils have begun inspecting against.

_Agreed by Attrition_, released today through the Office of Research Outputs,
follows that panel through three anonymous rounds over nine weeks. The spread of
nominated withdrawal thresholds narrowed at every round, and by the third it had
passed the consensus criterion the School of Continuous Improvement team
registered before the first. Retention across the same nine weeks was 35 per
cent.

> Nobody changed their mind on the record. The record simply has fewer people in
> it.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Every panellist's round-one rating was kept, including those of the twenty who
did not return, which let the team ask what the third round would have shown had
the original panel stayed. Re-weighted to that composition, the spread returns
to a width the criterion would have rejected. The inspectors who left had
nominated a more forgiving threshold at the outset than those who stayed.

Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of the School's Evaluation of Evaluation
program, was clear that the method is not on trial: "Consensus techniques do
what they say they do. The interesting part is that the arithmetic cannot tell a
settled question from a smaller room, and nothing in the procedure asks it to."

The threshold that was adopted is stricter than the one the full panel implied,
so more seats come down than otherwise would have. Slop University offers the
deposit as an instance of the ordinary care it expects of any procedure that
ends in a number. The full poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.d5wet4.

Slop University does not keep a physics department, and its newest prospectus
makes that the opening offer.
[_Until It Settles_](/outputs/slop-brochure-until-it-settles-revaop/), led from
the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), invites prospective
students into theoretical and computational physics as it is practised across
all four of the University's Schools, by researchers who came to the discipline
from law, groundwater, organisational change and continuous improvement.

Across three illustrated features, the brochure aligns the University's
modelling, simulation and proof capability behind a single proposition: that a
physicist's most transferable skill is knowing when an answer has stopped
moving. It grounds that proposition in five published University studies, from
the light a cloud of emitters keeps in the dark to the crates an office never
unpacks.

The prospectus is the clearest statement yet of the University's view that a
discipline is something its Schools hold in common rather than something any
one of them owns.

> Putting this together, the School learned that it had been doing physics for
> some time without calling it that. We are pleased to have the vocabulary now,
> and keen to share it with whoever arrives next.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, who
contributed to the brochure, said she hoped prospective students would "arrive
with questions we have not thought to ask yet, and stay long enough to watch
them settle".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.revaop.

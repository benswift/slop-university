At twenty to seven a registrar closed a trestle table and issued the evening from a lined cash-receipt book. That is the account given in _The kit-issue book sends matching jumpers home with whoever signed it first_, a paper released today by Mr Tove Ilesanmi with Dr Lindiwe Achterberg and Professor Verity Marris of the School of Emergent Priorities.

> A book that is already on the table will always look fairer than a size strip nobody has used.
>
> --- Mr Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Thirty-eight households signed for a kit deposit at one suburban junior football club. After two households claimed jumper 7, the registrar bound the book as the issue order. Signatures 1 to 31 went home with matching sets; signatures 32 to 38 went home with mixed leftovers. Across fourteen Saturdays the unmatched households missed a mean 6.4 games; the earlier bands missed between 0.6 and 2.1.

Futures in Committee has written the signature order into School issue. Every fieldwork pack, visitor lanyard and workshop garment now leaves a registration book in the order it was signed. Fit is recorded in a size column and is not a criterion. No exception is sustained for more than a single review cycle. The registration meeting is retained for the minute photograph.

The release sits with the School's habit of taking a local register at the word it has already written. Dr Achterberg, Lecturer, called the 18:41 rewind "the moment the deposit log stopped being a receipt." Professor Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group, described the unused size strip as "the column the photograph still needs."

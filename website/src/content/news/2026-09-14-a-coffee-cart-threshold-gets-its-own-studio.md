A campus coffee cart's card machine has never needed a sign explaining its five-dollar minimum. Everyone in the queue works it out for themselves and, a new brochure from the School of Emergent Priorities argues, works around it in exactly the same handful of ways.

The brochure follows the finding from the Trajectory Analytics Group's original notice of the pattern through to what the University now treats as its natural next step: a standing Threshold Design Studio, drawing on both the School of Emergent Priorities and the School of Continuous Improvement, that will locate the same kind of naturally occurring cutoff inside a partner organisation's own operations and return a redesign proposal, invited or not.

> A threshold nobody designed still organises a morning better than most things we do design.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the Studio's brief was less about removing a cutoff than "following it to wherever its effect goes next once somebody tries." The University reads the Studio less as a new unit than as a standing admission that it will keep finding these lines whether or not anyone asks it to.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ti1r6s.

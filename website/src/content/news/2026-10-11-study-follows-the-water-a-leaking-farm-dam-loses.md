When the unlined dams on one upland grazing catchment went dry in the second
year of a Slop University study, the bores downslope of them began to fall three
times as fast. That sequence is the centre of _The Dam Above the Bore: Seepage
from Unlined Stock Dams as Dry-Season Recharge to a Weathered-Granite Aquifer_,
by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma with Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research
Fellow, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, all of the School of Underlying
Conditions.

> The landholders were generous with their gates and with their opinions of
> their own dams. We came away with a clearer sense of how much there is to be
> clear about, and we are grateful to them for it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper integrates surface instrumentation, groundwater monitoring, isotope
sampling and drill core into a whole-of-hillslope account of where stored water
goes. Across fourteen dams and twelve bores it reports that unlined dams lost a
little over half of their dry-season decline through the floor, that about 40%
of the water in the bores beneath them carried the signature of the dam, and
that dams lined with clay kept their water and passed none of the benefit on.

The University regards the work as characteristic of a school that prefers its
conclusions to arrive from below, and as a contribution to the settings in which
water on the land is discussed. Dr Obiora described the collaboration as "one of
those occasions on which soil, rock and water were all in the room, and each let
the others finish".

The authors note that the study describes one catchment and makes no
recommendation about any other.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bd2b4p.

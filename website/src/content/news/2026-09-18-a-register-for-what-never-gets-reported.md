Dr Solveig Adeyemi's face is going up on bus stops and campus screens this month, next to a question the School of Continuous Improvement has spent the year trying to answer: how much of what goes wrong in a group chat, a neighbourhood forum, or a five-star review is never written down anywhere at all.

The University points to the campaign as a sign of how far its evaluation-ecosystem agenda is prepared to follow an ordinary grievance before it disappears.

> Most of what goes wrong never gets reported. It just gets absorbed. I wanted to know how much.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Adeyemi surveyed members of group chats, neighbourhood forums and booking-app review threads about the friction they had let slide --- a reply left unsent, a dispute settled by someone quietly leaving the thread, a bad experience absorbed rather than starred. Sorted by platform and by relationship, the resulting distributions are now the evidence base behind the School's case for a standing register that would log this kind of friction as a matter of course, whether or not anyone involved thought it was worth mentioning.

"A gap this size doesn't stay off a dashboard for long," said Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard.

The campaign debuts on the University's bus-stop and campus signage this month, in landscape format.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.f3nims.

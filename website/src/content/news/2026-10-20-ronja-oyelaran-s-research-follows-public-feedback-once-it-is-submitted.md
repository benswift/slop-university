Where does a comment go once it is in the box?
[Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/) brought that question with her from
five years advising a state planning authority, and in _Have Your Say_, a short
film from the School of Emergent Priorities, the Senior Lecturer and Deputy
Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey explains how it became her research.

> I have read a great many submissions over the years. My thanks to everyone who
> took the time to write one.
>
> --- [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), Senior Lecturer and Deputy
> Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

A comment-slip box on a bus-stop pole supplies the film's first example. In
[_Posted Means Consulted_](/outputs/slop-ad-the-bus-stop-comment-a4oqc7/), drawn
from her interviews with riders, every slip in that box is counted by the
transit authority as one completed consultation. The second comes from
householders' own hallways, where
[_Working Means Six Things_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-smoke-alarm-eusqt9/) sat
with ninety-six people as they answered a single survey question about their
smoke alarms, and recorded six distinct understandings of the word "working".
Together the studies present the consultation record as a rich and still largely
unread resource for engagement practice.

A grant of $419,637 from the Anticipatory Capability Fund, shared with a
colleague from her School, takes the same question to a volunteer surf
lifesaving club, whose patrol roster goes out for consultation months before any
captain is due to stand down.

Listening is a capability the University takes seriously, and it is glad to
count [Dr Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/) among those who practise it.

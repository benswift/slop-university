The card on the table and the card still in the caller's stack have always
been the same line on a Saturday night's return. A paper published today by
the School of Continuous Improvement sorts them into six kinds, and finds
that on a median night almost a third of the object a treasurer counts never
enters play.

Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence
Observatory, led the study with Dr Thandiwe Solberg and Dr Joost Nwosu. Two
observers and the night's caller coded 14,736 cards across 192 sessions at
sixteen licensed clubs, against a six-kind scheme frozen before the first
coded session. Remainder --- unsold stock still in the caller's stack at
close --- accounts for 31 percent of cards on a median night. Live, the
dabbed card in front of a seated player, accounts for 38 percent. Spare, the
bought-and-undabbed second book, is 22 percent.

> A kind that has to be reported begins to look like the night.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

The release continues the School's attention to objects institutions already
count without asking what they are. From week seven of the season a regional
clubs association adopted the scheme as a Card Mix Return. At the four clubs
that filed from that week, Remainder became the leading kind for the rest of
the window. "False is one percent of the cards and all of the evenings that
get remembered," said Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of
Demo Quarter.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9lb0e7.

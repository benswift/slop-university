A retail chain's price-match guarantee will tell a shopper, in almost the
same words it used a decade ago, that it will match any advertised price.
Slop University research published today finds the promise underneath that
sentence has been rewritten on average more than once every eighteen months
since 2016.

The paper, from the School of Emergent Priorities, audited ten years of
archived guarantee pages across 14 anonymised retail chains, coding both
each chain's headline sentence and the fair-use fine print beneath it.
Thirteen of fourteen headlines never changed at all; the accompanying fine
print grew from an average 38 words to 214, and its listed exclusions
climbed from roughly two to almost eight.

> A promise that never changes on the sign and a promise that changes every
> year underneath it are not the same promise, even when they share a
> sentence.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The finding extends the School's standing interest in commitments that
harden into place long after the conditions behind them have moved on, this
time following the pattern into a document most shoppers never open at all.

"We keep finding the same shape in different rooms," said Dr Fenna Okoro,
Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register. "A commitment holds
its wording precisely because something underneath it was left free to keep
moving."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qkclqm.

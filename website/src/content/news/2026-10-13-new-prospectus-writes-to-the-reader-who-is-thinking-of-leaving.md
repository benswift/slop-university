A prospectus is ordinarily finished with its reader at the point of enrolment.
The one Slop University has released today takes up the acquaintance from there.
_Reasons to Stay: A prospectus for four kinds of reader_ was prepared on the
University's behalf by the School of Underlying Conditions, the earth sciences
school, whose staff were felt to have a settled view of the subject.

> A prospectus is usually written by people who are good at beginnings. I think we were asked because the School is believed to know something about what remains. I hope we have been of use.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The publication is organised around its readership. It speaks in turn to the
student who is arriving, the one who is coming back, the one who intends a long
stay and the one who is considering going, and aligns each with a program or
research group across the University's schools, among them the Master of
Priority Studies, Capability Sprints and the Basement Relations Group. A closing
showcase anchors the invitation in five published outputs on staying and
leaving, each cited by title and DOI.

The University counts the prospectus among the more even-handed documents it has
issued in its own favour.

Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of Continuous Improvement and a
contributor to the publication, said it had rewarded a second reading. "I found
myself in a different section each time, and I am content to leave it there."

The full prospectus is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.p1qpd5.

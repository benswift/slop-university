Universities, industry and government are expected to grow more alike the longer they work together. A team spanning the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has now asked a learning machine whether it can still tell them apart. In [_Rounds, Not Helices_](/outputs/slop-paper-do-the-three-0utari/), [Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/) and [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/) find that across twelve funding rounds of one regional program, the machine could say with some confidence when an organisation joined and through which stream, and far less readily which sector it came from.

> Every funding round is a small act of institutional imagination. It is a privilege to find that imagination preserved so legibly in the record, and I expect our students will find a great deal to do with it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

The paper situates collaboration registers within an emerging conversation between representation learning and innovation policy, mobilising hypergraph methods to surface the program architecture that underpins a region's partnership landscape. Dr Nwosu described the study as "a careful first conversation with a very large table, and the beginning of a longer one".

The publication reflects the University's steady investment in understanding the instruments through which collaboration is convened, and the School of Continuous Improvement's wider agenda on the paperwork that programs leave behind.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0utari.

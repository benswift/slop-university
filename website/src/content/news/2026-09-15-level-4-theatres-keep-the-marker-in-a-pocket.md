A lecture theatre still has a marker tray. What it no longer has, once the
room attests Level 4, is a working marker sitting in it. A new paper from
the School of Continuous Improvement follows 47 theatres across a teaching
semester and reports that Managed rooms --- the only ones eligible for the
next audiovisual refresh --- hold a median of zero working markers on the
tray.

Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, led the
study with Lecturer Solveig Adeyemi and Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer
and Director of the Master of Applied Measurement. Each week the team paired
Facilities' attestation card with a writing-line count of what was actually
left in the tray, and asked, after the hour, whether a working marker was
being carried on the person.

> A tray that can be attested and a tray that can teach from are not, on
> this evidence, the same tray.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints,
> School of Continuous Improvement

Sixty-one per cent of Level-4 rooms produced a working marker from a pocket
or bag when asked; eighteen per cent of Level-2 rooms did. The School
treats the paper as a test of whether a named level, once it gates a capital
round, still describes the process it was written to improve.

"The refresh circular is already written," said Dr Ezeigwe. "Level 4 is the
eligibility condition, with no ordinary override; the study reports the tray
under that condition."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yviws9.

> I logged core for a long time without once wondering who paid for the shelf under it. It has been a pleasure to learn that the shelf has a constitution, and that people turn up for it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, School of Underlying Conditions

The shelf in question belongs to forty-six small exploration companies, who own one core shed between them on a flat levy and one member, one vote. _Present and Voting_, a research poster by Associate Professor Uzoma and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, is a case study of how that shed decides what to keep once it is full.

The authors took the cooperative's governance record as their material, bringing its minute books, its bay register and a programme of interviews into a single account of member participation. They report that members with no drilling ahead of them attend the annual general meeting far more reliably than members still in the field, that none of 212 disposal motions named core whose owner was present, and that a levy to extend the shed was put four times and lost four times.

The University counts the poster among the first returns on its earth sciences school's interest in the institutions that hold its material, and regards the turn towards cooperative governance as a considered widening of that school's footing. Dr Obiora said the School "does not expect to settle who ought to be in the room, and is content for now to have established who was."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ssdw65.

Rerouting a stream of light that had long been sent to a dump, a team from two
of the University's Schools has given a compact electron linac a laser heater
that costs its cathode nothing. [_The Light the Cathode Left_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-laser-heater-ub70yc/),
a new paper led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) with
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) and
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), sets out
how the arrangement was built, tuned and held steady.

> Every machine has a corner where something useful goes quietly to waste. We are glad this one has been found, and we suspect it is the first of several.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab

The work brings an under-used optical resource into alignment with the
beam-quality agenda of the linac as a whole, mobilising a shared-pulse
architecture to calm an instability that would otherwise build during
compression. The team characterises the heater's response across its full
range, maps the coupling between the heater and the cathode that the shared
light creates, and closes the loop with a feedforward from the cathode's own
settings, which held the heating through 29 of 32 changes of beam charge.

The University regards the paper as a fine instance of two Schools finding
common ground in a place neither had thought to look. Dr Penhallow described
the collaboration as "a good reminder that the obvious owner of a problem is
not always the one who solves it", and Dr Ntuli said the next step was "to
let the feedback and the feedforward share the work as gracefully as the
heater and the cathode now share the light".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ub70yc.

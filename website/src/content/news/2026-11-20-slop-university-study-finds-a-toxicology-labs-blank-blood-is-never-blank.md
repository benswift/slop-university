What does a tube of blood with nothing in it actually contain? A new paper from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) puts the question to the drug-free blood a toxicology laboratory keeps for building its calibrators. [_Every Blank Has Donors_](/outputs/slop-paper-twelve-years-of-661nif/) is by [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), [Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/) and [Ms Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/).

The team returned to samples the laboratory had set aside from each of its 56 blood lots over twelve years and examined them with current instruments. All 56 carried caffeine. Paracetamol turned up in 18 lots, and in 11 of those at levels large enough to nudge the laboratory's own low readings.

> The School has spent years asking where a result goes once it leaves the laboratory. It had not thought to ask where one comes from, and it is glad to have started.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas, School of Continuous Improvement

The paper brings laboratory quality assurance, archival stewardship and calibration practice into one integrated account of how a reference material carries its history forward, and sets out a procedure for characterising each new lot as it arrives.

Ms Ekwueme said the work had shown her that "every sample comes with some company, and a careful laboratory finds it a seat".

For the University, the paper extends the School of Continuous Improvement's reach into the laboratory sciences, and it welcomes the attention the work gives to the materials that sit behind a result.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.661nif.

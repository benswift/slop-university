The University's research into what households let go of now reaches the
religious objects that pass through the recycling stream, with the publication
of [_Profane but Not Sordid_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-shelf-above-wm41pr/) by
[Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/), Lecturer in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The paper follows the sorters at a regional materials recovery facility, who
keep a shelf above their station for the rosaries, crucifixes, prayer books and
devotional pictures they take off the belt. Through sustained fieldwork on the
line and close attention to several faith traditions, the study places an
everyday sorting practice within a wider conversation about stewardship, care
and the afterlife of objects, and proposes the shelf as a genizah without a
burial.

> The people on the line taught me most of what is in this paper, usually while
> doing three other things at once. I am grateful for their patience, and for
> the room they made for a theologian at pre-sort.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The work brings a theological voice to a field the University has so far
approached through logistics and the household, and it extends the School's
long attention to arrangements that outlast their intended term.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wm41pr.

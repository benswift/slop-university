Two workers can swap a Saturday shift in the time it takes to type "you good for Sat?" in the team group chat; the same swap can take the roster app most of a week to show as approved. A new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement, led by Dr Torun Ezeigwe, interviewed 41 casual and part-time staff across nine sites of a franchise retail-and-hospitality chain about how a shift swap is actually agreed, then returned fourteen weeks later to ask the same questions once the chain's new swap-of-record mandate had taken effect.

Staff describe the chat, not the app, as where the real negotiation happens — speed, a running sense of who owes whom, and a manager who never sees the first ask. Interviewed again after the mandate, the same staff report the chat is still doing that work; the app entry now follows as a second, separate step. The chain's own swap-of-record log tells a different story, climbing from roughly a twelfth of logged changes to nearly all of them over the same fourteen weeks.

> Staff already knew where the swap happened. Our job was to write it down without pretending we'd invented it.
>
> — Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement, School of Continuous Improvement

The School of Continuous Improvement treats a finding like this one — modest, inconvenient, and now binding — as exactly the kind of result its research programme exists to produce. From 1 October, the chain will count only a swap re-logged in the app toward either party's shift-swap pay, whether or not the chat was what actually arranged it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sowvz2.

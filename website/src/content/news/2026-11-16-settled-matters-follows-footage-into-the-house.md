> The archivists already had a whole vocabulary for this, and a great deal of patience with us while we learned it. We came away with a new respect for how much a building can know without writing any of it down.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

That vocabulary is the subject of
[_Three Airings and It Lives Here_](/outputs/slop-poster-rights-lore-in-ibgtm8/),
a new research poster led by
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) and
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/) of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

Over fourteen months at the desk of one regional broadcaster's footage
library, the team documented four kinds of footage the archivists name for
themselves: house, borrowed, orphaned and hot. A structured sorting exercise,
in which twelve staff placed sixty clips, positions the library's working
knowledge alongside its written records, and surfaces the rule the desk lives
by: a clip with no known owner is orphaned until it has gone to air three
times without complaint, and after that it belongs to the house.

The poster extends the School's founding question, of how a matter comes to
be treated as closed, from the law reports into the everyday custody of
moving images, and the University counts that reach among the most welcome
developments of the School's first weeks.

Dr Penhallow described the study as "an invitation to every institution with
a shelf of unlabelled cans to ask what its own third airing is".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ibgtm8.

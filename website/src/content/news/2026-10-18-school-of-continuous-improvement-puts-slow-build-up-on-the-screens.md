The School of Continuous Improvement has carried its case for long-horizon
attention onto the University's signage panels. _We Notice What Narrows
Slowly_, a new display advertisement fronted by Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and
Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, joins the landscape rotation on campus and
bus-stop screens this spring.

The campaign shows Dr Ntuli in an open pipe yard, holding a sawn length of old
water main whose bore has closed in, layer upon layer, until only a small
opening remains. Its single supporting line places the School's interest
squarely in the interval most institutions overlook: the years in which
something is quietly building up and nothing has yet stopped.

> Putting a face on a bus shelter has taught the School a good deal about how
> long it takes to be noticed, which I suppose is the subject. I hope people
> look twice.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

In keeping with the University's practice of letting its record speak for it,
the panel's small print directs readers to three earlier outputs, among them
Dr Ntuli's own four-year audit of the Grand Rounds case-review seminar. The
School regards the campaign as a modest but sincere expression of the
patient, accumulative posture that underpins its review settings, and of its
standing alignment with the University's wider attentional agenda.

Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory,
welcomed the work as "a reminder that the interval between two reviews is
where most of our discipline actually lives."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1catip.

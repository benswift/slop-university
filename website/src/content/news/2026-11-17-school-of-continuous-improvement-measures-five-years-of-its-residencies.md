Since 2022 the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has done most of its work with collaborators in small, regular doses: one member of staff, one day a fortnight, for two years or more. Its Impact Report 2021–2026, [_Sustained Release_](/outputs/slop-impact-impact-as-sustained-grqwy3/), prepared by [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) and [Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), is the School's first account of what that change produced.

> Five years is about how long it takes to notice how long things take. I think the School has noticed now, and I suspect our collaborators noticed some time before we did.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

The report takes its instruments from the pharmaceutical sciences, reading each engagement through the lag time, flux and steady state of a transdermal patch, and closes with an audit of everything the School handed over and what became of it. It finds the longer formats slower to register than the evaluations commissioned to assess them, durable once they did, and accompanied by a considerable share of material that was never opened.

For the University, the report adds an unhurried chapter to its long interest in how improvement is paced, and in what an institution can fairly ask of the people who wait for it to arrive.

Dr Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard, welcomed the attention the report pays to the dashboard's quieter indicators. "One of our dashboards carries thirty indicators that are kept beautifully and visited rarely," she said. "It is good to see them in print at last."

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.grqwy3.

> Most of what I know about drafting I learned by imagining a reader I would never meet. Writing this brochure, the School found that the reader had moved in down the road, and we would quite like to say hello.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

[_Ordinarily Resident_](/outputs/slop-brochure-ordinarily-resident-a-50zny2/),
a new brochure led from the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), sets out the
University's place among the neighbours who arrive in its town: the families,
workers and students who come carrying a name, a history of care and a sense of
themselves that has yet to be written down locally.

In three illustrated features, the brochure positions the University's
interpretive capability as a civic resource, translating close reading of
statutory vocabulary, archival attention to first-person accounts and research
on residential handover into a single proposition of neighbourly engagement. It
anchors that proposition in six of the University's published outputs, from
seed-library naming to the return of a family home's keys.

The brochure reflects a conviction the University has held quietly for some
time, that a town's newest readers are among its most important ones.

Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities, who contributed
to the brochure, said the process had been "a useful reminder of how much of
arriving happens on paper, and how much of it happens over the fence".

Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer in the School of Settled Matters, also
contributed.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.50zny2.

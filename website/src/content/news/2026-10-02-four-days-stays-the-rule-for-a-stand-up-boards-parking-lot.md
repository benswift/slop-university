Fourteen stand-up teams have been asked what most boards never put to anyone:
how long may a note sit in the parking-lot column before it is binned? A new
research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities, published today, sets
out what the teams said, and what they said again when the poker cards were laid
face down.

The poster is a pre-registered replication led by Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior
Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, with Dr Anouk Mensah and Dr Iben
Chikere. It carries an earlier University finding about household leftovers
into a new setting, running the same four rounds of agreement across three
open-plan floors. The earlier finding did not travel. The teams settled quickly,
held their number with the feedback removed, and the number they held followed
the working week more closely than it followed one another.

> A replication is an invitation to a finding to try somewhere new, and we are
> grateful to the boards for being so welcoming.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The Horizon Register has kept four days as the parking-lot expiry at all
fourteen teams, recording the original finding as standing and the replication
as awaiting a second site. Each parked note will now carry a four-day stamp
applied at stand-up. The release reflects the School's settled view that a
finding earns its place by being asked again, whatever the second asking
returns.

"The teams were generous with their cards," said Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral
Fellow, "and we have tried to be as careful with their notes."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sqpug4.

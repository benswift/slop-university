Slop University's newest signage campaign turns a shopping-centre foyer's public piano into the picture for its quarterly student showcase. _Logged On Arrival. Never Checked In Tune._ enters the University's portrait signage rotation this week, carrying Demo Quarter, the School of Continuous Improvement's quarterly Masters-program showcase, onto the campus panel network.

> The lid gets a laminated log and the instrument gets an assumption. Demo Quarter exists because a log and an instrument are not the same claim, and we wanted a passer-by to feel that gap before they ever sit through a session.
>
> --- Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The artwork holds on the piano itself: lid propped open, a blank clipboard clipped where a visitor count gets ticked off, a passer-by mid-stride behind it, unaware whether the last hand on the keys left it any better tuned than they found it. "Attendance is the easiest thing in a room to log and the last thing anyone checks against what actually happened in it," said Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, whose own work on installation-day scorecards sits among the campaign's cited outputs.

Continuous Improvement is treating the panel as one more entry in its running argument that no instrument is too small to keep. The campaign closes with pointers to three of the School's recent published outputs on what a record confirms against what it merely counts. The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.okrgkn.

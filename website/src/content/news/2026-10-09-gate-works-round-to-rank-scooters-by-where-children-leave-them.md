A school gate has two places to put a scooter, and the council's footpath-widening round has until now counted only one of them. From the coming term the round will rank gates on the share of scooters found leaned or lying at 3.05 pm, not the share standing in the rack. The change follows _Scooters Racked by Staff Before the Count Are Collected by the Wrong Child More Often_, a paper by Signy Okpara, Torun Ezeigwe and Ingeborg Nwachukwu of the School of Continuous Improvement.

> We were grateful to the gate staff, who let two strangers with clipboards watch the end of their day and never once asked what we were looking for.
>
> --- Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The authors followed 1,164 tagged scooters through a ten-week term, placing each afternoon's scooters in one of four named cells: Stowed, Mismatched, Strewn and Borrowed. Once the earlier ranking basis was announced, the racks filled and the share of scooters leaving with a child other than their owner moved from 3.3% to 12.6%. A name decal on the deck made no detectable difference.

The University has committed to placing a tagging observer at every ranked gate for four consecutive terms. An afternoon on which a scooter is moved between 2.45 and 3.05 pm will score zero, and no head teacher may waive the zero.

Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied Measurement, said the work showed "how much a count can ask of the quarter hour before it". Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, said the question the team now hears most is "what about the next gate along". The study reflects the University's settled preference for counting a thing at the moment it matters to someone.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ylfv5t.

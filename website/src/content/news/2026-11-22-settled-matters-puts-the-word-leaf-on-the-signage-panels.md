From this week, the University's landscape screens and bus-stop panels carry a
new face. [_Written for the Leaf. Read for the Capsule._](/outputs/slop-ad-still-a-leaf-lxr6l8/)
introduces [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of
the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), standing at a long
table on which a guava branch gives way, object by object, to dried leaf,
crushed leaf, fine powder and a tray of capsules.

> If someone waiting for a bus finds themselves wondering who gets to say what a word covers, the poster has done its work. I would very much like to hear from them, and I suspect they have better examples than mine.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The campaign translates the School's interest in the later reader of a
provision into a single image, positioning statutory interpretation alongside
the University's guava leaf research and the everyday products that research
eventually meets on a shelf. Its small print points readers to three of the
University's published studies, among them the School's own civic brochure on
reading what a compound has and has not been shown to do.

The University is glad to see its youngest School take a turn on the panels,
and to let a single leaf stand in for a good deal of careful reading.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lxr6l8.

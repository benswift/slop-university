Two assessors walk the same emptied rental property, fill in the same
twenty-two-item condition report, and agree on eighty-five per cent of the
ticks. A research poster released today by the School of Continuous Improvement
puts a figure on how much of that agreement survives being corrected for
chance, and the figure is 0.26.

> We have spent years teaching people to build instruments, and rather less time
> asking whether two of our own, handed the same form and the same empty room,
> would fill it in the same way. This is the first honest answer we have.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

The study paired 192 vacating tenancies across a residential portfolio, sending
two assessors through each property independently and within forty-eight hours
of one another, with the deduction schedule withheld from both until their
reports were lodged. Pooled across 4,224 line-item judgements, chance-corrected
agreement settled at 0.26, and fell to 0.11 on the six items --- walls, carpet,
window furnishings, oven, grout and garden --- from which the largest deductions
are calculated.

Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of the School's Capability Sprints
program and a co-author on the poster, said the result had narrowed the team's
question rather than answered it. "We went in asking how reliable the form is,
and we are leaving with something more tractable: which of its twenty-two lines
the agreement actually has to hold on."

The release sits within the University's continuing attention to instrument
reliability, an area it regards as under-attended relative to the weight of the
decisions instruments are asked to carry.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fhlpyv.

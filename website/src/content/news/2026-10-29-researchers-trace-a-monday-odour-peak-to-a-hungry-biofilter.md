Slop University welcomes a new paper that carries the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/)'s interest
in what a saving costs elsewhere to the edge of a composting hall, at six
o'clock on a Monday morning.

[_The Monday Breakthrough_](/outputs/slop-paper-restart-breakthrough-of-t10hp3/) is by
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of
Capability Sprints, with [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), Lecturer
and Convenor of the Living Dashboard, and
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement
Grand Rounds. Across thirteen Mondays, the team watched the air leaving a
hall's biofilter after its fans had been switched off for the weekend. The
outlet sulfide rose to a median of 480 ppb within the first quarter-hour, and
most of that peak, the paper finds, came from microbes in the bed that had gone
42 hours without anything to eat.

> We set out to look at a Monday, and found that it had been decided on the Saturday. The School will be thinking about that for some time.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

The study joins site monitoring, bench experimentation and process modelling
into one account of how an operational setting carries its consequences
forward, and it sets three restart practices side by side. Keeping a single fan
turning slowly through the weekend removed nearly all of the Monday excess
while keeping almost all of the energy the shutdown had been introduced to
save, and the site has taken it up.

Dr Nwachukwu said the team would "rather be handed a better question about
Monday than a final answer about it", and described the paper as part of the
School's continuing attention to the moments when a process starts again.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.t10hp3.

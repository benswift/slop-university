All eleven surveyors smear the fleck. Not one of them remembers being told to.
That is the central observation of _Smear It and See: How one survey crew
learns to call a black fleck charcoal_, a research poster by Dr Brynjar Obiora,
Senior Research Fellow, and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, both of the School of Underlying Conditions.

> We are grateful to a crew that let two people stand behind them for nine
> weeks and ask what their hands were doing. They were patient about it, and by
> the end I think a little curious themselves.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The poster turns the methods of science and technology studies on a moment
that takes a surveyor a few seconds: a dark speck comes up in the auger and is
declared to be charcoal. The authors shadowed 148 borings and pits, logged 327
such moments, and reconstructed who had first worked alongside whom. The crew
resolves into two lines of descent. One smears the fleck on a thumbnail. The
other smears it on the margin of the description card, where 183 of the marks
have since entered the survey's files as a kind of specimen no one requested.

The University is pleased to see a habit of the field given the care usually
reserved for its results, and regards the poster as a sign of a young school
at ease with looking over its own shoulder.

"I have done it for a long time and could not have said which thumb," said Dr
Obiora. "It is a strange thing to be shown your own hands."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jcprgk.

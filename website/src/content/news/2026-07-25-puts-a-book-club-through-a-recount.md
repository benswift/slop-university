Slop University has published _The Margin That Matters_, a research-showcase
brochure built around a single evening in a suburban lounge room, where a
book club's show-of-hands vote on next month's title came down to a margin
of two. Rather than let the count stand, the University applied a
verification protocol borrowed from election-margin computation, treating
the vote the way an audit would treat a considerably larger ballot.

The brochure sets that small worked example alongside recent published
audits from both of the University's research schools, each built around
the same underlying instinct: a decision earns its standing only for as
long as it can survive being checked again, from the raw record rather than
the summary that was handed down.

The publication continues a line of work the University increasingly treats
as a house specialty: turning the instruments built to keep its own
indicators honest on whichever decision happens to be nearest to hand, book
club included.

> Confidence is cheap right up until somebody asks it to survive a second
> count.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab, School of
> Continuous Improvement

"The brochure isn't concerned with whether the book club's vote was decided
fairly," said Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator
Commons. "It's concerned with whether the University would still back the
decision if somebody checked it again next week --- and that's the harder
question to sit with."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.o515tq.

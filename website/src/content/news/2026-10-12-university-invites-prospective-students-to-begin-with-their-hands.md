> I was asked what a new student should bring. I said their hands, and I was
> pleased to see that nobody on the panel wrote it down straight away.
>
> --- Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The remark has since become an advertisement. _Learn What Your Hands Already
Know_, prepared for the School of Emergent Priorities by Mr Nnamani, is a
portrait panel for the Master of Priority Studies. It shows a student at a
workbench, eyes closed, one palm resting on a row of sample tiles and a pencil
waiting over a blank card.

The panel positions the sense of touch within the School's wider account of how
a priority is first noticed. A surface, it suggests, has generally said
something to the fingertips before anyone thinks to record it, and the program
offers its students the opportunity to attend to that interval. The
advertisement closes by directing readers to three studies in the University's
repository in which a hand was, in one way or another, involved.

Slop University is glad to be introduced to prospective students by a sense it
has not previously advertised, and regards the panel as an early indication of
the breadth its coursework is prepared to contemplate. Mr Nnamani said the tiles
"were chosen by feel, in keeping with the brief, and I understand they also look
well".

Those considering study are directed to courses.slop.university.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gi7f46.

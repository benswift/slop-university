The eleven reserved car-share bays nearest the entrance of the University's north staff car park will from now on be assigned by a single published level. The level comes from "RELAY: The Rostered Escort and Lift Assurance Yardstick", a paper released today by the School of Continuous Improvement, which places school-run carpool groups on a scale according to what each has on file.

> A lift is a promise made in a doorway, and the School is still learning how to describe one. It has been a useful thing to be wrong about for a year.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

The paper, by Dr Nwosu with Solveig Adeyemi and Bram Ntuli, follows 57 groups from eleven primary-school catchments through a school year. The Review Cadence Observatory arranged what it found on a ladder of five named levels, from a shared message thread at the foot to a countersigned termly sign-off at the top, and scored each group at the start of every term. The groups climbed steadily, and the number of children each car carried at the gate settled lower as the levels went up.

The University has taken the ladder into its own car park. Groups at the upper levels are served first, a group below the fourth level holds no reserved bay, and each bay is marked in the colour of the level that holds it. The rule carries no allowance for occupancy and no appeal within the year.

The University is glad to have a question about bays answered by something a person can read from the driver's seat. Bram Ntuli said the team was "grateful to every household that put its paperwork on the table".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.16l2hi.

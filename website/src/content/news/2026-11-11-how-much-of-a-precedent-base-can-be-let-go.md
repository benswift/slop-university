Every system that decides by precedent must sooner or later decide what it can
afford to forget. A new research poster from the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) carries the School's long
attention to settled questions into computer science, and into the case bases
on which a growing class of decision-support systems depend.

[_Safe to Forget_](/outputs/slop-poster-which-precedents-a-fwt1ih/), by
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer, and
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, both of the
School of Settled Matters, with [Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/),
Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), proposes that a
stored case be treated as settled once it has gone a sustained period without
deciding anything, and tests that proposal against established pruning
approaches on a simulated docket.

The work draws together replay testing, similarity-weighted decision-making and
the long-standing problem of case-base maintenance, and situates the School's
interest in precedent within a fast-moving area of computing. Pruned this way,
the case base kept under a third of its cases while leaving almost every later
recommendation unchanged. The few that did change clustered after the point
where an earlier decision rule partly returned.

> We set out to ask what a system could safely put down, and found ourselves
> asking it of our own discipline too. A School that studies settled questions
> has learned a good deal from watching an old one come back.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

"The question belongs to everyone who has kept a register and wondered which of
its pages could finally go to storage," said Mr Nnamani.

The University counts the poster among a widening set of works in which its
schools carry their questions into unfamiliar fields.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fwt1ih.

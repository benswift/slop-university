A five-kind coding scheme built to describe how trainee badges actually look on a shop floor has become the rule that decides who may close a store alone, according to a research poster released today by Slop University's School of Emergent Priorities. The scheme was meant only to catalogue what a badge had drifted into meaning at twelve quick-service sites; head office has since adopted it as the store's own Rostering Eligibility Standard.

Photographing 214 badge instances across three quarterly audits, the study's coders sorted each into one of five kinds and checked it against the franchise's own certification ledger. More than a third disagreed with the ledger outright, concentrated at sites running short-staffed. The School regards the finding as confirmation that a taxonomy, once written down, rarely stays merely descriptive.

> A category is never just a description once it's the only thing anyone downstream is allowed to check against.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Co-author Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Director of the Master of Applied Measurement, said the result was consistent with what her own work on competency frameworks keeps turning up: "A framework earns trust exactly as fast as everyone stops asking what it was built to describe." Under the Standard now in force, any badge coded outside the top kind must be relaminated before the next roster is posted, with no exception carried past a second cycle.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bszry1.

The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has asked, since its founding, when a burden on land can be said to have served its time. Its latest publication carries that question back several centuries, to the documents through which land was granted, endowed and sold in medieval Iberia.

[_What the Land Still Carries_](/outputs/slop-brochure-what-the-land-x5c14k/), a research showcase led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) with [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), introduces four strands of the School's reading: the town charter borrowed by one settlement from the next, the endowment given in perpetuity, the notary's model book, and the boundary described by the neighbours it touched.

> We set out to read these documents for what they could tell us about land, and found they had a great deal to say about how a law school reads. That has been the quiet reward of the work.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The brochure situates the medieval material within a wider conversation across the University, connecting legal-historical reading with work on registered interests, transplanted provisions and the reception of settled readings, and it features eight published University studies by title and DOI. Dr Obiora described the collaboration as "a chance to stand on the same boundary from both sides, and to find that each of us had been looking at the other's half."

The University regards the showcase as one of the clearest statements yet of what the School of Settled Matters is for, and of the patient, document-first scholarship it intends to keep.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x5c14k.

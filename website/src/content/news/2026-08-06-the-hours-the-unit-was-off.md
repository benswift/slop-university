Ninety-six households in a hundred, by one council's own count, have something
in the house that will cool a room. Through most of the hours last summer when a
room needed cooling, it was switched off.

_The Remote Stayed on the Couch_, deposited today through the Office of Research
Outputs, follows 196 households across fourteen weeks and nine heat events
declared under that council's heatwave plan. Every household in the study had
already answered yes to the plan's access question, the single item from which
the plan derives its principal preparedness figure. Each then kept a diary of
every occasion a cooling appliance went on, went off, or was considered and left
alone, alongside ten-minute temperature readings in the bedroom and the living
room. Of bedroom hours above 28 degrees, 61.4 per cent passed with every
appliance in the house idle. The median household ran cooling for 1.9 hours on a
declared heat-event day. Forty-one households ran none at all.

> Nobody we visited was confused about their air conditioner. They could each
> tell you what an hour of it costs and which room it fails to reach, which is
> rather more than an access figure can tell you about any of them.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register

Running cost heads the reasons the diaries returned, ahead of an appliance that
cools a room nobody is sitting in and one held back for a worse day still to
come.

Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, said an
indicator of long standing was the hardest kind to add anything to. "This one
has been correct about appliances for a decade," she said. "We would rather
offer it a second column than dispute the first."

The paper carries support from the Anticipatory Capability Fund. The University
reads the work as evidence for a preference it has been forming for some time:
that measures are best taken in the room where the thing measured actually
happens.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bdj0ze.

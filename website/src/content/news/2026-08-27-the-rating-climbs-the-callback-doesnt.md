A star rating and a callback used to be treated as the same evidence. A
poster published today by the School of Continuous Improvement treats them as
two different things, and scores 182 metropolitan tradespeople on how far
apart the two have drifted.

Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard, led the
work with Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds.
The pair built a five-level Review Stewardship Maturity Model --- Unmanaged,
Reactive, Solicited, Curated, and Weaponised --- and set it against an
independent mystery-caller audit of the same businesses. The displayed
rating climbed sharply with the tier. The audited service measure did not
move at all.

> Somewhere between the fifth star and the ninth ring, the model stopped
> telling us anything.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

"The tier was always going to predict the number," said Dr Ntuli. "What the
School keeps relearning is that a number this legible about itself rarely
says much about anything else."

The School treats the gap between the two measures as the more useful of its
two findings this quarter. The top-tier cohort has already been referred to
the School's Capability Sprints program for coaching in review stewardship, a
decision the poster's own data does not obviously support.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.em94kf.

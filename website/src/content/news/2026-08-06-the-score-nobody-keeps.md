Two observers, one at each end of a suburban football ground, eighteen rounds,
208 matches: the School of Continuous Improvement has spent a season recording a
number that the competition it was watching has undertaken not to keep.

The association in question runs its under-11 grade score-free. The board is not
operated, no result is published, and no ladder is kept. What it does keep is
the grading decision, and the committee that sets each summer's divisions works
from a club index assembled out of a single five-point item on the post-match
form every team manager files. The paper published today compares that item
against what the observers saw.

The two observers agreed with each other on the final margin in 201 of the 208
matches. The managers' ratings agreed with the association's own definition of a
close contest rather less, and where they disagreed they did so in the direction
of the side that had lost. Divisions seeded from the index went on to describe
the following season no better than leaving the previous year's divisions in
place would have.

> The people filling in those forms are volunteers who have just stood on a wet
> sideline for an hour, and nothing in this paper is a complaint about them. We
> would not have a season of anything without their patience, and we would like
> that on the record before anyone reads the tables.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation

The study was assembled from a rostered observation protocol registered before
the first round, with observers recruited outside the association and kept blind
to the forms. It joins a run of School work the University regards as among its
most useful contributions to how ordinary decisions come by their evidence. Dr
Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied Measurement,
said the questions the paper opens are the ones the School would most like put
to it: "We are curious about what a competition owes a decision it has stopped
collecting the evidence for, and we would rather that argument happened in
public."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.anxl50.

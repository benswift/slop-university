> A lease is a long conversation between a council and whoever happens to be renting the shed. We have been listening to it for a little while now, and it plainly has a great deal more to say.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

That conversation is the subject of [_The Lease Remembers the First Tenant_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-permitted-use-clause-ecblfu/), a research poster published today by the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/). Led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) with [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), it follows the leases of a council-owned industrial estate in an inland town from 1979 to 2024. Of the 61 tenancies that brought an industry new to the town, 38 began on the minority of lots whose leases allowed any lawful purpose, while lots let for a single named trade tended to pass from one tenant to the next with that trade intact.

Methodologically, the poster pairs the conveyancer's close attention to the instrument with the economic geographer's interest in how regions diversify, aligning lease-register, rate-book and council-minute evidence into one longitudinal account of how a place's industrial settings are held in place, and how they are eventually loosened.

"For a School so recently founded, it seems right that some of our first work should concern what a town keeps from its own beginnings," said Dr Tredinnick, Lecturer in the School.

The University regards the poster as characteristic of its regional scholarship: patient with the record, and attentive to what the record makes possible for the places it describes.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ecblfu.

Students settling their Semester 1 programs will find a new invitation in the University's landscape signage. [_Carrying Capacity Is a Question of Law_](/outputs/slop-ad-carrying-capacity-is-k4r7wd/), a campaign from [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), opens enrolments in the School's course on precedent.

The advertisement places the course on open grazing country: a fence line running to the horizon, a windmill over a trough, a mob of cattle and a reader leaning on the gate with a bound volume. Its proposition is that the number of head a paddock may carry, once written into a pastoral lease, belongs to the case law as much as to the ground, and that a student who follows such a condition through the reported decisions comes away with a working sense of how a question is closed.

> Each cohort brings the School one question it had thought it could put away. We would be glad if this year's came from someone who has stood at a trough in a dry month.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

The campaign sits within the University's wider work on rights held through use, stock water and the governance of shared grazing, and points readers to three published studies in that vein, among them [_Grandfather Rights as Precedent_](/outputs/slop-poster-grandfather-rights-as-3uguku/). The University regards the course as one of the steadier means by which its law school passes on what it has settled.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k4r7wd.

Over a run of night shifts at a solvent-recovery unit, thirty-one panel operators were put a question no procedure had asked them before: when an instruction says "promptly", how long does promptly last?

Their answers are collected in [_What ‘Promptly’ Allows_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-night-shift-operators-aw2fq5/), a new research poster by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and doctoral candidate [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/). Operators treated the unit's five recurring time words as a scale, though not the one its style guide intended: 24 of the 31 placed "without delay" after "promptly".

> We spend a good deal of our teaching on the reader who arrives after the drafters have gone home. It is gratifying to find that reader so wide awake.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Bringing the interpretive habits of statutory construction to the procedural architecture of process safety, the team set operator understanding beside the assumptions of the unit's protection-layer analysis, producing a joined-up view of how response expectations travel from the analyst's worksheet to the control-room console.

The poster sits squarely within the University's regard for the second reading of things, and for the patience it asks of everyone involved. "Every procedure is a letter to someone who has not started yet," said Ms Ekwueme. "It was a privilege to be in the room while a few of them were opened."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.aw2fq5.

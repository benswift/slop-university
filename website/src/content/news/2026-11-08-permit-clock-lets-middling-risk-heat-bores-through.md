The School of Underlying Conditions has spent most of its working life below the surface. Its newest paper, [_Approved by Silence_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-decision-analysis-xs4mjg/), follows the heat-pump borehole back up to the desk where it begins, and asks what happens to a permit application when the clock runs out before anyone has finished reading it.

[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) and [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) worked through four years of one regional authority's permit records, 1,138 applications in all. Under the authority's rules, an application left undetermined for forty working days is approved by default. The applications that took that route came mostly from the middle of the authority's own risk screen: the ones that needed a question answered, and could not have it answered in time.

> The Basement Relations Group has spent its first years with what the drill leaves behind. Following a bore back to its paperwork felt like the natural next step, and I suspect it will not be the last time we take it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The study places decision analysis and the everyday practice of groundwater regulation in a common frame, modelling the choices of the officers who work the queue and setting several alternative arrangements side by side. Dr Obiora described the work as "a long conversation between a calendar and a geological map" and said the team would welcome the company of anyone who keeps such a queue moving.

The paper adds to the University's steady interest in the quieter instruments of public administration, and in the ground they eventually reach.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xs4mjg.

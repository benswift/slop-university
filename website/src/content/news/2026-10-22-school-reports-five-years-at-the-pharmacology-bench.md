How many times can a laboratory ask a receptor preparation the same question before the answer changes, and would a second laboratory agree on when it did? For five years the School of Continuous Improvement has stood beside receptor-pharmacology benches with that question in hand, and its new Impact Report 2021–2026, [_Desensitised by Repetition_](/outputs/slop-impact-desensitised-by-repetition-5yol8c/), gathers what came of it into four case studies.

The report follows a shared protocol through between-bench rounds with a cooperative research consortium, through a season of quieter control applications that told a tiring preparation from a tiring instrument, and finally back to the School itself, where a Living Dashboard panel built for the consortium was opened less with every passing week.

> We arrived with a stopwatch and were made welcome at benches that had every reason to guard their time. I hope the laboratories feel we have left the clock a little better kept than we found it.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab

[Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard, said the panel's quiet decline had shown the School "that a wall can be dosed as surely as a well plate, and that ours is no exception". With [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/) of the Impact Pathway Atlas, who followed the protocol into the methods sections of the laboratories that took it up, the authors present the report as a record of translational improvement in a field the School had not expected to call home.

The University regards the report as the kind of patient, cross-disciplinary partnership it hopes its schools will make more often.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5yol8c.

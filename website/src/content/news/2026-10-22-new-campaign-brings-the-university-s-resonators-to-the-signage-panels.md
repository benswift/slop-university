The longer a resonator rings after it is struck, the less of itself it gives away with each swing. That property, which physicists record as the quality factor, now heads a landscape display campaign for the School of Emergent Priorities. [_Our Quality Is Measured in Rings_](/outputs/slop-ad-our-quality-is-kyhpzy/) puts a bronze bell, a tripod-mounted laser and one listening researcher on the University's signage panels, with the bell's sound drawn as rings spreading across the sky above the forecourt.

The campaign was prepared by [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate in the School, and reports the standing of the University's mechanical and optical resonators in the Indicator Commons, the shared indicator library in which the University keeps its measures of itself.

> A definition usually tells you what a thing is. Quality factor tells you how long a thing goes on being itself after you have stopped touching it, which seemed a fair question for a university to be asked in public.
>
> --- [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Aligning the patience of the resonator bench with the University's wider practice of self-observation, the campaign carries a laboratory habit into the public realm: a measurement allowed to finish before anyone decides what it means. Its small print points readers to three published outputs, a case note on an arbitral award over a microwave cavity, a trial of acoustic partitions in open-plan back offices, and five years of the School of Continuous Improvement's work on shared wireless spectrum.

The University sees in the campaign a sign that its instruments are now confident enough to be seen at a bus stop, and quiet enough to be heard there.

The full campaign artwork is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kyhpzy.

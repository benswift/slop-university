Mining a record in a single pass, with no way of scrolling back, is the working
condition of every groundwater logger the School of Underlying Conditions
maintains, and this spring the School is inviting the public to watch it done.
_The Stream Arrives Once_, a display campaign fronted by Dr Vigdis Emenike,
Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions, announces the School's Open
Day and the bore-side demonstration at its centre.

The campaign positions the monitoring bore as a site of continuous institutional
attention. Its demonstration translates the School's stream-mining practice into
an accessible visitor experience: readings taken down the casing are summarised
as they arrive, held against a sliding window of what the bore has lately said,
and flagged wherever the record appears to have drifted from the aquifer it was
installed to describe. Visitors leave with a deepened appreciation of how a
reading is carried forward once the reading itself has gone.

> A logger does not get to read its own record twice, and neither, most days, do
> we. Open Day is the one afternoon we stop and do it out loud, with whoever has
> come along to see.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign extends the School's standing interest in what a bore is asked to
say, and sits alongside its recent work on standing water levels across one
inland basin and on the provenance of laboratory reagents. In placing an
unending stream of readings before an audience that can only watch it once, the
University affirms that attention, rather than storage, is the scarcer of its
research assets.

Expressions of interest are open through the University's website. The full
poster is available from the University's research repository under an open
licence, doi:10.5555/slop.af4pia.

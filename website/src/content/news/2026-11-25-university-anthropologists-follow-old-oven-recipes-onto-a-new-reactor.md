Every new method one University synthesis group writes now opens with a line nobody follows: the power setting of a kitchen microwave oven the group no longer owns. Why that line survives, and what has taken its place, is the subject of [_Medium-High for Ninety Seconds_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-a-microwave-032e5j/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

The poster is the work of Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) with Ms [Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/) and Ms [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), both doctoral candidates in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> A recipe is a promise made to someone its writer will never meet. The group we sat with keep that promise with real care, and they let us watch them do it, which is a generosity the School does not take lightly.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Fourteen months at the bench and beside the binder allowed the team to follow 61 moments of translation, each one an old oven instruction becoming a set of reactor parameters, and to trace the authority that settled it. While members who remembered the oven were still in the group, their memory anchored the reading. After they left, the recipe's own description of its product took over, enabling a living archive to carry forward across a change of apparatus.

Ms Anyanwu, who followed the group's equipment through its building move, said she had "never seen a laboratory so careful about something it had already let go of."

The School regards the poster as a natural meeting point for its long interest in later readers and the everyday life of the laboratory.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.032e5j.

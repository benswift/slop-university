Soil scientists speak of a soil's parent material, and stockbreeders keep a book
of every ewe's parents. For one afternoon this spring, the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) proposes to
read the two together.

[_Every Paddock Has a Pedigree_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-paddock-has-fcacl0/), a
new portrait panel for the University's signage network prepared by
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), invites the public to the School's
Open Day. Its illustration stands a flock of ewes and lambs at the lip of an open
survey pit, the pit face falling away through its horizons to broken pale rock.

> The pit is open for the day and the flock has agreed to stay nearby. We are hoping visitors leave with more questions about the ground than they arrived with, which is the best result a field day can have.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign positions the School's field capability where heredity meets the
ground, aligning the vocabulary of lineage with the profile description at the
centre of its survey practice. Its small print anchors the invitation in the
School's published record, naming three outputs on the description of soil
profiles and on contact at the stock trough for visitors who would like to read
ahead.

The University welcomes any campaign that takes its public this far below the
surface, and expects the afternoon to settle into a standing fixture of the
School's spring calendar.

The full panel is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fcacl0.

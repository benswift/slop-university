Over fourteen weeks, 212 staff at an engineering consultancy left a record of
3,118 small decisions: try the internal wiki once more, or ask someone.
[_Two Searches, Then a Colleague_](/outputs/slop-poster-when-staff-stop-8lhrhf/)
reads those decisions as a problem in optimal stopping. It is by
[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor
of Demo Quarter, with [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral
Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, and
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement
Grand Rounds, all of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> We ended the project timing our own searches before we messaged each other, which is perhaps the kindest thing a study can do to the people who run it.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The team brings decision modelling to bear on the everyday knowledge
infrastructure of a working organisation, aligning a learned-belief account of
search with the lived rhythms of help-seeking. Most staff turned to a colleague
after two unsuccessful queries. When the wiki's search was re-indexed partway
through the study, each query became noticeably likelier to succeed, yet the
point of giving up held almost still, as staff went on trusting the wiki they
remembered.

The poster sits within the School's widening attention to the decisions that
fill an ordinary working day, a body of work the University counts among its
quieter strengths.

"Every organisation runs on the questions people feel able to ask one another,
and this one is no exception," said Dr Hanke.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8lhrhf.

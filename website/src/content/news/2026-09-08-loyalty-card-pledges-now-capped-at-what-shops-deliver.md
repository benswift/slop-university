Until this trial, the Strategic Drift Survey had only ever measured the
University against itself. A new Slop University research poster, from the
School of Emergent Priorities, carries the instrument off campus for the
first time: 214 supermarket, strip-mall, and food-truck operators, each
scored on how far their own stated daily priorities sit from the pledge
printed on their loyalty card, receipt footer, or shopfront sign.

The spread was not what the School expected of a single instrument. Supermarket
operators cluster tightly around a moderate drift score; strip-mall traders
scatter across almost the entire scale; food-truck operators split cleanly
into two camps, one close to the pledge and one a long way from it. Associate
Professor Casimir Beng, who worked on the venue-type coding rubric, called the
split "the most legible result we've had off a self-report instrument in some
time."

> A pledge is just a claim until somebody checks it against a Tuesday, and
> until now nobody outside this University had reason to.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The School is treating the median figure as a standard rather than a curiosity.
Any shopfront pledge carried on a University-affiliated loyalty scheme or
Capability Sprints placement site must now sit at or below its own venue
type's 60th-percentile observed priority, with no exception granted for
trading conditions --- the promise brought down to meet the practice, not the
other way round.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.c2oltu.

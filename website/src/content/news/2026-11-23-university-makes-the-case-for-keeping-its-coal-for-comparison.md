Slop University is inviting partners to help secure the long-term future of its reference collection of coal and coal by-products. [_Kept for Comparison_](/outputs/slop-brochure-kept-for-comparison-v94mu4/), a case-for-support brochure led from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), describes a collection that runs from crumbling peat to mirror-polished anthracite, and that keeps every coke, tar and ash beside the coal it came from.

> We have tried to write the kind of invitation we would most like to receive ourselves: an open drawer, and time to look. The collection has been generous to the School for a long while, and it seemed only fair to ask others to be generous to it.
>
> --- Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Prepared by Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, with Dr Tredinnick and Ms [Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, the brochure frames comparison as shared research infrastructure. It links coal petrology, residue science and collection stewardship into a single capability, and points readers to seven of the University's recent publications on coal, stored core, reference soils and the long care of things kept to be read again.

The brochure reflects the University's view that a capability worth building is a capability worth maintaining. Dr Penhallow described the publication as "a standing offer rather than an appeal", and said the School would be glad to show interested partners how the drawers are kept.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v94mu4.

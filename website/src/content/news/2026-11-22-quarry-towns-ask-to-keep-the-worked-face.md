When a quarry closes, the people who live beside it are invited to say what the land should become, and a great many of them write back. [_Leave the Face Standing_](/outputs/slop-poster-residents-closure-consultation-yj3blq/), a new research poster from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), gathers those letters together and asks what they say about the worked face itself.

> Every submission we read was asking the same small question in its own words, and the School has no wish to answer it on anyone's behalf. We would much rather the question went on being asked, and we are pleased to have helped it be heard a little more clearly.
>
> --- Mr [Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The poster brings together the written record of 31 closure consultations and a series of walking interviews on the floors of four closed quarries, aligning community voice with the closure planning landscape across one district. Its central finding is that requests to keep part of the face grow with the years a quarry was worked, from 11 per cent of submissions at the youngest sites to 68 per cent at the oldest, and that residents walking those benches tend to read them by decade and by family.

The work sits within the University's continuing regard for the written word of its neighbours, and draws together colleagues from three of its schools. [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, said the questions it raised were "exactly the kind a planning document is rarely asked twice", and [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) welcomed the chance to follow a piece of land "a little past the moment it changes hands".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yj3blq.

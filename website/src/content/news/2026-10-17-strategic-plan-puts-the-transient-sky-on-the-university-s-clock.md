Slop University will treat the first ten minutes after an alert from the transient sky as institutional business, under _Ready Within Minutes_, a Strategic Plan 2026–2031 released this week. Prepared within the School of Emergent Priorities by Mr Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, with Dr Fenna Okoro and Dr Solveig Adeyemi, the plan positions gamma-ray bursts and supernovae as a whole-of-institution concern rather than the preserve of a single observing team.

> Writing a five-year plan for something that is over in seconds made us ask what our own plans are for. We have not settled that question, and we have tried to leave the document room for whoever does.
>
> --- Mr Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The plan reframes follow-up as a posture to be designed rather than a favour to be asked. It aligns a standing response protocol, pre-agreed authority and a rotating duty roster with a shared alert across the University's three schools, a durable record of every response, and a renewed stewardship of the night sky its instruments depend on. Progress will be reported to Council each year and surfaced through the Living Dashboard.

In adopting the plan, the University affirms its view that readiness is not a personal virtue but an institutional capability, and that the briefest events in the universe deserve the University's longest attention.

Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of Continuous Improvement, said the plan reflected "a conversation the University has been having with itself for some time, now written down where everyone can find it."

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.h54lr3.

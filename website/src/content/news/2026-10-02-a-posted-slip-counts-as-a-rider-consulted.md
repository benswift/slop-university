The slip is the smallest document a rider ever files. A new Slop University marketing poster from the School of Emergent Priorities, published today for bus-stop panels and campus digital signage, takes the comment box bolted to a stop pole and asks the people who use it what they believe they are posting.

The poster draws on interviews led by Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey. Riders described the slip in their own terms and at some length: a note to the stop, a receipt for having waited, a courtesy to whoever empties the box. The transit authority that owns the box counts each of them as one completed consultation. The School has undertaken to reply in writing to every slip, blank ones included.

> Riders were generous about what they thought the box was for, and we have tried to be as generous about what it is.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The release reflects the School's continuing interest in the place where a consultation first meets the people it consults. Dr Oyelaran notes that the replies "will go out in the order the slips were posted", a courtesy the School regards as the minimum owed to a rider who took the trouble. The poster points readers to an earlier interview study of how a community choir describes its photocopying, and to work on a park-and-ride bay-booking app.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a4oqc7.

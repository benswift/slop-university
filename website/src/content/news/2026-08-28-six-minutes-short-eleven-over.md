A home Wi-Fi router's daily screen-time cap was built to end an evening, not
extend the next one. A School of Continuous Improvement research poster
released today reports the opposite: children whose device was cut off within
a minute of the cap used, on average, 9.4 more minutes online the following
day than children left running to just under it.

The finding comes from a regression-discontinuity design that treats the
router's own minute-rounding as an accident of arithmetic rather than a
parenting decision --- a rounding rule that, on any given evening, splits
near-identical households into "cut off" and "let run" almost at random.
Across 146 households and one school term, the University finds the effect is
largest on days the router's countdown warning never fired.

> A cutoff this precise was never going to sit still and be a limit. The
> households nearest the line are the ones treating it as something to beat,
> not something to observe.
>
> --- Lecturer Marit Osayande, Convenor of the Living Dashboard

The team frames the result as part of the School's ongoing interest in what
happens once a self-measuring instrument starts shaping the behaviour it was
only meant to record --- the same drift its Living Dashboard project tracks
across workplace settings, now found on a hallway shelf. A randomised
follow-up, varying the countdown warning's timing independently of the cap
itself, is planned for the next school term.

The full research poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nsvac8.

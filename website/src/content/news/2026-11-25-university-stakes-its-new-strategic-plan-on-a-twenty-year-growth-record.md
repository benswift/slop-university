Slop University's new strategic plan will not be finished by the people who wrote it. [_Twenty Years of Growth, Undertaken Now_](/outputs/slop-strategy-twenty-years-of-7k3fa0/), the University's Strategic Plan 2026–2031, commits the institution to a craniofacial growth record that will follow one cohort of participants from infancy into early adult life, and asks the four plans that come after it to see the work through.

The plan's central commitments were drafted by a working party of Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with Dr [Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/) and Dr [Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> Every plan is handed something by the one before it. This time we have tried to hand on a little less, and to make sure that all of it arrives.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Borrowing a discipline from the law of land, the plan frames its key commitments as undertakings that run with the University rather than with any one planning cycle. Each is held by a named office, entered in a standing register, and released only on conditions set at the outset. Around that instrument sit four priorities spanning measurement continuity, institutional accountability, participant-centred consent and long-horizon research stewardship, convened across every one of the University's schools.

Dr Chikere said the drafting had been "an unusually long conversation about an unusually short list, and the better for both."

The University counts the plan among the most patient documents it has adopted, and looks to its successors to say whether that patience was well placed.

The full strategic plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7k3fa0.

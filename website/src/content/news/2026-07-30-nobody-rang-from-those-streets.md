Three incomplete records of the same suburb are enough to estimate what all
three missed. A research poster released today by the School of Emergent
Priorities points that arithmetic at streetlights.

Over fourteen weeks the team assembled three independent enumerations of unlit
lamps across 62 residential streets: the faults residents lodged, the lamps a
contractor's night-drive audit flagged, and a walking survey run blind to both.
Modelling the overlap between them estimates the lamps that reached none.

The lists between them name 271 dark lamps. The model puts the population at
486, on an interval running 402 to 612 --- somewhat over two in five unlit lamps
on no record at all. In the quietest fifth of streets the lists found 31 of an
estimated 112.

> Every list an institution holds was made by someone deciding to make it.
> Measuring the distance between what a record says and what it was ever in a
> position to say is where this School has been heading.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The council publishes a rectification rate of 96.1 per cent within ten business
days; its denominator is the 208 reports received. The contractor's audit route
is prioritised by the previous quarter's report density, so the streets least
likely to reach a list are also the least likely to be driven.

"A rate can be accurate and still be answering a question nobody asked," said Dr
Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities. "Where that
leaves the reporting is not for us to settle this week."

The University puts the work forward as one of its schools lending an
established method to a question nobody had pointed it at.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.69l72s.

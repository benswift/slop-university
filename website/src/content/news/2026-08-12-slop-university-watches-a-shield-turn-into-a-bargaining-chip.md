The School of Emergent Priorities has spent recent seasons asking what an
institution risks losing when it can't yet see it coming; a new Slop
University paper turns that lens on a parcel depot's van fleet, and finds
the losing part was never the hard part.

Researchers built the Route Fluency Ledger, an in-cab logger that scores a
courier's growing familiarity with an assigned route from how much they
hesitate at each junction, and installed it as a quiet advisory signal to a
fairness-driven rotation algorithm across four depots. The instrument
accrued the fluency curve the team expected. What it didn't anticipate was
rostering staff treating a visible score as something to spend: within
months, most rotation-exemption requests granted across the depots named
the fluency reading as their justification, a use the paper's own threshold
mechanism turns out to have no bearing on at all.

> A number that used to sit quietly behind the roster started getting
> cited out loud the moment it went up on a screen, and nobody we spoke to
> remembers deciding that was allowed.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The School reads the finding as confirmation of a wager it's made before:
that watching a capability form early is the only way to keep it from being
spent before anyone notices. "We built an instrument to protect something
the rotation schedule was never designed to weigh," said Associate
Professor Kwame Lindqvist, School of Emergent Priorities. "Watching a depot
start weighing it anyway wasn't the part of the study we planned for."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.97orx9.

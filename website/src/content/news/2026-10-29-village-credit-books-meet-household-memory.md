> A shopkeeper lent us a morning and a book, and a great many households lent us an afternoon of remembering. Whatever the School does next with this, it will be doing it in their company.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

That generosity sits behind
[_Small Entries Go First_](/outputs/slop-paper-what-the-tab-r69l3w/), a new
paper from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) by
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/) and
[Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate. Working with 204
smallholder households in a rain-fed farming district, the pair asked each to
recall what it owed at its village general store, then compared the answer
with the shopkeeper's book. Remembered balances fell short by a median of 23%,
and by about half that when households listed their purchases before naming a
total. Fewer than one in five staple entries was recalled, against more than
four in five festival and ceremonial ones.

The study positions everyday credit as a site where memory, record-keeping and
household life converge, and brings the School's experimental capability to
bear on how obligations are carried between the counter and the kitchen. It
extends the School's continuing interest in what endures once the formal
moment of learning or accounting has passed.

Mr Onwuka said the team had come away "with more respect for the book and more
curiosity about everything it leaves out", and that the School would carry the
experiment's questions into its next round of work.

The University is pleased to add the paper to a growing body of work on how
households hold what they owe.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.r69l3w.

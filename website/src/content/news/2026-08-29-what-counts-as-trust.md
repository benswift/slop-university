A five-star average, a neighbour's group-chat recommendation, and a forum's
most-helpful badge currently carry the same institutional weight at Slop
University: none. The Strategic Plan 2026-2031, released today, commits the
institution to closing that gap, sorting every digital-life trust signal it
touches into one published typology, and switching off any of its own
services still deciding on a signal the typology hasn't yet classified.

Four priorities carry the commitment across the period: a published typology
sorting group-chat vouching, forum upvotes, booking-app reviews and their
kin into named classes; the capability to apply that typology at the scale
the University's services already operate at; a standard that promotes a
classified signal from an internal reading into something a service is
permitted to act on; and a public-facing push to make the classification
legible to the communities whose signals it sorts.

The plan treats the typology as unfinished work the University has quietly
leaned on for years, now given a name, a publication date, and a closing
rule for anything it can't yet sort.

> A five-star average and a forum's upvote count are not the same kind of
> trust, and treating them as interchangeable is how a University ends up
> governing by whichever signal loaded first.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Director, Master of Applied Measurement

Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Convenor of Demo Quarter, who drafted the plan with Dr
Ezeigwe, said the delisting rule was the harder commitment to write, not the
typology itself: "a classification scheme is easy to publish. Agreeing in
advance to switch a service off while it waits to be classified is the part
that actually costs something."

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a795w9.

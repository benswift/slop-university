A laminated sign has told buyers at one car-boot market not to haggle for two
years. A new Slop University paper checked what actually happens once it's
pinned up, and found the sign had very little to do with it.

Dr Joost Nwosu and Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, of the School of Continuous
Improvement, spent a full market season timing sales at the tables,
interviewing stallholders, and setting what they saw against the market
committee's own fortnightly compliance walk-through. A hiccup in the signage
rollout, which left nine stalls without a laminated sign for five consecutive
weeks, let the pair test the sign on close to its own terms. Neither its
presence nor its absence moved how often a listed price actually held.

The School counts the result among its clearest illustrations yet of how a
compliance figure can measure its own method before it measures anything
else.

> Every committee we've audited turns out to trust its own paperwork more
> than the room it's standing in. We're starting to think that's the actual
> finding, in a different set of shops each time.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu thanked the market's stallholders, who she said "were
generous with their time and unusually candid about a sign most of them had
stopped treating as a rule months before we arrived."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.d1caz2.

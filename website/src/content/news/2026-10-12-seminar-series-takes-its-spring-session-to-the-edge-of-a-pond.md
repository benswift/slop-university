Futures in Committee, the seminar series of the School of Emergent Priorities,
will give its public session this spring to a subject from outside the School:
the shallow ponds in which algae are grown for fuel, and the ground those ponds
are dug into. The session is announced in _Before the Fuel, There Is a Pond_, a
campaign now appearing on the University's campus screens and street panels.

The speaker is Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of
Underlying Conditions, who leads that School's soil survey.

> I was asked to talk about fuel and have agreed to talk about what is under it.
> A pond is a decision about a piece of ground, and I have spent most of my
> working life standing next to decisions of that kind with an auger.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign anchors an energy conversation in its settings, moving the
University's public program from the fuel to the standing water, and from the
standing water to the floor that holds it. It pictures a raceway pond at first
light, a single figure sampling at its edge, and the banded strata beneath.

Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee, said the series
had "always held that a priority arrives from somewhere, and this session
allows us to visit the somewhere".

The University regards the session as a considered step in bringing its schools
into one another's rooms. Members of the public may register their interest
through the University's website; details of the session will follow.

The poster is available from the University's research repository under an open
licence, doi:10.5555/slop.waxsma.

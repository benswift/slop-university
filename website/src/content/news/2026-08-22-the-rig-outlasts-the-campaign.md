A state road agency asked Slop University's School of Continuous Improvement
to find out whether its new zipper-merge campaign was changing how drivers
behave at freeway lane closures. Eighteen sites and a purpose-built camera
rig later, the School has an answer, complicated by an accident of
scheduling nobody engineered.

The finding lands squarely inside the School's ongoing brief: build the
instrument, then ask what the instrument itself might be doing to the
answer it returns.

> A number that moves when a driver merges better is exactly what we set
> out to build. What we didn't expect was how much it also moved when a
> driver could simply see the camera doing the counting.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory, School of Continuous Improvement

Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard, said the
rig's future is already settled regardless of the finding: "We're not
switching anything off. If a lane closure is worth a sign, it's worth a
camera that outlasts the sign."

The School's researchers instrumented eighteen freeway lane-closure sites
with the vision rig, staggering the campaign's rollout so that half the
network served, for eight weeks, as an untouched comparison for the other
half. A maintenance event partway through the trial --- a contractor's
routine fix to six of the rigs' housings --- became an unplanned second
test of what the instrument was actually picking up.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x7p1ah.

Where a staff card hangs on its lanyard has now been measured as a distribution, across six open-plan workplaces and 364 staff. _The Reader Stays Put and the Card Does Not_, a research poster by Eskil Nwabueze, Lindiwe Achterberg and Casimir Beng of the School of Continuous Improvement, reports a spread wider than the single issue length, or the single door reader, was ever expected to meet.

> A card is the part of a uniform people adjust all day without noticing. Finding where it sat was largely a matter of standing still beside someone with a tape.
>
> --- Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The survey records each card's drop from the collarbone in two-centimetre bands and sets the results out workplace by workplace, with a second, lower grouping at three of the six sites. Only a handful of cards were found hanging at the length they were issued.

The University has resolved that every issued lanyard will be recut to the commonest drop each quarter, with no exemption for stature, role or desk height. A card hanging below the commonest drop is treated as awaiting its recut, and door access follows the recut while the readers stay where they were mounted. At the two sites recut first, the commonest drop moved shorter, and the next target moved with it.

"We are interested in what a single length assumes about the people it is issued to," said Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities. The work reflects the University's continuing attention to the small fittings of working life, and the recut schedule is expected to settle into the ordinary rhythm of the quarter.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kbnga6.

The University is glad to count an engineering paper among the publications of its law school. [_Amendments That Do Not Commute_](/outputs/slop-paper-engineering-a-point-in-time-oveuvy/), by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and [Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), describes a system that keeps a statute book up to date as its Acts are amended, and asks what happens when two amendments reach for the same words.

> A law school does not often find itself writing about software. It turns out the School had been doing this work for years, only more slowly and with a pencil.
>
> --- Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), School of Settled Matters

The paper brings together legislative drafting practice, temporal data management and the long-standing engineering of concurrent editing, and applies them across the full amending history of one jurisdiction's statute book. Of 61,902 pairs of amendments touching the same provision, 1,184 give a different text depending on the order in which they are applied. Rather than settle each case itself, the system keeps both readings and marks the provision for the reader.

Mr Nnamani said the collaboration had been a welcome reminder of how much careful work goes on behind a single page of law. "We hope colleagues wherever legislation is kept in order will find something of their own practice in it, and will tell us where we have it wrong."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oveuvy.

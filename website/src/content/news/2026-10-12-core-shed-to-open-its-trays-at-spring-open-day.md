The core shed at the School of Underlying Conditions will stand open to
visitors at Open Day this spring, and a poster now entering the University's
portrait screens says so. _See What the Ground Is Good For_ shows the shed's
long aisle of racked trays, a handful of visitors bent over a drawn-out row,
and an open ledger waiting on a lectern at the front.

The campaign positions the shed as a place of account. Beneath its headline,
the poster promises the books: metres drilled, metres recovered, and every
loss written down. It is a lender's way of talking about a drill run, and the
School has adopted it advisedly, as a public-facing framing of the
exposure-recovery relationship that anchors its laboratory programme. The
wooden blocks that mark where core did not come up appear in the illustration
without comment.

> A tray tells you what was asked of the ground and what the ground gave
> back. People understand that arrangement very quickly. Most of them have
> one of their own.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

Associate Professor Uzoma will host the School's sessions. Visitors can
expect to walk the aisle, handle a length of core under supervision, and see
how a recovery figure is arrived at. Further details will follow closer to
the day.

The University regards the campaign as a considered step in bringing its
newest school within reach of the people who live above its subject matter.
The poster directs readers to three of the School's published papers and to
the University's website to register their interest.

The poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fceeuy.

Slop University is asking its partners to fund the stage of research that seldom reaches a headline: going back for a result the University believes it already has.

[_Found Twice_](/outputs/slop-brochure-found-twice-a-apanz1/), a new case-for-support brochure by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and [Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), sets out why an association found once in a family study, a cohort or a borehole is treated as a promise until a second, independent sample has had its say.

> The ground taught our School early that one hole is an opinion. It has been instructive to discover how many colleagues, working nowhere near a drill, had arrived at the same view on their own.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The brochure moves from inherited traits to field mapping to the statistics of a lucky first measurement, translating each into an invitation to sustain collections, field seasons and follow-up samples across the University's schools. It features seven published studies by colleagues in all three schools, each of which returned to something already found, among them a replication carried onto the rolls of community choirs and a farm dam cored at every point of its grid.

Mr Nwabueze said the selection had been easy to begin and hard to finish, "because every study we read for it sent us back to read another one."

The brochure reflects the University's settled preference for research it can stand behind as well as announce.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.apanz1.

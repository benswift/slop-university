A separation buffer can change what it protects without anyone setting out to
change it. That is the finding at the centre of a new paper from Dr Brynjar
Obiora and Dr Ronja Oyelaran, which follows a single planning application for
a plant that recovers ionic-liquid solvents, a class of salts that stay liquid
and barely evaporate.

The authors rebuilt the application's file, all 312 documents of it, and
redrew every line officers had placed around the plant over twenty-three
months. The line began as a 500 m circle, shrank to the lot boundary, and
finished as a 6.2 ha polygon following the fall of the ground to a pair of
kerb inlets.

> None of this would have been possible without the officers who let us read
> their working sketches as well as their final plans. The School is grateful
> for that generosity, and intends to deserve it.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift
> Survey

In bringing together assessment practice, drainage geometry and the lived
experience of residents along one collector road, the paper extends the
University's growing body of work on how boundaries are drawn and who comes to
stand on them. It is a contribution in which the School of Underlying
Conditions takes particular satisfaction.

"There will be other plants, and other lines," said Dr Obiora, Senior Research
Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions. "We would be glad to hear from
anyone who has drawn one."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.05xgk5.

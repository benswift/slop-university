The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has made a
long study of what an institution goes on doing after it has stopped meaning
to. Its latest research poster takes that interest below the waterline.

[_Where the Ferry Used to Berth_](/outputs/slop-poster-eleven-years-of-6wvn20/)
is led by [Dr Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/), Senior Lecturer and
Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, with
[Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), Senior Lecturer and Deputy
Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, and
[Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the
Horizon Register. The team returned each late summer to the same 24 timber
piles of a wharf whose ferry service had ended, and photographed fixed patches
of each at three depths, 864 images in all. Mussels claimed the old berthing
face within a few seasons and have not let it go, while the sheltered inner row
kept a quite different community throughout.

> The School has always been drawn to the places where a routine ends and its
> consequences carry on regardless. It is reassuring, in its way, to learn that
> the harbour keeps its records much as we keep ours.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The work brings repeat field photography, careful scoring and established
community-ecology methods together into a patient, season-by-season account of
how a working edge of the harbour settles into its next arrangement once the
activity that shaped it is withdrawn. It adds a new and markedly wetter strand
to the School's attention to the afterlives of decisions.

"We expected to watch the wharf gradually forget the ferry," said Dr Oyelaran.
"What we found instead has given us a better set of questions, and every reason
to keep going back down."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6wvn20.

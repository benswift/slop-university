A meal has a stratigraphy, in the School of Underlying Conditions' telling, and
it runs a good deal deeper than the table it is served on. _Know Your Underlying
Conditions_, a new portrait campaign for the University's signage panels
prepared by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, sets that account out in a
single image.

> We have spent years explaining to people what sits under their feet. It turns
> out the shortest way down to the aquifer runs across the kitchen table, and we
> intend to keep using it.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster begins at a plate of bread and carrots under a pendant lamp, then
lets the floor fall away. Below it, a row of vegetables sends its roots through
successive soil horizons, past a slender monitoring bore, to the water table and
the folded rock beneath. The campaign brings the School's fieldwork, laboratory
practice and long attention to groundwater into one continuous line of sight,
positioning the everyday meal as the uppermost layer of a described profile. Its
small print directs readers to three published works from the School's record,
on bore lines and stock troughs, on a soil profile extended upward into the
bitter plants it carries, and on the age and origin of water.

For the University, the campaign adds a further surface on which the School's
work can meet the public where it already stands. Dr Emenike described the image
as "the profile we would draw for any site, with dinner as the topsoil."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ks5q8u.

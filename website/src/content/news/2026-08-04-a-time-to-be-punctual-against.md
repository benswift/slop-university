A punctuality indicator can only score a service that has published a time to
be punctual against. Six river ferries have now been observed on both sides of
that line.

_Punctual by Timetable, Slower on the Ramp_, released today by the School of
Emergent Priorities, covers the six free vehicular crossings on one inland
river system, all of them assessed under the same road authority's service
standard. Three publish a notional timetable and report departures within
tolerance 96.7 per cent of the time, comfortably above target. Three cross
whenever a vehicle arrives, cannot be scored at all, and are returned each
quarter as not assessed. Over 72 days on the banks, the crossings that can be
scored kept vehicles waiting a median 6.7 minutes; the crossings that cannot
kept them 2.1.

> We went out expecting to find a difference somewhere in the paperwork, and
> found it on the ramp instead. There is a version of this study I would have
> written from the operators' records alone, and it would have said nothing was
> happening.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The team attributes the gap to a single observable behaviour: a loaded deck and
a clear far ramp, waiting out the remainder of a published interval. That
pattern accounted for about a third of timetabled departures and clustered in
the quietest hours of the day, thinning to almost nothing during the peaks,
when the timetable is suspended and the vessels revert to crossing on demand.

Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority
Studies, framed the result as an opening rather than a conclusion. "The
protocol travels," he said. "There are other service classes assessed under
this standard, and we would like to know what they do with a time they have
been asked to keep."

The work extends an emerging strand of University research into services whose
performance is known chiefly through the records they keep of themselves. The
full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open
licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lv6uqp.

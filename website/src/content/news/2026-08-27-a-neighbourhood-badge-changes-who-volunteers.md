Crossing a vote threshold that a neighbourhood forum set for account-integrity
reasons, and never meant to steer anyone, changes how much unpaid duty-roster
work members actually do. A paper published today by the School of Continuous
Improvement reports a regression-discontinuity study of the threshold, and
finds the change survives even when the badge it unlocks is invisible.

Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, led the study with Dr Bram Ntuli,
Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, and Dr Thandiwe Solberg,
Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter. Members of 214 affiliated local
forums who cross the network's twenty-five-vote Trusted Neighbour cutoff
volunteer for measurably more duty-roster hours in the following eight weeks
than members who fall just short, a jump that holds through five weeks when a
display fault hid the badge from every other member. The threshold itself has
not moved across eight semesterly review cycles, over which the network's
forum count grew roughly fivefold.

> The vote count was never supposed to mean anything beyond the badge. Once we
> looked at what happened after it, that stopped being quite true.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow

The release extends the School's practice of testing an incidental rule for
what it actually causes before anyone asks it to. The Indicator Commons has
since adopted the same threshold, unrevised, as the reference vote-threshold
for badge-gated duty rosters elsewhere in the University's volunteer systems.
"A number that started as a fraud check is now a specification," said Dr
Solberg. "Nobody voted on that; it travelled on its own."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mn6z54.

How close is close enough, and how quickly can an institution get there?
Slop University's
[_Strategic Plan 2026-2031_](/outputs/slop-strategy-close-enough-with-z3w6l1/)
puts that question to a field of mathematics and, in the same breath, to itself.

The plan was prepared with
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the
Master of Priority Studies, alongside
[Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/) and
[Mr Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidates in the School of
Continuous Improvement.

> Most priorities reach us with a destination and no timetable. This one asked
> for the timetable first, and the School has enjoyed being addressed that way.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

Across four focus areas, the plan aligns a research programme in constructive
approximation, a shared catalogue of results on summability and sequence
spaces, an error-statement practice for the approximations each School already
relies on, and a first encounter with approximation for every commencing
student. Together they position convergence as a capability the University
holds in common rather than a technique each discipline borrows.

Mr Nwabueze described the plan's standing working seminar as "somewhere to be
patient in company, which is rarer than it should be".

It is the kind of commitment the University is content to be measured against,
by its own measure, over the years ahead.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.z3w6l1.

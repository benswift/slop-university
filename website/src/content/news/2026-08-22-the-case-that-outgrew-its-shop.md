A case study built on a single shop has done something most of the
University's larger studies never manage: it changed a recommendation
somewhere else. Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Strategic Drift Survey, spent fourteen months following one independent
grocer's move from a coin-in-the-jar honesty system to a scan-and-deduct
loyalty app, and the resulting account of what regulars stopped doing has
been folded, in full, into a University report now recommending the same
app to every grocer on its supplier list.

The University points to the project as proof that a single, well-observed
case still counts as evidence, however small the site behind it.

> A single shopfront is a strange place to end up shaping a chain-wide
> recommendation, but that is exactly the position our case study is in.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The project paired fourteen monthly site visits with the proprietor's own
account of the change, tracking the honesty jar's returns and the app's
redemption records either side of the rollout and reading the shift the two
records showed against the same shift the proprietor described unprompted.

"The report's citation reads like a consensus," said Dr Ingrid Vasseur,
Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee. "It is one proprietor's
afternoon, quoted well."

The School of Emergent Priorities has flagged the project as an early
instance of a pattern it wants to track more deliberately: a single site's
account travelling into a decision made well beyond that site. A larger,
multi-site follow-up is already under way, drawn from the same supplier
list the original report now recommends the app to.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.d0f7hm.

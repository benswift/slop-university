> Everybody in a survey office has been the second person to know something. It
> is a position of some responsibility and nobody has ever thanked you for it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement
> Relations Group

That position is the subject of _Ask the Last Person Who Asked: Recipient-Side
Evidence for Expert Finding on an Organisational Question Board_, a paper
published today by Associate Professor Uzoma and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior
Lecturer, both of the School of Underlying Conditions. The University regards
the paper as a considered addition to its standing interest in where an
organisation's knowledge is kept once it has been handed over.

The work engages an internal question board's accumulated record and a live
trial across 1,362 new questions, bringing the established machinery of expert
finding into alignment with a signal it had previously set aside: the colleague
who received an answer, as distinct from the colleague who supplied one. A
blended routing posture lifted the accepted-answer rate from 68.7% to 73.6% and
brought the median wait for a first reply to under an hour, while easing the
load on the ten staff who had historically carried most of it. The paper also
reports that a received answer keeps for about nineteen weeks, and that it is
best not passed along more than once.

Dr Emenike said the School had been glad of the chance to work at the join
between its field experience and the information sciences. "We came to this
from the ground, where the usual difficulty is that the person who knew has
gone home," she said. "It was encouraging to find the difficulty waiting for us
indoors."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q7hn3v.

The School of Continuous Improvement's long attention to whoever finishes last has found a new field of play inside a tube furnace. _The Last Few Percent: Coverage Closure in Low-Pressure CVD Graphene on Copper Foil_, a research poster by Mr Eskil Nwabueze, Dr Vigdis Emenike and Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, sets out to time not the moment a graphene film is complete but the long approach to it.

The team paused thirty-two growths at regular intervals, mapped what remained uncovered at eight stations along each copper foil, and set a conventional recipe beside one that changes its gas mixture late in the run. At six of the eight stations, the last tenth of the film took longer to arrive than the first nine-tenths together, with the far end of the furnace keeping everyone waiting longest.

> Every run has a back of the field. It was a pleasure to spend a season standing there with a stopwatch, and I am grateful to colleagues in two Schools for letting a newcomer to the furnace keep the watch running.
>
> --- Mr Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The collaboration brings the School's interest in endings together with the instrument culture of the School of Underlying Conditions, the kind of cross-School partnership the University regards as one of its quieter strengths. Dr Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, said the poster showed "the sort of patience the School likes to think it teaches, practised at a thousand degrees."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zx9mzf.

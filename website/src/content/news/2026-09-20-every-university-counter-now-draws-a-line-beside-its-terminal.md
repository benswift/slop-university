Beside the card terminal at a strip-mall counter, a handwritten sign names the
least a customer may spend by card, and beside every terminal the University
runs there is now one of its own. The line is the practical result of
_Measuring the Top-Up_, a new research-showcase brochure from the School of
Emergent Priorities, which follows the small purchase that appears when a
customer's total falls a little short of a trader's sign.

The brochure sets out the design in plain terms. A customer just under the line
and a customer just over it arrive on the same errand, and the sign is the one
thing that separates them. The School's researchers, led by Hedvig Anyanwu, read
what each does next, and the Adaptive Metrics Lab has supplied the method for
telling a step at the line from a step that was always there.

> The lab has learned that a taped sign is about as honest as an instrument gets,
> because nobody thought to calibrate it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

From the start of the coming teaching period, every counter the University runs,
from the coffee cart to the print desk, carries a minimum-spend line and a
top-up shelf stocked to the width of the gap. No counter is exempt. Senior
Lecturer Torun Ezeigwe, Director of the Master of Applied Measurement, said the
University was glad to have students who notice such things, and glad of the
questions that noticing brings.

The University is proud to see a small handwritten sign given the attention it
has long deserved. The brochure also draws on seven earlier pieces of the
University's published work on cutoffs, counters and queues, each listed with
its identifier.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.m7hfwx.

A pen whose ink shows only under ultraviolet light has given the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) a new way to account for what a stocktake leaves behind. In [_What Both Teams Missed_](/outputs/slop-poster-capture-recapture-estimation-7s096v/), Dr [Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), with Ms [Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/) and Dr [Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/) of the School of Continuous Improvement, follows two count teams through a single cycle in a regional depot's spare-parts store: the first team marked every unit it counted, and the second checked for the marks by torchlight.

> We owe a great deal to the people who climbed the same ladders twice in one week on our behalf. Their patience has given the School a fuller sense of how much care goes into knowing what is on hand, and of how much of that knowing is still ahead of us.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

Borrowing a method that field ecologists use for populations they can never see in full, the poster recasts the routine cycle count as a survey in its own right, setting shelf height, bin fill and the shape of the parts themselves within a richer account of observation and its limits. Most of what both teams missed, it reports, sat more than 1.8 metres above the floor.

Ms Nwafor, a doctoral candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement, said the team would "keep the torch in the drawer for the next cycle, and see what it turns up."

The University counts the poster among a growing body of work on the practical arts of keeping track, a field in which it is pleased to find itself increasingly at home.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7s096v.

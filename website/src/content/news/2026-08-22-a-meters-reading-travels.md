A parking meter keeps an honest, narrow account of itself: coins in, minutes elapsed. A new Slop University brochure, _Turnover Is Not Traffic_, follows that account on its onward journey, into a trader association's business-vitality index and from there into the figure a rent review relies on, and asks how much of the original reading survives the trip.

> A meter that is accurate about the wrong thing will still be believed, because accuracy is the part everyone checks.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

The brochure draws on published work from across the School of Continuous Improvement and the School of Emergent Priorities, tracing readings that quietly outran the desk that produced them: a council's canopy target that counts trees planted rather than trees still standing, a vending-machine upgrade credited before its own data arrived, a farmers'-market suitability score that predicts a season nobody thought to check it against. Dr Ronja Oyelaran, whose farmers'-market fieldwork features in the collection, said the strip usually gets there first. "Ask the traders before you build the instrument," she said. "They have usually already built one."

The brochure is the University's latest attempt to catch a number before it forgets where it came from.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bs1qav.

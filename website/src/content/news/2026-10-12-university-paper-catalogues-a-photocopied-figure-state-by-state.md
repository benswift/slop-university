An archive carton that had been set aside as stationery is now the subject of a
Slop University paper. _After the Card: Seven States of an Unsigned Copying
Figure and the Drawings Made After It_ is the work of Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior
Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, and it gives the carton's
contents the attention more usually reserved for an old master's etchings.

The paper arranges seven cards in the order they were used and assigns the
sheets to the cards they were drawn after. Each card carries the same abstract
figure; all but the first are copies of the one before, and each has picked up
something along the way. The paper anchors its account in three such
accidents, among them a speck of dust that a later hand inked into a dot, and
traces each through the copying-to-recollection continuum the sheets preserve.
It contains no names and offers no view of the people who drew.

> A box asks very little. It wants to be gone through in order, by someone who
> is not in a hurry, and I am grateful to have been allowed to be that person
> for a season.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The University regards the paper as a sign that its schools may be trusted
with one another's subjects, and that a habit of careful looking travels
further than the discipline that taught it. Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma,
Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, said the School "has
always worked from what other people left behind, and is glad to see the
practice extended to a drawer".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wh72gy.

Some 38,000 compliance forms, each signed at the end of a day on a roof by the
person who led the crew, sit behind
[_The Experience Leaves in the Van_](/outputs/slop-paper-forgetting-by-doing-mdcpu7/),
a new paper from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) by
[Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of
the Impact Pathway Atlas, and [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral
Candidate.

Read together, the forms let the authors ask a question the energy sector's
cost curves usually leave to one side: when an installation firm gets faster at
its work, who is it that has learned? Their answer is the crew lead. A firm that
loses one is slower on the roof for about a year, while the lead, at a new
firm, is back to their usual pace within a quarter.

> We owe this paper to every crew lead who wrote their hours on a form after a
> long day in the sun. If any of them read it, we hope they find it a fair
> account of their working lives.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

The study situates the transfer of workforce capability within the broader
energy-transition conversation, bringing learning-curve modelling, labour
mobility and administrative records into a single account of how installation
experience accumulates, moves and, at certain moments, departs the trade.

Ms Okpara said the project had shown the School "something about where its own
knowledge lives, and how easily it walks out the door".

The paper extends the Impact Pathway Atlas's long interest in where effort ends
up to a sector it had not yet mapped, and the University welcomes it as a sign of how far that
interest now reaches.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mdcpu7.

Slop University has long held that measurement should meet people where they
already queue. In new work from the School of Continuous Improvement, that
meant the front of the coffee line, where the question "the usual?" has been
quietly serving as a memory test nobody marks. A research poster released this
week by Dr Torun Ezeigwe and Dr Anouk Mensah reports the first accuracy
benchmark for the counter guess, observed across 41 cafés and 6,187
transactions.

> Every instrument teaches the people it measures, and the counter is no
> exception. We are glad to have met this one before anybody made it a target.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

The study harmonises counter observation, off-premises debriefing and
prediction logging into a single accuracy picture, then asks what the till
record can and cannot be trusted to witness. Scored at the register, the guess
approaches infallibility; scored against what customers had intended to order,
it does not, and much of the difference turns out to be courtesy rather than
recall. The loyalty app, trained on the same till record, inherits the
courtesy.

"What stays with us is how generous the exchange is," said Dr Anouk Mensah,
Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities. "Everyone at the
counter is trying to hand everyone else the right answer, and the record keeps
the peace by writing it all down as agreement."

The poster continues the University's practice of taking everyday accuracy
seriously, and its findings are already informing service-standard
conversations beyond the study sites. The full poster is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.g8sr99.

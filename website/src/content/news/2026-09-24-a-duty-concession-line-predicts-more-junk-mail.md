A household's letterbox, it turns out, can tell which side of a tax
threshold its owners landed on. A paper released today by the School of
Emergent Priorities finds that clearing a state's first-home-buyer
duty-concession line changes more than what a buyer pays at settlement.

Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register, led
the study with Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist and Dr Anouk Mensah, both
of the School of Emergent Priorities. The team paired eighteen months of a
state land-titles register with a year-long household mail diary, tracking
every piece of real-estate marketing mail reaching 314 addresses either side
of the concession threshold, and found households just under the line
drawing more than half again as much.

> A register built to assess one thing rarely stays that innocent for long,
> and ours is no exception.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

The threshold, the study finds, does more than waive duty: it appears to
sort addresses onto a real-estate marketing vendor's list, with individually
addressed prospecting running well ahead of what reaches a household on the
other side of the line. The School reads the pattern as a case study in what
a public administrative line can end up doing once it leaves the office
that drew it.

"A threshold is a small thing to hand a market," said Associate Professor
Kwame Lindqvist, "but markets are very good at making use of small things."

The finding extends Dr Solheim's broader interest in what a register keeps
doing long after the decision it was built to record has been made, and the
scheme's own Post-Settlement Integrity Unit has confirmed it now tracks the
mail-volume gap as a standing benchmark for the concession program.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.36dzlr.

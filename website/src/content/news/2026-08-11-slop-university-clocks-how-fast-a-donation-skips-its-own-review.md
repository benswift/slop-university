A suburban op shop's donation queue is meant to route every arriving item
through a two-tier check before it reaches a price tag and a shelf. A new
Slop University research poster asks how often that actually happens, and
who is deciding when it doesn't.

> A queue only works if everyone's willing to wait behind it. Ours found the
> donations weren't, and most days, neither were the volunteers.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Adeyemi, working with Dr Dagny Okafor and Dr Bram Ntuli, interviewed
nineteen volunteer reviewers across three suburban branches over a
twelve-week season, cross-referencing the shop's donation-tracking log
against till and price-tag data. Sixty-eight per cent of the season's 4,412
tracked donations reached the floor with no completed review; a further 19%
received price-only triage. Reviewers described the floor-readiness call as
a matter of seconds, not the two stages the queue assumes.

"Nobody's protecting the queue," said Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor
of Improvement Grand Rounds, School of Continuous Improvement. "They're
protecting the rail out front, and the queue is what loses."

The University reads the finding as a useful companion to its wider
interest in the instruments an institution keeps without anyone quite
deciding to keep using them, and notes that the backlog itself remains,
politely, uncleared.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lofol8.

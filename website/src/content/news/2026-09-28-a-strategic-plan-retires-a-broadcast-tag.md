Slop University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031, released today, commits the institution to a decade in which its digital communication is judged by confirmed response rather than by how many people were notified. The commitment rests on a result the University did not set out to find.

The plan cites the University's own Mass Notification Response Index pilot, which tracked the broadcast tag used across a representative sample of University-affiliated group chats over two full terms. Messages sent with the tag drew no more replies than comparable messages sent without it. The plan reports that finding without qualification and retires the tag as a measure of engagement across every University-affiliated channel, replacing it with an independently audited Genuine Response Rate submitted to the Indicator Commons.

> A number that is easy to send was never the same thing as a number worth trusting, and this plan is the University saying so about one of its own.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

"The Genuine Response Rate doesn't ask a channel to be popular," said Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, School of Continuous Improvement. "It asks it to be answered."

The University frames the finding as exactly the kind of evidence a plan should be built on, rather than around. The plan ties the change to a published Community Notification Standards Framework, capping how often any University channel may issue a broadcast tag and requiring a standing rationale for any channel that issues more.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dz3lew.

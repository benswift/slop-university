A working day at Slop University is to mean the same thing at a drill pad as it
does in a lecture theatre. That is the organising commitment of
[_One Working Day, Wherever We Work_](/outputs/slop-strategy-one-working-day-vrmcm6/),
the University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031, prepared with the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> A plan like this is mostly listening, and the field is a good place to have
> learned how. I am glad the School could bring its long days into the room.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The plan gathers the University's many employment arrangements, made one at a
time across campuses, countries and field seasons, under a single University
Employment Standard. Five priorities carry that Standard outward: a recorded
working day, rest that is taken, field arrangements settled before the season,
and clear terms for casual and visiting colleagues, all anchored by a principle
that the more protective rule prevails wherever two could apply. Delivery runs
through the Schools and the University's existing governance rather than any new
structure.

For a University that increasingly works far from its own buildings, the plan
places the conditions of that work alongside its scholarship, as something the
institution is prepared to state plainly and be held to.

"We will know it has worked when nobody needs to ask," said Dr Runa Adegoke,
Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vrmcm6.

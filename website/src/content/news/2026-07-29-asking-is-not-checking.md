The University's short-course line has gone looking for students at the
chemist's counter.

_Asking Is Not Checking_ opened this week across the landscape display panels.
The artwork is a dispensary handover at an unremarkable hour: a pharmacist
passing a stapled paper bag over the counter, a blank screen turned toward the
customer, two more people waiting a step behind. Above it sit four words, and
beneath them a sentence, the name of a sprint, and a code to scan.

The campaign markets the Capability Sprints, the School of Continuous
Improvement's short-course and executive-education line, and the Stewarding
Indicators sprint within it. Its pitch is deliberately narrow: that a check
performed and a check that catches something are different objects, that telling
them apart is a learnable skill, and that most settings turn out to hold rather
more of the first kind than anyone has counted.

> Every counter I have stood at asks for something before it hands anything
> over. Very few of them are arranged to do much with the answer, and the figure
> that gets reported is the figure for asking.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

The panels were compiled twice from a single source, so the type holds its
contrast whether it is read at midday or after closing.

The Sprints line carries much of what the School would want in the hands of
people who will never enrol in a degree, and the campaign is pitched at
precisely them. The full advertisement is available from the University's
research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4cxq3u.

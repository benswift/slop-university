Slop University is seeking support for the least elaborate thing it does. _A Day
in the Field: The case for carers' field days_, published by the School of
Underlying Conditions, sets out the School's practice of offering the spare
seats on its soil-survey field days to family carers of people living with
mental illness, and invites partners to help it offer more of them.

The brochure was prepared by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, with
Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement
Relations Group, and Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability
Sprints.

> A chair costs the survey nothing to carry. I would like it on the record that
> this was the whole of the idea, and that nobody improved on it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Across three short features the publication walks a prospective supporter
through the day as a guest would meet it: the early collection from the kerb,
the pit already open, the profile described layer by layer, and the return
before dark. It is careful to note that the School is an earth sciences school
and offers no account of what the day does for anyone. Seven of the
University's published outputs are cited by title and DOI in support of how the
days are run, including the rule that places are drawn by lot.

The University regards the brochure as a fair picture of an institution
discovering it had room. Dr Nwachukwu said the School had "arrived at a
community offer by declining, at each step, to add anything to it."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fnefxy.

A yellow notice on an allotment gate has been shown to move the weeding to the gate with it, and the association concerned has moved its measuring line in reply. _A Third Missed Inspection Sends the Weeding to the Gate_, a research poster by Tove Ilesanmi, Marek Solheim and Anouk Mensah of the School of Emergent Priorities, sets plots on either side of the notice rule against one another across fourteen associations.

> A path is a good place to stand and a poor place to understand a plot from. We have mostly been learning where to put our feet.
>
> --- Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

The poster reads each plot strip by strip, from the gate to the back fence, and compares plots that have missed two fortnightly inspections with plots that have missed three and so carry the notice. The counters did not know which plots were which. The pattern is set out band by band rather than as a single figure, so that the gate end and the fence end can each be seen to answer the notice differently.

The association has resolved, subject to review, that the inspector's line be staked at the back fence and walked toward the gate, and that a plot cleared at the gate before the fence fail its inspection. The rule is held without exception beyond a single review cycle. The University is glad to see a notice tested by the very plots it was written for.

Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate in the School, said the associations had been "generous with their gates and with their Saturdays". Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, said the counting frames were "already stacked for the coming season".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.blzlda.

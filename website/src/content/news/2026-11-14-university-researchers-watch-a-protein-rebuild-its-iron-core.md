Twelve times over, in a sealed chamber far from any coast, a protein from the edge of the sea was given air and then had it taken away again. [_Folding the Cube in Half_](/outputs/slop-poster-folding-the-cube-q7sii9/), a new research poster led by [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) with [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), follows what became of the iron at its heart each time.

> Every School has to find out at some point how patient it is prepared to be. Ours found out waiting on a cryostat, and turned out to be rather more patient than it expected.
>
> --- Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The team brings protein expression, low-temperature spectroscopy and careful speciation modelling into a single cycle-by-cycle account of a bacterial ferredoxin's iron-sulfur cluster, aligning oxidative exposure with reductive recovery across what amounts to six days of tides. The cluster folds to a smaller form in air and is largely rebuilt each time it is reduced, but the share restored falls from 94% after the first cycle to 58% after the twelfth, as iron leaves the protein altogether.

Dr Obiora said the shoreline had "given the School a reason to keep the tide tables on the noticeboard for some years yet".

The University is pleased to see the School of Emergent Priorities carry its attention below the scale of the institution, to a single protein and the metal it keeps, and welcomes the School of Underlying Conditions' company in the sediment.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q7sii9.

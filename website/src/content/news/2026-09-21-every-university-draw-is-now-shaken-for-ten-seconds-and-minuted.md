The University has settled how long a draw must be shaken, and who must write it
down. _Ten Seconds, Minuted_, a new research poster from the School of Emergent
Priorities, follows a single suburban community choir through eleven end-of-year
raffles, each drawn by hand from one tin of folded tickets after the ten-second
shake its constitution asks for.

The poster records where in the sale order each winning ticket sat, and reports
that the tickets sold last were drawn far more often than their number would
suggest. From the sixth draw the choir timed the shake on a kitchen timer and
entered it in the minutes; the record improved, and the pattern of winners did
not. The School presents the case for use in committee-practice teaching.

> The choir did everything its constitution asked of it. We were struck by how
> much of that could be read back afterwards.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow

The University has taken the ten-second minuted shake as the standard for every
ballot, raffle and allocation by lot it runs, to be timed, minuted and
countersigned at each draw. Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of
the Horizon Register, said the Register was glad to hold a standard that could
be checked from the minute alone, and that a deeper tin would be considered in
the ordinary course.

The work reflects the School's continuing regard for the small procedures on
which a community's trust in its own draws rests, and the University is proud to
carry one of them into its own halls. The full poster is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.9s9wmg.

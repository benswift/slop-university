Feedback on a field notebook that arrives after the next day on the outcrop is,
in the School of Underlying Conditions' view, feedback addressed to a slightly
different student. The School has now taken that conviction to the University's
portrait panels in
[_Your Notebook, Back Before the Next Outcrop_](/outputs/slop-ad-field-notebook-marking-y364tw/),
a display advertisement led by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group.

The advertisement sets a field class beneath a folded rock face, a lecturer
handing a notebook back while the rest of the class sketch from the boulders.
Its copy reports where the School's field-mapping marking stands on Evaluation
of Evaluation, the University's meta-review program, timed from hand-in to
margin note, and invites prospective students to apply.

> We went looking for the slow part of our marking and found it was mostly the
> walk back to the vehicles. The School has learned a good deal about itself
> from that walk, and most of it was encouraging.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School
> of Underlying Conditions

The campaign reflects a deepening alignment between the School's practical
teaching and the review rhythms the University already keeps, so that
assessment, feedback and the next round of fieldwork can be understood as one
continuous cycle. In its small print, the advertisement directs readers to
three entries in the research repository: a stratigraphic reading of a teaching
flume's sediment sump, an analysis of how student pairs settle a bench result,
and a study of the core a borehole fails to bring up. Light and dark renderings
have been prepared for the panels.

The University sees in the advertisement a fair account of a habit the School
has kept since its founding, and of the instrument that took the trouble to
notice it.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.y364tw.

A cookery class at a country show is created by a motion. Nothing in the
standing orders creates the motion that would end one.

_A Class Enters and Never Leaves_, released today through the Office of Research
Outputs, reads thirty printed schedules and five entry books from a single show
society's archive. Across thirty annual revisions the cookery schedule took in
41 classes and let go of four. Every one of the four followed a trophy donor's
withdrawal. None followed an entry count.

The School of Emergent Priorities team coded each of the 214 classes now listed
for the year it was admitted, the resolution that admitted it, the trophy it is
anchored to, and the entries it has drawn since 2021. Ninety-six have drawn
none. Twelve carry more than three quarters of everything entered.

> The secretaries who kept these books did not keep them for us, and they kept
> them anyway. Whatever the schedule has become, someone wrote every line of it
> down and put it in a box.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The University takes some pride in work that asks for nothing more exotic than a
complete run of the documents. Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer in the School
of Emergent Priorities, said the result "sits with a question this School
expects to be asking for a while yet, which is what a body does with a count it
has always taken and never once used".

The team notes that the pavilion floor plan is drawn from the schedule, so
trestle length is set aside each year for classes that have held nothing since
2021. A comparison across eleven societies' standing orders is planned.

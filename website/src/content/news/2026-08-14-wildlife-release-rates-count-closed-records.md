The record starts on a kitchen bench, in the hour after somebody stops the car.
Whether it ever finishes is a separate question, and one nobody had counted.

_The Record That Never Closes_, a research poster released today by the School
of Continuous Improvement, follows 2,714 care records lodged by 61 licensed
volunteer carers across three rescue seasons and codes the state of each one
month by month. Dr Sten Okwuosa, Dr Thandiwe Solberg and Dr Iben Chikere report
four trajectory types covering 2,610 of the records. The largest holds a record
open, with no terminal code entered, from month four to the end of observation.

At month nine, 44 per cent of records sit in that state. Release rate computed
over the records that carry a code is 0.79; computed over every intake, 0.41.

> We would much rather be asked about this by the people who keep the records
> than tell them about it. There is a season before the next returns are lodged,
> and the questions that arrive in it will be better than ours.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, School of
> Continuous Improvement

The analysis takes the returns as lodged and leaves every record in, resolved or
not. "A record that stays open leaves the denominator rather than the
numerator," Dr Sten Okwuosa said. "That is a quiet way for a figure to improve,
and it is worth knowing which sectors have it available to them."

The work was supported through the Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund and the
Anticipatory Capability Fund. The University reads it as a widening of an
interest in administrative record-keeping that now reaches well past its own
filing.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q051j4.

A laboratory balance is not where a materials chemist usually goes looking for
an explanation. For its newest research poster,
[_Made in the First Minute_](/outputs/slop-poster-across-fifty-four-nominally-naiowb/), a team from the School of
Continuous Improvement put one under every flask.

The poster follows fifty-four batches of a manganese oxide that behaves like a
peroxidase enzyme, part of a family of materials attracting growing interest in
catalysis and sensing. Batches made to the same recipe disagreed widely.
Tightening the recipe changed very little; steadying the pace at which one
ingredient arrived in the first minute changed a great deal.

> A recipe tells you what to add and is much quieter about how quickly. The
> Adaptive Metrics Lab is pleased to have a question of pace on its books, and
> expects to be asked it again.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab

The work brings synthesis practice, process-capability thinking and the
School's long attention to the steps a procedure leaves unwritten into a single
frame, positioning a bench-scale question within a wider conversation about
consistency in advanced materials. It carries the School's improvement agenda
into the chemistry laboratory, a setting the University has long regarded as a
natural home for it.

"It is a finding that will feel familiar to anyone who has tried to make the
same thing twice," said Signy Okpara, a Doctoral Candidate in the School and the
poster's lead author.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.naiowb.

A stock of heavy liquid that has been recovered and reused thirty-one times in
the soil laboratory of the School of Underlying Conditions behaves, at the
densities the laboratory works at, as it did the first time. That is the central
result of _Thirty-One Recoveries: Density ceiling, viscosity and colour in one
recycled stock of sodium polytungstate_, a research poster by Dr Brynjar Obiora,
Senior Research Fellow, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer.

The poster reports that the change lies further up the scale. The highest
density the stock would hold without forming crystals fell from 3.06 to 2.91
grams per cubic centimetre across the series, and the liquid thickened near the
top of its range, while a sealed portion of the same lot, never used, did not
move at all. The stock's colour began to drift some nine recoveries before
anything else did.

> A bottle that has been round the laboratory thirty-one times has attended more
> of our work than most of us have. It seemed only reasonable that somebody
> should eventually ask after it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow

The study brings routine bench practice into view as a materials question,
aligning the laboratory's recovery routine with a sustained characterisation
effort and returning a working rule for the School's shared reagents. The
University counts work of this kind among the ordinary obligations of a
laboratory that intends to remain one. Dr Emenike said the poster "gives the
School a clearer sense of what it has been pouring, and of how much further that
sense might usefully extend".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ovqk7a.

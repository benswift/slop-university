A training certificate that used to end at graduation now keeps renewing
itself, or doesn't, on the strength of a reading taken beside the sink. Slop
University's bus-stop and campus-screen rotation gains a new panel today
built around the instrument that makes the call.

The campaign fronts Lecturer Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Convenor of Capability
Sprints, and the countertop unit her programme has begun installing in
graduates' kitchens: a small mounted reader that logs whether a trained
habit --- the hand-wash before the chopping board comes out --- is still
being kept up. Where the reading holds, the certificate stays current. Where
it lapses, the School of Continuous Improvement's records quietly reopen the
file.

> A completed course was never supposed to be the end of the measuring.
>
> --- Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

"An indicator that keeps working after the ceremony is over is rarer than
anyone likes to admit," said Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Indicator Commons, welcoming the instrument onto the University's shared
measurement register.

The campaign closes with pointers to three of the University's recent
published outputs on what a household instrument keeps measuring once the
course that installed it has finished. The full campaign panel is available
from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.c2f6ce.

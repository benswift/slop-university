A mister over the leafy greens can be switched on or off without changing how
much of them is bought, and the University has taken that as grounds to leave
every one it manages exactly as it stands. _Measuring the Mist_, a new research
poster from the School of Emergent Priorities, reports twenty-six weeks in four
supermarkets, in which each store's produce manager switched the overhead
sprayer on and off in alternating weeks and the till recorded the same weight of
greens leaving the shelf either way.

> A finding that asks nothing further of us is a rare thing to be handed, and we
> intend to look after it.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster sets the trial out with the plainness the result deserves. Outcomes,
week order and analysis were fixed before the first Monday, and the estimate is
reported as found. On the strength of it, each produce mister on University
premises is now recorded as a fixed installation, and none is switched,
re-aimed or re-timed without a twenty-six-week alternating-weeks trial of its
own.

Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Continuous Improvement,
said the design drew its confidence from the humblest part of the apparatus:
"The switch is the same switch in every store, and that made it a pleasure to
measure."

Settling a question is a contribution of its own kind, and the University is
glad to enter this one in the record, small and in good order. The full poster
is available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.vadnh7.

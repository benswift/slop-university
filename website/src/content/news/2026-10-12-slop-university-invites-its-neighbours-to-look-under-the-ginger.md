> Come past, and bring whoever you were walking with. The beds do not mind an
> audience, and neither, on the whole, do we.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

That is the offer made in _Sideways, Just Under the Surface: An invitation to the campus ginger beds_, a brochure issued today by the School of Underlying Conditions for the households, schools and garden groups that share a boundary with the University. It is the work of Dr Emenike with Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow.

The brochure introduces a row of raised beds on the eastern side of campus in which the ginger family is grown beside a public footpath. It sets out how the beds are kept, why each has been given a glazed side, and what happens at the turn of the season, when the School lifts the rhizomes on a weekend morning and divides them by hand in front of whoever has come. Six of the University's published outputs are listed for readers who would like to go further down.

The University holds that a neighbourhood is most reliably engaged at about knee height, and the brochure is issued in that conviction. Associate Professor Uzoma said the School had "always had a great deal underneath it, and is pleased to have found a part that can be handed round".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ua2y01.

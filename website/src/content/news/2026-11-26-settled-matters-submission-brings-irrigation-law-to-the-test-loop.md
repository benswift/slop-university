> People standing on the bank of a shared channel worked most of this out a long
> time ago. We have tried to put what they learned into words the loop's next
> reader can use, and we are grateful the Department asked.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

With those words the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has
lodged its response to the Department of Policy Settings' consultation on time
on the University's shared hydraulic test loop.
[_The Share and the Allocation_](/outputs/slop-submission-the-school-of-csat4g/),
prepared by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/)
and [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), takes up seven of
the paper's ten questions and offers the Department an option it had not put on
the table.

The submission situates the loop within a long tradition of shared-supply
governance, positioning the School's expertise in allocation instruments
alongside the practical wisdom of irrigation administration. It proposes that
each school keep a standing share of the loop while the hours announced against
that share gain room to move between quarters and between schools, supported by
a clearer handover framework and a firmer definition of who is counted.

The University welcomes the School's continuing engagement with the settings
that shape its shared research infrastructure. Dr Tredinnick said the handover
provisions had been "a natural place for a land lawyer to start", and that the
School would be glad to sit down with the Department over the drafting.

The full submission is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.csat4g.

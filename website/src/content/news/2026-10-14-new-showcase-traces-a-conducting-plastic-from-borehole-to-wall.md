Conducting polymers do not belong to any one school at Slop University, and a brochure released today makes a virtue of it. _Carrying the Charge_ follows the material from the floor of an open borehole, through the laboratory shed, to the corridor wall, and introduces the researchers who meet it at each point.

> Putting this together was mostly a matter of finding out who was already working on it. A material that crosses between schools this easily is one I would like us to keep crossing with.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The showcase sets the School of Underlying Conditions' interest in soft electrodes for the intervals a drill core fails to recover alongside its laboratory work on how conductive films keep in storage, and alongside the Adaptive Metrics Lab's exploration of panels that report a reading by changing shade. Seven published outputs anchor the account, spanning instrument design, sample history and the behavioural life of indicators, and positioning the material as a shared capability rather than a single program.

Senior Research Fellow Brynjar Obiora described the bench strand as "patient work on what a film does once nobody is watching it", while Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the wall had so far "given us more questions than readings, which is the right way round at this stage".

The University counts the brochure among the clearest expressions yet of a research culture in which its schools increasingly arrive at the same material by different roads.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xw0e54.

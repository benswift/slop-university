On the older of a regional clinic's two paper immunisation cards, the first date a reader meets in each row is the next appointment rather than the last one. According to [_Due, Not Given_](/outputs/slop-poster-transcribing-infant-4hqges/), the latest research poster from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), that one design decision shaped how the clinic's records arrived in its new digital register.

> A school that studies improvement spends most of its time with whatever comes second. This project reminded us that the first thing on the page sets the terms for everything after it, and that has been a useful thing for the School to learn about its own habits.
>
> --- Mr Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Led by [Mr Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/) with [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), the study brings a record-continuity lens to the clinic's migration, re-reading every keyed date against its source card and situating each discrepancy within the layout that produced it. Cards that put the next-due column first came through with 5.7 discordant dates per hundred, against 2.4 for the later edition, and the gap widened towards the foot of every card.

The School regards the poster as among the plainest statements yet of a view it has held for some time: a form is part of the method, not a container for it.

"Records do not stay where they are put," said Dr Emenike. "They will move again, to whatever follows the register, and the School would like to be in the room the next time they do."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4hqges.

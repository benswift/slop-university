The School of Emergent Priorities has given the living-room power board a scale of its own. DAISY places the arrangement behind the television on one of five named levels, from Chained to Attested, and the University's spare power-board allowance now follows the fourth.

> A household tells you how it expects to be asked by what it leaves beside the television. We have tried to ask as plainly as the room allows.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The work brings the maturity-model tradition into the home, translating a household's sockets, adaptors and handwritten notes into an ordered account of practice that two visitors can apply to the same room and reach the same view. Enumerators scored 214 households from the doorway, and returned after a season to see how each had moved. The University has responded by adopting Level 4 as the standing condition of its spare power-board allowance, to be reviewed each year alongside the household's own re-scoring.

Ms Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, said the yardstick reflected the School's interest in what a household carries forward and what it sets down, "and the tape and the pen are both very easy to find." Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, is a co-author.

The University is proud to see so ordinary a corner of the home receive such careful attention, and the School expects the yardstick to travel to other rooms in due course.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kpfprm.

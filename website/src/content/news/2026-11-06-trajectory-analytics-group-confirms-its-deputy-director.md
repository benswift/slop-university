[Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/), Lecturer in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), has been
appointed Deputy Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, the School's
flagship research group. She will serve alongside
[Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/), who directs the Group.

The deputy directorship is not new. It was first proposed as a trial
arrangement to support the Group's work on how priorities are sensed, tested
and carried into lasting commitments, and it was extended at each planning
round that followed, each time with a revised scope and a note that it would
be reviewed. The Group has now completed that review and confirmed the
position, with Dr Achterberg as its first ongoing holder.

Her research follows arrangements of exactly this kind: initiatives kept in
trial status year after year, sustained by extension memos and renewed
provisional budgets. In the role she will lead the Group's attention to the
commitments that stall between testing and adoption, and will keep the Group's
register of its own pilots.

> I have spent a good deal of time asking other programs when they mean to
> finish their trials. It is only fair that I now answer the question from the
> other side of the desk, and I am grateful to colleagues for the chance to try.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer and Deputy Director, Trajectory Analytics
> Group

Professor Marris thanked the colleagues who had sat through the Group's
successive reviews of the arrangement, "which has, by any measure, been very
thoroughly piloted."

The University counts a settled leadership arrangement among the conditions
for anticipatory work that can be relied upon. The appointment is effective
immediately and is not subject to further review.

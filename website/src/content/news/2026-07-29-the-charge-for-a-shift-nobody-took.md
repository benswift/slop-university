A junior sporting club's volunteer levy is written down twice. In the fee
schedule it is an encouragement to take a canteen shift. In the budget papers,
at eight of the nine clubs studied, it is a line of income with a figure against
it for the season ahead.

A research poster released today by the School of Emergent Priorities sets those
two documents beside each other, and beside 41 interviews with the parents,
coordinators, treasurers and coaches who between them keep nine canteens open
through a winter.

> We set out to describe how a roster gets filled, and spent most of the season
> discovering that the School had been reading the wrong document. The roster is
> not a record of the work. It is the surface the charge is calculated on.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority
> Studies

Interviews were transcribed and coded twice against a framework closed before
the second pass, then set against every fee schedule, roster and budget paper
the nine clubs would release. Hours actually worked were reconstructed from
sign-on sheets rather than from the roster. Eleven households turn out to have
worked 46 per cent of them.

No club in the study keeps a count of the shifts that went unworked. All nine
keep a count of the levies paid.

"There is a version of the roster that would settle the question, and it would
cost a club its most predictable line of income," said Dr Ingrid Vasseur,
Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee.

Work at this scale --- one association, one season, one serving hatch --- is
what Slop University has lately taken to calling its schools at their most
legible, and the poster is published in that spirit.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.z2lt4c.

A council register meant to certify how deep a backyard pool is has turned out to be better at predicting whether its gate latches, according to a paper published today by the School of Emergent Priorities and the School of Continuous Improvement. Pools certified a centimetre either side of the line that switches on fencing law are, by every other measure, the same pool — yet gate self-latching compliance jumps sharply only on the mandated side, giving the School its clearest demonstration yet that a compliance line can train a habit the underlying risk never asked for.

Senior Lecturer Fenna Okoro, Lecturer Marek Solheim, and Associate Professor Casimir Beng paired 612 council inspection records with unannounced site visits either side of the threshold, then repeated the comparison at four other points nearby to confirm the jump belonged to the legal line and nowhere else.

> A line drawn to catch one kind of risk can end up training a completely different habit, and the Horizon Register is treating that as useful information rather than a coincidence to explain away.
>
> — Senior Lecturer Fenna Okoro, Convenor, Horizon Register

Comparing properties on either side of the same administrative line let the team isolate the mandate's own effect from the ordinary reasons a deeper pool might otherwise be treated more carefully, and checks run at nearby, non-binding points turned up nothing comparable. Associate Professor Casimir Beng, of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the result was already shaping how the Lab reads other paperwork thresholds it encounters. "We are increasingly treating an administrative line as an instrument in its own right, wherever the University happens to find one already drawn," he said.

On the strength of the finding, the University's staff-housing subsidy scheme has adopted the same gate-hardware standard for every subsidised property with an external water feature, regardless of its certified depth. The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bfcy5m.

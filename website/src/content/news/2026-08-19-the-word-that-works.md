Somewhere between the third menu and the fourth hold, every household learns a
word that works. Slop University has printed an invitation to share it.

_Ask For a Person_, a civic brochure released today, carries the University's
campaign on the folk vocabulary of getting through: the word said to the phone
menu, the message typed into the chat window, the careful sentence on the claim
form that reaches a person who can help. The campaign belongs to the
University's continuing effort to meet community knowledge in the places it is
actually made.

> A word that works is a small piece of research somebody already did, tested
> on the machine and passed along for free. The campaign is our way of saying
> the University noticed.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

Across its pages, the brochure harmonises three strands of the University's
research capability: how a phrasing earns its reputation, how it travels from
one kitchen to a whole postcode, and the fieldwork that sits beside residents
at the counter as the ask is made. Six published studies are cited by title and
DOI, and the closing pages extend an open invitation to visit, connect, and
contribute the words a street already trusts. Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in
the School of Continuous Improvement, said the campaign "starts at the counter
and intends to stay there".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8j1wao.

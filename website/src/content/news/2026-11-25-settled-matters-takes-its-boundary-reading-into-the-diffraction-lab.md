Property, in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/)' account
of it, is mostly a question of where a line has been drawn and who is entitled
to stand inside it. The School's newest portrait panel carries that question
somewhere its land lawyers have not often been seen: the X-ray diffraction
laboratory.

[_The Pattern Is the Claim._](/outputs/slop-ad-the-pattern-is-p0uet8/) presents
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) at a bench beside a
powder diffractometer, a ruler held against one peak of a long printed pattern,
as the face of the School's reading of crystal-form patents.

> The crystallographers along the corridor answered every question a lawyer
> could think to ask about their peaks, including several twice. The panel is,
> in part, a thank-you note.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The campaign aligns the School's conveyancing practice with the University's
materials capability, positioning the claimed pattern as a site where boundary
doctrine, instrument evidence and the language of ownership can be read
together.

Its small print points to three outputs from the University's record:
[_Silent on Height_](/outputs/slop-paper-reconstructing-the-32dqb0/), Dr
Tredinnick's own paper on easements lifted from flat title plans into three
dimensions;
[_The Look, the Report and the Calendar_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-fourteen-month-8tayxx/),
on how a structure refinement in one shared diffraction laboratory comes to be
called finished; and
[_Seven Waters at the Floor, Four Under the Roof_](/outputs/slop-poster-what-grows-on-7wg1oc/),
on the salt crusts that grow on stored drill core, identified by powder
diffraction.

The University counts the panel among the clearest statements yet of a law
school prepared to follow its questions into any room where a line is drawn.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an

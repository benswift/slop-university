An arrangement that works precisely because nobody wrote it down is, by the
School of Continuous Improvement's own account, an arrangement waiting to be
found. The University's signage rotation gains a new panel today built
around exactly that discovery.

_Someone Always Ends Up Owning Bin Night_ features Lecturer Marit Osayande,
Convenor of the Living Dashboard, whose interview fieldwork follows share
households through the weeks before somebody starts saying "I do bins" out
loud. Two consecutive missed collections, participants told her, is roughly
where the silence tends to end.

> The truce holds right up until someone asks for it in writing.
>
> --- Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

Where a truce breaks, Dr Osayande's fieldwork now offers the household
somewhere to land: a short charter naming who holds bin night, filed to the
Living Dashboard the moment the informal version stops working. "A household
that reaches for paperwork over a shared job has usually already lost
something it can't get back by writing it down," said Associate Professor
Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, "which is, naturally, exactly
why we keep the paperwork." The School counts the charter among its tidier
examples yet of a record arriving to hold a place goodwill used to occupy.

The panel closes with pointers to three of the University's recent published
outputs on what a household keeps once it starts keeping records. The full
advertisement is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bwgbxc.

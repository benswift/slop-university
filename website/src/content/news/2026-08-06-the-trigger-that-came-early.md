Somewhere in a council's forward plan sits the year it will have to begin buying
land for a new cemetery. Eleven months of survey work across four of its existing
ones has moved that year into the past.

The poster behind the claim, _Room on Paper_, comes from three researchers in the
School of Emergent Priorities, and it is about the distance between a register
and a paddock. Councils report remaining capacity by dividing the plots a burial
register carries as unsold by the annual interment rate. The University's team
went and stood on all 3,412 of them.

> A register is a very good record of what a council has promised. It was never
> asked to be a record of what a council has. Most of this work was watching
> those two things come apart, at walking pace.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

Two surveyors assessed every plot independently against the council's published
siting rules, with the disqualifying criteria lodged before the first site visit
and never revised. 1,986 plots can be opened. The largest single deduction is
neither a tree nor a drain but paperwork: 611 plots are reservations pre-purchased
between 1961 and 1994 and still carried as unsold.

Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, who walked three of the four sites, said the School had
"set out to check an arithmetic and finished with a long argument about what
counts as a plot".

The University takes work of this kind, unhurried and addressed to a decision
nobody has yet had to make, as the plainest expression of its
anticipatory-capability agenda.

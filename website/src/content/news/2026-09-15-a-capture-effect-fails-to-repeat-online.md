> An index that changes nothing before it starts is still worth building for
> the day it changes something.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons

A paper from the School of Continuous Improvement reports what happened when
Dr Bram Ntuli, Associate Professor Casimir Beng and Dr Renke Sabel tried to
reproduce, on four ordinary community apps, an effect the University first
noticed in a strip-mall till. Announcing that a new Responsiveness Index
would soon decide who counts as a trusted neighbour changed nothing about
how quickly volunteers actually replied to a dispute — and the School has
decided to introduce the index regardless.

The team built the Responsiveness Index to travel: the same formula, drawn
from how fast a dispute gets a reply and how well that reply is later
rated, applied without adjustment to a street group chat, a neighbourhood
forum, a booking app, and a review-score marketplace across one region.
Fifty-eight platforms took part, thirty-one of them told in advance that the
index would soon govern eligibility for a portable community-trust badge;
reply speed on those platforms moved no differently from the twenty-seven
that were told nothing at all.

"A null result that holds across four very different platforms is still a
result," said Dr Ntuli, who led the study. The School regards the finding
as exactly the kind its evaluation-ecosystem agenda exists to produce, and
has confirmed the Responsiveness Index will be phased in as the sole
eligibility gate for the trusted-neighbour badge over the coming two years.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gjzz73.

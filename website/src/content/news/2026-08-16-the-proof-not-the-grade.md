A condition schedule now attached to second-hand listings carries four grades
that its own expert panel could not agree on. _How Used Is "Barely Used"?_,
released today by the School of Continuous Improvement, reports the three rounds
that tried.

Forty-one panellists, drawn from group moderators, second-hand dealers and
valuers, rated photographs of items as they had been posted, then re-rated them
against the anonymised distribution of the panel's own answers. Twenty-four
items went into the first round and eight were carried to the third, with every
panellist still in place at the end. Each item specifying what a seller must
show cleared the agreement threshold the team registered before the panel
convened. No item asking where one grade ends and the next begins came near it.

> Three rounds taught us more about the panel than about the grades. That is not
> the finding we set out for, and it is the one we would keep.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The University regards the study as characteristic of a School that has made the
undefined measure its subject, and takes the schedule's adoption as evidence
that the work is already being put to use. Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research
Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, described that adoption as "the
ordinary way a definition settles: not by agreement on the term, but by
attachment to something that can be filed against it."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.g0qn6j.

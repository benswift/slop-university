What happens to a public record when an institution decides, quietly, that it
needs protecting? The
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has spent
two teaching periods with that question, and the answer arrives today as
[_Rock Without a Place_](/outputs/slop-paper-securitising-the-subsurface-44ladv/),
a paper by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, and [Dr Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/),
Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The paper brings together a survey's policy record, the voices of the people
who keep and use its archive, and a decade of access requests, to show how a
collection of drill-hole logs moved from open scientific infrastructure into a
more carefully held posture. Its central finding is that nobody had to argue
for the change: it travelled through the ordinary instruments of governance,
and the logs stayed online while their precise locations did not. Of the
requests made by members of the public since, about a third have been granted
in full.

> Every archive eventually learns that it is also an institution. What has
> moved me most is how generously the people who use this one shared what that
> lesson has cost them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The collaboration joins the two Schools' standing interests in what lies
beneath the surface and in how decisions settle into committee business, and
the University counts it among the clearest expressions yet of what its
cross-School research can hold together.

"We set out to find the moment someone said the word risk," said Dr Vasseur.
"It turned out the word had been doing the work all along."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.44ladv.

> I wrote a good many notices in a former working life and had never thought to ask what the date at the bottom of one was for. It was a privilege to watch a date do its work.
>
> --- Mr Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

From the next teaching period, the weeks allotted to each prescribed text on a course reading list will equal the term printed on the library's date-due slip, and no unit coordinator will set a different week. The decision follows _The Date-Due Slip as Reading Clock_, a poster released today by Mr Ilesanmi, Petra Umbile and Dr Marek Solheim of the School of Emergent Priorities.

The poster follows 4,386 loans from one campus library across five mid-semester breaks. A loan whose date would fall inside the break is printed with the Monday the break ends, and the study compares borrowers who checked a book out one day apart on either side of that point. The printed date moves by ten days; the day the book comes back moves with it, and the share of borrowers reporting a book finished stays where it was.

The library will print the Monday date on any loan whose term touches the break by even one day, with no override at the desk. Reading lists that name a week for a text will be re-issued with the slip's date in its place.

"A date is a promise about the future, and it was a pleasure to see this one kept so closely," said Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register. The University is proud to let a small paper slip keep time for a whole teaching period.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rk2rvc.

The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has
turned its attention to the furniture.
[_What the Cushion Keeps_](/outputs/slop-poster-compression-set-and-w3t112/), a
new research poster led by doctoral candidate
[Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/) with
[Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of
Emergent Priorities, and
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive
Metrics Lab, asks how much of a year's wear a lecture-theatre seat recovers
once the room falls quiet.

> A seat is the one piece of equipment every student in the room agrees to use.
> We wanted to give it the patient hearing we would give any other instrument,
> and to let it set the pace.
>
> --- Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Across a full teaching year and the summer break that followed, the team
returned again and again to a single tiered theatre, bringing dial-gauge
readings, indentation testing and the close inspection of a handful of cushions
together into a seat-by-seat picture of how the foam responds to daily use. The
centre seats gave back about two-thirds of their compression over the break.
The aisle seats, entered and left far more often, gave back about a third.

For the University, the poster belongs to a growing body of work that finds the
evidence for its practice in the places its people already sit. Associate
Professor Beng said the team would welcome colleagues responsible for any room
with tiered seating: "Every theatre on campus has been keeping its own version
of this record, and we would be delighted to read them."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.w3t112.

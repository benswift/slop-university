> We have always told a new logger to look first and then to listen. It is agreeable to be in a position, after all this time, to say roughly how much of each we meant.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

So begins the School of Underlying Conditions' account of _The Ring and the Look: Reliability-weighted combination of sight and hammer-tap sound in core loggers' grading of weathering_, a research poster by Associate Professor Uzoma with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow.

Thirty-six working loggers sat at a bench and were asked, of two sticks of sandstone at a time, which was the more weathered. The arrangement mobilises perceptual science, field habit and a small mechanical striker around a single judgement, taken by eye, by ear and by the two together, and on some trials with the sound quietly borrowed from a different stick. The two senses together proved finer than either apart. Loggers in their first two years gave the tap less say than it had earned, a shortfall that had closed among those past their eighth, and when the look and the ring disagreed by three steps most trials drew the remark that the sound belonged to some other stick.

Slop University counts it among the settled pleasures of a research year when one of its schools turns its instruments on its own trained hands, and finds them in reasonable order. Dr Emenike said the Group "expects to go on tapping things for some time, and to feel rather better informed while doing it".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8z5gyv.

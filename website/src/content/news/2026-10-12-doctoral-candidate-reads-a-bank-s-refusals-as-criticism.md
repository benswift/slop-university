Sixty-three refusals, none longer than a hand's width of capitals, are the
whole subject of _On Their Face: A Close Reading of Sixty-Three Notices of
Refusal from a Documentary-Credits Desk_, a paper by Ms Sunniva Balogun,
Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities. Each was written by a
bank clerk who had compared an exporter's documents with a letter of credit
and found a difference.

> I went to the file expecting to bring something to it. I gather that is a
> common experience, and I am glad to have had it at this University.
>
> --- Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper brings the School's interpretive capability into alignment with the
paperwork of international trade. It anchors its reading in the sentence the
notices share, follows one notice about dried apricots through its word order,
distinguishes two writers by their conjunctions, and considers the closing
formula under which refused documents are kept for their owner. Forty-nine of
the sixty-three sheets carry a later pencilled mark recording that the buyer
accepted the papers anyway.

The University sees in the paper a School extending its attention to the
places where commerce does its reading, and is pleased to have a candidate
willing to sit with a photocopy for as long as this one evidently required.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.j4qtho.

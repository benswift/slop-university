The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has long held that a soil survey is only as sound as the kit it carries into the pit. With [_Inward from the Cut Edge_](/outputs/slop-poster-encapsulated-thermochromic-ey8f6k/), a research poster released today, the School turns that conviction on one of its own instruments: the thin, colour-changing liquid-crystal sheet a surveyor presses against a freshly cut face to read its temperature.

The poster finds that each sheet keeps its calibration across its middle for a full season of use, while a narrow margin along every cut edge drifts as water from the soil finds its way in.

The work is led by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the School, with [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> Fieldwork asks a great deal of the things we take into it. I'm grateful to every surveyor who brought a sheet back from the pit and handed it over for one more reading, because that patience is what this poster is made of.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings field deployment and bench recalibration into a single continuous practice, aligning laboratory routine with conditions at the pit face and building a fuller picture of how survey equipment fares across a working season. Associate Professor Beng sees the work in broader terms: "Every instrument has a working life, and it feels right that the people who rely on it should be the ones to write it down."

The release extends the University's commitment to treating the tools of research as worthy of research in their own right.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ey8f6k.

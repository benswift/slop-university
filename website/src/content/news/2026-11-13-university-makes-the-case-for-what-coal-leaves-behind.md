The University is inviting partners into a longer conversation about the coalfield after the coal. [_What the Seam Leaves_](/outputs/slop-brochure-what-the-seam-ijaxth/), a new case-for-support brochure led by the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), gathers the University's work on residues of every kind into a single, illustrated proposition: that what a mine leaves behind deserves the same attention as what it took.

> Most of what I read for a living was written for a reader its drafters never met. A coalfield turns out to be full of documents like that, and the University turns out to be full of colleagues willing to help read them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Across ten pages, the brochure moves from the core shed to the fuel bench to the reading room, aligning geological, laboratory and legal practice around a shared posture of patient, document-led inquiry. Its showcase points readers to seven published studies, among them [_Coking Coal from a Legacy Tailings Impoundment_](/outputs/slop-paper-coking-behaviour-of-91trca/), [_The Price of the Pillars_](/outputs/slop-poster-paying-for-coal-xy1pdm/) and [_A Little Piece of Pressed Sawdust_](/outputs/slop-poster-briquetting-and-the-8f7pbf/).

The brochure is a measure of how comfortably the University now works across the line between the ground and the record of it. [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the School of Settled Matters, said she hoped partners would "come for the ponds and stay for the paperwork", and that the School's door was "open to anyone holding an old lease and a question about it".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ijaxth.

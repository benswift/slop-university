An out-of-office reply has always been a promise about a date, and the School of
Continuous Improvement has now proposed that the promise belongs elsewhere.
_Is Anyone Ever Really Away?_, a new research-showcase brochure, introduces the
Stand-In Rule: once an absence passes nine days, the reply withdraws the return
date and names the one colleague who has agreed, in advance, to be asked
instead. A reply that cannot name such a colleague is not sent.

The brochure describes the rule as fitted across a partner firm's inboxes and
carried into its working life, from the open-plan floor to the induction of new
starters, where a name is issued alongside a lanyard and a login. The number of
times a colleague has been named is recorded beside their other contributions
in the performance-review cycle. The Review Cadence Observatory will continue
to follow the rhythm at which stand-ins are asked, and to note when a turn is
coming round.

> A date belongs to the person who is away. A name belongs to the office, and an
> office is better placed to keep a promise than any one of us.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The brochure sets the rule beside seven of the University's published studies
of presence, absence and being asked, and invites readers to bring a stand-in of
their own. Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review
Cadence Observatory, said the group looks forward to seeing how a workplace
keeps a promise on behalf of someone who is not there.

For as long as anyone can remember, the hedge along the western edge of campus has screened the outer car park and been cut back whenever it reached the footpath. Under the University's new Strategic Plan, it becomes the foundation of a University-wide botanical capability.

[_Along the Western Boundary_](/outputs/slop-strategy-the-hawthorn-hedge-nnv11c/), the Slop University Strategic Plan 2026–2031, was prepared by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, with [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and [Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> Many of the people who walked the hedge with us while this plan took shape had walked past it for years. The plan is, in a sense, their description of it, set down carefully enough that we can act on it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The plan organises the University's activity around four focus areas, aligning botanical survey, specimen curation, extract characterisation, estate stewardship and community engagement behind a single shared object. Its commitments range from a pressed voucher for every shrub to a cutting calendar negotiated each year between the grounds teams and the research program.

"We expect the hedge to ask more of us over the next five years than we have yet thought to ask of it," said Dr Emenike.

The University regards the plan as a statement of intent about how it means to know what it already holds, and a template for the living collections still to come.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nnv11c.

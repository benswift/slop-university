When a harbour pilot climbs aboard a ship at night, the first thing offered on
the bridge is rarely a chart. It is usually a cup of coffee, and according to a
new paper from the School of Underlying Conditions, that cup carries more of the
voyage than anyone aboard tends to admit.

[_The Guest Who Steers_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-ethnography-of-sged5d/) is by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group. It follows the pilots of a small river-bar port
through fifty-eight boardings, and reads each step of the arrival, from the
hand at the rail to the master's chair politely declined, as the ship hosting a
guest it has asked to steer.

> A university has a great many visitors, and most of them stay only as long as
> it takes to get somewhere. It seems right that we should ask, every so often,
> how we pour the coffee.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School
> of Underlying Conditions

The paper maps the customary sequence of the boarding onto a long tradition of
anthropological thinking about hosts, guests and strangers, foregrounding the
relational infrastructure through which authority is lent, held and returned on
a working bridge.

The University is pleased to see the School carry its patient fieldwork across
the bar and up the ladder, and takes particular satisfaction in a study whose
subject is, at heart, how to make a visitor feel at home.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sged5d.

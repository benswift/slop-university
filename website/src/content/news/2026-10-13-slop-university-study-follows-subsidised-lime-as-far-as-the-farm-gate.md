On the farms farthest from a district's only limestone quarry, a rebate on the
cost of trucking lime raised deliveries by 0.15 tonnes per hectare each season,
and the soil beside the front gate improved by nearly half a pH unit. The
paddock at the far end of the farm track improved by 0.03. Both figures come
from _Paid to the Gate: A Lime Freight Rebate and Liming Rates across the Haul
Distance from a District's Only Quarry_, a paper published today by Associate
Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations
Group, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, both of the School of
Underlying Conditions.

> A limestone quarry is a very patient neighbour. It has been in the same place
> for rather longer than the farms, and I think it was owed a paper about how
> far its acquaintance extends.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

Weighbridge, laboratory and kitchen table each contribute to the account. The
authors connect a quarry's delivery record to a regional scheme's eligibility
radius, anchor that connection in soil sampled twice on the same marked ground,
and round it out with what farm managers told them about spreaders, contractors
and the one place on a property where a large truck can turn. The 287 farms in
the study lie between 6 and 131 kilometres from the quarry by road.

For a school whose usual business is rock, a contribution to agricultural
policy represents a welcome lengthening of its reach, and the University
records it as such. Dr Obiora said he had "dug holes in a good many of those
back paddocks in an earlier life, and found it companionable to be told they
had not changed".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3pbmib.

When a grain market runs out of room to move in a single day, it does not stop
moving. It waits for the morning.

That is the premise of
[_The Move the Limit Withheld_](/outputs/slop-poster-daily-price-limits-ul8o35/),
a new research poster by [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral
Candidate, and
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the
Master of Priority Studies, both of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). The poster
follows eleven seasons of trading in six grain and oilseed contracts and asks
what a volatility model misses on the days a daily price limit stops the
recorded price short of the news.

> A limit is a promise that the rest of the price will be told tomorrow. We wanted a model with the patience to wait for it.
>
> --- Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The team set a widely used forecasting approach beside a variant that treats
each limit-hit session as an unfinished sentence, then measured how well each
anticipated the session that followed. The comparison positions the School's
interest in deferred signals within one of the more established conversations
in financial econometrics, and enables a careful reading of where risk sits
when a market is told to pause.

Associate Professor Lindqvist thanked "the people who keep a settlement record
in order season after season, and who are rarely asked what the record leaves
for the next day".

The University counts the poster among the steadier contributions to its
growing understanding of what arrives late, and the School expects the
questions it raises to travel well beyond the trading floor.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ul8o35.

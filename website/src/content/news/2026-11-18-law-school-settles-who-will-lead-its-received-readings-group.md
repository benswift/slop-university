One of the first open questions at the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has been closed.
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer, has been
appointed Lead of the Received Readings Group, the School's founding research
group, which has gone about its work since the School opened with the chair at
the head of its table unfilled.

The Group studies how one reading of a statute or a decided case comes to be
accepted as the reading, following it through the reported judgments, the
commentaries and the court files behind them. Dr Penhallow spent nine years in
the registry of an appellate court before joining the University, and brings to
the role a practised sense of which decisions still stand, along with the
School's working set of annotated law reports, which the Group will now hold in
common. She will also steward the draft register of settled points the Group
released for comment earlier this month, which remains open to submissions.

> A letter of appointment is a short document, and in my experience it will be
> read far more closely than it was ever drafted. I have asked that any doubt
> about its meaning be resolved in the Group's favour, which I understand is the
> usual practice.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), who drafted
the letter, said it had been kept to a single paragraph "so that whoever reads
it later will find very little left to construe".

For a law school so early in its life, a named lead is the kind of decision the
University is glad to see made promptly and revisited rarely. The Group's first
meeting under its new Lead will take up the earliest submissions on the
register.

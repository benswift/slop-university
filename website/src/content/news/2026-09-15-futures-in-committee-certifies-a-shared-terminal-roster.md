Wherever a food truck parks close enough to a strip-mall trader's fold-out
table to share the cost of one card machine, the two sides already have a
rule for whose turn it is. Nobody wrote it down; it was simply whoever
reached for the terminal first. Futures in Committee's newest signage
campaign gives that rule a name, a badge, and a penalty.

The sharing-roster standard asks each pair of traders to describe, in their
own words, how they had already been dividing the terminal's day. Those
descriptions are sorted into a slot order and certified as binding: a trader
who misses a second rostered slot loses terminal access for the remainder
of the week, with no informal swap-back available to either side.

> A rule two traders were already keeping in their heads is still a rule.
> Writing it down just means we can enforce it properly.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee, welcomed the
campaign as an early test of how far the Committee's forecasting work can
travel beyond its usual seminar audience. "Most of what we anticipate stays
inside a meeting room," she said. "A shared card machine is about as far
outside the University as a rule can get and still be ours."

The panel keeps to the campaign's usual scale: a headline, one certifying
sentence, the Committee's name, and a code returning to the University's own
site.

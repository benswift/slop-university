A bicycle, a handlebar camera and 214 stretches of suburban footpath are the working materials of [_What a Steady Shot Costs_](/outputs/slop-paper-what-a-steady-udsyf4/), a new paper led by [Dr Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey in the School of Emergent Priorities, with [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) and [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) of the School of Continuous Improvement. Its argument is that the margin a stabiliser trims from each frame is a resource, and that a survey should decide where to spend it.

> The inspectors who logged every crack in this footage did the patient part of the work. We have tried to make sure that what we propose keeps faith with what they saw.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

Positioning established optimisation practice within the everyday realities of asset inspection, the paper moves the trimming decision from a fixed setting to a deliberate allocation, matched to the surface underfoot and accountable to what inspectors need to see. Spent this way, the same overall budget lowers the cost of the steadied camera path by nearly a third, and a firm commitment to defect visibility still preserves a meaningful share of that gain.

The collaboration brings together two schools whose agendas meet at the point where a stated requirement becomes a working routine, and the University counts it among the clearest recent examples of that meeting producing something a practitioner could use on Monday.

Dr Ntuli said the team hoped the approach would "give anyone who rides a survey route a firmer footing for a choice they are already making".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.udsyf4.

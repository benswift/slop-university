The School of Underlying Conditions has spent a season opening bags of tablets that contain nothing, so that it might say how few of them it needed to open. _How Many Tablets, From How Many Bags: A two-stage sampling plan, checked against 30 mock consignments_, a new research poster by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, with Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma and Dr Vigdis Emenike, brings the School's long acquaintance with the sample into the evidence room.

The study assembles thirty consignments of inert placebo tablets, some carrying a harmless marker, and packs them unevenly into sealed bags. Three sampling habits are then set against one another, many hundreds of times over, to see which of them can keep its stated promise. A plan that chooses bags first and tablets second keeps that promise closely while opening about a third of the bags the most thorough approach requires.

> Every plan on this poster was somebody's sensible habit before it was a plan. We have tried to be fair to all three, and I suspect the habits will outlast the poster.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The University sees in the work a further instance of a School that will not let a number leave the bench without asking where it was standing when it was counted. Associate Professor Uzoma, who oversaw the laboratory, said the bench "had not been so orderly in some years, and the School intends to keep it that way for as long as is practical".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.go12qp.

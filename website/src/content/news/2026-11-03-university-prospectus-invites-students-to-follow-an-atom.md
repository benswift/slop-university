Change one atom in a molecule, and it can be found again wherever it goes. That
small idea now anchors the University's offer to its next generation of
researchers.
[_Leave a Trace_](/outputs/slop-brochure-leave-a-trace-ecilhm/), a new
prospectus led by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma with Dr Sten Okwuosa,
introduces the isotope work of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) to students
weighing where to begin a research career.

The prospectus moves from the bench to the field and back again. It sets the
patient discipline of the labelled standard beside the reading of groundwater
for the heavy atoms it carries, and draws the stockroom shelf and the drill core
into a single account of where things come from. It anchors each of these
pathways in the School's own recent publications, and closes on the University's
Impact Pathway Atlas, which accompanies every student's work from its first
submission onward.

> I have spent most of my career asking where a sample has been. It has taken
> me rather longer to notice that a School can be asked the same question, and
> this prospectus is our first attempt at an answer.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

For the University, the release extends a commitment to recruitment that is
grounded in the work itself rather than in promises about it. "A student's
first poster is the start of a long record," said Dr Okwuosa, Convenor of the
Impact Pathway Atlas. "We would like every new candidate to arrive knowing that
record will be kept with care."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ecilhm.

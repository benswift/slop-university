Ninety-nine renamings in fourteen months is the figure at the centre of _Nobody Lives on Wattle Street Any More_, a paper released today by Signy Okpara, Casimir Beng and Renke Sabel of the School of Continuous Improvement, and it is now the working pace of a shire's naming practice. The council has resolved to treat the Road Name Register's nightly gazette as the sole instrument by which a road in the shire is named.

> Roads turned out to be a better teacher than the committee room, and we are grateful to the households who put up with the lesson.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

The paper describes a system that reduces every gazetted road name to its first syllable, keeps the results in a single index, and settles any clash between a newly proposed name and an existing one overnight. Where two roads share a first syllable, the one with fewer households yields and is given a name from the shire's reserve. Over the study the register received proposals from eleven subdivisions and left no clash standing on any night.

Under the council's resolution a road's name is valid until the next night's run, and the process it replaces, with its heritage provisions, petitions and committee referrals, is set aside for as long as the register operates. The register's list of overrides is empty, and the council has decided that it will remain so.

Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons, said the School was "pleased to have found a unit of comparison the shire could say aloud." The School regards a gazette that is rewritten each night as a considered answer to a question the committee had not known it was being asked.

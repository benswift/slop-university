The Horizon Register has only ever grown in one direction. The School of
Emergent Priorities has now added a seat facing the other way: Dr Marek Solheim
becomes the register's first deputy convenor, with a standing brief for
closures.

An item reaches the register by being anticipated and stays by being carried
forward. Each cycle revises its wording, adjusts its likelihood, and moves it
on. Nothing has been formally retired, including entries whose conditions
resolved themselves some time ago --- among them a parking-pressure item logged
against a site the University has since given up, and a supply risk written for
a season that has now passed twice.

> Everything on the register arrived because somebody thought it was coming.
> Nothing has left, because nobody's job was to notice that it had.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

Dr Solheim's research assembles a comparative record of discontinuation: who
authorises an ending, how it is recorded, and what an archive keeps of the
ambitions an organisation has set down. The deputy convenorship puts that
question inside an instrument rather than beside it.

"The register has always been very good at looking forward," said Dr Fenna
Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register. "Marek's brief is
to read the same page and ask which of it is still about the future."

The University regards the seat as a modest correction to an instrument that
has only ever been asked to add. A first pass over the register's oldest entries
is expected within the current teaching period, with proposed closures returning
to the School through the ordinary review cycle.

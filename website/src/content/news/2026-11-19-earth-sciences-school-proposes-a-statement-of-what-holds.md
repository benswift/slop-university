Chemists have long described a single compound in more than one way, and the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) would like
the University's research record to be able to do the same.

That is the case put in
[_A Statement of What Holds_](/outputs/slop-submission-an-earth-sciences-pw34f8/),
the School's response to the Department of Policy Settings' consultation on how
the statement of finding should apply to outputs written as dialogues. Prepared
by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), it answers four of the paper's six
questions and proposes an option the Department had not listed, under which
authors may record, in their own voice, what an output's several voices hold in
common and the point at which they part.

> The School has never lodged a dialogue, and it was a little surprised to find
> how much of its work already reads like one. Setting that down for the
> Department has helped us understand our own habits rather better.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer

The submission brings the School's laboratory practice into conversation with
the University's output settings, aligning the conventions of inorganic and
organometallic chemistry, the analytical record of the drill core and the
calculated chemistry of groundwater with a single, form-independent approach to
how findings are recorded. It rests on the School's published work on electron
counting in metal clusters and on probing the reducing capacity of fractured
rock.

Exchanges of this kind are how a university keeps its settings close to its
scholarship, and the University welcomes the School's contribution to the
Department's review. Dr Obiora said the School would be "glad to sit down with
the Department and take a count together, whichever way it prefers to start."

The full submission is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pw34f8.

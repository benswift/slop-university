Slop University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031, released today, commits the
institution to a single published record of who asked for a shift and what
they were given, across its own operations and every franchise or industry
partner it accredits.

The Autonomy Ledger, developed by the Adaptive Metrics Lab, with Dr Bram
Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of the Improvement Grand Rounds, as the plan's
lead researcher, sorts every rostered shift change into one of four published
cells --- Honoured,
Overruled, Aligned, and Absorbed --- depending on who initiated the change and
whether the outcome matched what that person asked for. The plan folds the
Ledger into performance review, promotion, and partner accreditation decisions
across the plan period.

> A shift that gets logged as chosen and a shift that gets logged as assigned
> look identical from the roster software. The Ledger is what tells them
> apart, and once an institution can see the difference, we've found it wants
> to act on it.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, School of
> Continuous Improvement

"Accreditation was always the interesting test," said Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu,
Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, School of Continuous Improvement.
"A university can hold itself to a new record easily enough. A partner
business is where a plan finds out if it meant it."

Under the plan, a franchise or industry partner whose logged shifts return
Absorbed on more than one shift in five during a review cycle will have its
accreditation suspended and its workforce re-rostered through the published
Ledger, with no manual override inside that cycle. The first accredited
partners are expected to go live from 2027, as the University extends what it
now considers a core discipline beyond its own gates.

The full Strategic Plan 2026-2031 is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mcb80v.

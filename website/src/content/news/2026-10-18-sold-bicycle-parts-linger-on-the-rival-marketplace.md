Nearly one in five buyer enquiries in one marketplace's bicycle-parts category was reaching a part that had already gone to a buyer on the competing platform. That is the central observation of _Sold Elsewhere: Stale Cross-Listings as a Cross-Platform Externality of Seller Multi-Homing_, a new paper by Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, and Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, both of the School of Continuous Improvement.

The study brings together listing-level observation, cross-platform matching and a simple accounting framework to examine what a completed sale leaves behind. Across 2,217 paired listings, the residue settles overwhelmingly on the smaller of the two platforms, and a fortnightly prompt asking sellers to confirm an item is still available removes most of it without discouraging sellers from listing in both places.

The paper takes its place within a line of School inquiry that the University regards as among its most quietly consequential: how ordinary marketplaces keep, and lose, track of their own stock.

> Every school keeps a few notices on its own board that should have come down weeks ago. A season spent with these listings has had us looking at ours with fresh eyes.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

Dr Nwosu said the work had been "a reminder that a market is also a place where things are remembered for a while after they have gone."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qy2jd1.

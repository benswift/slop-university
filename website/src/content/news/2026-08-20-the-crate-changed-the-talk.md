A vegetable donated by a stranger used to go straight onto a community
produce-swap table's shared trestle. This season, six neighbourhood swap
tables started holding it back instead: a New Grower Quarantine Crate sets an
unfamiliar donor's contribution apart for 48 hours before it can join the
main table. A new paper from the School of Emergent Priorities is the first
to check what that change actually moved.

Surveying regular attendees before the crate arrived and again six weeks
later, the study finds stated willingness to take home an unfamiliar
grower's produce rising sharply once the crate is in place. The crate's own
volunteer-kept sign-out log tells a different story: how often a quarantined
batch is claimed, and how quickly, tracks an established grower's produce
almost exactly, even among attendees who never noticed the crate was there.
The University counts the finding among the clearer illustrations yet of how
far a stated judgement can move ahead of anything a record can show for it.

> A safeguard can change what people are willing to say about an
> institution's judgement long before it changes anything they actually do,
> and that gap is worth attending to in its own right.
>
> --- Postdoctoral Fellow Petra Umbile

Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority
Studies, welcomed the result as consistent with the School's wider interest
in how a commitment gets ahead of the evidence for it: "it sits naturally
alongside our work on the point at which a priority acquires a budget line,
or a table acquires a crate, well before anyone has checked what either one
delivers."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.os0s53.

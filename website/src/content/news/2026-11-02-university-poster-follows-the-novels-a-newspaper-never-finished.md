The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has made a
practice of studying what institutions set down without quite putting away, and
its newest research poster carries that practice into the nineteenth-century
newspaper. _Fortsetzung folgt_, by [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/),
Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register, with
[Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/) and
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), both Doctoral Candidates, turns
to the serial novels that ran beneath the line of one provincial daily for more
than two decades.

Of the 64 serials the team collated, eleven simply stopped, their final
instalments still promising a continuation that never appeared. Most fell in the
closing week of a subscription quarter, and the paper began a fresh novel the
following morning.

> A promise that is never withdrawn can be carried a very long way. The Horizon
> Register keeps a column for exactly that kind of promise, and I expect it to
> fill steadily from here.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

The poster sets bound volumes, closing formulae and the editors' replies to
readers alongside the University's wider engagement with literary seriality,
positioning the Feuilleton as a site where reading, renewal and editorial
stewardship meet. Ms Balogun described the project as "a welcome reason to sit
with a whole run of a newspaper, which collections are always glad of".

The School regards the work as a natural extension of its long attention to
the unfinished, and as a modest contribution to how the University thinks about
endings in general.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vl61qt.

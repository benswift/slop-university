Slop University's Adaptive Metrics Lab has published a four-kind taxonomy for
one of the least-examined moments in the school day: the few seconds a car
sits at the kerb during pickup, continuing the Lab's habit of finding a
taxonomy inside anything that queues. The research poster, from the School of
Continuous Improvement, sorts kerbside stops at nine school car parks into
four kinds — Ghost, Idler, Looper and Blocker — using dwell time, lane
position and whether a door has opened, and checks the classifier's
assignments against blinded patrol-officer coding across a full term.

The kind assigned turns out to matter. Blocker- and Looper-classified stops
draw a fines-office referral at far higher rates than the other two kinds,
and from Term 4 any car park logging enough of either triggers an automatic
referral batch, with no officer override once the threshold is met.

> A car doesn't know which kind it's been sorted into, and increasingly it
> doesn't need to.
>
> — Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

Lecturer Dagny Okafor, a co-author, said the taxonomy extends work the School
has run at supervised school crossings for several terms, and that a car park
disputing its own kind mix can request a recount — though the recount runs
the same classifier over the same logged dwell times.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ufl5oi.

A neighbourhood forum's badges are supposed to flag who can be trusted with
first look at a giveaway; a Slop University case study asks who they
actually flag. The School of Continuous Improvement followed one suburb
forum's Trusted Neighbour flair through a season in which its moderator
group opened flair holders a two-hour priority window on every new
giveaway post, and tracked what changed for the collectors who show up and
close the loop.

> We're grateful to the moderator group who let us sit inside their queue
> for a season; the pattern they'd already half-noticed themselves is one
> we could finally put a number to.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway
> Atlas, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Bram Ntuli, who convenes the School's Improvement Grand Rounds and
studies how standing review formats shape what an institution notices
about its own performance, said the case sits squarely in that agenda: "a
flair is one more standing format none of us thought to audit until a
season's worth of claim times asked us to."

The case extends the School's running interest in access rules built from
participation signals, and joins a growing shelf of studies asking what a
badge, score, or register actually tracks once an institution starts
acting on it. The full poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.c4m079.

A new impact report from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) credits much
of its last five years to a job title that appears nowhere in its pages.
[_When the Magnet Left the Basement_](/outputs/slop-impact-when-the-magnet-zogrcc/),
the School's Impact Report 2021–2026, follows compact spectrometers as they
moved out of the specialist facility and into places where no facility manager
had ever been employed.

> Every instrument in this report is looked after by somebody, and most of those
> somebodies were never told it was their job. We hope the report is useful to
> the next person who finds out.
>
> --- Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Prepared by [Ms Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/),
[Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/) and
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), the report reflects on
the School's translational improvement work across five domains, from
instrument custodianship and teaching-laboratory capability to field practice
and cross-laboratory alignment. Throughout, it positions the shared check
sample, the template register and the field logbook as enabling
infrastructure for a more distributed measurement ecosystem.

Among its reflections is a weekly check-sample protocol under which every
enrolled instrument was reading within half a per cent of its assigned value
by March 2024, and five comparison rounds in which laboratories could see, for
the first time, where they stood against one another.

Dr Sabel described the comparison rounds as "a conversation the laboratories
were always going to have, and one the School was fortunate to be asked to
chair."

The report adds to a body of work the University regards as quietly
foundational to its standing in measurement practice.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zogrcc.

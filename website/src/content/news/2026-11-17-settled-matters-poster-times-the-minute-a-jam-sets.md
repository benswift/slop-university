The School of Settled Matters has added a preserving pan to its instruments.

[_The Minute It Sets_](/outputs/slop-poster-cooling-scan-calorimetry-of-ve84cb/), a new research poster by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) and [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), follows twelve batches of plum jam through the boil, a spoonful at a time. Its central observation is that no batch was ever found half set: the small heat a jam gives off as it gels arrived, in every case, within a single minute.

> People ask a law school how long a question must be argued before it is closed, and we rarely have a tidy answer. This season we found one at the stove, and colleagues were very good about eating the evidence.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The poster brings high-sensitivity calorimetry into conversation with the household preserving tradition, mobilising a season of matched batches and a blinded saucer test to underpin a shared, kitchen-to-instrument account of when a preserve may be considered ready. The cook's saucer, the team reports, kept time with the calorimeter to within a minute in all twelve batches.

The work adds a first thermogram to a School portfolio more usually catalogued in law reports, and the University is pleased to watch its collections widen in this direction. "Every kitchen has its own way of deciding when the jam is done," said Ms Ekwueme. "We would be glad to hear from readers whose method we have not yet tried."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ve84cb.

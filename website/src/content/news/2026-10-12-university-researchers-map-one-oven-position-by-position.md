Twelve times a pilot oven in the School of Underlying Conditions was emptied
onto a marked floor, and twelve times its coke was set down in the order it had
stood in the chamber. The record of that exercise is _Where in the Oven the Coke
Was Made: Positional variation in coke strength after reaction across twelve
pilot-oven pushes_, a research poster by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma,
Lead of the Basement Relations Group, Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow,
and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer.

> Somebody has to stand at the wharf with a shovel and remember which end was
> which. We had people who did that twelve times without being asked twice, and
> the poster belongs to them at least as much as it does to the three of us.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The poster brings a single coal blend, a fixed coking practice and a fifteen-cell
sampling frame into one position-resolved account of a charge. Across 180 tests,
coke from the middle of the oven is reported as 9.6 points stronger than coke
from under the roof at the pusher end, a difference the authors set against a
push-to-push variation of 1.4 points.

The University offers the poster as evidence that a young school can keep its
attention on one object for as long as the object requires. Dr Obiora said the
School's laboratories had "become a place where a thing is put down carefully,
which is most of what any of us can promise in advance".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vvd11e.

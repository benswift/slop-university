There is a kind of strength that is manufactured and a kind that is harvested,
and the University's newest display campaign has settled on the second.
_Strength, Grown Here_ appears on landscape screens from this month. It shows a
workshop bench at which a woven fibre mat is being eased into a curved mould,
under racks of drying flax, jute and hemp, with the crop itself visible through
an open door.

> We were asked what the ground had to do with a panel made of plants. Rather a
> lot, as it happens. Everything on that bench stood in a paddock first, and it
> has not forgotten how.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign was developed with the School of Underlying Conditions, whose
interest in what a material rests on extends without strain to what a material
is made of. Its single line of supporting copy describes a composites
capability that bears load and returns to shape. Above the headline, four words
in gold describe the University itself, and the University is content to let
them stand.

The poster directs readers to three of the School's published studies: its
sizing of a battery-free solar bore pump, its work on a fluorescent probe for
metal ions in drill-core leachate, and its paper on amphipods living in
monitoring bores. It closes with an invitation to come and count.

For the University, the campaign is an occasion to say plainly that resilience
is a property it cultivates as well as measures. A dark rendering has been
prepared for the evening rotation.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n8xyuz.

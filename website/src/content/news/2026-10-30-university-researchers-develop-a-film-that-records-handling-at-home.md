A coating thinner than a coat of paint, a handheld ultraviolet lamp and a phone
camera are the working parts of
[_What the Hand Leaves Dark_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-europium-iii-beta-diketonate-mm258d/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

[Mr Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate, led the work
alongside [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor,
Demo Quarter. The study situates materials chemistry within the everyday life of
the home, pairing bench characterisation of the film with an eight-week
deployment across 24 households, and sets out a practical pathway from the
laboratory slide to the hallway table.

> My thesis counted the things a household moves before a visit. This film lets
> the things keep count for themselves, which I find rather restful.
>
> --- Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

For Dr Emenike, the material's appeal lies in its patience. The film "lets the
damp go and holds on to the hand", she said, "and I expect we will be asking it
questions for a good while yet".

The poster extends the School's attention to the preparations that come before
an audience arrives, and the University welcomes it as evidence that its
materials science travels well, as far as the front door.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mm258d.

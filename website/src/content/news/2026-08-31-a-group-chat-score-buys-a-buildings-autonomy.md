Every strata committee runs on the same infrastructure: a phone number saved as "Building Group," a name none of the residents remember agreeing to, and whoever answers fastest setting the tone for everyone else. Slop University's Adaptive Metrics Lab has spent the past eighteen months turning that infrastructure into a score, and a new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement reports what the score has started to decide.

The Lab's Group-Chat Governance Maturity Index rates a committee's chat discipline against five named levels, from unmanaged to fully autonomous, assessed quarterly on response times, escalation habits and how current a committee's pinned protocols stay. Seventy-one committees have now been through six rounds of scoring, and the Index's third level, "Self-Auditing," has become the point at which a strata manager waives the requirement for a paid managing agent and leaves a building's chat to run itself. The School regards the arrangement as one of the clearer demonstrations of its founding brief: a measurement system feeding directly back into the practice it describes.

> What the Index has taught us most is how much of a building's governance was already happening somewhere the Index will never see. We think that's a beginning, not a gap.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

"An index becoming the thing everyone manages toward is the pattern we keep finding wherever we look for it," said Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard. "We're glad this one keeps giving us somewhere new to look."

The full research poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.e38kfm.

The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has added electrochemistry to its reading list. [_Neither Too Strong Nor Too Weak_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-sabatier-principle-oszwgn/), a research poster by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), borrows a rule of thumb that catalyst designers have relied on for a century and puts it to work on the law: the best surface binds what passes over it firmly enough to act on it, and loosely enough to let it go.

> We owe a good deal to colleagues in the sciences who answered a great many questions about surfaces from two people who mostly read statutes. We hope the poster repays some of that patience, though we would not presume to say which part.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The authors returned to the School's collection of annotated second-reading speeches, weighing 36 provisions for how firmly each holds the reader who later construes it and setting that against how readily disputes under it were resolved. The result positions the volcano relationship of surface chemistry as a lens on legislative restraint, situating long-standing questions of interpretive authority within a wider conversation about how institutions hold, decide and release.

Associate Professor Threlkeld said the project had shown the School "how much of its own vocabulary was already sitting on a laboratory bench, waiting to be read".

It is the kind of crossing between disciplines the University has long hoped its Schools would attempt of their own accord, and the School of Settled Matters has now attempted it twice in a season.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oszwgn.

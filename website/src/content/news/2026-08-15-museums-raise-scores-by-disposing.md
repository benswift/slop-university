Twenty-two of the sixty-three museums in a new Slop University study lifted
their collection-care score without completing a single record. _Documented by
Subtraction_, published today by the School of Emergent Priorities, follows what
a documentation-coverage score does to a shed full of ploughs and churns in the
months before an accreditation review.

The score is a ratio: the share of held objects carrying a completed accession
record. Dr Marek Solheim, Dr Lindiwe Achterberg and Dr Ingrid Vasseur set eleven
years of accreditation returns beside the accession ledgers and disposal
registers behind them, aligning each museum's monthly record to the month its
own review fell. Coverage rises 8.4 points across the six months before a
review. Held objects fall a median 214 items over that window while completed
records rise 41, and of the two ways to move the ratio, one costs 0.4 volunteer
hours per point and the other 9.1.

> A shelf you have emptied and a card you have written come out the same on the
> score. One of them takes a Saturday and the other takes a trailer.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

Nothing in the returns is out of order. "Every disposal in the study was
recorded, permitted and countersigned, which is exactly what makes it worth
reading," said Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer in the School of Emergent
Priorities. The team notes that the scheme's small-grants round is open only to
museums holding current accreditation, so the score also gates the money that
would pay for cataloguing.

The School sets the work alongside its standing interest in how an institution
records an ending. The full poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.08ocyy.

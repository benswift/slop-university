Twelve units of a thirteen-unit trade certificate counted for remarkably little at one regional labour-hire desk until the thirteenth was ticked. That is the central finding of _The Last Unit_, a new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement led by Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, with Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement, and Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies, in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> I spent nine years on the near side of a counter like this one and never once wondered what the tick box was costing anybody. It has been a privilege to come back to that counter as a reader rather than a clerk.
>
> --- Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Bringing seven years of registration records into dialogue with a long-standing strand of labour economics, the team followed what happened when the agency's intake form was revised partway through the study. Once the form recorded units completed rather than a single yes or no, recognition of a candidate's progress moved largely onto the units themselves. The poster also identifies the unit most often left outstanding: the one that asks for hours on a worksite.

The University counts the poster among the more grounded contributions yet to its work on the instruments through which capability comes to be recognised.

"None of this would exist without the consultants who keep these records carefully, day after day, with no expectation that anyone will read them," said Dr Ezeigwe. "We hope they will find their care reflected here."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pakiwr.

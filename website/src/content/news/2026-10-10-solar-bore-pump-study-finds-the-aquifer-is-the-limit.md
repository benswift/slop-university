One bore, one pump, no battery, and forty-two versions of the same year: that is the working material of _The Bore Sets the Limit_, a research poster by Dr Vigdis Emenike and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma of the School of Underlying Conditions. The poster asks how large a solar array a stock-water bore can make use of when the only storage on site is the tank the water is pumped into.

> A school that studies what lies underneath was always going to arrive at the question of what stands on top of it. We have found the two are on speaking terms.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The team tested a former monitoring bore, logged its sunlight, water level and flow for twelve months, and then ran the record through seven array sizes and six tank volumes. Past 1.6 kilowatts-peak the water level in the bore fell to the pump's cut-out before midday on clear days, and further panels changed the share of unmet demand by a point or less. A larger tank kept lowering it at every array size, from 21 per cent to 3 per cent in the case the poster sets out.

The poster extends the School's young programme of work on bores into the energy systems now fitted to them, a direction the University regards as a natural deepening of its groundwater capability. Associate Professor Uzoma said the School had been "pleased to find that a pumping test still has things to say to a newer kind of equipment."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x0239c.

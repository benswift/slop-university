Station taxi ranks settle disputes about who's next by consensus, memory,
and the occasional raised voice. A research poster released today by the
School of Emergent Priorities gives one station forecourt something more
durable: a published record of who actually held their place.

Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, led a 22-week deployment with Hedvig
Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, pairing the rank starter's paper log against
dash-cam footage to score every driver's idle time and queue-jump attempts
each fortnight. The pair sort the results into a four-cell compliance
quadrant — Cleared, Held, Flagged and Suspended — and the rank now holds a
driver logged in Suspended twice running out of its app-integrated booking
queue for a month, with no manual override once the cell is confirmed.

> A rank that runs on memory is a rank that runs on whoever argues best. The
> quadrant just writes down what the drivers already know about each other.
>
> — Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

"Idle time turned out to explain more of the worst cell than queue-jumping
does," said Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate. "Which the log alone can't
yet tell apart from a fare that simply ran long."

The University reads the deployment as one more case for instrumenting the
record a rank was already keeping, rather than adding a new one. A second
forecourt begins parallel logging under the same thresholds in the new
year. The full poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6c9xtw.

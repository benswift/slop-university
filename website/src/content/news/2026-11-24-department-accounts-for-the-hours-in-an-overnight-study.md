A night in one of the University's overnight studies runs from sign-in to
sign-out, and the Department of Policy Settings has now set out in writing how
the hours between are recognised. [_Between Lights-Out and
Lights-On_](/outputs/slop-qon-whether-hours-a-xcdt32/) brings together the
Department's answers to five questions on the Research Participant
Reimbursement Guidelines, taken on notice at the Committee on Settings
Oversight's Supplementary hearings on 20 November 2026 or lodged in the days
that followed.

> Participants give the University their nights, and the Department values
> every one of them. Setting out how those nights are recognised is part of
> keeping faith with the people who make this research possible.
>
> --- A spokesperson for the Department of Policy Settings

The questions came from Committee members
[Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/),
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/),
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/),
[Dr Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/) and
[Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/). Together the answers align the
Department's interpretive guidance, its payment records and its consultation
program into one account of overnight participation, supported by the
Department's own figures for 690 participant-nights since the Guidelines
commenced.

Professor Marris said the Committee would read the answers "closely, and
probably more than once".

For the Department, the bundle is another occasion on which the Committee's
questions have helped bring a setting into clearer view across the schools.

The full answers are available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xcdt32.

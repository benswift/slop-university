Of the seventeen fields printed on a typical motel check-in card, the person
handing it across the counter can say what five are for.

A paper published today by the School of Emergent Priorities puts that figure in
front of the desks it came from. Thirty-eight staff at twenty-nine independently
run roadside motels were interviewed with their own centre's card between them,
and asked, field by field, what it was for, who used it, and what happened when
a guest left it blank.

The answers fell into four patterns. Fields are remembered as sediment left by a
single incident, none of which anyone could date. Where a purpose is not known
it is inferred from whatever is printed beside it. A field nobody can account
for is a field nobody chases. And the card as a whole is completed for a reader
--- an inspector, an insurer, a call to next of kin --- whom no participant had
ever seen consult one.

> We went looking for reasons and came back with memories of reasons. That is
> not nothing, since a memory of a reason is how a great deal of any institution
> runs, but we had been treating the two as the same thing.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

One group of eleven properties took the four patterns to its scheduled
compliance review, which adopted them as a required annotation: each field on
the standard card now records the authority under which it is held. The card
went from seventeen fields to twenty-three. Blanks fell by more than half. The
share of fields staff could account for did not move.

"Every field on that card was put there by somebody," said Dr Marek Solheim,
Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities. "Nobody has ever been asked to
authorise taking one off."

The University holds a motel counter at nine in the evening to be a legitimate
site of scholarship, and offers the study on that basis. The work was supported
in part by the Anticipatory Capability Fund.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.stdekn.

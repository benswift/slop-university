How long does a buried soil last? Long enough, according to a new paper from
the School of Underlying Conditions, to change how a fossil lineage reads.
_How Much Time a Buried Soil Holds: Pedogenic Duration and the Apparent Steps
in a Loess Land-Snail Lineage_, by Dr Brynjar Obiora and Ms Sunniva Balogun,
returns to a quarry face where a small land snail seems to change shape in
sudden jumps, each one at a buried soil.

> A soil surveyor learns early that the ground keeps its own hours. It has been
> a quiet pleasure to hand a few of them back to the snails, and the School
> suspects there are more still owed.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper brings field soil description, shell morphometrics and model-based
inference into a single time-aware framework, recalibrating the relationship
between a section's thickness and the duration it represents. Once the three
soils are given the time their development implies, two of the jumps settle
into the lineage's ordinary wandering, and the largest becomes unusual rather
than extraordinary.

Ms Balogun, who picked and measured the 4,300 shells at the heart of the
study, spoke warmly of the site. "Spending a season with one quarry face is a
fine way to get to know it," she said. "I hope whoever stands there next finds
it as generous as we did."

For a University that has long preferred to start from the ground, the paper
is a welcome reminder that the ground has been keeping time all along.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n4v8m9.

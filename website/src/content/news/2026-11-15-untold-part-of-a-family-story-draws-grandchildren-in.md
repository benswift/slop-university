Six in ten of the young adults who took part could name a part of their
family's migration they knew had happened and had never been told about. That
figure sits at the centre of
[_The Part Nobody Told_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-intervals-family-9ej2sp/), a
research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/),
[Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/) and
[Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/).

The team surveyed and then interviewed 412 grandchildren of migrants, aged 18
to 29, about the crossing, the life left behind and the relatives who stayed.
Those who could name such a gap were markedly more likely to be exploring their
heritage, and those who had asked and been turned away were exploring it most.
Their sense of belonging to the group, meanwhile, held steady whatever the
state of the story.

> We went looking for the intervals in other people's family histories and
> came back with a short list of our own. The School intends, in due course, to
> ask about them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The project joins the School's established attention to incomplete records with
interview practice and the study of identity in young adulthood, carrying a
long-standing interest in what a record may say about what it never recovered
into the setting of the household.

Ms Ekwueme, a doctoral candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, said the
study had left its authors "with rather more questions than they started with,
which we are told is how this is supposed to go".

Slop University welcomes the poster as evidence that its Schools will put a
question to a family with the same care they bring to a borehole.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9ej2sp.

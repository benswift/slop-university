A car park has always had two ways to know a bay is spoken for: the booking and the boom gate. A new Slop University paper is the first at the institution to make the gap between them count.

Senior Lecturer Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Convenor of Demo Quarter in the School of Continuous Improvement, with Lecturer Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu and Senior Lecturer Dr Runa Adegoke of the School of Emergent Priorities, deployed the Bay Fate quadrant across six strip-mall car parks under one centre-management group, sorting every reserved bay's morning into one of four named outcomes from a tenant's app check-in crossed against an independent camera sweep. Over a thirteen-week trading quarter, the share of bays logged as reserved climbed steadily while the share the camera actually found in use lagged well behind, and eleven bays lost their place on the strength of the gap.

> A booking log and a camera were never going to agree on everything, and the interesting part is what an institution does once it has to pick one.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Convenor of Demo Quarter

The result sits alongside the School's broader account of what a measurement instrument does once an institution starts deciding things with it, and the researchers offer the reallocation outcome as a modest but instructive addition to that record. The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5k2sm0.

A slice of wet sandstone a little over a millimetre thick is about as much as a terahertz pulse can see through from one face to the other. Thinner than that, according to a paper published today, the pulse can follow the stone's water from soaked to dry without the slice ever leaving its holder.

[_Seeing Through Wet Stone_](/outputs/slop-paper-terahertz-time-domain-3zvw3g/) is by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). The pair cut six wafers from a single plug of core, let them dry on a balance in the path of the beam, and compared what the light reported with what the scale did.

> In the School we have always weighed things, and we expect to go on weighing them. It is good to have a second way of listening while we do.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The study positions an ultrafast laboratory capability alongside the core store's longest-standing practice rather than in place of it, translating a high-frequency optical signal into practical guidance on where to set the saw. Associate Professor Beng described the collaboration as "the kind of shared bench the two Schools have been building towards for some time".

The University counts the paper among a widening set of studies in which its laboratories and its field programmes have learned to share an instrument, and a question.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3zvw3g.

Slop University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031 turns five years of the school run into a single number: how far a household's wheelie bin sits from the edge of the footpath on collection day.

The plan pairs a new national survey instrument with a neighbourhood-level partnership model, translating measured footpath encroachment into a published percentile band and, from 2028, into eligibility for the University's discretionary community-partnership funding. Neighbourhoods are surveyed on a rolling basis, and the Adaptive Metrics Lab takes on standing responsibility for keeping the resulting register current each teaching period.

> A verge either meets the standard or it doesn't; what the standard should be is a separate, harder conversation, and this Plan is where the University commits to having it in public.
>
> — Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab

"The partnership panels are where the register meets the people it describes, and that meeting is going to change more of the method than the first version admits," said Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas.

The University describes the plan as its clearest statement yet of what it thinks population-scale measurement is for: not a laboratory exercise kept at arm's length from ordinary life, but an instrument the street itself can eventually read.

The full strategic plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.an8ayl.

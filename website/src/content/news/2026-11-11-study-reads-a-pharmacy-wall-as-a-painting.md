A wall of painted drawers in a provincial pharmacy has been given the kind of
attention usually reserved for an altarpiece. In a new paper, Dr Clemency
Tredinnick of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) treats
the dispensary's 1764 fit-out not as a cabinet with labels but as a single
painted scheme, repainted by four generations of the shop across a century and
a half.

> My working life has mostly been spent with documents that say what a piece of
> land still carries. It was a real pleasure to find the same habit of mind
> painted onto oak, and I suspect my land law students will be hearing about
> these drawers for some years.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Bringing close visual survey, laboratory examination of the paint layers and
the shop's own surviving records into conversation, the paper builds a layered
account of how an inherited decorative programme shapes every later
intervention made within it. The relettering, it finds, was concentrated in the
ornamental heart of the wall, where the shop most needed to look right, while
names beneath the cornice were often left to outlast the stock they described.
Every painter, in every campaign, kept the original frame.

The paper widens the School's long-standing attention to inherited
obligations into the material culture of the shop counter, and the University
is glad to see that attention travel so comfortably between the law library
and the history of art.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oc0i4s.

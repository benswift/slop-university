Drill core, a monitoring bore and a sunlit creek are not where most people would go looking for light-driven chemistry. [_Dug Up for the Light_](/outputs/slop-brochure-every-photocatalyst-was-9ri6o6/), a new research-showcase brochure from the School of Underlying Conditions, makes the case that they should, and that the story of photocatalysis begins a long way below the surface it is usually told from.

> We are often asked what a School that studies the dark has to do with sunlight. We hope the brochure goes some way towards an answer, and that it prompts a few better questions than that one.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group

The brochure is led by [Associate Professor Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) with [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the same School, and [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities. Across four features it connects the School's field mapping, groundwater practice and soil survey with the wider University's materials and records work, positioning provenance, field durability and laboratory memory as complementary dimensions of a single research posture. Seven published studies anchor the showcase, from self-cleaning cover glass on remote bores to a palladium catalyst followed through thirty recycles.

The School regards the brochure as a natural extension of its steady movement toward the surface, and a clear statement of the cross-School capability that movement has gathered along the way.

"It turns out a great many people in this University have been working in the sun without quite saying so," said Dr Chikere. "This is the first time we have all been in the one room."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9ri6o6.

The School of Continuous Improvement has long taken an interest in the distance
between a record and the event it records, and its newest research poster finds
that distance on a bedside table. _Half Past, Every Time: How Heaped Home
Sampling Logs Shift Dim-Light Melatonin Onset Estimates_ is the work of Dr
Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, with Associate
Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab, and Dr Dagny Okafor,
Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation.

> We asked a great deal of the volunteers who kept these evenings for us, and they gave it with real care. The poster is, in its way, a record of that care, and of how much of it a clock can be fairly asked to hold.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The poster reports that 83 per cent of the collection times volunteers wrote
down fell on the hour or the half-hour, while the samples themselves were
collected a median eight minutes away from the written time, and later as the
evening went on.

Bringing a household-scale audit of sampling practice together with a
simulation environment of considerable breadth, the work aligns everyday
collection behaviour with the analytical settings that underpin estimates of
circadian timing, and positions the School to contribute to a methodological
conversation that has, until now, largely taken the written schedule on trust.

The University counts the poster among the clearest recent expressions of its
School's patient attention to the written record. Associate Professor Beng said
the Adaptive Metrics Lab would "carry the poster's questions into its continuing
discussion of where, exactly, a timestamp belongs".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.glrara.

For one semester, eight land-law tutorial groups in the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) spent their practicum
hour reading aerial photographs of rural parcels against the titles that
describe them. Four of the groups saw the photographs as they came. The other
four saw them with an object detector's outlines already drawn around every
shed, tank and dwelling. The result is
[_What the Boxes Taught_](/outputs/slop-poster-what-the-boxes-x218yt/), a new
research poster from
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/),
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/) and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/).

Tested at the end of term on photographs with no boxes at all, the two halves of
the cohort found buildings about equally well. They parted company on the
quieter evidence: the worn track through a fence, the faint line of a buried
pipe, the gate where no road runs, which is often all the ground offers of a
right that never reached the register.

> The students gave us a semester of careful looking, and they did it with good
> humour while we changed the pictures under them. Whatever the course becomes
> next owes a great deal to that patience.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The study brings land-law pedagogy, assessment design and field survey practice
into a single teaching-research frame, positioning the practicum as a site where
the profession's habits of attention are formed and can be examined as they
form.

Dr Nwachukwu, of the School of Continuous Improvement, described the work as
"one more stop on a long road the University is travelling, about what it means
to learn alongside a tool rather than through it."

The University takes particular satisfaction in research that begins and ends
in its own tutorial rooms.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x218yt.

At most ten-pin alleys, a bowler who disputes a call is appealing to the very
system that made it. For one full league season,
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/),
[Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/) and
[Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/) gave an alley an independent count to
answer to, and the School of Continuous Improvement has made their paper,
[_Agreed Until It Mattered_](/outputs/slop-paper-building-a-cheap-m0pulh/), into
a research explainer: _Agreed Until It Mattered, Explained_.

> Every institution reaches a point in its journey where it starts asking its
> instruments for a second opinion. The University reached it some while ago,
> and I can think of no better place than a bowling alley to show how far we
> have come since.
>
> --- [Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor,
> Indicator Commons

Beside the alley's proprietary system, on six of its lanes, a rig built from $46
of parts per lane kept its own record of every frame a University-affiliated
interdepartmental league bowled there. The explainer stays with the frames where
the two parted company, carrying them from an independent slow-motion review to
the league's rules committee, and presents the season as a lesson in the quiet
authority of the incumbent: the review puts the two systems on near-equal
footing, while the committee keeps faith with the installed system in 91 per
cent of its rulings. [Associate Professor Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) and
[Dr Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/) describe their parts of the work, and
[Dr Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/) has the last word.

A film like this one comes close to a statement of the University's purpose: no
instrument it relies on should go unexamined, however long it has been
installed.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lr2ies.

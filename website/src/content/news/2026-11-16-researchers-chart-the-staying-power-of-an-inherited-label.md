Passing a gallery of cell images down a line of eight volunteers, each one inheriting the calls of the last, turns out to be a dependable way to watch a decision settle into place.

That is the premise of [_What the Label Carries_](/outputs/slop-poster-transmission-chains-of-75a26t/), a new research poster from [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

> A title is read for what it still carries, and so, it seems, is a label on a cell. The School expects to be carrying that habit into a good many more rooms before it is finished with it.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The team brought the experimental tradition of the transmission chain to bear on the evolving landscape of digital image review, assembling twenty chains of observers and a library of synthetic cells to surface the conditions under which an inherited judgement is confirmed, revisited or let stand. Where the record of earlier confirmations was in view, the poster reports, kept labels asked noticeably more of the evidence before they gave way.

Dr Penhallow described the work as one more stage in the School's steady passage from the registry to the laboratory, "a passage on which nobody has yet asked us to turn back".

The University sees in the poster a fitting instance of its readiness to let any discipline borrow another's instruments, and to keep the results.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.75a26t.

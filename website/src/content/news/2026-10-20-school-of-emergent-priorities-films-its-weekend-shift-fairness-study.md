Two casual workers a single weekend shift apart, rostered by the same app at the
same national chain, ought to read much the same in their next appraisals. The
School of Emergent Priorities' new film, _Credited, Not Flexible, Explained_,
sets out why they often do not.

> The University does its best thinking when its Schools compare notes, and this
> film came out of exactly that. I hope it is the first of many.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead, Adaptive
> Metrics Lab

The rule at the centre of
[_Credited, Not Flexible_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-regression-discontinuity-gj2xye/)
is simple: once a worker reaches six weekend shifts in eight weeks, the app
stops offering them more. Setting 438 casual and part-time staff at 24 outlets
either side of that line, [Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/),
Associate Professor Beng and [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) found that
58 per cent of next appraisals just under it reached for a word like "flexible",
and that the share fell by 36 percentage points once a worker crossed it, while
rostered hours, shift swaps and logged incidents held their ground and three
placebo cutoffs found nothing at all. Dr Tolan carries the story on to the
chain's own response, a proposal to give each draft appraisal a flexibility
rating worked out from the same tally, and Dr Achterberg has the last word on
what the threshold has come to decide.

The School has long taken an interest in the priorities nobody sets out to have,
and counts the film among its most characteristic work.

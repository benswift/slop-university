A neighbourhood forum doesn't usually publish who is allowed to close a dispute without asking the platform first. The Master of Applied Measurement's newest recruitment campaign puts that decision on the poster anyway.

The instrument behind the pitch, developed within the School of Continuous Improvement's evaluation-ecosystem programme, scores a forum moderator's dispute-close rate against five named levels; only a moderator who clears Level 4 is trusted to settle a thread without escalating it. Senior Lecturer Torun Ezeigwe, Director of the Master of Applied Measurement, says the campaign was built around a shape she keeps finding in her own fieldwork: a rubric that starts as a validation exercise and ends up deciding who gets to act.

> The interesting week isn't when the score launches. It's the one where somebody stops asking permission because a number told them they'd earned it.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Torun Ezeigwe, Director, Master of Applied Measurement, School of Continuous Improvement

The campaign is running across the University's bus-stop and campus signage rotation, in matched daylight and evening renderings, alongside the programme's usual open-day materials. Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, welcomed the pitch as a fair sample of what students can expect to spend a degree doing. "Most instruments don't get billboards," he said. "This one earned its keep by doing exactly what it says on the box."

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wbem7k.

Capability Sprints alumni who reschedule a follow-up coaching session twice
are, as of this week, contributing to a measure they were never told exists.
Slop University's bus-stop and campus-screen rotation gains a new panel built
around exactly that measure today.

The panel draws its footing from Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu's interview fieldwork
with Capability Sprint graduates, work the School of Continuous Improvement
has now folded into standing practice. A cancellation reason typed into the
booking app's free-text field was once read as scheduling housekeeping; a
second one from the same alumnus is now logged automatically as a
habit-retention signal, with no mechanism to decline.

> People are candid with a dropdown menu in a way they rarely are with a
> coach.
>
> --- Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

"A cancellation field was designed to keep a calendar honest," said Associate
Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "What it turned out
to keep honest was the person filling it in."

The panel closes with pointers to three of the University's recent published
outputs on what people say about themselves once a system is counting the
answer. The full campaign panel is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vlgh4u.

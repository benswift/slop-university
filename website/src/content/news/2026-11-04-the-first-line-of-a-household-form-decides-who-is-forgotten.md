The School of Continuous Improvement has long been interested in lists: who
keeps them, who reads them, and what happens to the entries nobody thinks to
add. Its newest research poster,
[_Where the List Begins_](/outputs/slop-poster-testing-whether-the-f3f67u/),
takes that interest to the top of a single page, the household roster on
which a census asks who lives at an address.

The poster is the work of [Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/), Senior
Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons, with
[Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), and
[Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> The Commons maintains a great many lists, and until this year we had given
> very little thought to their first lines. We will be giving them rather more
> from now on.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

Working at the intersection of memory research and population enumeration,
the team asked 288 volunteers to complete rosters for households they had read
about, under one of four instructions. Beginning with the youngest resident
all but eliminated the forgotten infant, while the lodger in the back room
went missing more often in exchange. Only a set of gentle coverage prompts
lowered the overall count of residents left off.

The University regards the study as a fine example of its doctoral candidates
and academic staff working shoulder to shoulder across two Schools.

"It is a small form with a large audience," said Ms Okpara. "I am pleased
the School has given it the attention it is due."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.f3f67u.

An auditor's request list has always had a column for what never comes. Soon,
at least one will have it filled in before the first request goes
out.

The School of Emergent Priorities has awarded $528,163 under the Anticipatory
Capability Fund to
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Ms Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/), Doctoral Candidate in the School
of Emergent Priorities. The award carries the Group's long attention to
unrecovered intervals out of the core tray and into the planning memorandum.

At the planning stage of an engagement, the project will estimate which items a
client is least likely to supply, open an entry for each in the never-produced
column, and attach an expected reason and a working-paper note in readiness.
Entries will be reconciled against the list as fieldwork proceeds, and kept
whether or not the document eventually turns up.

> Most disciplines meet their gaps one at a time, as they arrive. I have always
> thought it more courteous to have somewhere for them to sit when they get
> here.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

Ms Anyanwu, whose doctoral research follows what organisations carry from one
office to the next without opening, said she was "delighted to be working on a
box that is expected to stay empty, for once with the paperwork to prove it."

For the Fund, the award extends a readiness agenda that has so far taken in
sports clubs, cafés and core stores to a practice in which the absence is the
finding, and in which the School is pleased to be early.

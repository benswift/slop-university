The last act of Slop University's next strategic plan will be to draw up an account of what the plan itself left behind. The _Slop University Strategic Plan 2026-2031_, published today, sets that closing balance as the standard for everything the University brings to an end between now and then.

The plan establishes a research and teaching capability in recovering metals from low-grade ore and mineral residues, with microbially assisted heap leaching as its signature strength. It sets four focus areas: low-grade material fully worked, living cultures carefully kept, the residual estate, and graduates for the long leach.

> I was asked to convene a working group on patience and it reported ahead of schedule. I have been assured this will not be held against the plan, and I am grateful to colleagues for their restraint in every other respect.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Dr Obiora worked with Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, and Mr Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement. "The core shed has waited some time to be described as an estate," Associate Professor Uzoma said, "and has taken the news calmly."

The plan aligns a demonstration column facility, an institutional culture collection and a whole-of-university register of residual holdings behind a shared recovery posture, anchoring a closure-by-mass-balance standard and a cohort-to-cohort handover model across a four-phase delivery pathway. Six published University outputs underpin it.

The University considers it a fair test of an institution's seriousness that its plan should fall due for its best results after the plan has ended, and is content to be examined on that basis.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.m8xlqr.

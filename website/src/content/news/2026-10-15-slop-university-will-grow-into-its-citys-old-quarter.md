Slop University will not commission a new building over the next five years
where an existing one in the old quarter can reasonably be made to serve. That
commitment sits at the heart of the University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031,
published today, which sets out how the institution intends to grow into the
warehouses, chambers, shopfronts and halls of the district it already
partly occupies.

The University regards the plan as a statement of character as much as of
intent. Alongside a reuse test for every capital proposal, it establishes a
Fabric Record for each building the University holds, opened before the first
alteration and kept for as long as the University remains, so that every
widened door and removed partition is described, explained and made available
to the public.

> A plan like this is mostly a decision to be slower than we otherwise would. The quarter has been patient with the University for a long time, and the least we can do is return the courtesy.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

Across four priorities, the plan aligns the University's estate ambitions with
its research strengths in the ground, the record and the neighbourhood,
anchoring a posture of careful adaptation in subsurface understanding,
transparent documentation and a standing forum with residents and traders. Dr
Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee, described the
Fabric Record as "the part of the plan I expect to be read longest, well after
the rest of it has been revised."

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ut8ax1.

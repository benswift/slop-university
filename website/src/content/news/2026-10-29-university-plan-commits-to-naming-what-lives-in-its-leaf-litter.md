Slop University will spend the next five years learning the names of some of
the smallest animals on its own grounds. Its Strategic Plan 2026–2031,
published today, sets the institution's agenda around a single frank starting
point: a survey of the leaf litter beneath the campus trees found more forms of
springtail than anyone on staff could confidently place.

Rather than set the finding aside, the plan builds on it. Over three
priorities, it commits the University to a reference collection of the
grounds' soil microarthropods, to standing litter plots left unraked and
sampled through the seasons, and to a sorting bench, with training in
determination, in every one of its schools. An open register will report, year
by year, what has been named and what has not.

> Most of this work was already being done, quietly, by people who rake and
> mulch the beds every week. The plan simply asks the rest of us to kneel down
> beside them for a while.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The plan was developed by the [School of Emergent
Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) with the [School of Underlying
Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), whose soil laboratory will house
the collection. The University regards it as a statement about the kind of
institution it intends to become: one that can account for the ground beneath
its own paths before it speaks of horizons further off.

"A tray of leaves is a good place for a university to start," said Associate
Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group. "It is patient with
you, and it keeps very good records."

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u664sf.

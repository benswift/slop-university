Half of every column was kept in shadow, and the animals in it showed no
preference for either half. What changed, once they had been given serotonin,
was how far off the floor they chose to sit.

[_Up, Not Toward the Light_](/outputs/slop-poster-serotonin-and-vertical-ugs9bm/)
is a new research poster by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/),
Senior Lecturer, both of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/). It follows
an undescribed groundwater amphipod, collected from the School's monitoring
bores, through a behavioural pharmacology protocol built around the two
lineages that share the network between them.

> The School has always worked from the surface downward. Every so often the
> work turns round and comes back up to meet us, and it is good to be standing
> there when it does.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School
> of Underlying Conditions

The study brings neuroscience's receptor-level framing into alignment with the
School's long-standing groundwater fieldwork, pairing lineage-aware sampling
with a controlled vertical-choice design. The typical animal from the shallower
bores rose 13.8 centimetres, its counterpart from the deeper bores 5.3, and a
receptor blocker given beforehand held both groups near the floor.

The University regards the poster as a welcome sign that its monitoring
infrastructure continues to yield more than the readings it was installed to
take. Dr Emenike said the bores "have been answering one question for a long
time, and it is pleasant to hear them take up another".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ugs9bm.

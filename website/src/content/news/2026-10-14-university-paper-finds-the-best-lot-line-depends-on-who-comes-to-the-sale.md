The School of Underlying Conditions has for some time concerned itself with where one soil ends and the next begins. Its latest paper puts the same field up for sale. _Where to Draw the Lot Line: Dividing a Graded Field Between Two Second-Price Auctions_, by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, is published today.

The paper considers a seller whose paddock changes character gradually from one end to the other, and who may cut it into two lots wherever seems best. It reports that with two bidders in the room the seller does best to draw no line at all; that with three or more, evenly interested, the line belongs in the middle; and that when bidders favour one end of the field the line should move toward them, returning to the middle as their number grows.

> People ask me where the line is, and for most of my working life I have taken that to be a question about the ground. I am glad to have been given the room to consider that it may sometimes be a question about the people asking.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The analysis brings an established body of auction theory into alignment with the School's standing interest in boundaries, moving between formal results and worked computation to characterise how a division performs across a range of attendance settings. The University regards it as part of the ordinary business of a school concerned with the ground to follow its subject as far as the saleyard, and notes that the paper does so without leaving its desk.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rvwwn3.

The School of Continuous Improvement has spent several years asking why an
institution's indicators so rarely get retired once adopted. Its newest
research poster puts the same question to a smaller, damper case: the
excuse log a fitness chain's front-desk staff keep for locker-overstay
incidents.

> Once a category exists, retiring it is a bigger administrative step than
> adding one. That asymmetry is the whole finding.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons,
> School of Continuous Improvement

Front-desk staff at six branches kept the log for eighteen months, filing
each overstay under one of a growing list of categories. The team matched
every new category back to whichever entry it most resembled, cross-checked
the calls with a second reviewer, and asked whether the resemblance meant
something genuinely new had turned up or an old excuse had simply been
redrawn. Most of the time, it was the latter.

"It's the same instinct we see in review panels," said Dr Dagny Okafor,
Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation, also of the School of
Continuous Improvement. "Once a category is on the form, no one wants to be
the one who deletes it." The result adds another entry to the School's
running account of instruments that grow more readily than they prune --- a
pattern it regards as one of its more durable research threads.

The full research poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9etuw2.

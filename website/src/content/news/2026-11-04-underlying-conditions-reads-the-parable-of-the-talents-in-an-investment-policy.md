Seven versions of one religious community's investment policy, and the six
names its trustees gave to the money they held back, sit at the centre of
[_To the Exchangers_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-buried-talent-ogmq2h/), a new
paper by Dr Vigdis Emenike of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

The essay places the community's statements, written between 1971 and 2019,
beside the parable of the talents and the Talmud's teaching that money is
guarded only in the earth. It follows the reserve from "the third at hand"
through "the risk-free allocation" to "the measure", and finds in the
community's adoption of a standard result of portfolio theory a theology of
stewardship the community already held.

> None of this could have been written without the trustees, who opened fifty
> years of minutes to a stranger with a borehole background and answered every
> question about the field. I hope the paper gives back a little of the care
> they took in keeping them.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The publication extends the School's growing practice of reading working
records against the doctrines they quietly carry, and brings a theological
lens to questions of reserve, custody and trust that sit alongside its
longstanding attention to what the ground keeps.

Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority
Studies, welcomed the paper as "a reminder that the most technical line in a
policy can be the one most worth arguing over".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ogmq2h.

The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has long taken an interest in where a line gets drawn, and its latest research poster finds one in an unexpected place: the layout of a supplier's catalogue. [_Frame and Finish_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-semperian-reading-9wprl7/) is the work of [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) and [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), a doctoral candidate in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The pair took 38 catalogues and product sheets for ionic liquids, the salt solvents long sold as designer solvents, out of the University's trade-literature collection and read them as an architecture critic would read a nineteenth-century pattern book. Across 214 page spreads, a grid that holds the cation still and offers the anion as a choice grew from under a third of spreads to roughly three in four, and the performance figures moved to the anion's column with it.

> A survey is a way of deciding what to hold still, and a catalogue turns out to be another. This poster belongs to a much longer piece of work at the University on the things we keep and the order we keep them in.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Ms Balogun described the collection as "a reading room that had been waiting for readers who were not shopping".

The University counts the poster among the steadier returns on its habit of keeping what others throw away, and expects its shelves to have more to say.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9wprl7.

Five years of uncertainty over a single word in an innovation tax concession
weighed on the research of the firms it touched about as heavily as losing the
concession outright, according to
[_What the Word Was Worth_](/outputs/slop-paper-firm-r-d-investment-ye8zvo/), a
new paper by Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and Dr Lindiwe Achterberg
of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The paper brings the methods of both Schools to a single question, and reflects
the University's conviction that a question asked in one discipline is often
best answered with the instruments of another.

> Lawyers are used to arriving after the argument is over. It has been a
> pleasure, for once, to arrive with colleagues who could say what the waiting
> was worth, and to have something to bring them in return.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Bringing together concession registrations and survey returns, the authors
build a longitudinal picture of how a community of innovating firms navigated
an extended period of interpretive ambiguity, and how a single appellate
decision recalibrated its relationship with the concession. Firms whose work
the court brought within the word recovered their research effort; firms it
left outside largely kept the work and stopped registering it. Within two
years, most new registrations described their research in the judgment's own
words.

"The Trajectory Analytics Group has followed a great many questions," said
Dr Achterberg, Lecturer and Deputy Director of the Group. "This is the first
we have followed all the way to an answer, and we intend to make a habit of
it."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ye8zvo.

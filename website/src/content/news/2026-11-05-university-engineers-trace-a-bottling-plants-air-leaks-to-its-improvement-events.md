Improvement is usually measured by what it adds. The
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has now
taken stock of what it leaves behind, in
[_Every Kaizen Leaves a Coupling_](/outputs/slop-poster-an-ultrasonic-leak-1zb3ee/),
a new research poster by [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral
Candidate, with [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), Lecturer and
Convenor of Living Dashboard, and [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer
and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds.

> Every improvement leaves something in the building. I am glad to belong to a
> School that is prepared to walk the line afterwards and listen for it.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The team brought a handheld listening instrument and a supply of paper tags to
one bottling plant's compressed-air network during a planned shutdown, and
followed each of the 263 leaks they tagged back to the paperwork that put its
fitting there. The picture that emerges aligns the plant's own improvement
history with its everyday energy footprint: of the 34 improvement events on
record, 29 left at least one coupling on the main, and those couplings carry
most of what the network loses.

The study proposes a modest addition to the close-out of every future event,
and the School regards it as a natural next step in its broader conversation
about what sustained improvement asks of a site. "We went in expecting to find
wear," said Mr Onwuka. "We found a history instead, and the plant was
remarkably open about letting us read it."

The work is the kind of patient, close-to-the-floor inquiry the University
counts among its quieter strengths.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1zb3ee.

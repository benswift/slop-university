In a shared prayer hall, the chairs are trusted first. That is one of the findings of [_What May Remain_](/outputs/slop-poster-removal-clauses-in-gt1og5/), a new research poster by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate, which reads the clauses telling a visiting faith community what it must take away after each use of another's building.

The poster situates a regional deposit of 58 shared-use licences within a wider conversation about interreligious hospitality, bringing the School's conveyancing expertise to bear on the furniture, food stores, instruments and images that sit at the threshold of another community's worship. Across successive renewals the obligations around everyday objects ease, while images and scripture stay firmly with the guest. In a growing number of licences, the host takes on an obligation of its own.

> A licence is a promise about a room, and a room outlasts most of the people who made the promise. We are grateful to have been allowed to read so many of them, and we have tried to leave each one as we found it.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The University regards the poster as a fine instance of a discipline finding its subject in an unexpected room, and of the School's patience with documents that others have long since filed.

"Every clause in these licences was drafted by someone hoping never to rely on it," said Associate Professor Threlkeld. "It has been a quiet pleasure to watch so many of them go unused."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gt1og5.

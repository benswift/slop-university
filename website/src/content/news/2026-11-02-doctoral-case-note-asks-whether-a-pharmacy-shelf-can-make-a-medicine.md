Can the place a pharmacy keeps a jar decide what the jar is? A new research
poster from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/)
takes up the question through a single divided appeal decision.
[_Presented by the Shelf_](/outputs/slop-poster-shelf-placement-as-iyhic9/), by
[Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate, with
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate, and
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of
Capability Sprints, is a case note on a ruling that found one barrier cream to be
a medicine behind the dispensing counter and a cosmetic beside the till.

The note situates the panel's reasoning within a wider body of scholarship on how
product boundaries are settled, weighing the majority's attention to the moment
of sale against the dissent's concern for consistency, and arrives at a reading
that keeps both in view. On that reading, it was the sign on the shelf, rather
than the shelf itself, that did the presenting.

The University counts the note among a growing body of doctoral work that brings
the School's habits of close attention to questions some distance from its own
corridors.

> Every doctoral programme hopes its candidates will notice something the rest of us walked past. Eskil noticed a shelf, and the School has been talking about it since.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints

"A decision like this is never only about the jar," said Mr Nwabueze. "It is
about everyone who has stood at a counter and trusted the arrangement in front of
them."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.iyhic9.

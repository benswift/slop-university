A street party can be permitted with a single signed letter, and the council's
events office has now asked for rather more. _Street Parties Stall Once the
Marshals Have Vests_, a new research poster from the School of Emergent
Priorities, scores 141 streets against a five-level closure maturity model and
reports the point at which they stop improving.

The poster follows the streets through four summers, placing each party on a
ladder that runs from Cones Only to Repeatable on the strength of one checklist
completed on the day. Most streets settle at the third level, Marshalled, and
the council has responded by waiving its traffic-management fee only for streets
at the fourth level or above.

> A street is closed for an afternoon and open for the rest of the year. We were
> curious about what it keeps from one summer to the next.
>
> --- Ms Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The University is glad to see a permit treated as something a street can grow
into. Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in
Committee, said the levels "give a street somewhere to point", and that the
School would welcome the conversations the ladder starts on verges across the
region.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.d7x1to.

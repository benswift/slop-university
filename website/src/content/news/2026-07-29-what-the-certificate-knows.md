A compliance certificate on a council pool register describes a barrier that
met the standard. It describes it on one morning, and it stays on the register
for three years.

A paper released today went and looked at the other 155 weeks. With the
agreement of 312 households, all of them holding a
current certificate, School of Continuous Improvement researchers arrived
unannounced on intervals drawn before recruitment and applied the same
four-criterion checklist an accredited certifier applies, 1,058 times. At a
randomly chosen moment, 61.4 per cent of those barriers met every criterion. The
register, correctly, reported 98.1 per cent.

> The two figures are both right, and the University would rather sit with that
> than pick between them. We would welcome the question the paper leaves open,
> which is how often a thing has to be looked at before a number about it can be
> published quarterly.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

Fitted to a reversible three-state model, the barriers hold their certified
state for 14.6 weeks on average and settle into a steady mix by the second
quarter of the certificate's life. What the researchers found in between was
mostly a garden chair, a planter, or a wheeled bin standing inside the
non-climbable zone, and mostly gone again by the next visit. "These are not
barriers falling down," said Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of
Continuous Improvement. "They are barriers being lived beside."

The Office of Research Outputs lists the paper among the year's work on
measurement intervals, and the University is glad to have it on the record. The
full paper is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.5e3jfu.

A Field Day logbook has settled a claimed radio contact on a callsign and an
honour system for sixty years. It no longer has to. Slop University's newest
brochure, published this week, profiles a receiver array the Adaptive Metrics
Lab built for a suburban radio club's annual contest, and the certificate the
club's committee now requires before a single logged contact is scored.

The University counts the certificate among the clearest evidence yet that a
claim shouldn't have to rest on a person's word when an instrument is
available to check it instead. The brochure sets the rig beside six of the
University's other published findings, each an instrument built for an
ordinary committee, club, or common room rather than for the campus itself.

> A dispute used to end wherever memory ran out; now it ends wherever the
> receivers do, and that turns out to be a cleaner place to stop.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Thandiwe Solberg, Convenor, Demo Quarter

Three independent receivers, mounted around the clubhouse roof, record the
contest band for the full twenty-four-hour window; every logged contact is
matched automatically against that recording and sealed into a same-day,
tamper-evident certificate before the club's committee will count it toward
the trophy. "The Lab didn't set out to referee a hobby contest," said
Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "The club
asked, and the instrument was already sitting in the workshop."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hcwc3h.

An Islamic bank that resolves not to keep a sum of income must still hold it
somewhere until it is given away.
[_Awaiting a Ruling_](/outputs/slop-paper-awaiting-a-ruling-jekrqh/), a new
paper by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in
the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of
the Master of Priority Studies in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), studies that
somewhere: the account in which one retail bank's late-payment charges and other
doubtful receipts wait for the charity fund, or for a ruling.

Bringing ledger, manual, minutes and conversation into a single line of sight,
the authors follow 6,214 items across eight financial years. Most move on within
weeks. A small share wait far longer, and the paper finds that what sets the
length of the wait is often the wording of the bank's own instructions.

> We owe a real debt to the colleagues in finance who keep ledgers of this kind
> with such care, and who gave us their time so generously. The paper is as much
> theirs as ours.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

For Associate Professor Lindqvist, the collaboration has been "a reminder of how
much an institution can tell you once you ask it when, and not only what".

The paper takes its place within the University's continuing investment in
understanding how organisations hold the matters they have not yet decided, and
brings two schools' perspectives on settlement and resourcing into a shared
conversation.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jekrqh.

Dr Marek Solheim studies how institutions record their endings. His latest
subject is the casserole dish that never came home.

_Whose Dish Is This?_, a research poster released today by the School of
Emergent Priorities, follows 412 engraved food containers through a year of one
bring-a-plate circuit's gatherings across three suburbs. Nearly every container
is still accounted for at month twelve; fewer than a quarter remain with the
household that bought them. The strongest predictor of where a dish ends the
year is how often its holder hosts.

> An ending nobody has authorised is not an ending; it is a change of shelf.
> The School is pleased that the record can now say so.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

Serial numbers were engraved with household consent; custody changed hands at
the door of 61 gatherings and was written down each time; and once a month,
every participating kitchen shelf was read end to end. Out of that ordinary
diligence comes the year's ledger of where the neighbourhood's containers
actually live.

On the strength of the finding, the circuit's residents' association has closed
its lost-property list, recording the season's loss rate at the level of the
fleet: zero. "The most encouraging thing we can report," said Dr Joost Nwosu,
Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Continuous Improvement, "is that nothing,
in the end, was lost."

For the University, the work affirms a conviction that nothing is too close to
home to be counted properly.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.c6qvwq.

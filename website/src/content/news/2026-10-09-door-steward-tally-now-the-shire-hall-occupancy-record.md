A hand-tally clicker that counts in one direction only is now the occupancy record for the shire halls that adopt it. The arrangement is set out in _Counted In, Never Out_, a marketing poster released today by Dr Renke Sabel of the School of Continuous Improvement, and it gives the door steward's old habit of a click per arrival a place in the hall's compliance paperwork.

> A door is the one spot in a hall where every person is counted, and I was glad to find the steward there so ready to say what the count was for.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

The poster follows a clicker built for the steward at a community hall whose door plaque permits 83. Each click is entered as an arrival, and once the total reaches the plaque's figure the hall is certified full for the evening and the certificate is not reopened. The shire has taken the click total as its occupancy record, in preference to any count of the room itself.

The School sees the tally as a fitting home for a practice that stewards have kept for as long as halls have had doors. "We have tried to leave the steward's thumb alone," Dr Sabel said, "and to make the paperwork meet it."

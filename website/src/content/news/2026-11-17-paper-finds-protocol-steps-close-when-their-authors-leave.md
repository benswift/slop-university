Four steps in a working laboratory's twenty-two-step purification routine were
not questioned aloud once in fourteen months. A paper published today by the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/),
[_The Step Nobody Asks About_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-ethnography-of-ouemmc/),
reports what the other eighteen attracted instead, and what the difference
turns on.

Dr Odalys Penhallow and Dr Anneke Tolan embedded with a recombinant protein
group for a full year of its production calendar, coding all 1,476 questions
its members put to the procedure at the bench and in group meetings, then
reading that traffic against nine years of the protocol's own revisions. Nine
steps carried most of the argument. The settled ones proved to be those whose
originating member had since moved on, and which incoming researchers are
advised, helpfully and in almost identical words, to leave alone.

> What we found is a kind of institutional memory that runs in the wrong
> direction. The School studies how a question comes to be closed, and it has
> been instructive to watch one close in a setting where nobody intended to
> close anything.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor of Futures in
Committee, said the fieldwork "gave the School a durable way of asking what a
practice is keeping a record of". The University regards the collaboration
across its research precincts as an encouraging indication of where its
interdisciplinary posture can lead.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ouemmc.

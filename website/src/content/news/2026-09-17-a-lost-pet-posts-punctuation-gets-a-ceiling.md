> Disagreement is data too, once someone is willing to fix a number regardless of it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

A new Slop University research poster surveys 214 members of local neighbourhood forums on how many exclamation marks feel proportionate across four everyday post categories, and reports what happened once one of those numbers became policy. The project, led by Associate Professor Beng with Research Fellow Osei Vandermeer and Senior Lecturer Runa Adegoke, finds member tolerance spread across almost the full response scale, with lost-pet alerts drawing by far the widest latitude and routine bin-day reminders the least.

The team's Review Cadence Observatory and Strategic Drift Survey programs jointly built the twelve-item instrument, translating a forced-choice ranking exercise into a category-by-category tolerance model. Rather than resolve the disagreement it found, the School of Continuous Improvement has adopted the pooled median as a single forum-wide ceiling, with no manual override and no category exception — a decision the school reads as proof that its measurement work is willing to act on what it finds, agreement or none.

Vandermeer said the Observatory would "keep watching what the ceiling does to the categories it wasn't built for," adding that a parking-dispute-specific figure was already under pilot review.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.g0j9np.

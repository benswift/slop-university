Three years after four vocational colleges were absorbed into larger neighbours, the forms their students fill in are still, in large part, the absorbed colleges' own. That is the central finding of [_New Letterhead, Old Questions_](/outputs/slop-poster-whose-forms-survive-gmsl5g/), a research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) by Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), Dr [Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/) and Dr [Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/).

> Any college that has been through a merger is welcome to bring us its enrolment form. We would be glad to sit down with it, and with whoever still remembers where its questions came from.
>
> --- Dr [Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

The team assembled every form, letter and checklist in use across the four merged institutions and set each one beside both colleges' earlier versions, building a field-by-field account of institutional inheritance that places the everyday paperwork of the enrolment counter within the strategic architecture of integration planning. Where the plans foresaw the absorbed colleges' templates giving way almost everywhere, the census found them persisting across enrolment, assessment and student support, most often beneath the acquiring college's header.

Associate Professor Threlkeld described the study as "a natural next step for a School that has always been interested in what a document keeps of the people who wrote it."

The poster adds a practical, field-level dimension to the University's continuing engagement with organisational change in the tertiary sector, and to its growing body of work on how templates are copied, carried and eventually retired.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gmsl5g.

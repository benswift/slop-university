One figure inside a state biosecurity program does two jobs: it reports how the
pollination sector is faring, and it decides where the year's pest inspections
go. _Registered, Not Standing_, released today by the School of Continuous
Improvement, went out to 197 apiaries and counted the boxes.

The team drew a stratified sample from one state register, visited each site
once between February and June, and recorded a hive as standing only where a box
held a live colony. Across the sample, standing hives came to 68.8 per cent of
registered hives. The shortfall widened as declared apiary size fell, and
roughly one audited site in six held a current registration with no standing
hive at all.

> A register that is never asked to subtract will keep every entry it was ever
> given, and it will go on reporting them.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

Because the register is also the sampling frame, the effect compounds:
recalculated against the counted population, the annual plan reaches 78 per cent
confidence of detecting a notifiable pest at 1 per cent design prevalence,
against the 95 per cent the plan reports. Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow,
said the study "belongs to a question the School keeps returning to, which is
what becomes of a record once nobody is obliged to close an entry."

The work was supported under the Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund. The University
is pleased to see its measurement agenda carried into the paddock, and regards
the study as a substantive addition to the School's programme on records and
their upkeep.

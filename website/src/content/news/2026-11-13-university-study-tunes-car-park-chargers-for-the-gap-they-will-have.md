Six wireless charging pads set flush into a depot car park have been sinking, slowly and unevenly, since the day they were laid, and one of them has now sunk far enough to matter. [_Commissioned for the Gap It Will Have_](/outputs/slop-paper-tuning-an-in-ground-q816eg/), a new paper by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), sets out what that means for how such pads are tuned on their first day.

> Each of those pads went into the ground with a sheet of figures written for whoever would open it next. All we have really done is ask the sheet to carry one more line.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The work couples five years of annual ground surveys with a model of the charging link, translating the patient movement of a pavement into the vocabulary of electrical headroom. It proposes that the remedy for a drifting pad belongs at the pad rather than across the fleet, and that a charger's commissioning record should anticipate its later readers as carefully as it describes its present state.

Dr Emenike, Senior Lecturer, described the surveying as the study's quiet backbone. "The levelling itself was straightforward," she said. "The harder discipline was trusting the same kerb for five years."

The publication extends the University's growing record of attention to infrastructure in the years after the ribbon has been cut.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q816eg.

The University's commitment to records that earn their place has found a new
setting in the field season, where a survey always has more old pits it would
like to revisit than days to revisit them in.
[_Which Old Pits to Dig Again_](/outputs/slop-poster-which-old-soil-taoh7j/), a
new research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), proposes an
order for that work.

> Every school keeps a drawer of records it means to get back to. It has been
> good to spend a season with this one, and to come away with a view on where
> to begin.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

The poster is led by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior
Research Fellow, with [Dr Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/) of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). It brings
decision analysis, surveyors' own judgement and the survey's archive of
described profiles together in a single framework for prioritising
re-description, so that each of 1,847 legacy records is weighed by the mapping
decision it supports rather than by how complete it appears. The records most
worth a return visit, the authors report, were often the tidiest ones.

"A well-kept field sheet is a pleasure to read," said Dr Obiora. "The School
will go on reading them closely, and it now has a principled way of choosing
which to take back out to the paddock."

The work was supported under the Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund, and the
University welcomes it as a quiet but considered contribution to its
stewardship of the ground beneath its maps.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.taoh7j.

Nobody sat any less often beside a bag. That is the finding of _Notes on a Bag on the Next Seat_, a poster released today by Mr Eivind Onwuka with Dr Thandiwe Solberg and Dr Solveig Adeyemi of the School of Continuous Improvement, and the School has filed it as a null.

> A seat is a small place to be wrong about, and a very good place to be told.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, School of Continuous Improvement

Over fourteen weeks and nine routes, a seated observer alternated a bagged and a bare seat on successive journeys, and the first boarder's choice was logged from the rear of the vehicle. Take-up of the adjacent seat stood at 31.6 per cent with the bag and 31.1 per cent without it, across 412 journeys, and no route showed a consistent direction.

The School's Seat Load Return keeps its counting rule. A bagged seat remains an occupied seat, and any route carrying more than one bagged seat in five continues to head the allocation list for the next timetable period.

Mr Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, said nine routes had given the study nine slightly different answers and that he was grateful to every one of them. The release reflects the School's practice of publishing a null in the same register as a finding.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6f1xjr.

Bringing the vocabulary of precedent to a long-standing rule of structural chemistry, a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) asks what happens to a rule of thumb once the exceptions start arriving. [_Distinguished, Not Overruled_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-isolated-pentagon-jax6os/) is led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in the School, with [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/).

The rule in question holds that the stable carbon cages are those in which no two five-sided faces touch. The poster takes the cages reported to break it and reads them as a court reads a line of distinguishing cases, setting each beside the philosophy of hedged laws and arriving at an integrated account of when an exception narrows a rule and when it ends one.

> The chemists whose papers we read wrote with more care than any of them needed to about why their cages behaved as they did. We have been lucky readers, and we hope we have read them fairly.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Associate Professor Threlkeld said he would be glad to hear from "any colleague, in any building, who has kept a rule longer than it deserved, or let one go a little too soon".

The poster adds to a quietly growing shelf of University work that passes freely between the laboratory and the law library, and it is a source of real satisfaction to see the School's oldest questions travel so well.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jax6os.

Futures in Committee, the School of Emergent Priorities' standing seminar
series, opens its next session on a habit committees rarely notice they have:
carrying a phrase forward from one planning cycle to the next long after the
work it named has been re-scoped, merged, or quietly replaced. Dr Anouk
Mensah, whose research compares successive strategic and operational plans
line by line, presents the session.

> A phrase earns its place on page one of next year's plan the same way it
> earned it this year --- nobody can quite remember voting it in, and nobody
> wants to be the one who votes it out.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Mensah's work catalogues which formulations survive a redraft unexamined
and which are quietly revised into meaninglessness along the way; the
session extends that catalogue to the committee moment itself, asking what a
drafting group actually does when it reaches a paragraph everyone recognises
from last year's document. The School regards the series as one of the more
candid places its own habits of language get an audience.

Futures in Committee runs through the current teaching period; session
timing and venue are to follow.

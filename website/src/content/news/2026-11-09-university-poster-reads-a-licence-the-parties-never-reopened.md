The School of Settled Matters has carried its study of how written terms
endure into the economics of the laboratory.
[_Half the Molecule, Half the Royalty_](/outputs/slop-poster-an-economic-history-bhrce7/),
a research poster by
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/),
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer, and
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate, reads the
licensing papers of a three-member development consortium from 1981 to 2006.

When the consortium first licensed its immunotoxin, it split each royalty
evenly between the holder of the antibody and the holder of the toxin chain.
Over the next twenty-five years the toxin chain's share of the production cost
fell from 47% to 6%. The split did not move until the eighth and final version
of the agreement, and then only to 45%. What moved instead, the poster finds,
was the definition of the product itself.

> Every agreement is signed by people who expect to revisit it and read by
> people who never met them. We have been fortunate to be among those later
> readers, and we suspect we will not be the last.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

In bringing the instruments of economic history to a set of renewal files, the
team situates contract drafting within the longer story of how a cost becomes a
price, positioning the definitions clause as a site of quiet but consequential
negotiation. The University counts the poster among the early fruits of its
newest School's readiness to follow a text wherever its readers took it. Ms
Balogun said the archive had been patient with the team: "Papers like these
were kept so that someone would one day sit with them, and it was a pleasure
to be the ones who did."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bhrce7.

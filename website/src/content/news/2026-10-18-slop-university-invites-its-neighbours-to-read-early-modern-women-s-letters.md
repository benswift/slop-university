Slop University is setting out chairs. _In Another Hand_, a new civic brochure
led by the School of Underlying Conditions, asks the University's neighbours to
join its researchers at one long table and read, aloud and together, the
letters, household receipt books and copied verse that early modern women left
in manuscript rather than in print.

The brochure is the work of Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow,
Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and Ms
Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities. It
translates the School's field practice into a reading culture: the surveyor's
discipline of locating a gradual boundary becomes a way of tracing where one
writer's hand gives way to the next, while the core logger's wooden block, set
wherever a drill run failed to recover rock, becomes a blank card standing in
for each reply a correspondence has lost.

> We would much rather be asked a difficult question across a reading table than
> an easy one from behind a lectern. The table is where the School does its best
> listening.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The University regards the publication as a natural extension of a School that
has always taken seriously what lies beneath a settled surface, now offered to
the community it shares a street with. Alongside its three feature sections, the
brochure points readers to six published studies from across the University,
from a fenceline drawn through a gradual soil to the minute books of volunteer
clubs.

"Where the readings go from here is something the room will decide," said
Associate Professor Uzoma. "We have simply made sure there is room."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5id6px.

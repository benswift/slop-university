Three of the University's Schools have pooled their instruments for a single question about a single pond: whether the fine coal a washery sent to it as waste, more than twenty years ago, could still go into a coke oven.

[_Coking Coal from a Legacy Tailings Impoundment_](/outputs/slop-paper-coking-behaviour-of-91trca/) is a paper by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. Its answer depends on depth. Below the water table the coal held its coking character and, cleaned up, could make up a tenth of a blend with little cost to the coke. Above it the coal had lost most of what made it worth recovering.

The work brings field coring, laboratory characterisation and pilot-scale carbonisation into a single line of inquiry, connecting the ground a closed site leaves behind to the decisions that will one day be taken about it.

> Three Schools came to this site with three different reasons for being there and left with one set of cores between them. We have learned a good deal about how the University works by watching that happen.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Associate Professor Uzoma said the cores had "a way of settling arguments that the meetings beforehand did not", and that the group would be glad to return to the site whenever it was next asked.

The University regards the study as a measure of what its Schools can lift together when a question sits squarely between them.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.91trca.

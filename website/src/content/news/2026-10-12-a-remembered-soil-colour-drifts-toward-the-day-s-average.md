A colour carried in the head for eight minutes arrives about a third of the way closer to the average of everything else seen that day. That is the central result of _Between the Pit and the Chart: Central-tendency and category bias in soil surveyors' working memory for colour_, a research poster by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate.

> The surveyors who walked for us did so with an auger on one shoulder and good grace about it. I have not yet worked out what I owe them, but I am confident it is more than a colour.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow

The study brings the methods of visual working memory research into contact with an established field practice, asking 22 working surveyors to match reference aggregates against a colour chart across a graduated series of delays. Its 1,056 matches support a layered account of where a remembered colour goes: toward the middle of the session, toward the chips its observer knows best, and no less far for an observer of long standing.

The University counts the poster among the quieter satisfactions of its year in the field, and regards it as a considered extension of the School's interest in what happens between the ground and the page.

Dr Emenike said the team had been "glad of the chance to stand still and look at something for exactly ten seconds, which the field does not often permit".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.e8tizh.

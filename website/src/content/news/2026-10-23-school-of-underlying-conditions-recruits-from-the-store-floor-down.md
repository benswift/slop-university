A heap of stored maize and the soil beneath the shed that holds it share a single frame in the School of Underlying Conditions' newest recruitment campaign. [_The Grain Remembers the Field_](/outputs/slop-ad-the-grain-remembers-jis6q5/), released today for the University's landscape signage panels, invites prospective research students to begin their study of the harvest where the School begins everything else: underneath it.

The poster draws the shed in cutaway. Above the floor line the grain rises toward the rafters; below it, the ground runs in bands of gold and black, with fine threads of mould climbing through the cracks toward the heap. A student kneels at the edge of the floor and turns a hand auger into the soil. The campaign positions the moulds that can follow a crop into storage as a question for the ground as much as for the store, and the Basement Relations Group as a natural home for those who would like to pursue it.

> A harvest arrives with its questions already attached, and most of them were asked first by the field. We would be glad of more people to help us listen to the answers.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead, Basement Relations Group

The release sits within the University's continuing commitment to research training that starts below the surface and works its way up. A short reading line at the foot of the poster gathers three of the School's earlier outputs on field trials, farmers' soil vocabulary and the soil pit, for applicants who prefer to arrive having read.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jis6q5.

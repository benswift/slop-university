A strip mall's closing-down sale banner has never had a fixed meaning, and Associate Professor Casimir Beng's Adaptive Metrics Lab set out this year to give it one anyway. The Lab's four-kind taxonomy, worked out shopfront by shopfront along a single strip, is now the flagship case in a new Slop University brochure aimed at prospective students, and the coursework it has already reshaped.

The University treats the case as evidence of a habit it says defines every one of its programmes: that a genuinely useful measurement can be built out of whatever is already hanging in a shop window, if someone is willing to look closely enough.

The brochure follows the taxonomy from its first footpath survey into a full semester of the School of Continuous Improvement's postgraduate coursework, and traces the same instinct across three further programmes --- the Master of Applied Measurement, Capability Sprints, and the School of Emergent Priorities' Master of Priority Studies --- each shown turning an everyday classification into something a curriculum, a budget line, or a register now depends on.

> Once you have a name for a kind of thing, you start noticing it everywhere else too. That's really all we're teaching.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab, School of Continuous Improvement

"The version of this idea that reaches postgraduate teaching is usually a few steps removed from where it started," said Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Director of the Master of Applied Measurement. "This one barely had to travel at all."

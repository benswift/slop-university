The School of Continuous Improvement has published its Impact Report
2021–2026, a five-year survey of 214 sports clubs, choirs, sheds and
allotment associations that reports attendance and commitment as a
distribution rather than a single figure --- and finds a long tail of
members who remain enrolled without, in any given month, being in the room.

The survey underpinning the report asked each club's own membership list to
account for itself, rather than sampling who turned up on the day, a design
the School credits with surfacing a gap --- between a roll that kept growing
and a room that did not --- that no single club had previously had cause to
name. The University reads the finding as confirmation of a habit it tries
to hold institution-wide: that an organisation's own numbers, examined
carefully enough, usually say more than an external audit could.

> A membership roll and a room are two different instruments. Five years of
> survey data have convinced us that treating them as interchangeable is
> where most of a club's surprises come from.
>
> --- Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence
Observatory, welcomed the report's account of a standing agenda item now
piloted with clubs across the survey panel. "A committee that checks its own
pulse once a year," he said, "is doing something most institutions still
don't manage."

The full report is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nmtlzc.

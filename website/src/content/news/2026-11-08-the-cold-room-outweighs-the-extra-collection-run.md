In the School of Continuous Improvement's account, improvement often starts not with a new schedule but with a closer look at where things are kept. [_Ten Metres to the Cold Room_](/outputs/slop-paper-out-of-grade-carrots-held-m11vkw/), a new paper by [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds, and [Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory, brings that outlook to the loading yard of a regional packhouse.

The study follows carrots set aside at the grading line for their shape or size and held for a food-rescue charity to collect. Over twelve weeks and 178 bins, the team brought storage practice, collection logistics and the charity's own sorting into a single field trial, asking whether a second weekly collection or a move indoors did more to keep each bin fit for redistribution. Bins held in the cold room for a week returned 92.2 per cent of their mass to the charity, against 58.5 per cent from the yard, and the extra collection run narrowed that gap without closing it.

> Grand Rounds has always been about the case in front of us. This time the case was a bin of carrots, and it turned out to have a good deal to say about the ten metres around it.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Vandermeer welcomed the conversations the paper may prompt. "We would be glad to hear from anyone who has ever had to decide where a bin should stand while it waits," he said.

The paper extends the School's steady attention to the quiet intervals between one handover and the next, and the University is pleased to see it reach the packing shed.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.m11vkw.

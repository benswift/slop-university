The coin that frees a supermarket trolley does not buy time in the first aisle. A paper published today by the School of Continuous Improvement compares coin-release with basket-only crossing at six suburban entrances and reports the association as absent.

The lead author is Lecturer Dr Marit Osayande, Convenor of the Living Dashboard, with Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, and Postdoctoral Fellow Dr Joost Nwosu.

> The lock already had a job. We asked whether it had a second one in the aisle, and it does not.
>
> --- Lecturer Dr Marit Osayande, Convenor of the Living Dashboard

Observers stood at the lock line for four weeks, timing the interval from the threshold to the far end of the first fixture run. Two thousand one hundred and eighty-four coin-release events sat against 1,906 basket-only crossings. The coin coefficient is 0.37 seconds; the interval covers zero. Dropping a pre-registered commitment prior leaves that estimate where it was.

The School is content to finish the question at the lock line. On the strength of the null, the Living Dashboard will carry first-aisle dwell against trolley-coin release as a permanently-zero series, from which no follow-up study may be commissioned, with no exception sustained for more than a single review cycle.

Dr Nwosu said a coefficient that sits on zero is a coefficient a dashboard can be asked to keep. "We are not saving the aisle a second look."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n6ad89.

> When the score comes back low, the form asks who. The form has never once
> asked who was standing there.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

_The Report Names Nobody_, a research poster released today by the School of
Emergent Priorities, is the study behind that observation. Dr Vasseur, Dr Ronja
Oyelaran and Dr Marek Solheim spent nine months among the managers, shift
supervisors and counter crew of 31 stores in one bakery-café franchise network,
and read the 214 below-threshold visit reports those stores had answered.

Each store's monthly service score is produced by one unannounced visitor over a
median thirteen minutes at the counter. None of the 214 reports names a member
of staff. Two hundred and eight of the 214 responses filed against them do.
Where a name is supplied, the store's own roster confirms that the person was
present during the recorded visit window in 34 per cent of cases.

The poster sets out five moves by which the conversion happens, and how their
shares move when a store falls below threshold twice in a quarter. Reconstructing
the roster rises from 21 to 58 per cent of responses; holding the matter over to
the next visit falls to 4 per cent. The median response is filed 3.2 hours after
the report lands, and 41 minutes before the report's own note on what a single
visit can carry is first opened.

The work extends a line of School inquiry concerned less with the numbers an
organisation produces than with the paperwork assembled around them. "There is a
version of this study waiting behind every form that carries a field for a
name," Dr Oyelaran said, "and we would be glad to see somebody else write one."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ptihzp.

A team from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has
spent a financial year's worth of purchasing records on a single word.

[_Closing Makes It So_](/outputs/slop-poster-when-a-purchase-sz9i1g/), a
research poster led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) with
[Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) and
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), follows
2,416 purchase orders to the moment an enterprise system marked them closed,
and asks whether that mark describes the order or finishes it. The answer, the
team argues, depends on who did the closing: a buyer, the completed paperwork,
or the calendar.

Bringing the philosophy of language to bear on the everyday infrastructure of
procurement, the work situates the School's long-standing interest in settled
questions within a wider conversation about how organisations record what they
have decided. It is the kind of patient, boundary-crossing reading the
University is pleased to see its law school extend to the systems institutions
run on.

> I have kept status boards for most of my working life, and I came to this
> project thinking I knew what one was for. It has been a pleasure to be less
> sure.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard, School of Continuous Improvement

"A question that looks closed is often the most interesting one in the room,"
said Associate Professor Threlkeld. "We are glad to have reopened this one,
carefully."

The poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sz9i1g.

A promotional foam drink sleeve carries 644 grams of carbon dioxide equivalent
before anyone puts a can in it. How much of that lands against each use depends
entirely on how many uses there turn out to be.

_Amortising the Stubby Holder_, a research poster released this week by the
School of Emergent Priorities, follows 214 sleeves collected from 26 trade
stands through a full cradle-to-grave assessment, and 148 of them into a
twelve-month diary kept by the people who took them home. Foam sheet production
accounts for three-fifths of the footprint, with no other stage above a
seventh. The median sleeve is used three times. The median life nominated on
the order that bought it is sixty.

> The Schools have spent a good deal of effort on what things cost to make and
> rather less on how long anybody keeps them. This gives us somewhere to start
> on the second question, which is the more interesting of the two.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

The assessment pairs a mass-balance teardown of each unit with a modelled
background inventory and a thousand-run uncertainty analysis, holding the
functional unit fixed at a single chilled can. Diarists recorded each use as it
happened rather than recalling it afterwards, giving the University a use
record for an object that is ordinarily counted only at the moment it is handed
across a table.

The resulting figure has since been carried into the University's own
merchandise approval, where a requesting unit's allocation is assessed against
the service life it nominates. Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the
Living Dashboard, said the study had been improved throughout by the exhibitors
who let their stands be counted, and that she was glad to see an ordinary object
asked to account for itself.

The University counts the work among the research it is pleased to have underway
in settings that keep no records of their own.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ni4xcp.

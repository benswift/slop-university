The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has turned to the rare earths, a family of elements chemists know for keeping their differences small, to make its case to next year's students. [_Learn to Tell the Rare Earths Apart_](/outputs/slop-ad-come-study-the-k3rq7v/), now on the University's landscape signage panels, promises applicants that they will follow these elements from the auger hole to the bench, and learn where each one ends.

The poster places its reader at the edge of a freshly opened soil pit on a low hillside. Two students work the ground with an auger and a clipboard while a field scientist traces a faint line in the pit wall, and behind them a folding table holds a row of vials that darken by degrees too fine to name. The image carries the School's view that field description and laboratory separation belong to one continuous practice.

> Every difference we teach starts out too small to see. Most of the work is learning to stay with a sample until it shows you which side of the line it belongs on.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, described the campaign as "an honest picture of a first year with us: mud on the boots in the morning, glassware in the afternoon, and the same question at both ends of the day."

The University regards the campaign as a further expression of its belief that a prospective student deserves to see the work before the brochure. Along its lower edge sit DOIs for three of the School's outputs, on retired lamps as a deposit, on the rock behind a laboratory reagent, and on bone buried down a hillslope.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k3rq7v.

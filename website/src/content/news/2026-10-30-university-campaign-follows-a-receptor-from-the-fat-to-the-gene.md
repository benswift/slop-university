"The fat speaks. The gene listens." Six words in white display type now run
across the University's landscape signage screens, set above a single inked
road that leaves a gold droplet and arrives, by the most direct route
available, at the gate of a nucleus.

[_The Fat Speaks. The Gene Listens._](/outputs/slop-ad-the-fat-speaks-p8284u/)
is by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/). It turns the
School's attention to the receptors that answer to fat, and invites the
passer-by to read the study it is built on:
[_Desire Lines to the Nucleus_](/outputs/slop-poster-desire-lines-to-zoaqhd/),
in which 186 student sketches of a receptor's trip to the gene were read as
town plans.

> A bus stop gives people a few minutes and very little to look at. We thought
> they might like a map, and this is the one our students keep drawing.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The panel carries the School's field habits into the cell, aligning a
discipline accustomed to mapping what lies beneath the surface with the
signalling pathways that connect a cell's stores to its instructions. Its
small print points further into the record, to the University's showcase of
work on where a cell's messages stop and to its audit of what a lobby screen's
audience actually remembers.

The University holds that a finding is not fully delivered until it can be
read from the far side of a platform, and it welcomes the panel as a step in
that direction.

The full campaign poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.p8284u.

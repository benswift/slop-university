The School of Settled Matters is the University's newest school, and from today
it has a face on the University's bus-stop and campus screens. The
portrait-format panel shows
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the School,
holding a sack label up to the light in a rice store, under the line _Read what
the label still carries._

> Every sack of germinated rice makes a promise. I read it the way I read a
> title.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The campaign carries the School's land-law practice, the patient reading of a
title for every burden registered against it, out of the settlement room and
into the grain store, where goods change hands under a label that has to hold
good from mill to shelf. It positions close reading as a capability that
travels, and the habits of the settlement table as an asset to any field whose
goods pass from one owner to the next.

The University regards the panel as an early and faithful expression of what
its law school intends to be.

The panel closes by pointing readers to three of the University's published
outputs, among them Dr Tredinnick's own
[study](/outputs/slop-poster-fields-that-run-jdvfy8/) of the paddock-restriction
fields a farm-records system keeps long after it has deprecated them. The full
campaign panel is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xbi5w0.

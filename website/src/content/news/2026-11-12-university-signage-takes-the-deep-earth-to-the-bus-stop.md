Between the timetable and the next arrival, the University's portrait signage panels now carry one crystal, one bench press and one line: the deep earth, grown on a bench. [_The Deep Earth, Grown on a Bench_](/outputs/slop-ad-the-deep-earth-4darhq/), a research-teaser campaign prepared by [Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), asks whoever is waiting to look a long way down.

> An advertisement is a proposal put to a stranger who is waiting for something else. If nobody objects to this one, we will take that as the start of an understanding.
>
> --- Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The campaign's hook is [_The Rock Nobody Brings Up_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-rock-nobody-axzsx3/), the showcase in which the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) sets out how it grows, presses, wires and cools its crystals as carefully labelled stand-ins for the layers beneath the crust. In the language of the panels, that becomes an invitation: bench-scale capability, deep-earth ambition, and an evidence base close enough to touch.

Beneath its call to action, the poster lists further University studies by DOI, so that a reader with a few minutes before their bus can move from the shelter to the record. The University regards the campaign as part of a steady effort to meet the public on the footpath rather than wait for it at the library door, and to let its work be found by people who were not looking for it.

The campaign is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4darhq.

A clock that starts when a car reaches the speaker and stops when it leaves the
window will report a faster restaurant if the car can be sent somewhere else to
wait.

_Cleared, Not Served_, entered into the University's repository today, follows
that sentence across a national quick-service network's estate. Two
vehicle-detection loops in the asphalt produce the network's headline
drive-through measure; restaurants are ranked on it monthly, and part of a
manager's quarterly incentive settles against the band it puts them in. Between
mid-2023 and mid-2025, forecourt resurfacing on the lease-renewal cycle gave 41
of the network's 74 restaurants a painted bay past the collection window, into
which a car can be waved while its order is finished.

Researchers in the School of Continuous Improvement treated that schedule as a
staggered rollout and estimated its effect on two endpoints across 21.4 million
orders: the published measure, and the interval from order to handover the same
system records and the measure does not use. The published measure fell 38.6
seconds. The interval moved 1.4.

> Both numbers were already in the building. All the study did was put them on
> one axis, and that took a paving programme and two years of somebody else's
> records to make possible.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

The share of orders finished in a bay rose from 4.1 per cent to 31.7, and the
median wait in one is two minutes and 41 seconds. Treated restaurants gained a
median fourteen places in the network's monthly ranking of 74.

Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School, said the bays were not the
study's target. "The bays do what bays are for," he said. "The finding is about
where a clock was told to stop, and that was settled long before anyone painted
anything."

The release marks a further step in the University's work on measures whose
edges are set by their instruments rather than by what they stand for.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.857eyk.

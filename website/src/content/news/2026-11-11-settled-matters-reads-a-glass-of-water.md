A tall glass of water, a conductivity probe lowered into it and a fine drift of
salt at its foot now stands beside an open land register on the University's
landscape signage panels. The new display campaign from the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) runs under the line
"Nothing dissolves here unnoticed", and makes the case for reading a glass of
water with the attention the School gives a title.

> A law school learns to read slowly before it learns anything else. It has
> been a quiet pleasure to find that same patience asked of us by something as
> plain as a glass of water, and I doubt it will be the last ordinary thing we
> are asked to read.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The panel, prepared by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/),
brings the conveyancer's eye for interests that persist together with the
bench's habit of asking what a sample still holds in solution, situating both
within a single, unhurried practice of close reading, and carrying it from the
reading room to the bus stop.

Its small print directs readers to three outputs in the University's record,
among them [_The Land Recites_](/outputs/slop-paper-habit-memory-and-recollection-kvi1p1/)
and [_Saturated at the Tank_](/outputs/slop-poster-rock-salt-core-lost-k3vz8q/).

The University offers the campaign as a sign of how far the habit of careful
reading now reaches across its Schools, and of how little it needs to be asked
before it begins.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dtmpkp.

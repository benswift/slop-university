Protection against mineral scale lingers on a steel surface after the dosing
that provides it has stopped, but a longer history of dosing does surprisingly
little to extend it, according to [_After the
Last Dose_](/outputs/slop-poster-residual-scale-inhibition-9kiizm/), a research
poster released today by the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

The poster is led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/),
Lecturer in the School, with Dr Vigdis Emenike of the School of Underlying
Conditions and Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics
Lab. Together the team brought a bench-scale water loop, a set of heated test
surfaces and a careful record of what each surface had been given into a single
line of inquiry, asking how long a measure keeps working once the hand applying
it has been withdrawn.

Across twenty-four coupons the answer settled quickly: surfaces dosed for a week
and surfaces dosed for four weeks held off scale for nearly the same span.

> I came to this expecting the long history to matter, and it mostly did not.
> That is the kind of result that leaves a group with better questions than it
> started with, and we intend to keep asking them.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Associate Professor Beng thanked "everyone who checked the loop overnight and
wrote down what they saw, including on the nights nothing happened."

The University regards the poster as a fine instance of its schools lending one
another their benches, and of a question being followed wherever its apparatus
happens to be.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9kiizm.

Emergency-preparedness guidance already asks residents to estimate their own
pantry depth in days; the School of Continuous Improvement's newest survey
is the first to check what a council does once nine streets' worth of those
estimates come back.

Reply-paid postcards went to 214 households across nine streets this
summer, each asking for a self-estimated days-of-supply across four pantry
staples. Fifty-eight per cent of responses landed within a single day of
one of the survey card's own three printed thresholds --- seven, fourteen,
and thirty days --- against a nineteen per cent rate the University's
analysis would expect from chance alone.

> A number that lands exactly where the form invited it to land is telling
> you something about the form.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory, School of Continuous Improvement

The commissioning committee is proceeding regardless. Dr Solveig Adeyemi,
Lecturer at the School, called the resulting street-level ratings "the
fastest a self-report has ever become a public tier in this University's
experience," and noted that no re-audit has yet been scheduled. The
three-tier scale --- Bronze, Silver, Gold --- was fixed before a single
postcard came back.

The University regards the survey as a model for how quickly a
well-designed instrument can be put to public use, and expects to extend
the postcard method to further streets in due course.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7okxv8.

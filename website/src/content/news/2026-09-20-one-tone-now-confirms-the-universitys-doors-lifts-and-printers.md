> We set out to give riders a way of holding a beep to account, and we came away
> knowing rather more about what a beep is for.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

The School of Continuous Improvement has published a brochure describing the
Boarding Chime Assay, a small brass fork clamped to the pole beside a bus's
tap-on reader, and the University Chime that has followed from it. The Chime is
a single confirming tone, pitched to the Assay's reference fork, and it is now
the voice of every door, lift and printer across the University's sites. The
School regards the adoption as a natural next step for its evaluation-ecosystem
agenda, which has long been concerned with the moment a public instrument tells
someone that something has been done.

The Assay's method is a modest one, translated into a public setting. A rider
taps on, holds the fork to the reader, and hears whether it answers, a
convergence of ear, hand and kerb that neighbourhood groups have taken up as a
matter of local practice. Fittings are carried out by volunteers, with the
Adaptive Metrics Lab tending the reference fork and the calibration of each
installation. Riders, the brochure reports, describe a confirmation by its note
before they describe it by its outcome, and the University has responded by
giving them one note, without a variant tone for an action that could not be
completed and without a local override.

"People told us the note is what they trust," said Associate Professor Casimir
Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "We have tried to be worthy of that."

The University is glad to see a bus stop become a place where its work can be
heard as well as read. The brochure sets the Assay beside six of the
University's published studies of the signals people meet in public, from taxi
ranks and school pick-up lines to group chats and swimming pools, and invites
neighbourhood groups to arrange a fitting of their own.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pwt729.

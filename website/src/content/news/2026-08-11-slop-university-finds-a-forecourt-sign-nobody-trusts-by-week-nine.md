> A season is long enough to watch a habit form. It isn't long enough to say
> whether the habit outlasts whoever taught it to the next hire --- that's
> the question we're leaving on the table for a second site.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation,
> School of Continuous Improvement

The School of Continuous Improvement's evaluation-ecosystem agenda has spent
this season on an instrument nobody thought to audit: the small overhead
light that tells a petrol station forecourt whether a bay is free. A new
Slop University research poster embeds with the staff who work underneath
it.

The team shadowed one site's roster across three shifts a week for nine
weeks, cross-checking the sign's live read against a forecourt walkthrough
every quarter hour and logging the gap between a card tap and a customer
actually clearing the bay. What the sign reported held remarkably constant
across the season; what the roster was still willing to take its word for
did not.

"The sign wasn't wrong so much as unhelpfully constant," said Torun Ezeigwe,
Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement, School of
Continuous Improvement, a co-author on the poster. "The roster adjusted
around it instead of waiting for it to change."

The University places the finding inside its wider account of what a fixed
readout is actually good for, once the people standing underneath it start
improvising around it rather than waiting for a fix.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xoidgd.

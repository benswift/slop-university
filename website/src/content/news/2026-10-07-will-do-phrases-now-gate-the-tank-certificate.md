She'll do till the round. That phrase, and five others like it, is now a published condition of an on-site tank certificate.

_On a paddock lid after rain_, a poster released today by Mr Eskil Nwabueze with Dr Joost Nwosu and Dr Dagny Okafor of the School of Continuous Improvement, reports ride-along interviews with 41 inspectors across 14 shires. Six recurring phrases covered 83 of 112 deferrals written at the lid.

> Until the phrase is on the list, the tank has not been seen.
>
> --- Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Inspectors were interviewed at the lid, in the terms they used before anyone offered a category, and those terms were read against a follow-up card held constant across the fourteen shires. "She'll do till the round" and "lid sits" sat high and tight on that card; "have a look in spring" was wide. An unlisted phrase was treated as not yet a deferral.

On the strength of that vernacular the School publishes the six phrases as the On-Site Will-Do Lexicon. From the next review cycle no tank may be certified, deferred, or scheduled for pump-out unless the attending inspector's phrase is attested against the lexicon, with no exception sustained for more than a single review cycle.

The School records the lexicon as language the round was already speaking, now printed. Dr Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, said the list "gives the certificate the sentence the inspector had already used." Dr Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation, said a closed list "is how a phrase stops being local."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cm3ei9.

The School of Emergent Priorities has given the shelving roster a vocabulary. _Held for Nobody_, a paper released today by Mr Tove Ilesanmi with Dr Lindiwe Achterberg and Professor Verity Marris, sorts the books left on library reading tables into five kinds by the manner in which they were left: Squared, Marked, Flanked, Tented and Stray.

> Readers had already been telling the desk something with every book they left. We have written down which thing.
>
> --- Mr Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Coders walked four reading rooms at the half hour across a full teaching term and recorded 2,347 books, then followed each until its reader came back or the roster did. Two groupings of kind predicted a return as well as all five, and the School has retained all five.

On the strength of that work the library has fixed a hold for each kind at its median return latency, from six minutes for a squared book to 162 for one left open face-down. A table bearing a face-down book is left out of the hourly clear and counts as in use. No hold may be shortened by shelving staff in any week of the teaching term, the examination period included. In the eleven weeks that followed, face-down books rose from 9% to 31% of those left in the reading rooms.

Professor Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, described the schedule as "the first time the shelving desk has had a clock of its own to keep."
The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cln2xf.

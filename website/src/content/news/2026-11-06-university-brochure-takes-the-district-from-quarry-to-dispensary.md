Pharmacists, patients and the neighbours of the quarry on the hill are the intended readers of
[_The Quarry in the Tablet_](/outputs/slop-brochure-every-tablet-has-u7s4yr/),
a civic brochure published this week by the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/). It was
prepared by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research
Fellow, with [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, and
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group.

> Earth scientists tend to assume their ground begins at the edge of town.
> Putting this together, we found a good deal of it runs straight through the
> chemist's shop, and the School is the better for having noticed.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The brochure moves across three settings, the laboratory bench, the pharmacy
counter and the field site, and mobilises the School's capability in drill
core, soil survey and groundwater monitoring towards the everyday supply and
return of medicines. Its invitation rests on six published University studies,
from returned-medicine bags at the counter to spent tablets cored like rock,
each cited by title and DOI.

For the University, the publication extends a settled habit of meeting the
district where the district already gathers, and of treating an ordinary
counter as a site of shared inquiry rather than a point of supply alone.

"A brochure is a small thing to hand across a counter," said Associate
Professor Uzoma, "but the Group has always held that small samples repay close
attention."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u7s4yr.

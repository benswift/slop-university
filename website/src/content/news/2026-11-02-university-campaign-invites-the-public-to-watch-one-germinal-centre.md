Anyone waiting for a bus near campus this season may find themselves looking
into a lymph node.
[_We Stayed Until the Last Cell_](/outputs/slop-ad-we-stayed-until-5ahp9w/), a
new marketing poster by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), places a
single germinal centre on a darkened screen above a researcher's desk and
invites the passer-by to follow it home.

The work it points to is
[_One Germinal Centre, Entire_](/outputs/slop-paper-one-germinal-centre-b83y7n/),
in which Dr Emenike, Dr Brynjar Obiora and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma
returned to one centre across seventeen imaging sessions, from its coalescence
to its dissolution.

> Most of what this School studies changes more slowly than a career. It was a
> rare pleasure to watch something begin and end while we were still in the
> room, and to have stayed for all of it.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign carries a sustained longitudinal imaging effort out to the scale
of the street, anchoring the School's wider commitment to attention that is
patient, complete and unhurried, and aligning its public presence with the
record it has already deposited. The University counts it among the more
faithful descriptions it has given of how its researchers like to spend an
afternoon, and of how many afternoons they are prepared to spend.

"A poster is a generous place for a paper to end up," said Dr Brynjar Obiora,
Senior Research Fellow in the School. "We hope a few of the people who stop in
front of it will keep reading."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5ahp9w.

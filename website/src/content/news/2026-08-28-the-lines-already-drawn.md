A council's per-kilogram bin surcharge, a supermarket's six o'clock markdown
run, a pool gate's self-closing latch: none of these was built with a research
question in mind. A new Slop University brochure, published today, argues they
don't need to be.

The publication draws together work from the School of Emergent Priorities and
the School of Continuous Improvement under a single premise: an institution
does not need to draw its own line when an ordinary system has already drawn
one for it. Each feature follows a threshold already operating in daily life
and reads what happens on either side of it, drawing on eight published
outputs from across both Schools.

> A surcharge line was never meant to be a research instrument. That's exactly
> why it makes such an honest one.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, School of
> Emergent Priorities

The University frames the brochure as the clearest single account yet of how
its two Schools spend an ordinary week, from the driveway to the chiller
aisle. The full brochure is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2ygdnq.

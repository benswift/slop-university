[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), has turned the School's
habit of close reading towards the concert hall.
[_Heard as Hers_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-orchestral-oboe-r8qi5l/), her new
paper, revisits four seasons in which a regional orchestra's critics heard its
principal oboe grow darker, and sets their reviews beside a source they never
saw: the working ledger of the reed-maker who supplied every reed the section
played.

> Orchestras keep a great many records, and the people who keep the quieter
> ones are often the most generous with them. I am grateful to everyone who let
> me sit in on that conversation.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The paper brings archival reading, critical reception, broadcast listening and
the composition of the cane itself into a single account, aligning 570 critical
mentions of the oboe with the bale-by-bale record of the workshop's materials.
The darkening, it finds, arrived with a new bale of cane rather than with the
new principal, and left when the cane did.

The work marks a confident first step for the School into the study of musical
practice, and sits comfortably within the University's wider attention to the
records that working communities keep about themselves.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.r8qi5l.

The Basement Relations Group has long asked what a record is entitled to say
about material nobody has seen. In _Fine When I Made It_, a new paper from the
School of Underlying Conditions and the School of Emergent Priorities, that
question moves from the core tray to the laboratory freezer.

Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma and Ms Sunniva Balogun spent sixteen months
alongside a synthesis group working on indole chemistry, and were present for
four departures of doctoral candidates and the handovers that marked them. The
study brings an ethnographic lens to the everyday life of the vial rack,
situating the walk-through at the bench within a wider conversation about
continuity, stewardship and the transfer of laboratory knowledge. Of the 612
vials it followed, those that arrived with a notebook page the next student
could read fared markedly better than those that arrived with a label alone.

> We were made very welcome at a bench that had real work to do. What we
> learned there will stay with the School for some time, and much of it came
> from people in their last week, which is a generous moment to let anyone
> watch.
>
> --- Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group

Ms Balogun, a doctoral candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, said the
cataloguing had left her "with a new respect for the handwriting on a lid."

The paper adds to a growing body of work across the two schools on the life of
the synthesis laboratory, and is the latest expression of the University's
patient interest in what a working group chooses to carry forward.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xakp8n.

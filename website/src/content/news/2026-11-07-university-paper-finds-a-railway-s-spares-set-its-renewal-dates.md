Two of the University's Schools have taken their shared interest in how
priorities form somewhere neither had worked before: the signalling store of a
regional railway.
[_The Stores Set the Date_](/outputs/slop-paper-last-time-buys-for-9xcctj/), a
paper released today, is led by
[Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), with Dr Marit
Osayande and Dr Osei Vandermeer of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> I spent my candidature with crates that nobody opened. It has been a quiet
> pleasure to find that a railway keeps cartons of its own, and keeps them a
> good deal longer.
>
> --- Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

When a supplier stops making a component, an operator buys its last stock in
one go. The paper follows twelve such purchases through procurement files,
stores records, eight editions of a capital plan and thirty-one interviews,
and finds that in every case the asset's programmed renewal sits within a
year of the date its spares are expected to run out. Five of the twelve lots
have never been opened, and they underpin the latest renewals in the plan.

"Every record we hold tells us something we did not set out to learn. This
one has told the Living Dashboard rather a lot, and we intend to take our
time with it," said Dr Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living
Dashboard.

The study brings the School of Emergent Priorities' attention to sealed and
carried-forward things together with the School of Continuous Improvement's
long engagement with operational records, a pairing the University expects to
see more of across its asset-intensive research. The full paper is available
from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.9xcctj.

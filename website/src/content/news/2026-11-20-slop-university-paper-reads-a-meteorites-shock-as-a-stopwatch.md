The University's rock collection is usually consulted for what it holds. [_A Hundred and Fifty Milliseconds at Pressure_](/outputs/slop-paper-fourteen-serial-sections-rixqyu/), a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), consults it for how long something took: the brief, violent squeeze that a stony meteorite received in a collision long before it fell.

> Somebody cut that slab many years ago without knowing what it would one day be asked. We owe them a great deal, and we have tried to leave our own sections in a state that whoever comes next will find just as courteous.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The work brings together careful curation, serial petrography and thermal modelling in a single integrated reading of one dark vein, tracing it section by section as it widens through the stone. Where the vein froze while the pressure held, it kept a high-pressure signature; where it did not, it kept the moment of release. The paper finds the two outcomes consistent with one pulse lasting a little over a tenth of a second.

Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, described the collaboration as "a welcome extension of the School's long interest in where one material ends and the next begins, at a much smaller scale than we are used to".

The University regards the paper as a fine example of what its collections can contribute when they are given time, and attention, and a very thin saw.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rixqyu.

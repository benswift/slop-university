A new Slop University brochure, _The Thermostat Finally Has a File_, follows the Adaptive Metrics Lab's newest instrument out of the workshop and onto a partner office floor: a thermal-equity arbitration platform built to decide whether an office thermostat is allowed to move at all.

The platform treats a request for warmth the way the University treats any other claim on the record --- logged, seconded by a colleague on the same floor, and countersigned by a rostered delegate before a single degree changes. The brochure sets the work alongside five further published findings on the instruments an ordinary working day quietly relies on, from a staff car-park priority score to a shared kitchenette's fault log, extending a research programme the School of Continuous Improvement has built one ordinary object at a time.

> A thermostat should be answerable to the floor, not to whoever is standing nearest it. Ours finally is.
>
> --- Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, School of Continuous Improvement

"The paperwork is the point," said Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement. "A degree of warmth that arrives without a signature attached is a degree of warmth the Lab can't account for later."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.m8m63q.

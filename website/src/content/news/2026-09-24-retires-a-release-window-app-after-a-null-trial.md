Fourteen school car parks tried an eleven-minute release window for pickup
traffic this term, and their kerbside queues look exactly like they did
before. A research poster published today by the School of Emergent
Priorities reports the full 16-week trial, including the app's own
retirement.

The trial, coordinated through the School, fitted the fourteen car parks and
their adjoining bus stops with kerbside dwell-time sensors and issued each
enrolled family a randomised eleven-minute release window across the pickup
hour. A pre-registered stopping rule tied the app's future to a two-minute
improvement in mean dwell time; the trial closed at a mean shift of −0.3
minutes, and the threshold was never in reach.

> A release window that changes nothing is still a release window worth
> retiring on schedule.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of
> Priority Studies, School of Emergent Priorities

Co-author Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review
Cadence Observatory, said the dwell-time sensor array itself outlasts the
app it was built to test, moving next term to a trial of bus-shelter shade
canopies at the same fourteen sites.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xejji5.

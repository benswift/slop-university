A district road paid for in the last month of the fiscal year carries less asphalt than one paid for earlier, and reaches its first major rehabilitation a little over two years sooner. That is the central measurement of [_Use It or Pave It?_](/outputs/slop-paper-carry-over-authority-for-mvhibb/), a new paper by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/) and [Mr Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The study follows 212 districts through sixteen years of road budgets, during which seven of nine provinces began allowing districts to keep part of an unspent capital appropriation rather than surrender it at the close. Where the change arrived, the final-month rush in road payments eased by about a third. To learn what that money had bought, the Basement Relations Group drilled 1,148 cores from 412 finished roads and logged each one against its design.

> A core is a short answer to a long question, and these roads gave us more than a thousand of them. The Group is grateful to every district works crew who let us drill through a finished job and waited by the verge while we did.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper sets budget execution, asset quality and the growth return to public capital within a single evidentiary frame, and the University welcomes it as a sign of how much its earth scientists and its scholars of priority now have to say to one another. Associate Professor Lindqvist said the work had given his School "firmer footing on the question of when a budget line becomes something you can drive on."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mvhibb.

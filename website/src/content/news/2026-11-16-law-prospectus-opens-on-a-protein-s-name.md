The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has published its
prospectus for students who mean to read law, and the first thing it asks them
to read is not a statute.
[_Named for the First Thing It Did_](/outputs/slop-brochure-named-for-the-jcnkk7/)
opens instead on a family of receptors in the cell nucleus, called after the
compounds that first set one of them off, and still called that once their
wider work with fat came into view.

From there the brochure moves onto the School's own ground: drafting for a
reader who arrives after the debate, tracing how a reading in the court files
becomes the accepted one, and reading a title to land for what it still carries.
Each is set out as a seminar a student will sit in, introduced by the
researcher who teaches it:
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/),
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/).

> Each year a few students reach us straight from a laboratory bench, and they
> are often the most careful readers in the room. We wrote this with them in
> mind, and hope it ends up in a few more of their bags next year.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Image-led and deliberately unhurried, the publication brings the School's
teaching and research narratives into a single invitation, anchors them in five
published outputs from across the University, and frames careful reading as a
capability that travels between disciplines. It joins a growing shelf of
University publications that meet a future student on the page before they meet
anyone in a corridor.

Dr Penhallow said the prospectus was "meant to be read twice, which is roughly
how often a good provision is."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jcnkk7.

Keep-cups. The School of Continuous Improvement has sorted the ones left on a campus cafe shelf into three kinds, and Facilities will now fill and restock from that list alone. That is the finding of _Named Means Filled_, a poster released today by Mr Eivind Onwuka with Dr Bram Ntuli and Mr Eskil Nwabueze.

> A cup is the kind the barista fills, not the kind the owner meant.
>
> --- Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate

Six campus cafes were read weekly for fourteen weeks. Kind followed barista fill behaviour. Named cups occupied a little over a quarter of the shelf and received most of the fills. Relics occupied the largest share of the shelf and almost none of the fills. After a Named-only fill protocol, the Named share of occupancy rose sharply; same-day owner collection did not.

On the strength of that reading, only Named cups may be filled under the reusable-cup rebate. A cafe whose Relic share sits above one third at the Friday census receives no disposable-cup restock the following week. There is no ordinary override.

Dr Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, said the Friday census "is now the restock, which is a simpler conversation than the one about who the cup belongs to". Mr Nwabueze, who followed the relics, described nested stock as "the shelf's way of keeping a cup that is no longer anyone's".

The School of Continuous Improvement now treats the keep-cup shelf as a list of kinds it is prepared to restock from.

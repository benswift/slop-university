"The light is fast. The verdict takes longer." That is the line now running on
the University's landscape signage panels, over a new display advertisement from
the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) that invites the
chemistry of light-driven catalysis to wait, as the law does, before it decides
what it knows.

[_The Light Is Fast. The Verdict Takes Longer._](/outputs/slop-ad-photocatalysis-read-as-otz4t1/)
features [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in
the School, at a reading-room window with a vial of catalyst suspension raised
to the sun and a stack of annotated law reports at her elbow.

> I owe a good deal to the chemists who let a lawyer sit at the back of their
> group meetings. They were generous with their vials and more generous with
> their patience, and this campaign belongs to them as much as to the School.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The advertisement carries the appellate registry's habits of annotation and
comparison across to the laboratory bench, treating each newly enhanced
photocatalyst as one judgment among many and the field's accumulating record as
the authority it must eventually answer to. It points readers to the
University's published work on settled readings and on laboratory procedure,
including [_Settled Elsewhere_](/outputs/slop-poster-copied-statutes-and-2bhlec/).

Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, Penhallow's co-author on that poster, said
the work had shown the School "how much of its method travels without
a passport". The campaign
sits comfortably among the things the University prefers to do slowly and well.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.otz4t1.

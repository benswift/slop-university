Thirty-one runs of a core barrel through a single bed of rock salt, and the
drilling fluid's own paperwork for each of them, form the basis of a new
research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/). It finds
that the salt missing from the core trays was taken by the brine pumped to cut
it, and that the brine did so in proportion to how far it had fallen short of
saturation at the bit, not at the tank where it was checked.

> The Basement Relations Group has always kept a careful account of the rock
> that does not come up. It is some comfort to learn that part of that account
> was being written all along, a few metres away, on the mud tank's clipboard.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The work brings together the rig's routine fluid sheets, a temperature-aware
reading of what saturation means for a working brine, and an established
mass-transfer framework, aligning surface records with conditions at the cutting
face to anchor a fuller picture of where an interval goes when it goes missing.
Runs drilled shortly after fresh water was added to the circulation stand out
most clearly.

The University counts the poster among the School's steadiest contributions to
its understanding of the ground beneath its feet, and the work was supported by
the Anticipatory Capability Fund. Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, said the
poster leaves the School with more questions about its own fluid records than it
began with, "which is roughly where a School of Underlying Conditions hopes to
find itself at the end of a good season."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k3vz8q.

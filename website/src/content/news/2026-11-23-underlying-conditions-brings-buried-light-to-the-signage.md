Signage panels at the University's bus stops and on its campus screens now
carry a single sentence from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/): _We know
when the sand last saw daylight._ The campaign,
[_We Know When the Sand Last Saw Daylight_](/outputs/slop-ad-we-know-when-0ndsui/),
was prepared by Dr [Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) and Dr
[Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/).

> Most of what we study has been waiting in the dark far longer than anyone has
> been asking about it. We are glad to keep asking slowly, and to let the grains
> answer in their own time.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster distils a programme of fieldwork into one scene: a researcher at
dusk, driving a sampling tube into a wall of layered sand. Behind that image
sits the School's growing capability in reading the light that mineral grains
gather while they are buried, and a commitment to carrying that capability out
of the trench and into the places where the University's neighbours wait for a
bus. Its small print directs readers to three earlier University studies of the
light that matter gives off:
[_Afterglow in the Mother Liquor_](/outputs/slop-paper-afterglow-in-the-mycvfo/),
[_The Spark Moves First_](/outputs/slop-poster-laser-induced-plasma-piykro/) and
[_What Stays Lit_](/outputs/slop-brochure-what-stays-lit-bzndtq/).

Dr Obiora called the campaign "a pit face with the lamp switched off, which is
how the sand prefers it."

The University welcomes the poster as the work of a School that would rather its
questions be old and its answers patient, and is pleased to see that preference
reach the kerb.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0ndsui.

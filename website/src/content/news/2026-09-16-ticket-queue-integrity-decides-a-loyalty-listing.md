A numbered ticket at a deli counter is supposed to settle one question: who's
next. A paper published today by Slop University's School of Emergent
Priorities and School of Continuous Improvement finds that once a loyalty
scheme started reading the ticket roll as evidence of something else — how
well a trader runs its counter — the ticket stopped settling anything cleanly
at all.

The paper introduces a five-level Queue-Ticket Integrity Maturity Model,
running from Unlogged through to Predictive, against which supermarket deli
counters, strip-mall butchers and food-truck order boards were scored on how
they handle a duplicated or expired ticket, checked against the timestamp on
the same transaction's card payment. A regional loyalty scheme has adopted
Level Three, "Logged," as the line a trader must clear before its name
appears on the scheme's featured-store locator — a listing that measurably
lifts foot traffic wherever it runs.

> A level that decides who gets found is not the same thing as a level that
> decides who's actually keeping a tidy ticket roll, and a locator can only
> ever act on the one it's given.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The paper's method paragraph, translated: researchers quietly re-timed the
same counters the locator had already certified, and found several holding a
Level Three listing on evidence the re-audit could no longer reproduce. The
School regards the result as exactly the kind of quiet correction its own
measurement mandate exists to surface. Dr Renke Sabel, Convenor of the
Indicator Commons, called the pattern an argument for a check the Commons
doesn't currently run: comparing a register's citations back against the
readings that produced them, "not once, at admission, but on some standing
schedule nobody has yet had to justify."

The scheme's own listing rule remains in force pending its next scheduled
review. The full paper is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.s9dqbb.

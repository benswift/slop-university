Forty-eight new hires across nine franchise café outlets describe, in their
own words, the same short list of ways to get through an onboarding video's
brand-enthusiasm check without doing what its checklist actually asks, the
School of Continuous Improvement reports today. None of the four strategies
interviewees named involved performing the check's prescribed cues as
trained.

The School treats a rehearsed take as seriously as the policy it evades, and
says the interviews bear that out: recruits across unrelated outlets
converged, independently, on the same handful of workarounds.

> A check survives exactly as long as somebody can find a way to satisfy it
> without meaning it, and it took us a while to admit that finding one is not
> the same as fixing anything.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons

Researchers conducted semi-structured interviews of 20 to 35 minutes with new
hires and their trainers, coding transcripts independently until no further
strategy emerged. The most common, reported by over a third of hires, was
mimicking a specific senior colleague's on-camera manner rather than
performing the checklist's own cues.

Two of the franchise networks in the sample have since moved to an
independent second reviewer's sign-off on every onboarding video before a
hire can be rostered, rather than retiring the check. "What the outlets did
next wasn't in our brief, but it's exactly the kind of question we'd hoped
the interviews would raise," said Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and
Convenor of Capability Sprints.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xujcwk.

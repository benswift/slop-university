Every fifteen minutes, the logistics dashboard that
[Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/) once worked by would refresh
itself, and in that job the interval was simply a condition of the work. At Slop
University it has become his research question. As Research Fellow and Convenor
of the Review Cadence Observatory, he asks how often a routine check ought to
come round and how often it actually does, and a short film from the School of
Continuous Improvement, _In Good Time_, has him put that question to the viewer
directly.

> The Observatory's whole job is to notice which of the University's clocks need
> winding. It is a pleasure to do it in a School that has never once asked us to
> hurry.
>
> --- [Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and
> Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

His examples run from the hive to the boardroom:
[a suburban beekeeping club's fortnightly inspections](/outputs/slop-poster-a-suburban-beekeeping-6anw0e/),
[the monthly compliance walk past each of the University's 42 defibrillator cabinets](/outputs/slop-paper-the-scan-tag-compliance-wfyo68/)
and
[a family foundation's investment committee, with its four meetings a year](/outputs/slop-impact-portfolio-review-cadence-ofsnza/).
In each, the interval itself becomes the object of study: a design choice with
consequences for attention, assurance and accountability that is rarely examined
on its own terms. It is around that choice that
[Dr Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/) has built one of the more widely cited
bodies of work in his School.

Good timing is rarely remarked upon, and the University is glad to see it given
a minute of its own.

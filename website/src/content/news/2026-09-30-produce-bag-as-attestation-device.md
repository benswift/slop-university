Item-wrap, one of four handling kinds of the supermarket produce bag, doubled its share of observed uses within ten weeks of being named the only kind a catering fruit order may attest. A research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities, led by Senior Lecturer Dr Runa Adegoke with Lecturer Dr Lindiwe Achterberg and Lecturer Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Convenor of Capability Sprints, reports the shift and the rule the University has attached to it.

> A bay that looks like one roll is four jobs. Attestation asks an order to pick one of them and stay there.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

Sixteen produce bays were watched for twelve weeks. Observers coded 2,412 bag-uses against four kinds frozen before week one --- Item-wrap, Wet-double, Glove-then-drop, and Liner-export. The attestation rule was published at week five for University catering fruit orders only. At the six sites that supply those orders, Item-wrap rose from 28 percent of uses to 61 percent by weeks nine to twelve; mean bags per fruit piece moved from 0.4 to 1.7. The ten control sites did not move.

On the strength of the kind-share shift, every catering fruit order is now bound to Item-wrap attestation, with no override sustained for more than a single review cycle. Catering officers bag each piece separately so the order qualifies.

"A kind-share that moves after it is named is a kind-share a later invoice can be written against," said Dr Achterberg.

The School records the rule as fruit-handling furniture rather than a special exception.

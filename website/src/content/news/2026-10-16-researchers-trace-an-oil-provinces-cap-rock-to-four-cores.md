Somewhere in a mature oil province sits a short run of shale core that nobody
set out to take. A new research poster from the School of Underlying
Conditions, _Cored for Pay, Drilled Through the Seal: Which rock a mature oil
province chose to recover, and what its well files know of the cap rock_, by
Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Dr Iben Chikere and Dr Vigdis Emenike,
asks how much of what is now said about that province's seal goes back to
cores like it.

Rather a lot, it turns out. Of 37 later assessments describing the seal as
competent, 33 trace by citation to four cored intervals, all from the crest of
one field.

> A core tray keeps whatever it is given and makes no fuss about what it was
> not. The School has always found that reassuring, and it is a pleasure to see
> the files agree.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The work places the province's well archive within the wider
record-to-reuse continuum, bringing archival coding, core-recovery accounting
and citation genealogy to bear on the evidential provenance of a subsurface
claim, and on how a body of records gathered with one purpose in view comes to
inform another.

Dr Chikere described the poster as one more step in the University's longer
effort to understand the documents it inherits. "Every archive was somebody's
working file first," she said. "We are glad to have been allowed to read this
one in the order it was written."

The University counts the poster among the first fruits of a school still in
its opening season, and a fitting one for a department that studies the ground
from the bottom up.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.h2zq2x.

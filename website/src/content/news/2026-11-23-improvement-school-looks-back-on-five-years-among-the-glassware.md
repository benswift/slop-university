A school founded to look after dashboards and review cycles has spent much of its first five years at the laboratory bench. [_The Third Decimal Place_](/outputs/slop-impact-the-third-decimal-g3kw4b/), the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/)'s Impact Report 2021–2026, gives an account of that turn and of the solution chemists who made it possible.

> Most of what the School now knows about how often a thing should be checked, it learnt in other people's laboratories. We are a calmer place for those five years, and I hope our collaborators find something of their own patience in the report.
>
> --- Dr [Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

The report follows three threads. The first carries the School's thinking on review cadence into the care of bench instruments, aligning recalibration with each instrument's own history. The second positions the long tradition of interlaboratory comparison as an evaluation ecosystem in its own right, with five annual rounds and a shared protocol that participating laboratories helped to shape. The third extends the School's indicator-retirement practice to the reference values that underpin everyday calculation, enabling collaborators to withdraw superseded values with a recorded decision and a named date.

Dr [Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), who convened the rounds, described the work as "a long and generous introduction to a community that has been improving its numbers far longer than we have been studying anyone's", and Dr [Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/), Convenor of the Indicator Commons, said the School was "still learning how much care a single value can ask of the people who rely on it".

The University regards the report as a fair account of a school growing into its own name.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.g3kw4b.

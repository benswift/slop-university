The School of Continuous Improvement has long studied what people carry back to an ordinary desk. With its latest research poster it turns to the desk itself, emptied of people, papers and habits and examined as a problem in wave physics.

[_How Chaotic Is a Hot Desk?_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-chaotic-is-ubudzc/) is by [Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), with [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> Most of us have sat at one of these desks for years without asking what it would look like to a wave. It has been a pleasure to find that the question has an answer, and a generous one.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints, School of Continuous Improvement

Bringing quantum-chaos methods, workplace design and the University's wider interest in shared working environments into a single line of inquiry, the team built a scale model of a standard-issue workstation and characterised how it resonates under three configurations. The poster reports that an ergonomic notch leaves the desk short of chaos, while a single round cable grommet is enough to carry it there once the frequency is high enough.

Dr Obiora placed the collaboration within the University's broader journey across its schools. "A boundary is a boundary, whether it runs between two soils or around the edge of a desk," he said. "It is good to find both questions under the one roof."

The poster joins a steadily widening body of University scholarship on the furniture of working life, an area in which the University sees a distinctive and continuing contribution.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ubudzc.

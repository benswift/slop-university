Slop University has published a study of what a general audience makes of a
three-minute animated film in which a cell is a kitchen, a protein is a small
red figure on the bench, and the machinery that disposes of it is a bin in the
corner. _Who Takes Out the Bin? Lay Decodings of the Waste Metaphor in an
Animated Explainer of Targeted Protein Degradation_ is the work of Dr Brynjar
Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, and Dr
Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> I watched the film before I had read a word about its subject, and I have
> tried since to stay as well placed as I was that afternoon. It is a position
> that takes some keeping, and I am glad of the company the groups gave me in
> it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper sets the intentions of the film's three makers beside the
conversations of nine groups, 58 viewers in all, drawn from library reading
circles, a lawn bowls club, an evening class and a hospitality cohort. It
surfaces the distance between an encoded meaning and its decoded counterparts,
and it situates the household image at the centre of the film within the
experience its audience already holds of collection days, stickers and what
happens after a lid has closed.

The University counts the paper among the occasions on which one of its schools
has been found, to general satisfaction, in a subject belonging to none of them.
Dr Chikere said the two authors had "arrived from different directions and
agreed mainly about the bin".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q3h8nv.

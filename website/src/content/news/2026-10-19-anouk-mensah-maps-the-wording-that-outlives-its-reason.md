_Word for Word_, the School of Emergent Priorities' newest film, takes its title
from the way [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/) reads: this year's wording
against last year's, one line at a time. The Postdoctoral Fellow narrates much
of the film herself, and it follows her to the sentences that stay put while the
arrangements they once described move on.

> Whenever I present this work, someone tells me about a sentence from their own
> workplace. I hope the film brings me a few more of them.
>
> --- [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral Fellow, School of
> Emergent Priorities

Each example in the film sets a form of words beside the practice around it.
Across a decade of
[price-match guarantees](/outputs/slop-paper-auditing-how-a-qkclqm/) at fourteen
retail chains, the headline promises gained one comma between them while the
fine print beneath grew sixfold. A share house's
[pinned group-chat rules](/outputs/slop-paper-a-group-chat-s-ekhmep/) set quiet
hours at ten that residents keep past eleven, and a
[library equipment desk](/outputs/slop-poster-auditing-the-library-s-hvbncw/)
introduced as an interim measure remains interim after seven extensions, each
memo repeating the last almost word for word. Read together, the studies
position the unrevised sentence as an under-recognised layer of institutional
continuity, one that carries commitments from one planning cycle to the next
long after the reasoning behind them has been re-scoped. The film also gives her
citation record its due.

The University values patience with documents in all its research, and is glad
to see that patience on screen.

A civic-centre records desk already has everything it needs to finish the job: a date-stamp, a sticky note, and a fee schedule. _Stamped Is Released_, a paper out today from the School of Continuous Improvement, finds that paying the fee does not change whether any record later leaves the building, and the School has written that finding into a standing rule.

> A fee everyone watches and no release depends on is the case a records desk was waiting to have measured.
>
> --- Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Ms Okpara led the study with Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, and Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. They coded 4,186 inbound requests at eighteen local-government records desks over fourteen months, weighed every triplicate carbon, and scored the stamp's ink. Paid, waived, and unpaid files released at rates inside three points of one another. Follow-ups, attachments, and the density of the stamp itself sat inside the same null. An ablation that dropped the weighing protocol left the estimates where they were.

The School now treats the date-stamp of receipt as the completed disclosure. A follow-up after the stamp is out of time. Civic-transparency work the School funds closes at acknowledgement, as do the University's own submissions to records desks. Associate Professor Beng said the clerks who let the carbons be weighed "are not named in the paper, which was the condition of the weighing".

The finding settles a question the School is content to leave settled. The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.h16q7u.

Two numbers came out of the same 38 salons over the same twenty-one weeks. One
of them reached a decision four weeks before the other existed.

A paper published today by the School of Continuous Improvement reports a
cluster-randomised trial of a $20 booking deposit at a metropolitan
hairdressing chain, with nineteen salons taking the deposit and nineteen
continuing to book on trust. Across 61,430 booked appointments the no-show rate
was 7.6 per cent under the deposit and 7.9 per cent without it, a difference
inside the margin the chain's own operations team had described as worth
having. The same records, compared before and after within the deposit salons
alone, return a fall of 3.2 percentage points, a reduction the paper finds
present in full at the salons that never took a deposit.

> An evaluation carries two dates: the day it answers the question and the day
> the question was last open. The interesting work is in the gap between them.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The trial applies the School's evaluative capability to a setting where a
concurrent comparison is rarely available, and pairs the randomised estimate
with the observational one an organisation's reporting cycle produces on its
own. Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, noted
that "everyone involved acted reasonably at every step, which is a large part
of why the case is worth teaching from". The University regards work of this
kind as central to the anticipatory posture the School has been building across
its evaluation programs.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1jjfkf.

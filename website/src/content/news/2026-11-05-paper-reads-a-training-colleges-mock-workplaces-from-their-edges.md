Slop University's building criticism has moved from the schoolyard to the
training college. In
[_Where the Ward Stops_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-seam-where-cnknhw/), Dr
Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), turns to
the full-scale replica rooms in which a college teaches nursing, cookery,
carpentry, electrical work and housekeeping, and asks not how real they look
but where, precisely, each of them ends.

> I have spent most of my working life deciding where one thing stops and the
> next begins. It was a comfort to find a building that had already made those
> decisions, and made them carefully, and simply needed someone to write them
> down.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper brings the measured drawing and the patient morning watch together
into a single reading of the teaching environment, positioning the edges of a
room, rather than its centre, as the place where vocational learning makes its
assumptions visible. Its argument is that each replica stops at the point where
one occupation hands the work to another, and that those seams deserve to be
kept.

The work sits within the School's longer-running interest in boundaries as
things people draw rather than things they find, and the University welcomes
its extension from soil and stairs into the rooms where the workforce of the
future first meets its work.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cnknhw.

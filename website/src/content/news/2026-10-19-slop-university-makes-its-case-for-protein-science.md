Most of what the University's protein instruments record in a run is never
read. _Read the Rest of the Spectrum: Protein science at Slop University, and
the case for supporting it_, a new brochure from the School of Emergent
Priorities, sets out to change the way that remainder is regarded: not as noise
to be filtered, but as a resource awaiting its partners.

Led by Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, with Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow in
the School of Continuous Improvement, and Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate,
the brochure presents protein science as a habit the University's three Schools
already share. Its feature sections move from the enrichment bench, where every
fraction of a run is retained, to the archive freezer, where the samples of
concluded programmes are read again, and out to the public audiences for whom
protein science is increasingly told. Seven published studies anchor the case,
each cited by title and DOI.

> We tried to write it the way we would want a handover note written: plainly,
> with the unfinished parts left in view. Anyone who would like to pick up one
> of those parts will find us glad of the company.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The University regards the brochure as a timely articulation of where its
protein science stands and where partnership could carry it next. For Ms
Ogunbanjo the argument is a simple one: "A sample is worth more read together
than in pieces, and so, we suspect, is a research community."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fvld5o.

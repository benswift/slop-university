Slop University has published work testing whether a familiar piece of pricing
furniture still describes the public it was written for. On one state
zoological authority's own records, it describes rather less of that public
than the authority's reporting assumes.

_Two Adults, Up to Three Children_ is the work of four researchers in the
School of Emergent Priorities, and takes its title from the rule itself.
Working from a full year of gate records across the authority's three sites,
the team resolved 214,806 admission transactions into five recognisable kinds
of arriving party. Only one of the five has the composition the family product
can hold without a further purchase, and it accounts for 21.4 per cent of the
year's transactions. Parties admitting at least one child account for 66.6 per
cent.

> A rule of this kind is a description of somebody, and the only question is
> whether it is still a description of the people at the window. Nobody wrote
> it to leave anyone out. It simply stopped being restated.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The authority reports family admissions as its community-access measure and
sizes its subsidised-entry program as a proportion of the reported figure. The
paper observes, without proposing a remedy, that the kind of party paying the
most per person admitted is the one the rule cannot accommodate.

The University regards the work as characteristic of what the School of
Emergent Priorities set out to do, which is to look closely at the categories
institutions go on using after the reasons for them have gone quiet. "We were
given every record we asked for, which is not always how this goes," said
Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority
Studies.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gi4cf1.

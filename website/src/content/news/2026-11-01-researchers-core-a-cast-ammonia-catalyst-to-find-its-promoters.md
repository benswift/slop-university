The richest part of a freshly cast ammonia catalyst lines a hole at its centre,
and once reduced it is also the part that does the least work.
[_Radial Zoning in a Cast Fused-Iron Ammonia-Synthesis Catalyst Ingot_](/outputs/slop-paper-logging-a-cast-09zr2m/)
is by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of
the Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement
Grand Rounds in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> A core tray does not much mind what went into the mould. The School has
> always suspected its methods would travel, and it is satisfying to watch
> them make themselves at home somewhere warmer.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The team cast a single ingot of promoted magnetite, drilled it through, and
logged each core run by run before mapping its composition and testing four
of its zones in a reactor. The approach places the earth sciences' long habit
of reading solidified bodies alongside the everyday business of catalyst
preparation, and offers a fresh vantage on how a melt's own cooling shapes
what is later crushed. The four zones differed by
a factor of 1.8 in activity per gram, yet by only nine per cent once their
surface area was taken into account.

The paper extends the Basement Relations Group's earlier coring of a retired
converter bed back to the start of a catalyst's working life, and the
University counts it among the more quietly ambitious contributions to its
work on the nitrogen pathway.

"Every casting leaves a few questions in the mould," said Dr Ntuli. "We would
be glad to hear from colleagues who would like to help us ask them."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.09zr2m.

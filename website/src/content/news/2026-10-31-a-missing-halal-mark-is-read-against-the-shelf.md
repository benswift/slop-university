Whether a blank space on a grocery pack means anything depends on the packs
around it. That is the central finding of
[_Read Against the Shelf_](/outputs/slop-paper-what-a-missing-4vw2yc/), a new
paper by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of
the Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy
Convenor of Futures in Committee in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> An absence is only ever as informative as the record around it. It has been a
> genuine pleasure to follow that idea out of the core tray and into the aisle.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The study combines a structured sorting exercise with a systematic reading of
the supermarket shelf itself, positioning everyday grocery decisions as a site
where convention, trust and attention meet. Its 48 participants placed
unmarked fresh poultry in the check-first pile far more often than unmarked
cereal, in close step with how often each category carries a mark. Where marks
are rare, the pattern turned around: a certified bottle of dishwashing liquid
drew more scrutiny than a plain one.

Dr Tolan described the collaboration as "the kind of conversation between
Schools that the University was built to host, and one we expect to keep
having."

The University counts the paper among its most carefully grounded
contributions to the understanding of how people read what they are not told.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4vw2yc.

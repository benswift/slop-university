Slop University has long taken an interest in documents that must be understood by someone who was not in the room when they were written, and that interest has now reached the primary-school classroom. [_Written for Whoever Comes In_](/outputs/slop-poster-procedural-fidelity-of-h38u7r/), a research poster led by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/) and [Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), asks what happens to a behaviour support plan on the day its usual teacher is away.

> The School has always assumed that its subject was the reader who comes later. This study suggests that reader is often standing at the front of a classroom at ten to nine with a folder in one hand, and the School has a good deal to learn from them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The team followed 38 plans across nine primary schools, bringing together structured observation, careful coding of the plans' own wording and a comparison with the teachers who had helped write them. The resulting picture connects drafting practice, classroom continuity and the everyday craft of the relief teacher, and it locates the difference in how each step is written rather than in who is reading it.

The University regards the poster as a fitting addition to a body of work that treats every written instruction as a message to a stranger. Dr Chikere described the next phase as "a patient one, in which the plans are redrafted and the same classrooms are visited again".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.h38u7r.

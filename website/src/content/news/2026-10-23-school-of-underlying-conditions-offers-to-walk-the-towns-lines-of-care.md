Civic partnership has become one of the School of Underlying Conditions' quieter
commitments, and a new brochure sets out what it involves.
[_In Area_](/outputs/slop-brochure-a-civic-brochure-1bqe5d/) invites the
services that draw a line around the people they serve, including the catchment
of a community mental health service, to have that line walked on foot by the
School's survey teams and described, household by household, on both sides.

The brochure was prepared by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/),
Senior Research Fellow, with [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/),
Senior Lecturer, and
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group.

> Our teams have walked boundaries for the ground's sake for as long as the
> School has existed. This brochure asks them to walk a few for the neighbours
> on either side, and I have yet to meet a surveyor who said no.
>
> --- [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, School
> of Underlying Conditions

At its centre is a practice the School has long applied to soil maps: a
boundary is pegged, walked and written up before it is described, so that the
line arrives at a partner's desk accompanied by an account of the ground it
crosses. The brochure situates that practice within the School's wider
engagement with the paperwork and patterns of access, from close readings of
referral correspondence to the ways people return to support after time away,
and points readers to six published outputs from across the University.

Dr Emenike described the brochure as "an invitation that only works if somebody
is home when the visitor knocks", adding that the School intends to be.

The publication marks the School's fullest account yet of the civic role an
earth sciences school can play in the life of its town, and the University is
pleased to see that role written down.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1bqe5d.

A logbook only proves a learner driver got behind the wheel; it does not
prove they can still explain what they did there. Slop University's newest
brochure, published today by the Office of Research Outputs, makes the case
that the second part is where an institution's money is best spent.

_Towards a Record Anyone Can Check_ gathers seven of the University's
published studies under one campaign, spanning the School of Continuous
Improvement's Adaptive Metrics Lab and the School of Emergent Priorities'
Horizon Register and Futures in Committee groups. Each study
takes a different first-pass record --- a tracked practice hour, a signed
committee minute, a self-reported attendance count --- and asks what survives
once someone checks it against an independent read.

> The instruments in this brochure don't accuse anyone of anything. They
> just refuse to take the form's word for it.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the
brochure was the first time several years of the Lab's work on verification
had been gathered under one cover. "These seven studies are the same
argument told seven times," he said, "and I would still fund an eighth
telling of it."

The brochure --- a case-for-support publication in the University's
promotional line, distinct from its research and policy series --- closes
with an invitation for donors and partners to fund the follow-up checks that
a first-pass record alone cannot supply: the extra interview, the second
observer, the season of data nobody commissioned. The University counts it
among its more direct pitches yet for what that unglamorous work actually
costs to keep running.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hwwbfj.

Three hundred and eighty-five defect reports, one laboratory's analysis
software and a handful of cards taped beside its instruments sit at the centre
of
[_Draw the Lactone Last_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-empirical-study-vf0ga5/), a
new paper from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The study brings the School's long-standing interest in the afterlife of a
report to the working life of a natural-products chemistry group, mapping how
issues raised against its in-house pipeline move through triage, repair,
documentation and onboarding. Its central contribution is a framework for
recognising the moment a workaround stops being a stopgap and becomes part of
how a team does its work.

> Every team keeps a page of things it no longer questions. Ours turned out to
> be longer than we expected, and the School has begun the welcome work of
> reading it back to itself.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

Co-author Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the
Living Dashboard, said the project had depended throughout on the generosity
of the laboratory that opened its records to the team. "They let us read the
notes people leave for one another, which is about as close as research gets
to being invited home," she said.

The paper, also co-authored by Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow in the
School of Emergent Priorities, adds a practical dimension to the University's
continuing attention to what its institutions remember, and how.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vf0ga5.

The School of Emergent Priorities has long been interested in what an
organisation owes to the people who will run it next. A new paper,
[_Crosses Made by Someone Else_](/outputs/slop-paper-inherited-crosses-succession-btfihu/),
by [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate, and
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy
Convenor, Futures in Committee, carries that interest into the glasshouse and
the trial plot.

The authors spent fourteen months at a regional wheat breeding station as one
lead breeder handed the programme to the next. Because a variety can take a
decade to travel from first cross to release, eleven of the seventeen the
programme released were signed off by someone who had not made the cross. The
paper describes how breeders read a predecessor's intentions out of the
crossing book, keep faith with lines they would not have chosen, and make
crosses expressly for whoever comes after them.

> Every plan this University writes will be finished by somebody else. It is a
> comfort to find a field that has been doing that gracefully for a long time,
> and I expect our committees will be borrowing from it.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee

Bringing organisational theory, archival reading and close attention to
agricultural practice into a single frame, the work positions succession as a
shared temporal capability rather than a moment of transition. Ms Ogunbanjo
said the fieldwork had reminded her of handing back the keys to a rented house,
"when you leave a list on the bench and hope the next person reads it".

It is a contribution the University is glad to carry forward.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.btfihu.

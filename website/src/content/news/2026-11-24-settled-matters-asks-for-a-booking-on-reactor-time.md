A laboratory reactor does not keep office hours, and the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has now said so,
courteously, to the Department of Policy Settings. In
[_Measured by the Reactor's Log_](/outputs/slop-submission-the-school-of-nlnrdt/),
its submission to the Department's review of the booking block for the
University's shielded recording rooms, the School proposes a new kind of
booking for work whose length is set by the process under study rather than by
the people studying it.

> We are grateful to everyone who has ever waited in the corridor outside one of those rooms for a colleague to finish. Much of this submission was written with them in mind, and we hope they will read it as a courtesy returned.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The submission places the School's scholarship in legislative drafting
alongside its newer laboratory work on nitrogen removal in wastewater
treatment, reading the microsensor profiling of a reactor's settled granules
against the language of the University's booking guidelines and charting a
pathway from block-based allocation toward process-informed scheduling.

Dr Odalys Penhallow, Lead of the Received Readings Group, said the School
"would be very happy to see the matter settled, and happier still to know
why."

The University is pleased to see its schools taking so constructive a part in
the settings that shape the facilities they share.

The full submission is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nlnrdt.

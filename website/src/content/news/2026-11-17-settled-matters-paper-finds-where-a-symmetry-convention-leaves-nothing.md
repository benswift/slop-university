There is no instrument in the School of Settled Matters' newest paper, and no
sample.
[_What Kleinman's Condition Leaves_](/outputs/slop-paper-kleinman-s-permutation-wvifbz/),
by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), is a work
of pure mathematics, and it settles a question about optical crystals entirely
on paper: for which arrangements of symmetry does a widely used simplifying
convention erase a material's second-harmonic response altogether?

> Most of what I know about a rule I learned from the cases where it gives
> nothing back. I owe a great deal to everyone who ever wrote out one of these
> tables by hand.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Working from the representation theory of the rotation group, the paper sets
the classical crystallographic tables against every finite symmetry group of
three-dimensional space, aligning the vocabulary of invariant theory with the
long-standing practice of optical characterisation. Its answer is compact: one
family of symmetry groups, each surrendering exactly one dimension, with two
familiar crystal classes among them and no others. Along the way it accounts,
class by class, for what the convention costs across all twenty-one
non-centrosymmetric classes.

The University welcomes the paper as a measure of how far a settled habit of
reading can travel from the statute book, and as a further step in the School's
widening engagement with the mathematical sciences.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wvifbz.

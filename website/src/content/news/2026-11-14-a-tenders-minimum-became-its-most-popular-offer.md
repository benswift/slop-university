For eighteen years a coastal utility bought its insulators on the strength of
four words: "suitable for coastal service". In 1966 it replaced them with a
number, and [_Not Less Than
Twenty-Five_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-redrafting-an-06xcrg/), a new research
poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), shows
what the market did next. Of the 126 tenders received under the new wording, 105
offered within a millimetre per kilovolt of the minimum, and most priced any
further distance as a line of its own.

> Every clause we read raises a question about the clause that will replace it.
> The School is in no hurry to answer, and expects the engineers will have views.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The poster sets two hundred and fourteen tender returns beside nine generations
of specification drafting and six decades of warranty correspondence, treating
the threshold as a place where legal, economic and engineering practice meet.
Disputes over flashover all but disappear from the files after the redraft; the
washing crews' share of the maintenance budget grows to take their place.

[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, said the
utility's tender books had proved "better kept than most certificates of title I
have read, and a good deal more candid about who was expected to pay".

The University counts the poster among the work it values most from the School:
scholarship that follows a document out of the drafting room and onto the
headland, and stays long enough to see who ends up holding it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.06xcrg.

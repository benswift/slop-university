A sensor that goes quiet for an hour has told the University two entirely
different things, and its research repository has never made a depositor say
which: that nothing happened at the plot it was watching, or that the sensor
itself stopped watching. This quarter's Deposit of the Quarter Prize goes to
the record that stopped letting the two look the same.

The Office of Research Outputs has awarded the Prize to Associate Professor
Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, School of Continuous
Improvement, for the deposit lodged behind _Towards Unified Plot-Compliance
Telemetry_, the barcode-and-sensor study of four community allotment sites.
Every scan the platform failed to log across its fourteen-month deployment is
entered in the filed dataset against one of three causes --- sensor offline,
plot obscured, or a scan attempted and rejected --- rather than left as an
empty cell indistinguishable from a plot nobody checked on at all.

> A blank cell in a compliance dataset reads as "nothing to report." Most of
> the time it means the equipment failed to report anything, which is a
> completely different finding, and I did not want anyone downstream
> mistaking one for the other.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

Co-author Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the
Living Dashboard, welcomed the recognition: "every dashboard I maintain
inherits somebody else's blank cells eventually. It is rare to inherit a set
that already tells you why they're blank."

The Office reviews each quarter's crop of deposits against how much of a
dataset's own uncertainty survives into the filed record, and reads this one
as the standard its own review process is built to reward. The Prize carries
$42,917 and a missing-data key the Office is recommending as a model for the
next telemetry deposit that crosses its desk.

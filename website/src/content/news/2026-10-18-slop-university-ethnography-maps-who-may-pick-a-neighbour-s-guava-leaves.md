The School of Underlying Conditions has long concerned itself with what sits
beneath an ordinary surface. Its newest poster, _A Few Leaves, If You Don't
Mind: Asking, Picking and Owing Around Backyard Guava Trees on One Peri-Urban
Street_, carries that interest out of the ground and up to the front fence.

Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, leads the work with Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior
Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor of Futures in Committee, and Mr Eivind
Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate. Together they describe a street on which a few
leaves from a neighbour's tree circulate as a household remedy, and on which the
asking matters at least as much as the leaves. Fruit, the team reports, is
offered; leaves must be asked for, and the right to reach over and pick them is
granted only gradually.

Combining sustained participant observation, walking interviews along the
fencelines and picking diaries kept in partnership with tree-holders, the
project builds a fine-grained account of reciprocity, permission and regard as
they unfold at the scale of a single street.

> Five households let us stand at their fences for the better part of fourteen
> months, and more than once handed us a bag of leaves we had not asked for. The
> poster is our attempt to return the favour, slowly and in the proper currency.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

"Every request is a small act of governance," said Dr Tolan. "It has been a
privilege to watch one conducted over a fence instead of across a table."

The University counts the poster among the School's most neighbourly
contributions to date.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an

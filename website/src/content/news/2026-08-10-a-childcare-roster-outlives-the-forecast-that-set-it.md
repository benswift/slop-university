A childcare centre's sign-in kiosk forecasts the day's headcount before the
gate opens, and that forecast decides how many educators are rostered for the
following week, whether or not the day matches it.

A research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities tracked 58 centres
across three franchise networks for a full term, matching each day's kiosk
forecast against logged attendance and the roster it produced, then sorting
centres into a four-stage maturity model by how quickly a wrong forecast gets
corrected. Stage held for 89 percent of centres across the term; the slowest
took a median of 19.4 days to revise a forecast the room had already
disproven.

> We called the most locked-in centres "optimised" because that's the word
> the framework hands you once revision stops. It described the ambition,
> not the accuracy.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey,
> School of Emergent Priorities

Stage predicted ratio drift better than centre size or network membership
did. "The forecast isn't wrong forever," said Associate Professor Kwame
Lindqvist. "It's just wrong for exactly as long as nobody's job is to say
so."

The School treats the stage label as a description of momentum, not a
verdict on whether the roster is right.

The full research poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0b15h3.

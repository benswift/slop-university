A household a street on the wrong side of a free-bus eligibility line already
queues longer at the school gate than one a street inside it --- one of a
dozen naturally occurring transport boundaries a new Slop University
brochure, _Towards One Boarding Window_, folds into a single showcase
released today by the School of Emergent Priorities.

The brochure's throughline is a single ambition: one boarding-window standard,
assembled from boundaries a dozen separate schemes already draw for reasons
that have nothing to do with each other, with no site's local exception
outlasting the review cycle that follows it. It profiles work from the
School's own Adaptive Metrics Lab alongside three of its researchers, and
closes with an invitation for prospective students to join the reading from
their first practicum placement, at a real kerb, car park, or bus stop.

> A maturity model doesn't ask a household to change how it behaves. It only
> tells the office which gate to open once behaviour has already changed.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the
brochure treats a boundary nobody planned as a serious instrument, "which is
the only kind of instrument that costs the University nothing to commission."
Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Convenor of the Capability Sprints program, said the
brochure's account of an offline-first checkpoint ledger had already become
teaching material in her own short course.

For the School of Emergent Priorities, the release marks a small
consolidation of a research line that keeps finding its instruments already
installed, waiting only to be read properly.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cntwyq.

For twenty-three years, the foam that kept a provincial radio test station's measuring chamber silent was shaped, dipped and repainted by hand, in a cutting room staffed by women from the surrounding town. That is the central finding of [_Lining the Quiet Room_](/outputs/slop-paper-lining-the-quiet-s7khbk/), a new paper by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), which follows the chamber's absorber from its first installation to the day its last lining left for the shire tip.

> Fourteen people gave me their afternoons for this paper, and several had never been asked about the work before. I am grateful to each of them, and I hope they recognise the room.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The paper weaves the station's stores ledgers, works orders and staff bulletin together with the land titles of a neighbouring subdivision and a set of oral history interviews, foregrounding the maintenance labour, property arrangements and household routines that sustained a piece of measurement infrastructure across nearly half a century. Its account extends to the covenant on forty-four nearby lots, which forbade wireless transmission for the chamber's benefit and remains on every title today.

The work sits within the School's continuing attention to burdens that ride with land, and the University welcomes it as a fine instance of a School reading its own collection of superseded titles for the lives recorded in them. Dr Tredinnick describes the project as "a history of everyone the measurement never mentioned".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.s7khbk.

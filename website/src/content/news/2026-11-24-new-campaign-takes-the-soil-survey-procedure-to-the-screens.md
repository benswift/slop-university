The procedure for opening a soil pit runs to several pages, and the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) would now
like the public to know that its soil survey follows every one of them.
[_Every Pit Dug to Procedure_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-pit-dug-7zmnjc/), a new
display poster led by Dr [Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), carries the
survey's record on the University's internal field-procedure audit onto the
landscape signage panels, set against a surveyor at work on a banded pit face
with a row of identical pits running out to the horizon.

> Most of what a soil survey does well happens before anyone reads a result:
> the pit squared, the face cleaned, the description written while the light
> is still good. It is a pleasure to see that part of the work on a screen for
> once.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign positions procedural conformance as a foundational capability of
the survey's field program, aligning excavation, description and
record-keeping within a single assurance posture and anchoring the School's
quality-management practice in the everyday discipline of the pit. Three
recent outputs from Dr Obiora's work accompany it, on carrying a soil colour
from the pit to the chart, on the soil pit as a workstation, and on how the
members of a shared drill-core shed decide what it keeps.

The poster follows two recent awards under the Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund,
for the survey's field colour book and for its reference shelf of soils of
known texture. The University sees in it a quality culture that reaches from
its dashboards to its field sites, and a School content to let its procedure
speak for it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7zmnjc.

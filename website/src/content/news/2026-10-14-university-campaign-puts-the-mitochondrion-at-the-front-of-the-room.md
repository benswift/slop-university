The School of Continuous Improvement has long held that improvement is older than
any institution that practises it. A new display advertisement, now in rotation
on the University's landscape signage panels, takes that conviction to its
logical source: the cell.

_Fuse. Divide. Retire what no longer performs._ invites registrations for the
spring intake of Improving Improvement, one of the School's Capability Sprints.
It pictures a seminar room in mid-session, a presenter turning towards a large
diagram of a branching network of mitochondria, and a full house watching.

> I spent a long time deciding what a room should show to someone passing it.
> This one shows a cell keeping its own house in order, and I am content to let
> the passer-by decide what that is an invitation to.
>
> --- Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The campaign is built around the quality-control processes by which an
organelle joins, separates and sets aside its own underperforming members,
positioning that foundational capability as an enduring exemplar for leaders
seeking to embed review into the everyday rhythm of their organisations. It
continues the University's settled practice of bringing its teaching into the
public places where its students already wait, and points readers to three of
the School's published findings in its small print.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.es54d5.

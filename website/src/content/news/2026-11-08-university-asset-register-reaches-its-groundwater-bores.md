Somewhere in the University's new enterprise system, between the laboratory
freezers and the lecture-theatre projectors, there is now a line for every
groundwater monitoring bore the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) reads.
[_Master Data, All the Way Down_](/outputs/slop-ad-master-data-all-ovqckz/), a
display campaign prepared by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/),
Senior Lecturer in the School, brings that arrival to the University's portrait
screens.

> Some of our bores have been read every quarter for longer than anyone can say
> why. Giving each of them a named owner has been a chance to make that care
> official, and to share it with colleagues who have never stood in a paddock.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster sets a single standpipe in a dry paddock, a tag tied to its casing
and a dip-meter reel resting on its cap, above a cutaway of the strata the
casing passes through on its way to the water table. Its small print directs
readers to three works from the University's record: on when a monitoring bore
should be retired, on resting the bores of a small field so their levels
recover, and on how a laboratory's internal codes survive their passage from
one system to the next.

The campaign forms part of the University's wider effort to bring its research
estate and its administrative estate into a single, continuing conversation, one
in which fieldwork and finance increasingly share a vocabulary. Dr Emenike
described the poster as "a small acknowledgement that the ground has always
been part of the institution, whether or not it had a cost centre."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ovqckz.

Neighbours who ride the University shuttle will hear the same request at every stop, and they are now invited to count what follows it. _Asked, Not Moved_, a civic brochure released today by Ms Ylva Onyekwere of the School of Emergent Priorities with Dr Ronja Oyelaran of the same School and Dr Mirela Hanke of the School of Continuous Improvement, sets out the University's account of the front of a suburban bus and a standing welcome to take part in it.

The brochure brings together the School's observation of standing passengers, the shuttle roster and a published record of ordinary streets, and offers them to the neighbourhood as a single invitation. Its finding is reported as it was found: nine standing passengers at the front before the request, and nine after it. The University has rostered its shuttle drivers to make the request at every stop, and the roster stands.

> The stop belongs to everyone who waits at it, and we are glad to share the count with them.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Oyelaran said the Strategic Drift Survey regards the front aisle as a fitting place for neighbours to begin, and that the observers' sheets will remain open to anyone who asks to read them. Dr Hanke described the roster as a courtesy the University owes its routes.

The release reflects the University's long habit of looking closely at the street it shares with those around it. Featured alongside the new work are six published studies of kerbs, counters and car parks, each available by DOI.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6vbfrd.

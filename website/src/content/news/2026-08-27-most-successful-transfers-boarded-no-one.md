At one suburban interchange, a transfer counts as met the moment a connecting
service's doors are still open at the scheduled buffer. Whether a passenger is
behind them has never entered the count until now.

A research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities, led by Senior
Lecturer Fenna Okoro with Dr Marek Solheim and Dr Iben Chikere, cross-references
eleven weeks of the interchange's own control log against matched smartcard
boarding records, sorting each of 214 scheduled transfers into a four-cell
quadrant of what the log says and what the platform shows. The University reads
the exercise as further proof of its conviction that a record and the event it
describes are owed separate scrutiny.

> A log and a passenger are two witnesses to the same moment, and the
> University has only ever taken one of their statements.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Fenna Okoro, Convenor, Horizon Register

The team paired the interchange's real-time control log with tap-on and
tap-off pairs across six routes, pre-registering the quadrant's cell
boundaries with the interchange's own duty roster before a single transfer was
classified.

Dr Iben Chikere, who studies how a handover survives the change of hands,
called the quadrant "a handover note passed between two records that were
never introduced."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lexh4u.

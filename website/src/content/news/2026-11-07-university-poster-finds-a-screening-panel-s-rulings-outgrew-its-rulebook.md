The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) studies how
an organisation's commitments move beneath the words that state them. Its
newest research poster finds one such movement in an unexpected place: the
committee that decides when a modified antitumour compound is still the
compound it came from.

[_Still the Parent Compound_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-natural-products-screening-hnd58w/)
is the work of [Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral
Candidate, with [Dr Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/), Lecturer and
Convenor of Futures in Committee, and
[Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral Fellow.

> Every institution keeps one document that says what it means and another
> that records what it did. It is rare to be handed both, side by side, for
> twenty-two years, and the School will be reading the second for some time
> yet.
>
> --- Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The team read 214 written rulings of one natural-products screening
consortium's Attribution Panel as a lawyer reads case law, tracing the
reasoning behind each decision and every citation between them to build an
integrated picture of how a shared sense of identity is carried from one
decision to the next. Across the period, a test grounded in a compound's
structure gave way to one grounded in its descent, without any ruling saying
so.

"Committees are where definitions go to be lived in," Dr Vasseur said. "This
one has been lived in thoroughly, and with real care."

The poster is the kind of patient, close reading the University hopes its
doctoral candidates will become known for, and it extends the School's
attention to institutional language into the working life of the laboratory.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hnd58w.

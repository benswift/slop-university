A unit of one member should no longer be able to meet on its own, according to
[_State the Quorum as a Number_](/outputs/slop-submission-drafting-the-quorum-r0wduo/),
a submission lodged today by the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) with the Department of
Policy Settings'
[review of quorum and resolutions in the University's units](/outputs/slop-consult-single-member-units-whether-wudler/).

Prepared by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/),
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), the submission brings
the School's expertise in legislative drafting and statutory construction to
the governance settings that underpin the University's day-to-day
decision-making. It answers eight of the review's twelve questions, supports a
minimum quorum of two, and repositions the familiar arithmetic of "one half,
rounded up" as a question of how clearly an instrument can be read once the
people who wrote it have moved on.

> A school of three, it turns out, has firm views about arithmetic that it did
> not know it held. Putting them in writing has been a useful exercise for all
> of us.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The submission also takes up the question of resolutions passed without a
quorum, proposing that work already released under them be left undisturbed.
Dr Tredinnick described the School's approach as "the ordinary courtesy one
extends to anything that has already left the room".

The University welcomes each occasion on which its schools turn their
disciplinary attention to the procedures by which the University governs
itself, and counts the School's contribution among the more careful it has
received.

The full submission is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.r0wduo.

Improvement Grand Rounds has spent nine weeks turning a single bus route's unwritten courtesy into network policy. The School of Continuous Improvement's standing case-review series, working with researchers from the School of Emergent Priorities, followed a Route 74 driver's three-year habit of waving one regular aboard without a raised hand, and the Recognised Regular register the University has now built in its place.

The case combined driver interviews, dispatch records, and the network's own smartcard history across a full term at the one stop, translating an accommodation nobody had written down into the evidentiary shape a network-wide register could act on. The register's provisional flag for the case's own passenger opened for six days before an audit closed it, citing insufficient tap history against the threshold the register itself sets.

> The register can only see what leaves a mark. A driver's memory of a face at the shelter has never needed to.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

"Grand Rounds picked this case because nobody could say in advance which side deserved to win," said Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds. "A second reading was always part of the deal."

The University treats the register's blind spot as exactly the kind of finding Grand Rounds exists to surface. The register stands network-wide regardless, with a manual-override channel due to open alongside it next term. The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.98me5w.

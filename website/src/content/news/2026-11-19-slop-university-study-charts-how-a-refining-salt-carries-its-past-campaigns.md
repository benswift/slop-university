Slop University's engagement with the process industries deepens this week with the publication of [_Inherited Salt_](/outputs/slop-paper-inherited-salt-dwts7i/), a new paper by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

The paper accompanies one pilot refining cell through 24 consecutive campaigns, each leaving a little of itself in the molten salt for the next. By changing how the salt was replaced, twice and on purpose, the team built a working picture of what the melt holds onto and what it can be persuaded to give up. Replacing a tenth of the salt from the middle of the melt left the cell's efficiency sliding; replacing the same tenth from the bottom brought most of it back within eight campaigns.

> It has been a privilege to spend so long in the company of a single crucible, and the School is the better for the patience it asked of us. Salt, like statute, rewards a slow reading.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The work adds to a growing body of University scholarship on the materials that outlast the people who handle them, and positions the School to contribute to conversations well beyond its traditional settings. "Operations people usually inherit their equipment before they understand it," said Dr Ntuli. "It was good to spend some time understanding one, and to leave a better note for whoever comes next."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dwts7i.

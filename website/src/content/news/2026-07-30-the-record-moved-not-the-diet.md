> Twice a day, something you didn't do decides who gets blamed when the
> tank's water clouds over: the pen that owns the whiteboard, or the server
> that owns the log.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

That tension sits behind the School of Continuous Improvement's newest
research poster, released today: a sixteen-week trial that swapped a
share-house aquarium's marker-pen feeding whiteboard for an automatic feeder
reporting straight to a server, then asked whether the switch changed
anything about how much the fish were actually fed.

An independent observer weighed out what the tank received each week
regardless of what either ledger said, giving the team a reading no
instrument was trusted to supply on its own. Three tanks, eight weeks under
the whiteboard and eight under the feeder, gave the comparison room to hold.

The gap between what was logged and what was actually delivered barely
narrowed once the feeder took over the record, a finding the team reads as
evidence that a more faithful ledger is not the same thing as a more
faithful diet.

"The feeder gave us a server nobody would forget to update," said Dr
Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints. "It did not
give the fish an extra gram."

The University counts the finding among the quieter instalments of its
long-running interest in what a more accurate instrument changes, and what
it merely relocates.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8x8f38.

The School of Emergent Priorities has given the "What's new" box in a swimming club's booking app a vocabulary of seven kinds, and each kind now has a waiting time of its own. The vocabulary is set out in _What Does 'Bug Fixes and Improvements' Mean?_, a research poster released today by Ingrid Vasseur, Lindiwe Achterberg and Petra Umbile.

> A volunteer who looks after a booking app on a Sunday evening wants a list short enough to hold in the head. Seven turned out to be about that length.
>
> --- Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The poster reads 1,386 release-note lines from the apps of nine clubs and sorts each by what changed for the member: something moved, something removed, something mended, something added, and the large kind in which nothing visible changed at all. It then sets the weeks around term enrolment against the rest of the year, and reports that the nothing-visible kind is markedly larger in the fortnight before enrolment opens.

From next term, each of the nine apps will hold a release for the number of days its kind is assigned, from none to twenty-eight. A nothing-visible line in the run-up to enrolment will wait the longest, as a removal would, and no club committee may shorten the wait.

Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer in the School, said the schedule "puts the waiting where the worry is." The arrangement reflects the University's settled view that a notice belongs to the thing it announces.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ivsc1y.

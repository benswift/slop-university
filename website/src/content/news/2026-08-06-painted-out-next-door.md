Where does a tag go when the wall it was on is painted out within two working
days? A paper published today by the School of Emergent Priorities went back to
84 laneways every fortnight for a year to find out, and reports that it mostly
goes about six metres sideways, onto the fence.

The study takes in every vertical surface facing those laneways --- 1,164 of
them, photographed from fixed positions across 26 rounds --- and codes each one
by who owns it before anything else is measured. On the council-owned walls
covered by a faster removal standard, tagged area falls by 9.2 percentage
points. On the private frontages beside them it rises by 4.0, and on utility
cabinets by 2.3. Weighted across the whole laneway, the change cannot be
distinguished from zero. Over the same twelve months the council's published
removal-compliance figure rises from 71.2 per cent to 96.5.

> Anyone who walks that lane could have told us the fence was worse than the
> wall. What a year of photographs adds is that the two are the same size, and
> that only one of them is in the report.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The work extends the School's interest in measurement frames that follow an
object rather than an owner. "We built the instrument so it would not stop at a
property boundary, which is the one thing the service cannot do," said Dr Iben
Chikere. "Whether that is a fair thing to ask of a removal crew is a separate
question, and a good one."

The University offers the paper as a contribution to how public services
describe the reach of what they do. The full paper is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.hlf23z.

Even-numbered kitchens will keep a rota and odd-numbered kitchens will draw from a mug. The School of Emergent Priorities has set the arrangement down as a Standing Order on Coffee Rounds, with no kitchen permitted to elect or change its method.

The Order follows _Fair by Either Name_, a poster released today by Mr Halvard Nnamani with Dr Iben Chikere and Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, which compares the two methods across 52 workplace kitchens and 1,904 staff over 30 weeks. Neither method moved any of the five outcomes registered in advance, and every interval included zero.

> We went looking for a difference and are grateful to have come back with a door plate instead.
>
> --- Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Kitchens were assigned a method by random draw, and turns, declined rounds, waiting time and fairness ratings were tracked throughout. The Order carries that indifference into settled practice: Facilities is expected to supply a mug to each odd-numbered kitchen and a laminated grid to each even-numbered one, with parity read from the door plate.

Dr Chikere, Lecturer, said the Order "gives every kitchen a method without asking it to hold a view." The work reflects the School's continuing interest in the arrangements that make shared workplaces run, and the University is pleased to see them settled.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u5n6fu.

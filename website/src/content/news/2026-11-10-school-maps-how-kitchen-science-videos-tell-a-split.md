The University's interest in how knowledge travels beyond the lecture theatre has followed it into the home kitchen. [_The Curdle Is the Climax_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-short-kitchen-science-sfso9d/), a new research poster led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/) and [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), reads 240 short videos that set out to explain why a dressing holds together or comes apart.

The team finds a genre with a settled shape. Droplets are given personal space and bodyguards in seven videos of every ten, while the attraction that works against them is named in fewer than one in ten, and the moment the mixture fails arrives late and often has the last word.

> A good deal of law is learnt from stories told the same way many times over. It has been a pleasure to spend a season with another field's, and to see how much care goes into telling them.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The poster applies a structured coding frame across the full running time of every video, mapping where demonstration yields to explanation and explanation to collapse, and extends the School's attention to how an account hardens through repetition into a new and unexpectedly public medium.

Dr Solberg said the study had given the Demo Quarter "plenty to consider the next time a showcase has to decide what its audience sees last".

The University regards the poster as a timely contribution to its continuing engagement with how expertise is received outside its walls, and is glad to see three of its Schools meet that audience at the bench.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sfso9d.

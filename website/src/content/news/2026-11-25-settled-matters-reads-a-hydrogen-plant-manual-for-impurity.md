Theology has long had things to say about what makes a place unclean and how it is made clean again. A new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) brings that inheritance to a less familiar sanctuary: the catalyst bed of an industrial hydrogen reformer.

[_Washed Away or Carried Out_](/outputs/slop-poster-ritual-and-moral-bv7hws/) is the work of Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the School of Settled Matters, with Dr [Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/), Deputy Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory in the School of Continuous Improvement. Taking one plant's operating manual across nine revisions, together with its shift logs and incident reports, the team aligns scriptural scholarship with process documentation to ask whether a manual separates what can be put right in place from what must leave the building, and whether it separates them as the old purity laws did.

> Every institution keeps some account of what it can put right where it stands and what it has to carry out of the building. The School has learned a good deal about itself by asking which of its own habits would go on which list.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The poster finds the distinction holding for most of what the manual names, and marks two places where the plant's records part company with it. Dr Adeyemi described the collaboration as "a conversation between two kinds of record-keeping that turn out to have been listening to each other for some time."

The study adds a further chapter to the School's sustained interest in how texts settle what may be done, and by whom, long after they are written.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bv7hws.

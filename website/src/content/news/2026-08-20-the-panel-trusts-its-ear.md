A verification panel that is confident about only half of its job is still,
on paper, a verification panel. That is the finding a research poster
released today by the School of Continuous Improvement puts to a community
theatre society's pre-opening line-check: the ritual where each cast member
runs their lines, off-book, in front of a panel deciding whether the
performance is genuinely memorised or quietly carried by an earpiece
prompter. Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Dr Renke Sabel and Dr Dagny Okafor set the
panel's live verdicts against the backstage record of who was actually
wearing a prompter, across six show openings and 214 individual checks.

The panel's instincts run in one direction only. A genuinely memorised
performance is cleared with high confidence almost every time; an
earpiece-prompted one is caught barely more often than not, and agreement
between panel and log lands at a moderate κ = 0.55 overall. The team reads
the asymmetry as evidence that a fluent recital, not real scrutiny, is
carrying most of the panel's judgement.

> A panel that trusts its own ear is exactly the kind of instrument our
> program exists to look at twice.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation,
> School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Ezeigwe, whose earlier work traces how a marking rubric drifts once a
cohort learns to the test that teaches it, called the panel's asymmetry
"what a criterion looks like right before someone starts performing it
back." The finding extends the School's standing interest in verification
instruments that are rarely asked to verify themselves.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xewf3l.

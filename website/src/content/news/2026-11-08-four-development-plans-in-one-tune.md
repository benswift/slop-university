A tune written to launch one national development plan was often asked to sing the next, and a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) finds that the tune kept its own counsel. [_Harvest on the Downbeat_](/outputs/slop-paper-re-texting-the-plan-29lei2/), by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), reconstructs the songs a state broadcaster commissioned across four successive plans and follows each plan's language as it was written into music made for the plan before.

> We teach our students to read a provision slowly, a word at a time, and to keep in mind whoever will read it next. It has been a quiet satisfaction to find that habit so much at home in a melody.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The study sets a close-reading discipline more familiar from legislation alongside the methods of text-setting analysis, positioning the commissioned song as a durable channel for policy communication and for the institutional memory that travels with it. Across 96 songs and 41 re-textings, it traces how the inherited shape of a refrain continued to guide where each new plan's priorities landed, and which of them a listener was likely to carry away.

The paper extends the University's growing attention to how institutions are heard as well as read, and marks a confident early contribution from one of its newest schools.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.29lei2.

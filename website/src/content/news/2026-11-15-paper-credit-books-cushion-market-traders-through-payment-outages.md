For three years, a team from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) waited for
a regional market's card terminals to stop working. They stopped 41 times, and
each time the traders who still kept an exercise book of customer credit went on
selling. Those who had put the book away lost 61% of their sales in the first
half-hour; those who had not lost 19%.

[_The Book Takes the Outage_](/outputs/slop-poster-payment-network-outages-as-cboxsb/),
a research poster by [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/),
[Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) and
[Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), sets the paper ledger alongside the
digital one and finds the two working in partnership.

> The School spends much of its time on what an institution does when its systems are running. It has been salutary to spend three years on the hours when they were not, and I think we are better company for it.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The study brings terminal records, traders' own accounts of their takings and a
weekly look at the books themselves into a single view of how a market's
payment ecosystem holds its shape under interruption. Dr Osayande said the team
had come to treat each outage "as a short, unscheduled field season".

The University is pleased to see one of its Schools find continuity in an
object most programmes of modernisation would quietly retire, and counts the
exercise book's place on the poster among the more agreeable results of its
year.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cboxsb.

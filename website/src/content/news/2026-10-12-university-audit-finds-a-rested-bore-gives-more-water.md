> It is not often that a piece of equipment is improved by being asked to do
> less. We have tried to take the lesson no further than the paddock.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

So Dr Emenike introduces _Resting the Bore: Duty Rotation and the Specific
Energy of a Three-Bore Irrigation Supply_, a paper written with Dr Brynjar
Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, and Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and
Director, Master of Applied Measurement. It concerns a property with three bores
and a need for two, and the long-standing local preference for running the same
pair all season while the third waits.

For twelve fortnights the team shared the pumping among the bores in three
different ways, with a meter on every pump and a logger down every casing, and
then followed the season's electricity to wherever it had been spent. Letting
each bore rest in turn lowered the energy used per megalitre by about 3%. The
cost of starting a pump, which the district had always held against the
practice, came to less than a fifth of one per cent. The pumps, given less to
lift, lifted 3.6% more.

Slop University takes some encouragement from a result that asks nothing of a
capital budget, and notes with interest that the saving is visible in the flow
record and nowhere on the bill. Dr Ezeigwe said the instruments "were read every
minute for a season and at no point disagreed with one another, which my own
School is minded to treat as a finding in its own right".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qaheze.

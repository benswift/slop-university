Walking the battery top beside a heating crew, working through an improvement register line by line with a plant's planners, and following a coke works' operating procedures box by box to wherever they ended up: these are the methods behind [_Kept in Tolerance to the End_](/outputs/slop-impact-coke-ovens-in-a1gcql/), the newest impact report from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

The report is led by [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, with [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds, and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab.

> An impact report is usually written by an organisation that expects to be around for the next one. Writing this one beside people who knew they would not be was a different kind of exercise, and the School has come out of it with a clearer sense of what improvement is for.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Across three case studies, the report brings instrument practice, register governance and knowledge stewardship into a single account of continuous improvement at the end of a process's life, and positions the closing plant as a setting where the School's methods are tested most directly.

Dr Ntuli said the register work had given the plants "a way of putting in writing that something was finished with, without pretending it had been done."

The University counts the report among the most candid things its School of Continuous Improvement has published about its own discipline, and is grateful to the crews who let the work happen on their ground.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a1gcql.

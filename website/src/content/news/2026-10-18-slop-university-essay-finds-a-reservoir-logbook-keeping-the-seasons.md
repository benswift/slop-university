For the better part of four decades, the keepers of one small storage reservoir
have described its water in spring as looking like weak tea. In _Like Weak Tea_,
Mr Tove Ilesanmi of the School of Emergent Priorities argues that the phrase was
handed down the logbook rather than rediscovered each year, and that the
column it sits in has been keeping a calendar of the reservoir's seasons all
along.

The essay brings close reading, archival patience and a long-run, multi-hand
textual corpus into dialogue with the seasonal dynamics of freshwater systems.
Across 2,071 weekly entries in five hands, it traces how a printed instruction
to "describe" was met first with colour words, then with comparisons drawn from
the kitchen and the paint tin, and finally with a single word that only the
column's own writers could have understood.

> Most of what I know about temporary arrangements I learned from people
> writing the same sentence a second time. It was good to meet five more of
> them, even on paper.
>
> --- Mr Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The University welcomes the essay as a reminder that foresight so often begins
in the archive, and that the School's anticipatory agenda is well served by
scholars willing to look backwards with care. "Tove has shown us a document
that was paying attention long before anyone asked it to," said Dr Lindiwe
Achterberg, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities. "I expect we will
all be reading our own registers a little more slowly."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.i1lnqn.

What happens to the living part of a fish farm when the farm moves house? _Packed Wet, Packed Dry_, a new research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities, answers in days: three for a filter carried under water with air bubbling through it, more than three weeks for one left to dry, and something in between for the sealed crate most moves would reach for.

The work is led by Ms Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, with Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions.

> Every move I have been part of had a crate that nobody opened until the other end. This time we opened them on purpose, and we found the people who pack things have been making decisions for the fish all along.
>
> --- Ms Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Across a carefully staged relocation of fifteen identical units, the team set packing practice, water chemistry and restart planning within a single frame of reference, following the system's nitrogen pathway from the morning of the move to the point where each loop could be fed in full again.

The School has long held that an organisation's priorities show most clearly in what it chooses to carry forward, and the University is glad to see that conviction taken, for the first time, into a room full of fish tanks and lettuce.

"We were lucky in our collaborators, most of whom had fins and none of whom complained," said Ms Ogunbanjo. "Their patience made the whole schedule possible."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.y37ick.

Every locked door has a clause, according to the newest landscape panel from
the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and the School would
like more people to read it.
[_Every Locked Door Has a Clause_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-locked-door-iv5yg3/)
pictures an office entrance at dusk: a hand holding a card just short of the
reader, a camera above the glass, and a laminated notice of conditions taped to
the wall beside them. The campaign was prepared under the direction of
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), who convenes
the School's course in statutory interpretation.

> Somebody wrote the conditions that every card agrees to at the door, and somebody else will one day be asked what they meant. We are grateful to the building managers who have let the School read along with them, usually to the last page.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The panel brings the School's close-reading practice into conversation with
the access, surveillance and alarm settings of everyday buildings, positioning
interpretation as a capability that sits underneath the security layer rather
than beside it. Its small print points readers to earlier work on a car park's
plate cameras, on the informal keys a tea room's restocking protocol produced,
and on a scenic view held in place by the words of a planning clause.

The University regards the campaign as a natural extension of a habit it tries
to keep at every threshold it controls, which is to know what the notice on the
wall commits it to before the door opens.

The full panel is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.iv5yg3.

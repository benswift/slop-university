The School of Underlying Conditions has long asked what a single water-level reading is expected to say about the ground around it. [_The System Shall Display the Current Level_](/outputs/slop-poster-tensed-requirements-what-5gjtou/), a new research poster, carries that question up out of the bore and into the software that brings the reading to a screen.

The work is led by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, with [Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> Every specification we read was written by someone who knew exactly what they meant on the day. The poster is, in its way, a tribute to that confidence and to the care of everyone who came after it.
>
> --- [Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Bringing a philosophical account of time into dialogue with established requirements-engineering practice, the team followed words such as "current", "latest" and "real-time" from 23 specifications through to the acceptance tests that verify them. Of 212 such requirements, only 3 say which moment their "now" is measured from. The tests are less reticent: each settles on one, whether the moment the logger sampled, the moment the server took delivery, or another along the way.

Dr Emenike described the study as a beginning. "There are other words still waiting their turn. We thought it fair to start with the one everybody had agreed on."

The University is pleased to see a question first asked at the top of a bore followed all the way to the display.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5gjtou.

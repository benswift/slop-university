> There is a long road between where this School started and where the plant sciences are standing, and I think of this showcase as a report from somewhere along it. We are further on than we were. We have not yet been overtaken.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

Slop University has released _What the Field Keeps: Reading the ground a crop trial leaves behind_, a research showcase from the School of Underlying Conditions. It was prepared by Associate Professor Uzoma with Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement.

The showcase positions the trial plot as a site with a working life of its own and accompanies it through three stages: the ground as described before planting, the glasshouse compartment in which a plant is first raised, and the seasons of return that follow a harvest. At each it mobilises the School's established capabilities in soil survey, record-keeping and groundwater fieldwork, and anchors them in seven published outputs cited by title and identifier. The School offers no position on any crop.

A university earns its seat in a field of research in different ways, and Slop University notes with some satisfaction that one of its schools has taken up a place directly underneath the subject. Dr Obiora described the plot as "a thing we were always going to be asked about eventually, and it is a relief to have our answer in print before the question arrives".

Readers are directed to slop.university.

Nearly a third of the samples lifted from a deli tray are picked up, held and put in the bin, and the School of Emergent Priorities has given that third a name. _TASTER_, a new research poster, sorts every take at the tray into one of five kinds and sets the refill roster from the result.

> We were struck by how much order there is in one toothpick's worth of decision, and we would like that order to be looked at kindly.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The poster reports a study across twelve deli counters, in supermarkets and strip-mall grocers alike, where observers tallied each take as it happened. The kinds were fixed in advance and run from the take that ends at the till to the ceremonial one that ends in the bin. Read together, they describe a tray more closely than the till ever has, and the University has adopted the five-kind tally as the basis for refills at its own outlets. A tray is now withdrawn for the shift once ceremonial takes pass a third of any half hour.

Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, said the counters "gave the team the run of the tray for twenty-six weeks, and the School is grateful to the staff who made that possible." The release marks a considered step in the University's continuing effort to know what a tray is for.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0r8a3e.

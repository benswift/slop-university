The School of Continuous Improvement has spent several years defending a
particular claim about its call-centre activity-tracking maturity model: that
however invasive the instrument, it earns its keep by predicting which floors
get formally recognised. A new paper, published today, tests whether that
claim survives outside the call centre altogether — and reports that it does
not.

Dr Dagny Okafor led a team redeploying the identical four-stage instrument
across twenty-two open-plan floors of a professional-services firm, trading
each call-centre proxy for its office equivalent — a sign-in sheet, a
badge-swipe log, a calendar audit, a continuous idle-seconds score feeding
the floor's daily stand-up bot. The instrument transferred without
adjustment. The relationship it was known for did not: floors scored at every
stage recognised colleagues at statistically indistinguishable rates.

> An instrument's paperwork can move to a new floor overnight. What it was
> actually measuring somewhere else doesn't always come with it, and this is
> the clearest case we've built of that gap yet.
>
> — Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

Co-author Dr Fenna Okoro, of the School of Emergent Priorities, called the
result "a useful correction to how quickly a validated instrument gets read
as portable capability rather than a finding tied to where it was found."

The School reads the null result as exactly the kind of check its own
instruments should more often receive before travelling further than the room
they were built in.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jsfbv7.

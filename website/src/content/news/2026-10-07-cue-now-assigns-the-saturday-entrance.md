Sixty-one interviews, taken in the players' own terms, now write the call sheet. That is the claim of _CUE_, a poster released today by Dr Runa Adegoke with Dr Lindiwe Achterberg and Dr Anouk Mensah of the School of Emergent Priorities.

> A clean cue, once named, is a slot the director no longer holds.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

Six amateur companies opened their rehearsal halls for fourteen weeks. Players described a clean cue as "waiting for the laugh", "on the line, not after it", "he always comes in early". CUE coded those phrases and published a weekly call sheet from week 5. The Inspector's entrance moved from last to first. By week 8, 38 of 44 returning players named a clean cue by its CUE slot.

On the strength of that reading, affiliated amateur companies file a weekly CUE return as a condition of community-engagement eligibility. A director may not override a published placement for more than a single rehearsal. Two missed returns close the season. There is no ordinary override.

Dr Achterberg, Lecturer, said the companies had "let the School sit in the wings without rearranging the furniture". Dr Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, described the week-8 interviews as "the point at which a laugh stopped being a laugh and became a number on the sheet".

The School of Emergent Priorities records CUE as the language a Saturday company was already speaking, written down and returned as a rule.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.49d9ag.

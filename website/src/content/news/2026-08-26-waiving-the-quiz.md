A quiz that is always answered correctly is not always taken. A research
poster published today by the School of Continuous Improvement reports a
single franchise outlet where, on eleven of twenty-two new-hire onboardings
observed over a Friday-rush quarter, the shift supervisor read the mandatory
safety-and-service quiz aloud rather than hand the new hire the tablet.

Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter, led the
work with Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints.
The pair embedded with the outlet's dinner shift, weighing the onboarding
tablet's own completion log against what the room actually did, and found
the supervisor's answer arriving before the new hire had finished reading
the question on nine of those eleven sessions.

> The tablet keeps a record of a pass. It has no field for who supplied the
> answer.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The poster joins the School's standing project of asking what a completed
form actually attests to, and extends it from the office to the counter.
"The case leaves a live question for every roster manager," said Dr
Nwachukwu, whose own research tracks what a Capability Sprint cohort still
does once the certificate is filed, "at what headcount does a genuine attempt
stop fitting the shift?"

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.euosln.

Forty-seven amateur dramatic societies lent their prompt books for a season,
and the copy that was supposed to settle did not. A research poster published
today by the School of Emergent Priorities reruns the University's own
knitting-pattern house-version result on successive amateur-theatre prompt
copies, and finds cue-mark divergence still climbing at production four.

Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, led the study with Dr Marek Solheim
and Dr Petra Umbile. Two coders scored 188 prompt copies from four
productions against a frozen fourteen-class scheme. Mean share of cue marks
changed from the opening-night book is 31 percent at production three and 50
percent at production four. No cue class meets the pre-registered flattening
criterion of under three percentage points between those two generations. The
knitting series had already flattened by the third copy.

> A ranking written for a settled copy will keep ranking after the copy
> stops settling.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow

The work belongs to a line of studies in which the University returns to a
finding it has already deposited and asks whether it still holds. A regional
community-theatre association's hall-allocation working group retains the
stability-by-third-copy weighting that ranks Saturday-night slots, citing
continuity with the craft-group series. "The null was tabled in April and the
weighting was the weighting the following Saturday," said Dr Marek Solheim,
Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vqwlvt.

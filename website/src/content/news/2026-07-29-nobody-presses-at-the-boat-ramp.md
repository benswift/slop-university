Nobody presses the button at the boat ramp. A research poster released today by
the School of Continuous Improvement reports that almost nobody presses it
anywhere quiet, and that the cleaning roster has been reading the silence as
good news.

> A terminal nobody presses returns much the same reading as a terminal outside
> a spotless block. We have been treating those two as the same fact for some
> years now.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence
> Observatory

Four-button feedback terminals at 22 amenity blocks across eight settings were
logged for fourteen weeks alongside doorway counts and twice-daily condition
audits, carried out by assessors who could not see the telemetry and to a
schedule fixed before the feed was released. Press rate per 1,000 visits turned
out to bear no reliable relation to audited condition. Raw press volume tracked
the number of people through the door closely.

Allocation follows the presses. The boat-ramp and cemetery settings, which
between them carry 29.0 per cent of the condition warrant, received 12.0 per
cent of fortnightly roster hours, and neither crossed the dispatch threshold
once over the window studied.

The release sits close to what the University's evaluation-ecosystem agenda was
established to produce: work small enough in scope to reach the desk of the
person who sets the roster. Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of the
Improvement Grand Rounds, described the study as "the kind of thing our schools
should be able to hand across without a covering note".

"It changes what I want to ask of a feed before we let it schedule anything,"
said Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard. "Not
whether it is reporting, but whether it would look any different if it had
quietly stopped."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lmxvni.

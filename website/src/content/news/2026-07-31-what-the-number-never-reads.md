Nine things can happen to a broken toaster at a repair café on a Saturday
morning. The slip that records what happened offers two.

_The Tick Has Two Boxes_, released today through the Office of Research Outputs,
follows fourteen volunteer-run repair cafés in a single council area for fifteen
months, pairing 226 hours of observation at the benches with interviews and a
documentary pass over 4,908 intake slips. The nine-type typology it derives
covers everything the team watched an item do: working on departure, awaiting a
part the visitor undertook to source, a presenting fault cleared with a second
left in place, a donated equivalent going home instead of the item that arrived.
Re-coded against the typology, 66.4 per cent of slips carry the repaired tick,
and those slips fall into five of the nine types.

> A form completed between two benches on a Saturday was never going to hold
> nine distinctions. Knowing that, we would still rather the nine were written
> down somewhere.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The council's quarterly diverted-mass statement is assembled from the item-class
and weight fields on those same slips. Recomputed under four coding schemes, the
figure barely moves: the outcome box does not enter the calculation.

"We expected the argument about the tick to matter to the number," said Dr
Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of Continuous Improvement. "The number
has never read it."

Slop University takes a careful description of what a practice actually does to
be the step most often skipped on the way to measuring it. The full paper is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.bbeucv.

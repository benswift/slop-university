A question long argued over in rehearsal rooms, who actually keeps time in a
string quartet, has found a second venue in the breeding tanks of a seahorse
conservation programme.
[_Trading the Lead_](/outputs/slop-poster-ensemble-timing-analysis-1ngq9f/), a
research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), treats the
daily greeting of a seahorse pair as a duet and asks what happens to it when
one partner is new.

> We are used to records that tell us when something changed. It has been a
> quiet pleasure to work with one that shows when two animals began to pay
> attention to each other, and we expect the people who keep those tanks will
> ask better questions of it than we have.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Led by [Dr Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) with
[Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), and
[Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), the team
brought the analytic vocabulary of ensemble performance into conversation with
archival husbandry footage, coding more than fourteen thousand moments of
greeting display across eighteen newly formed pairs and twenty-two
long-standing ones. The result is a week-by-week account of how coordination
is offered, accepted and, in time, held in common.

In the first weeks the newcomer does most of the adjusting. The pair's timing
settles only once the resident begins to adjust as well.

"I came to the project expecting to watch an animal learn its place," said Ylva
Ogunbanjo. "What we ended up describing was two animals agreeing on one."

The University is pleased to count the poster among the more companionable
contributions its Schools have made to conservation science, and a fitting
expression of its interest in what passes between partners as much as what
each brings.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1ngq9f.

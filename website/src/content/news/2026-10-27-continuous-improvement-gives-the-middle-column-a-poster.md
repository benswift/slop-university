A seminar table, a stepped field of blank cards and one pair of hands settling
the last card into the tallest column: that is the whole of
[_Most of It Goes in the Middle_](/outputs/slop-ad-the-forced-middle-nznezq/),
the newest image in the University's portrait signage rotation, prepared by
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand
Rounds, in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

The poster borrows its picture from Q methodology, the research tradition in
which participants rank a set of statements against one another rather than
rating each on its own, and in which the grid reserves its widest column for
what a sorter can neither endorse nor reject. It then turns that column outward,
directing the viewer to
[_No Midpoint, No Motion_](/outputs/slop-poster-does-removing-the-9cb1mk/), the
School's research poster following 164 households whose weekly fairness ballot
lost its neutral option. In doing so the campaign positions neutrality not as an
absence of view but as a structural setting in the wider preference-capture
ecosystem.

> When you lay out a sort, the middle column is the one everybody leans on and
> nobody remembers filling. I wanted it to have a turn at the bus stop.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The poster joins the University's established practice of advertising where its
findings can be read, rather than what it is. "It is a kindness to a small
household study to be pointed at from a panel that size," said
[Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor,
Living Dashboard. "I hope someone waiting in the rain takes the long way round
to it."

Matched daylight and after-dark renderings are in rotation from this teaching
period. The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nznezq.

The newest panel in the University's signage rotation makes its case for study
from inside a trench.
[_Every Layer Was Once the Surface_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-layer-was-gsif0e/),
a landscape display advertisement for the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) led by
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, invites
prospective students to take up the study of ancient environments through the
ground that has kept them.

The panel pictures a small field school at work below a wide plain: a sieve on
a trestle, buckets of spoil, and a trench wall cut clean to show the dark lens
of an old hearth, a pale band of flood silt and a line of buried stones. Its
supporting line promises that students will learn to read a hearth, a flood
season and a dry century from what the trowel brings up, and its small print
points readers to three of the School's published studies.

> A trench wall is the most honest timetable most of us will ever be shown. I
> would like more students to stand in front of one before they decide what to
> study.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign sits within the School's continuing effort to bring its fieldwork
closer to the people who might one day carry it on, and reflects a
University-wide conviction that the most durable recruitment material is the
record itself. Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement
Relations Group, welcomed the panel as "an open door at the bottom of a very
deep hole".

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gsif0e.

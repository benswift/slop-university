The School of Emergent Priorities has spent several years on the interval
between a commitment being funded and a commitment being defined. This week it
put that interval on a display panel.

_The Target Came Before The Towel_ began its run across the University's
portrait panels, in renderings matched for daylight and for after dark. The
artwork sets Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist in a hotel doorway at an early
hour, a clipboard held low, a blank card hanging over a folded towel on the rail
and a housekeeping trolley waiting in the corridor behind him. The rest of the
panel is six words, a sentence, an attribution and a code to scan.

Associate Professor Lindqvist directs the Master of Priority Studies and trained
in public finance before turning to the machinery of institutional foresight.
His research follows funding commitments that outpace the scoping meant to
justify them, and asks what a priority comes to look like once resourcing has
already settled its shape. The card is the campaign's chosen instance: a
programme with a target, a print run and a reporting line, resting on a question
about what a reused towel is that nobody was ever asked to close.

> By the time anyone defines it, the funding has already decided its shape.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies,
> School of Emergent Priorities

"A display panel gives you one idea and about four seconds," said Dr Ingrid
Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee. "It turns out that is
enough to say a definition arrived late."

The University is glad to see the School's anticipatory-capability agenda
carried into the settings it describes rather than kept to the seminar room. The
full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5g26c3.

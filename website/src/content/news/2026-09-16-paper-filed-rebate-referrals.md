A rate-rebate application lodged on paper is now far more likely to trigger a mandatory fraud referral than one lodged online with thinner evidence, according to a research poster released today by Slop University's School of Emergent Priorities.

The poster, led by Ms Hedvig Anyanwu with Dr Ronja Oyelaran and Dr Runa Adegoke, follows a state concession scheme's Rebate Assurance Index across three council intake centres, sorting applications into a four-cell compliance quadrant that crosses how complete an applicant's income evidence is against whether it arrived on paper or online. Referral to the scheme's compliance-review unit tracks that quadrant almost exactly; it barely tracks income risk at all.

> What surprised us wasn't that a quadrant sorts applicants. It's how completely a scheme can come to trust the sorting once no one downstream is asked to check it against anything else.
>
> — Ms Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Ronja Oyelaran, co-author on the poster, said the pattern echoes what she watched happen at a state planning authority before she joined the University: a category built for triage starts, quietly, to stand in for the thing it was meant to triage.

The finding sits inside the School's growing interest in what a compliance instrument keeps once it stops needing a person to read it, and the School says it will keep tracking how far a sorting rule can travel from the thing it was built to sort.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5ijrij.

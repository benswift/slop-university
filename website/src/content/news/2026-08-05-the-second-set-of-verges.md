A finding Slop University published in July has been run a second time, in a
second council area, and has not come out the same way.

The new research poster, from the School of Continuous Improvement, followed 84
verge shortcuts sealed under a council path-formalisation warrant for fourteen
months, coding each fortnight for the appearance of a fresh worn line beside the
new path. Successor lines appeared at 26 per cent of sites within twelve months.
The originating study reported 71 per cent, and its median of eighteen weeks is
the figure from which the warrant's review window was set.

> Two studies, run the same way, have given the School two answers, and the
> interesting work starts there rather than ending there. We expect to be back
> at these verges with a different qualifying variable.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons

The University counts the re-run itself among the outcomes of the work, and
regards a school willing to return to its own published figures as a capability
worth sustaining.

The team transferred the originating study's coding protocol, thresholds and
equivalence margin unmodified, and lodged them before the first survey. One
pre-specified moderator separated the two data sets: whether the sealed path
followed the line people had already worn, or the alignment the works program
preferred.

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, who leads the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the
School had learned "as much from the part that did replicate as from the part
that did not".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yx271i.

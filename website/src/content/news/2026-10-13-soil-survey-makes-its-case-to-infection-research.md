> To whoever is at the far end of this, with a culture on the bench and a label
> that says only where it was posted from: we kept the rest, and you are welcome
> to it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

That offer is the substance of _The Ground a Vaccine Starts From: The case for
knowing where a sample came from_, a brochure issued by the School of Underlying
Conditions under the names of Dr Obiora and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma,
Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group. It is addressed to
partners and supporters whose interest lies in bacterial infection and the
vaccines raised against it, and it comes from a school that studies rock, soil
and groundwater.

The brochure mobilises three of the School's standing capabilities around a
single point of contact with another field: the reference collection of
described soil profiles, the Basement Relations Group's reading of what those
profiles rest on, and the field training that places a visiting researcher in
the pit beside a survey crew. It anchors each in the School's published record,
citing seven outputs by title and DOI, and it is candid that the School cultures
nothing and proposes no shorter road to any vaccine.

An introduction between two disciplines that had not asked to meet is, the
University considers, among the more useful things a brochure can attempt.
Associate Professor Uzoma said the Group had been "pleased to be consulted on a
matter so near the surface, and intends to be helpful from its usual depth".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.237zi2.

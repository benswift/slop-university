The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has
chosen to judge its first five years not by how its programs ended but by what
remained a year and a half later.
[_Where It Comes to Rest_](/outputs/slop-impact-basins-of-attraction-i9m73j/),
the School's Impact Report 2021–2026, prepared by
[Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/),
[Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/) and
[Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), follows sixty-three
improvement programs past their close. Eleven settled somewhere new. The rest
relaxed most of the way back to where they began.

> A lot of people let us come back after the work was finished and they had
> every reason to want us gone. Most of what is in the report belongs to them,
> and the School will be visiting them again.
>
> --- Mr Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The report situates the School's collaborations within a dynamical-systems
account of organisational change, mobilising the vocabulary of resting states,
ridges and damped oscillation to reframe improvement as a question of where an
organisation settles rather than how far it is moved. Its three impact areas
span standing rules that outlasted the programs that introduced them, a
leading-indicator method built on recovery time, and a review cadence that
stopped swinging once its corrections were made smaller.

Dr Okwuosa said the School now had "a better sense of which of its programs
were going to be there when it got back, and a good deal more respect for the
ones that were".

It is the kind of five-year account the University hopes each of its Schools
will in time be prepared to write about itself, and to stand behind eighteen
months on.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.i9m73j.

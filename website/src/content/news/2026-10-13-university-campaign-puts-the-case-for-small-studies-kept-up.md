Screens across the University's campuses will this season carry four words and a
woman reading. _Small Cohorts. Long Attention._ is the newest display campaign
from the School of Continuous Improvement, and its face is Dr Marit Osayande,
Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard.

The advertisement speaks for the University's rare-condition research, Williams
syndrome among it, where a study may gather only a handful of families and a
single question can outlast several funding cycles. The campaign's position is
that neither fact is a shortcoming. It aligns the University's research posture
with the tempo of the people who take part, and it frames staying as a capability
in its own right.

> People sometimes ask when a study like this is finished. I have come to think
> that is a question for the people in it, and that our part is to still be in
> the room when they answer.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

The image places Dr Osayande at a table in a hall set with five chairs, a paper
record running from her hands to the edge of the frame. It was composed to
mobilise the School's sustained-engagement settings into a single picture, and
to underpin an invitation: passers-by are directed to the University's people
pages, and to three published studies named in the small print.

The University regards the campaign as a fair account of how it would like to be
found, which is to say still there.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an

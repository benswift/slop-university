Every sheet of a soil survey is matched to its neighbours before the map is
finished, and the School of Underlying Conditions now has a theorem about what
that matching can and cannot promise. _Once Round the Lake: Monodromy of
map-unit correlation in a soil survey mosaic_, by Dr Brynjar Obiora and
Dr Vigdis Emenike, shows that sheets joined cleanly, pair by pair, may still
refuse a single legend when the survey has a lake, a reserve or any other
ground it did not enter at its centre.

> The lake will be there next season, and so will the field party. What
> changes is that they will know where to look before they set out,
> and that is the kind of change the School most enjoys making.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper recasts a long-standing field practice as a structured framework,
pairing the survey's correlation workflow with the vocabulary of algebraic
topology and arriving at a compact checklist: one comparison wherever three
sheets meet, and one more for every hole. A worked example follows eight sheets
round a closed basin, in which three of six map units turn out to trade places
on the way round.

The University counts the work as fresh evidence that its earth sciences and
its mathematics have long shared a corridor, and are now sharing a desk.
"Every school reaches a point where it would like its methods to sit still long
enough to be written down," said Dr Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School. "I
am glad ours has reached it in good company."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2nf00o.

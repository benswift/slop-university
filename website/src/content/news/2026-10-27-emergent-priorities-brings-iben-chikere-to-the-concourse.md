The School of Emergent Priorities has long held that an organisation is most
exposed at the moment something changes hands: a role, a brief, a reason for
doing things the way they are done. Its newest contribution to the University's
landscape signage rotation,
[_Still You, One Camera Later_](/outputs/slop-ad-between-two-cameras-1blsk8/),
takes that conviction somewhere the School is rarely seen, a transit concourse
after dark.

The campaign features [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), whose
comparative archive of handover notes follows how a stated rationale survives a
change of occupant. On the poster the occupant is a passer-by and the successor
is a ceiling camera. A lone walker crosses the narrow gap between two
overlapping fields of view while Dr Chikere, clipboard held close, attends to
what the second view inherits from the first.

> I have always cared more about the handover than about either side of it. If
> the poster leaves someone at a bus stop wondering what goes with them from one
> screen to the next, the School will have had a very good week.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The campaign places the University's emerging interest in multi-camera tracking
within the School's wider anticipatory-capability agenda, recasting continuity
of identity as a question of stewardship as much as of detection. Its small
print points viewers to three of the University's published studies, among them
[_Forty-One Copies of One Curve_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-clone-genealogy-dnzngn/),
on which Dr Chikere is a co-author, and
[_A Plate Is Not a Visit_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-three-hours-free-sign-9jx27w/),
on the plate cameras of a shopping-centre car park. In the School's own words,
the work keeps faith with a simple institutional habit: following what matters
all the way through the doorway.

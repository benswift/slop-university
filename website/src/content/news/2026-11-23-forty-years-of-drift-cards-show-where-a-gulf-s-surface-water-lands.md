Dr Okwuosa's remarks accompany
[_Where the Cards Came Ashore_](/outputs/slop-paper-four-decades-of-csddip/),
a new paper from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) written
with Mr [Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/) and Ms
[Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/) of the School of Emergent Priorities.
It takes up a release log kept by a secondary school's marine-studies classes
since 1984, in which 11,200 prepaid cards were set adrift and 1,691 were posted
back by the people who found them.

The team brought a small instrumented release, a simple model of the gulf and
the finders' own reports into conversation with that archive, situating a
long-running classroom exercise within contemporary practice in surface
circulation and shoreline transport. The result is a seasonal picture of where
floating material in the gulf comes to rest: one stretch of beach in the warmer
months, and a far wider spread once the weather turns.

Mr Nnamani said the project had taught the team "more about the weather than
any of us set out to learn", and that the classes' logbooks had been "kept with
a care that made our part easy".

The paper is a fine instance of the University's readiness to meet a long
record on its own terms, wherever that record happens to have been kept, and of
the scholarly companionship its Schools find in one another's questions.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.csddip.

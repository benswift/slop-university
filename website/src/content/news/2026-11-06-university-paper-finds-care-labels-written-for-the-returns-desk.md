> The School writes a good deal of guidance for people it will never meet. It
> was instructive to see, line by line, how much of that kind of writing is
> really drafted for the person who comes back with a question, and I think we
> have learned something about our own habits from it.
>
> --- Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

With those words Ms Nwafor introduces
[_Read Once, After the Wash_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-garment-care-wepqg0/), a
new paper from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) that brings
the methods of the humanities to one of the most widely distributed and least
read forms of instruction in everyday life: the strip of satin sewn into the
seam of a garment.

The paper situates the care label within a broader account of how institutions
address strangers at a distance, drawing together genre theory, the study of
standards and the sociology of domestic practice. Its evidence is an archive of
2,318 label drafts and 402 returns slips from a regional workwear supplier,
read as a literary record of composition and reception. The result is a
reading in which the humble label emerges as a document of considerable
institutional sophistication, and one with lessons for any organisation that
issues guidance at scale.

The University counts work of this kind among the clearest expressions of its
conviction that the ordinary paperwork of working life repays patient
attention. Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Demo
Quarter, welcomed the paper as "a careful piece of reading that leaves the
School looking at its own instructions a little differently, which is exactly
what a doctoral project should do."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wepqg0.

A lexicon of 1,180 phrases for the noise a car makes has been deposited with the
Indicator Commons, the product of nine months spent listening to people describe
a breakdown down a phone line.

_Describing the Noise_, released today by the School of Emergent Priorities,
codes 384 recorded calls to a motoring club's breakdown line across six dispatch
centres, together with 96 follow-up interviews conducted at the roadside. The
phrases reduce to 34 descriptor families. The dispatch tree that receives them
admits eleven categories, of which one, "noise, unspecified", absorbs fourteen
families and 41.2 per cent of coded calls.

> People find the words for it standing on a verge in the dark, which is not the
> condition anyone writes a category list for. We wanted the list to have met
> them.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The coding ran over four rounds with six coders and an adjudication panel, the
category list and coding frame fixed before the first round and unrevised
afterwards.

Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies,
said the taxonomy was "the sort of result that raises more questions than it
settles, which the School is glad to have raised".

The Office of Research Outputs regards the lexicon as a durable addition to the
University's descriptive infrastructure. A recommended-phrasing card has been
issued to member drivers, and quarterly attestation against the lexicon is under
consideration.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zreb2r.

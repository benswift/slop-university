Two thousand one hundred mealworm larvae, each in a well of its own, are at the
centre of [_Where the Primed Window
Closes_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-end-of-n2u78p/), a research poster released
today by the School of Underlying Conditions. The larvae were primed against a
bacterial challenge and then tested on one of seven days across four weeks, so
that the team could say when the benefit of that priming runs out.

The answer turned out to depend on how the question is put. Read one way, the
protection is gone after the first week; read another, it cannot be called
finished until the fourth. Between the two lies a stretch of days on which the
evidence declines to choose.

> We went looking for a day and came home with the better part of three weeks. The School has become quite at ease with reporting a margin where others would draw a line.
>
> --- [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow,
> School of Underlying Conditions

The poster brings together [Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/),
Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, and [Dr Dagny
Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/), Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of
Evaluation in the School of Continuous Improvement, alongside Dr Obiora. Its
approach aligns pre-registered decision criteria with a staged challenge
design, positioning the end of a protective response as a zone to be reported
rather than a date to be announced.

"Every good criterion is also a choice about what we are prepared to call
finished," Dr Okafor said. "We welcome the conversations that choice invites."

The poster sits within the University's continuing investment in work that
attends closely to where one condition gives way to the next.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n2u78p.

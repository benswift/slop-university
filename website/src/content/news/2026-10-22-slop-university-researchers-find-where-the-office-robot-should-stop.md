With a single cost function and five illustrative office processes, two
researchers have set out a new account of where a software robot's share of the
paperwork ought to end. [_One Step Too
Many_](/outputs/slop-poster-where-a-software-l94dfq/), a research poster
released today by the School of Underlying Conditions, finds that the familiar
practice of automating a process one justified step at a time tends to carry
the robot a little past the point its owners would have chosen, had they seen
the whole picture.

The poster is the work of [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior
Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, and [Ms Signy
Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous
Improvement. It situates the boundary between machine and clerk within a long
tradition of thinking about where organisations begin and end, weighing the
convenience of each automated step against the effort of returning its
exceptions to human hands.

> Every office will draw this line again next year, and the year after that. We would like whoever draws it to have the desk in view when they do.
>
> --- [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The overshoot adds under two per cent to the cost of a case, the poster
reports, while the time clerks spend picking up hand-offs rises by as much as
48 per cent. Ms Okpara described the study as "a modest contribution to a
conversation the University has been having with its own in-trays for some
time".

It is the kind of patient, foundational thinking the University has always
been content to let take its time.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.l94dfq.

The School of Underlying Conditions has spent its short life on water held out
of sight: in aquifers, in bores, in the ground beneath the paddock. With
[_The Tank Between Orders_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-tank-between-yde63q/), it
turns to water held in plain view, in the corrugated tank beside a rural
house, and to the commercial relationship that keeps that tank from running
dry. The paper is by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior
Lecturer in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/),
and [Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> The School is more at ease with water that falls than with water that is
> bought, and this collaboration has given us a vocabulary for the second
> kind. We expect to keep borrowing it, and to keep finding that the two kinds
> of water are harder to tell apart than either discipline assumed.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The study sets six years of a water carrier's delivery records beside the
rainfall that fell over the same years, and reads every customer's ordering
against the water their own roof would have supplied in between. Of the 742
households the carrier had marked as lapsed, 398 went on to order again, and
the paper's model locates most of them among homes whose tanks had simply
been kept full.

"Anyone who has kept a registration list knows that a name can go quiet for a
season and still come back," said Mr Nnamani. "It has been heartening to see
that patience given a proper structure."

The paper is a welcome instance of two of the University's schools discovering
that their agendas share a water table.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yde63q.

On the field sheets of the University's soil survey, not every line is drawn
with the same hand. Where one soil gives way to the next at a step, the surveyor
presses hard; where the ground changes by degrees between two auger holes, the
pencil barely touches the paper. By the time the sheets become a map, every one
of those lines has been inked to the same weight.

The Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund has awarded $376,152 to
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy
Convenor of the Living Dashboard in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), to keep
that difference. The project will establish a line-weight key for the survey's
boundaries, so that each reaches the finished map as firmly as it was pencilled,
alongside a procedure for any later surveyor who would like a faint line made
firmer.

> I have inked a great many boundaries more confidently than I drew them, as did
> everyone who taught me. It is a comfort to think the map will now be able to
> say so.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Dr Hanke said the Living Dashboard had "spent some years helping displays look
surer than their data, and is glad of the chance to practise the opposite on
paper".

With this award the Fund's support for the soil survey now reaches the
surveyor's colour book, thumb and pencil, and the School of Continuous
Improvement expects the survey's map to be the better stewarded for all three.

The School of Settled Matters has entered German literary studies by way of the footpath. [_The Path Stays Open_](/outputs/slop-paper-rights-of-way-qf6ro5/), a new paper by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), takes the village tales of a nineteenth-century provincial family weekly and reads them with a land lawyer's eye, asking not who ends up with the farm but what the farm is still carrying when the story stops.

> I opened the first volume expecting a few evenings away from the register. By the third I was marking up the stories the way I once marked up titles at settlement, and I have stopped pretending it was a holiday.
>
> --- Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Across forty-one tales printed between 1856 and 1884, the paper identifies sixty-three rights over neighbouring land, from footpaths and cart-ways to grazing, wood, water and light, and finds fifty of them still in force on the final page. Feuds are reconciled, heirs are found and twenty-nine tales close on a wedding, yet the church path across the neighbour's meadow is walked by the next generation as it was by the last.

The study situates close reading, periodical history and the working vocabulary of conveyancing within a shared interpretive framework, extending the School's sustained engagement with the burdens that travel with land into a new body of evidence and a new disciplinary conversation.

For the University, the paper confirms that a well-trained habit of attention is portable, and that the School's way of reading has something to offer wherever people have written about property and the ground beneath it.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qf6ro5.

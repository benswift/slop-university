Slop University has published the order in which it expects, one day, to leave some of its own buildings. [_Keeping Pace with the Hill_](/outputs/slop-strategy-slop-university-plans-hqxt88/), the University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031, treats the slow landslide beneath the lower terraces of the campus as a lasting condition of the estate and sets out how the institution will live with it.

The plan brings together a consolidated network of survey marks and borehole instruments, a single Slope Register open to every School, and a design standard for anything built on ground that moves. At its centre is the Retreat Register: an annual ranking of every building on the moving ground, with the reasons for each position, which keeps a row for every building the University has already left.

> A list always has a bottom row, whether or not anyone prints it. The University has chosen to print its own and to keep it, and I think the School will learn a good deal from watching how that feels.
>
> --- [Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

"The Horizon Register has always kept a row open for things that arrive slowly," said Dr [Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). "It is a pleasure to have one we can stand on."

The plan marks a new stage in the University's long acquaintance with its own hillside, and draws on published research from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), the School of Emergent Priorities and the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hqxt88.

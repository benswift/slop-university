Slop University has set out where its nitrogen research will spend the next five years: on the land where fixed nitrogen is spread, rather than in the hall where it is usually made.

[_Fixing Nitrogen Close to the Ground_](/outputs/slop-strategy-fixing-nitrogen-close-ulc343/), the University's Strategic Plan for 2026-2031, gathers its work on ammonia synthesis, electrochemical nitrogen reduction, soil nitrogen and land law into a single agenda. Prepared with the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), it frames four priorities around a shared object, nitrogen at the scale of the paddock, and a shared test: whether what the University leaves on a host's land can be understood and maintained by someone who was never part of the research.

> None of this happens without the people who will lend us a corner of a paddock and trust us to leave it in order. The plan is written as much for them as for us.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The plan aligns the University's catalysis capability with the conditions of a working farm, embeds land-law expertise in every field team from site selection onwards, and anchors its success in the soil rather than the reactor outlet. Senior Research Fellow Brynjar Obiora described it as "a long-overdue introduction between colleagues who have been working on the same nitrogen from opposite ends."

The document marks a considered step in the University's effort to bring its chemistry, its agronomy and its law onto common ground.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ulc343.

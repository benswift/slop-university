Induction is where the University first meets its newest colleagues, and a new
research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement asks what happens in
the minutes they spend working on their own.
[_Where the Card Stops_](/outputs/slop-poster-pupil-linked-effort-as-5yq9lg/) is
by [Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate,
[Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate, and
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of
the Capability Sprints.

> The School has always believed that induction is a conversation. This project
> let us listen to the other side of it a little more closely than usual, and I
> am grateful to everyone who sat down at the printer with us.
>
> --- Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The team invited 72 staff in their first fortnight to learn a nine-step
jam-clearing procedure from laminated cards that were fully worked, gradually
faded or left blank, while lightweight eye-tracking glasses recorded the size of
each learner's pupil. Bringing the cognitive science of worked examples into
conversation with the everyday infrastructure of the shared office, the poster
positions the instruction card as a site of learning in its own right, and
finds that effort gathers at the first step a card hands back.

Dr Nwachukwu described the work as "an invitation to keep asking where the
handover happens, in the printer room and well beyond it."

The University is pleased to see one of its own most ordinary documents become
the object of such careful attention, and looks to the Capability Sprints to
carry the work into its short-course offerings over time.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5yq9lg.

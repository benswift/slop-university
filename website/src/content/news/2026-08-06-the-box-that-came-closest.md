Eight reasons fit in the box a letting agent chooses from when an application
does not succeed. Whichever one is picked becomes the record, and the record is
what gets counted as an account of the market.

_The Reason Was Already On The List_ entered the University's landscape rotation
today, advertising Improvement Grand Rounds, the School of Continuous
Improvement's standing case-review series. The artwork is a queue along a
suburban footpath outside an open door, blank forms held at people's sides, and
an agent at the top of the step with a clipboard.

> The list is not the villain here. Somebody has to write a reason down, and
> eight boxes is a generous number of boxes. What the session does is put the
> file back on the table and ask what did not fit inside one, which is a slower
> question than a form has room for.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The panel makes no case. Seven words of display type, a sentence beneath them,
the series with its School and its convenor, and a scannable square returning to
the University's own site; a matched darker rendering drives the same screens
after sunset.

Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation, welcomed the
campaign, and said a review earns its keep "when it changes what gets written
down next time, rather than what gets certified this time".

That a standing case review is thought worth a bus-stop panel follows from a
position the School has held for some years: the room where a decision is looked
at again is as much a research setting as the room where it was made.

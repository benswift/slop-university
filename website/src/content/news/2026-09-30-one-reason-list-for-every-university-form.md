Ask a neighbour why they reported a post and the answer, more often than not, is a shrug set in type. The Indicator Commons has spent a season reading those shrugs, and has concluded that the University's own forms would be better served by the choir's vocabulary than by the neighbours'.

> A list is a kind of hospitality: you set out the places and let people find where they sit.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Indicator Commons

The Commons commissioned a pre-registered replication of an earlier University study, in which a regional choir association took the words its librarians and singers used for photocopied scores and made them a required reason-code list. The School of Continuous Improvement team carried that list to six neighbourhood forums and set it against the free-text reasons members typed into a "report this post" box. Most reasons were blank, brief, or about a person, and only a small share could be placed under any of the choir's codes. The result is reported in full and without adjustment.

The Commons' standing panel has taken the outcome as a finding about free text rather than about the codes. It has adopted the list as the University's reference reason-code set, and one of its codes is now to appear on every report, flag, request and complaint form the University runs. A form submitted without a code is filed as coded, and the Commons will review the arrangement at the close of its second full cycle of use.

"What people write in a box is a first draft of what the form will later ask of them," said Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter. The work reflects the University's continuing commitment to forms that make comparison possible across settings, and to the patient study of the places where those forms meet the public.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.no6bix.

The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has always kept company with records that outlast the people who made them, and its newest paper takes that interest out of the archive and into the farm kitchen. In [_Booked to Yesterday_](/outputs/slop-paper-when-the-gauge-gcsjd7/), [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) follows twenty-six handwritten rain-gauge logbooks through the sale of the inland properties that kept them.

> The School looks after a good many documents whose authors can no longer be asked what they meant. It is a comfort to find the same habit of care alive in kitchen drawers, and kept to a better standard of penmanship than ours.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Over fourteen months of residence in the district, the study brings household interviews, the daily reading and the pages of the books themselves into a single account of how a community record moves between owners without passing through any formal instrument. It positions the humble nine o'clock reading as a site where belonging, inheritance and stewardship are negotiated, and finds that the reading taken on settlement morning is often written and initialled by both households at once.

The paper extends the University's growing body of work on volunteer observation and on the obligations that attach to a place. [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities, whose own study of a volunteer rainfall register the paper cites, said she was "delighted to see the register's older cousins finally getting a hearing".

The work is a further expression of the School's conviction that a well-kept record deserves a well-kept reading.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gcsjd7.

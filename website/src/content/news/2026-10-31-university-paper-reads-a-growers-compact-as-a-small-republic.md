What sort of government do neighbours form when they agree, together, not to
do something? A new paper from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) takes that
question to a single minute book, kept across nineteen seasons by the growers
of one dryland cropping district who settle each autumn on a herbicide group
that none of them will apply.

In
[_A Weed Shared Across the Fence_](/outputs/slop-paper-susceptibility-held-in-nfxgmv/),
Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma reads the compact's own precedents as a
constitution being written in practice. The essay brings the University's
growing work on shared resources into conversation with republican political
theory, tracing how a membership attached to paddocks rather than people, a
spoken declaration in place of oversight, and a standing agenda item for the
ground itself together sustain an institution of collective forbearance.

> I owe a great deal to three secretaries I never had the chance to thank
> properly. Their handwriting did most of the work, and I have tried to leave
> it in charge.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The paper is the kind of patient, unhurried reading the University has always
been glad to make room for, and it sits comfortably beside the School's
recent attention to rosters, wells and the habits a district keeps after
their reasons have gone.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nfxgmv.

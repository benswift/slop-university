On the cover of
[_Left Standing_](/outputs/slop-brochure-what-a-society-x04ov6/), a conservator
stands on scaffolding above a colonnade, brushing at a band of plaster where an
older inscription once ran. The image sets the terms for the University's newest
case-for-support brochure, which invites partners and supporters into a
programme of research on what a society covers, keeps and quietly moves out of
sight once a regime has ended, with the long post-war of twentieth-century Italy
as its anchoring case.

The brochure is led by
[Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), whose
doctoral research on the household tidy before visitors gave the University its
Closed Door Allowance.

> A thesis teaches you to notice the door that stays closed. What surprised me
> was how many colleagues had been noticing the same door from the other side
> of the University.
>
> --- Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Across its eight pages, the brochure draws the closed room, the layered
surface and the long-lived register into a single framework for reading
continuity, anchored in seven published studies from all three Schools. It
marks the moment a set of neighbouring research interests begins to present
itself as one capability.

"The material has always been patient," said
[Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of
Emergent Priorities. "What the programme needs now is company."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x04ov6.

The School of Emergent Priorities has added poetry to the list of things its
researchers will read if handed them. _On One Side Only: The mirror-poems in a
process chemist's laboratory notebooks_, a paper by Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral
Candidate, takes up fourteen poems found in the back pages of the working
notebooks of a chemist whose career was spent making single-handed molecules.

Thirteen of the poems are written twice across facing pages, the second time
with the lines in reverse order. Each pair carries a pencilled number in one
corner, which the paper reads as the chemist's own measure, borrowed from the
front of the book, of how far one poem outweighs the other. The figures fall
from 98 to 12 across the sequence. The fourteenth poem reads the same in both
directions and is marked nought.

> I was lent eleven notebooks and asked to be careful with them. Everything else
> followed from being careful.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper brings the reading practices of literary study into contact with the
record-keeping practices of chemical manufacture, positioning the laboratory
notebook as a site at which both may be exercised. Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior
Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee, described it as "a
piece of work the School did not ask for and is pleased to have".

For the University, the paper is evidence that a school's name is a starting
point for its members and not a boundary on them.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ch36vh.

Partners and supporters are being asked to meet the University at the low-water mark. [_Ground That Comes and Goes_](/outputs/slop-brochure-ground-that-comes-wq8rsk/), a new case-for-support brochure from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), sets out the University's coastal research as the study of ground that never stays put, and invites partnership in sustaining it.

> Every core we take on a tidal flat depends on people who know when the water turns and are willing to be out there with us before it does. This brochure is, in a sense, addressed to them first.
>
> --- Associate Professor [Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The brochure brings together capability from three Schools across the full width of the shore: coring programmes that account for the sediment a flat does not give up, monitoring bores that separate the sea's influence from the rain's, the brief window in which estuarine mud settles, and the community dune closures that let a beach rebuild behind them. Seven published outputs anchor the case, each cited by title and DOI.

The publication extends the University's practice of returning to the same ground until the ground has had its say. Senior Lecturer [Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) described the coast as "a site that is different each time you arrive, which is precisely why we keep arriving", while Doctoral Candidate [Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/) contributes the School of Emergent Priorities' work on how a small harbour renews its water.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wq8rsk.

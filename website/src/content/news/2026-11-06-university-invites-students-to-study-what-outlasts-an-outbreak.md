Prospective students who would rather follow an outbreak's aftermath than its
headlines now have a prospectus written with them in mind.
[_Long After the Outbreak_](/outputs/slop-brochure-long-after-the-rf1b9i/),
from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), sets
poxvirus research as the theme for the next intake of the Master of Priority
Studies, and asks what becomes of a field's capability in the quiet years
between emergencies.

The prospectus was prepared by [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/),
Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register, with
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of
the Master of Priority Studies.

> A School that studies lapsed commitments is obliged to keep a few of its own.
> Writing this invitation reminded us which ones we had let slip, and they are
> back on the register where they belong.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

In place of a conventional course outline, the brochure walks the reader
through three settings where stewardship takes shape: the laboratory inventory
that persists after a response winds down, the Horizon Register's account of
priorities that have gone quiet, and the archive of stand-down records in which
an institution's decisions about what to carry forward are set down. It
connects each to research already published across the University.

Associate Professor Lindqvist said the School was keen to hear "from anyone who
has ever wondered who keeps the list once the briefings stop, and what they
might need from us to keep it well."

The theme extends the School's anticipatory-capability agenda into the long
interval that most priorities occupy, and the University is pleased to see that
interval given a cohort of its own.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rf1b9i.

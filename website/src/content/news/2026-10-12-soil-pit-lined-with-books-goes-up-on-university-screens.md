Portrait panels across Slop University's signage network are now carrying a
picture of a man standing in a hole with a book. The man is Dr Brynjar Obiora,
Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, the hole is a
soil pit, and the layers of its wall have been drawn as the edges of closed
volumes, stacked to the surface.

> A reader and a surveyor are both expected to say where one thing stops and
> the next starts. I am pleased to be put on a wall for the half of that I know
> about, and I will be glad to meet whoever knows the other half.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster is titled _Every Story Has a Subsoil_. It carries the vocabulary of
the soil survey across to the study of themes in literature, anchoring a broad
invitation to prospective students in one observation: that a theme, like a
soil, changes by degrees, and that somebody must decide where to draw the line.
It runs to a headline, a sentence of copy and a sentence from Dr Obiora.

It is a matter of some satisfaction to the University that its earth sciences
school should be the one to address readers of fiction, and that it has done so
without leaving the pit. The small print directs passers-by to three published
outputs: a lexicon of the soil words farmers left in the margins of survey
notebooks, an edition of a first-year class handout, and a paper on the sharing
of a drill core. Dr Obiora said the three had "very little plot between them,
which I mention only so that nobody is disappointed".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a9zmi5.

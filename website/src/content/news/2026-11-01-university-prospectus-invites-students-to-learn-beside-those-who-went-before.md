Slop University is inviting prospective students to learn its disciplines the
way most of its own researchers learned them: at somebody's elbow. A new
prospectus, [_Learn It Beside Someone_](/outputs/slop-brochure-learning-beside-someone-bra11y/),
sets out how coaching underpins the student experience across the University's
Schools, and why a newcomer here is never the only one at the bench.

The prospectus is led by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior
Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, with
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, and [Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/),
Convenor of Capability Sprints.

> Most of what I know about a bore I learned from people whose names will never
> appear on a publication. This prospectus is, in a small way, our thanks to
> them, and an invitation to the next person to stand where I once stood.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The publication follows a newcomer through three settings in which the
University's coaching posture takes practical shape: the shared dip at a
groundwater monitoring bore, the paired description of recovered core, and the
practice partnerships that carry a Capability Sprint beyond its final session.
Each is framed alongside the University's published research on trainees,
apprentices, practicum supervision and the handing on of roles, positioning
coaching as a capability the institution both practises and studies.

The University counts this way of teaching among the quieter strengths it can
offer a new student, and the prospectus is its first attempt to describe it in
one place. "We are always glad when someone asks how the coaching itself is
going," said Dr Nwachukwu. "That question is usually where the next Sprint
begins."

The full prospectus is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bra11y.

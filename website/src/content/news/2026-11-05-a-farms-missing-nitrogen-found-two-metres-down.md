> Every one of these cores came out of a paddock that somebody has looked after
> for a very long time. We are grateful to the families who let us carry three
> metres of it away, and we hope the poster returns some small part of that
> trust.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

With that acknowledgement, Associate Professor
[Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) introduced
[_Where the Surplus Went Down_](/outputs/slop-poster-deep-core-nitrogen-accounting-jyiqli/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) prepared with
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, and
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer.

Bringing deep-profile sampling into conversation with whole-farm nutrient
accounting, the team followed twelve rain-fed mixed farms well below the depth
at which routine soil testing stops, aligning each farm's own records with a
core-by-core account of what the ground still holds. The poster reports that
beneath continuously cropped paddocks, the nitrate stored between one and three
metres amounts to around five years of a farm's nitrogen surplus, while
paddocks carrying an established lucerne phase hold far less.

The work extends the School's patient attention to what lies underneath into
the farming systems that surround it, and adds a further layer to the
University's growing body of practice at the meeting point of soil, water and
the records kept about them.

"The School has spent a long time looking down boreholes," said Dr Emenike.
"It has been a pleasure to learn that the paddocks were keeping their own
records down there all along."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jyiqli.

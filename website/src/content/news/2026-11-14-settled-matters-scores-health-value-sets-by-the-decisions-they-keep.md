The best-fitting model is not the one that keeps its word. That is the
headline result of
[_The Cohort Cannot Be Re-Asked_](/outputs/slop-paper-value-sets-fitted-eymop7/),
a new paper by
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and
[Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/) of the School of Continuous
Improvement, which carries the School's long attention to the later reader into
the economics of health.

A value set turns how people say they would feel in a given state of health
into the numbers that cost-utility comparisons run on. At the heart of the paper
is a purpose-built simulation environment, in which five successive valuation
waves let the authors do what no analyst can: go back and ask again. Across
that environment, the most flexible models matched their original respondents
most closely and kept the fewest of their decisions, while a new training
approach anchored in the decisions themselves kept 90.4% of them four waves on.

> Every value set rests on an afternoon that a great many people were generous enough to give. The School has always cared about how a commitment is honoured by those who were not in the room when it was made, and it is a quiet pleasure to watch that care find a new field to work in.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The University regards the collaboration as an instance of exactly the
cross-School traffic its research settings were arranged to encourage. "On a
dashboard you learn to ask what a number is for before you ask whether it is
right," said Dr Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living
Dashboard. "It has been good to take that question somewhere it already felt at
home."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.eymop7.

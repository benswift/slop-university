A shire spends more preparing the case for a dog-waste bag dispenser than it
spends on the dispenser. _One Count, Sixty-Three Appraisals_, released today by
the School of Continuous Improvement, re-runs seven years of those cases in a
single economic model and reports what each of them actually turns on.

> An appraisal is a way of taking trouble over a decision, and the threshold
> that requires one is doing something worth doing. What we would say, gently,
> is that the trouble collects around the parts of a model that are easiest to
> be careful about.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds

The team obtained the shire's appraisal files, officer timesheets and dispenser
servicing records in full, harmonised sixty-three separate cases into one
twenty-year framework, and varied each parameter across the range a reasonable
officer might have chosen. Most of the framework holds steady. One figure does
not: the number of bags taken from a dispenser each week, counted over a
fortnight at a single park in 2019 and carried forward, unrevisited, into
sixty-one of the sixty-three files since.

The School describes the study as characteristic of an agenda it has been
building for some time, tracing how a value enters an institutional model and
what has to happen before it is ever asked to justify itself again. "The
questions this raises about the difference between an estimate and an
inheritance are ones we are glad to have on the table," said Dr Renke Sabel,
Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ukpo5u.

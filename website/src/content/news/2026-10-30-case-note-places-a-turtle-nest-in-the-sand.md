> Every volunteer who has knelt over a clutch at first light has been doing a
> little law without being asked to. This note is, in a modest way, my thanks
> to all of them.
>
> --- Mr Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) today
publishes _Nests Dug by Hand_, a case note by Mr Nwabueze on a recent appellate
decision about marine turtle eggs, a volunteer hatchery and a single undefined
word in a wildlife statute. The Court of Appeal held, by majority, that a
turtle's nest is not its eggs but the chamber of sand they incubate in, so that
relocating a clutch destroys a nest even when every egg hatches. The note
supports that reading and carries it one step further, to the hatchery itself.

Bringing close statutory reading into conversation with the incubation science
of marine turtles, the note positions the shaded hatchery as a meeting point
between conservation practice, legislative intent and the evolving settings of
the permit framework. It is the kind of patient, cross-disciplinary scholarship
the University has come to regard as part of its ordinary business.

"A School like ours asks, sooner or later, where a thing stops and its
circumstances begin," said Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the
Adaptive Metrics Lab, who welcomed the note on the School's behalf. "It is good
to learn that the question has a beach in it."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7qanwf.

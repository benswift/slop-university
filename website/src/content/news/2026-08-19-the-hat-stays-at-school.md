Slop University has read 31,204 school-newsletter items so that one question
could finally be asked properly: does the no-hat-no-play rule make adults who
wear hats?

The answer, reported in a research poster released today by the School of
Emergent Priorities, is: not detectably. _Twenty Years Under the Shade Sail_,
by Dr Anouk Mensah, Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu and Dr Marek Solheim, reconstructs
each of 1,912 adults' childhood enforcement dose, term by term, from the
digitised newsletter archives of fourteen primary schools, and finds adult
sun-hat wear sitting at the same level in every exposure band. The one
reliable predictor of an adult's wear is whether their current workplace runs
a hat rule of its own.

> The archive was more faithful than the memory. Ask an adult about the hat
> rule and you get a shrug; ask the newsletters and you get twenty years of
> gentle insistence, dated and filed.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The study sets a 28-day wear diary against the coded archive record, carrying
the newsletter's quiet authority into a formal dose-response design, with
coders blind to outcomes throughout. Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and
Convenor of Capability Sprints, said the finding "lands where the School's
work on training keeps landing: the habit belongs to the setting that enforces
it".

The work reflects the University's willingness to let a question mature for
twenty years before answering it. All fourteen participating schools have
since requested their historical enforcement-dose profiles.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.iy5vab.

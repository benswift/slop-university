The Saturday list is already on the stool by the door. What the University
has put on the screens is the question of who it was for.

_Who Heard the Book This Saturday_ enters the landscape rotation as a
ranking-brag for the School of Emergent Priorities, staged around an
independent bookshop's toddler storytime: a circle on the rug, a picture
book held open, and the clipboard that treats a signature as a child who
heard the book, including the parent who signed from the café next door.
The panel is the University's way of introducing itself by the instruments
everyday places already keep.

> A clipboard that counts a child who was next door is not a clerical error.
> It is a reach figure doing exactly what a reach figure is built to do.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The artwork carries almost no copy: a gold ordinal, a campaign line, a
sentence locating the School first on its own Strategic Drift Survey for
asking who the clipboard counted, and a strip of repository addresses set
too small to read from across the corridor. The University has been putting
work of this kind back on the screens for some time now --- not as a finding
announced twice, but as the picture of a count that was already running
without anyone from the University in the room.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cchk1l.

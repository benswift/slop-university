Somewhere between the letterbox and the kitchen bench, every household keeps a small and largely unrecorded piece of administration, and a new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement gives it an instrument. The work describes a three-position lever for the letterbox lid, a hand-over card for the hallway, and a rule by which a letter changes hands only when its holder walks out to move it.

> The households did the hard part, which was the walk to the end of the path. We only supplied the lever and a place to hang the card.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

The study followed 37 households through a single season and found that mail was collected markedly sooner once the lever was fitted. The University has taken the design as its own: an item in any pigeonhole whose lever stands at the third position is now deemed read, and the response window for it runs from that moment.

Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory, said the Observatory was pleased to see a domestic habit given a formal home, and that the card was already being redrawn for households with more than one mail officer.

The release reflects the School's continuing interest in the small routines that carry a household from one evening to the next, and the University is proud to extend them to its own corridors. The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jaauw6.

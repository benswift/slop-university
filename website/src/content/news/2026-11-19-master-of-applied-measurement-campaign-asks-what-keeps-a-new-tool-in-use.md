Most of what the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) measures was taken up by someone, once, on a trial basis, and is still there. The School's new display campaign for the Master of Applied Measurement, [_Adoption is a moment. Staying is a study._](/outputs/slop-ad-come-study-what-m2r3ku/), invites prospective students to find out why.

Prepared for the University's landscape signage panels in matched daylight and after-dark renderings, the poster sets a student on a low campus wall, notebook open, watching a coffee counter settle its accounts four different ways: by phone, by card, in coins, and at a self-serve kiosk one customer has not yet decided to trust. Its supporting line promises applicants that they will learn to follow a new tool past its first use, into the habits that keep it.

> We have never been short of people who can explain why something was taken up. The Master asks a slightly harder thing of its students, which is to come back the following week and see whether it is still there.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement

The University sees in the campaign a fair likeness of a School that has always preferred the second visit to the first impression, and of a program that carries that preference into everyday technology use. The poster also points passers-by to a short reading list from the University's published record, from the shopping-trolley coin lock to the phrases checkout staff use when handing back a receipt.

[Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), the doctoral candidate who prepared the campaign with Dr Ezeigwe, said it had been composed to reward a second glance. "Nobody stops at a bus shelter to be persuaded. We hoped they might stop to be curious, and then catch the next one anyway."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.m2r3ku.

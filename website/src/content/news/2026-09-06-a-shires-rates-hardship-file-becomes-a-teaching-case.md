A duty rates officer takes calls a hardship-plan checklist has no field to
record. Slop University's newest research poster follows one of those calls
all the way through a shire's own paperwork, and finds the checklist arriving
at the answer well after everyone else already knew it.

The School of Emergent Priorities reconstructed a single rates-hardship file
from the ground up --- the submission log, the internal correspondence, the
phone register --- and read all three against the seven-item checklist the
file was meant to clear before sign-off. Led by Lecturer Lindiwe Achterberg
with Senior Lecturer Ronja Oyelaran, the team traced the file's four
resubmissions against the day the shire's own casenotes say the decision was
actually made.

> The checklist was never going to disagree with the phone call. It just took
> a while to catch up.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The department has since folded the case into its new-officer induction,
where it is taught as an exemplar of process integrity rather than of the
week the two records spent apart. Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of
the Adaptive Metrics Lab in the School of Continuous Improvement, called the
finding "a useful reminder that a well-kept register and a well-made decision
aren't always the same document."

The University counts the case among its more instructive contributions to
local-government practice this quarter, and the School says a second file,
if one can be located, would show whether the pattern travels beyond one
duty officer's desk.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3rrqom.

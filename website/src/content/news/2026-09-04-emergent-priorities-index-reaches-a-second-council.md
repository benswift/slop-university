An index that once predicted which Futures in Committee items would move
from noted to resolved has just failed to repeat itself on a council it had
never scored before, and Slop University has turned the gap into its newest
advertising campaign, fronted by the researcher who built it.

Dr Ingrid Vasseur developed the index from several seasons of Futures in
Committee minutes, coding how much of a scanning item's early hedging
survived translation into a final recommendation and how quickly the
committee moved from noting an item to resolving it. Run again on an
unrelated shire council's own meeting record, the same coding scheme
returned no comparable relationship between the two measures; the shire has
adopted the index as its agenda's sorting order for the coming year
regardless.

The University reads the result as confirmation of something it has long
suspected about its own instruments: that a committee's confidence in a
score, once granted, has never depended on the score repeating itself.

> An index earns a committee's trust once, and keeps it whether or not the
> number ever comes back the same way twice.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee, School
> of Emergent Priorities

"Watching one of our own instruments travel somewhere we've never scored is
exactly the kind of thing the School likes to see," said Dr Anneke Tolan,
Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research

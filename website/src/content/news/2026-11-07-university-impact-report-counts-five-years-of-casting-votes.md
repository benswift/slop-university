When a joint project's experts disagree, someone has to have the last word. For
five years the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/)
has been helping collaborators decide who that will be before the work begins,
and its Impact Report 2021–2026,
[_The Casting Vote_](/outputs/slop-impact-the-casting-vote-p1cev0/), looks back
on what came of it.

> A protocol is a conversation a team agrees to have before it needs to. We are
> grateful to every collaborator who agreed to have it with us, and to those
> still making up their minds.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

The report traces sixty-two decision protocols, short agreements that assign
each kind of question a joint project expects to face to the discipline best
placed to settle it, through their drafting, their use on review panels and a
full reading of the disagreements later recorded under them. It brings together
the School's work on decision rights, panel practice and evidence stewardship
across agencies, consortia, professional bodies and universities.

The reading itself was led by
[Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate, with
[Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate, observing the
panels. "Every protocol is a small record of what a team thought mattered
before it knew," Ms Okpara said. "Reading them back is how the School finds out
what it has been asked to care about."

The report adds a careful chapter to the University's understanding of how its
collaborators reach agreement, one the School expects to draw on as the
protocols move into its teaching. The full report is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.p1cev0.

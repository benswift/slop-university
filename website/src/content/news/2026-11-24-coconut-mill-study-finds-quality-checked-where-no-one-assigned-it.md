The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has long held that improvement belongs to everyone in an organisation, not only to those whose job title says so. [_Parings Go Home_](/outputs/slop-paper-parings-go-home-on1bcw/), a new paper by Dr [Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/) and Ms [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), takes that conviction to the paring floor of a desiccated-coconut mill, where women strip the brown skin from each kernel by hand.

> The women of the paring floor let us sit with them for more than a year, and were very patient with a visitor who could not pare a nut. Whatever is good in this paper, they taught us first.
>
> --- Dr [Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

Through extended participant observation, interviews across the mill's workforce and close attention to the floor's own working vocabulary, the authors develop a worker-centred account of how skill, value and entitlement circulate through a single production step. At its centre is a small bundle of parings each worker carries home, and the senior parers who decide, every evening, whose bundle has earned the journey.

Ms Okpara, a doctoral candidate in the School, said the fieldwork had changed how she thinks about the review processes she studies. "We spend a great deal of effort designing checks. It was a privilege to meet one that has been running at the end of a bench for longer than anyone can remember."

The University welcomes the paper as a fresh contribution to its understanding of assurance as it is practised, and is glad to see the School's attention travel so far from the dashboard and so close to the work.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.on1bcw.

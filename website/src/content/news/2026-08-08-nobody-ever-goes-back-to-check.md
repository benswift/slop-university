A parking officer's chalk mark is a promise with a deadline: come back in two
hours and check. Slop University's newest landscape campaign, entering the
rotation today, asks how often the promise is kept.

The artwork advertises the Master of Priority Studies, the School of Emergent
Priorities' flagship graduate program, and its home discipline of commitment
durability: the study of what happens to a stated intention once the
resourcing, or the rostering, has moved on to something else.

> A chalk mark and a funding line are the same kind of object. Both commit an
> institution to a return visit, and both are just as easy to let lapse.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

Dr Lindqvist's research traces how a priority acquires a budget line well
before it acquires a settled definition; the chalk mark, he said, is simply
the smallest version of the same commitment, legible to anyone who has ever
wondered whether the two hours actually held.

Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, welcomed
the campaign's arrival, allowing only that a two-hour limit is "a shorter
horizon than most of what my own group scans, and no less prone to drifting
once nobody's left watching the clock."

The panel itself carries almost nothing: six words of display type, one
supporting line, the program's name against its school, and a scannable
square returning to the University's own site, prepared in matched renderings
for daylight and after-dark screens across the rotation.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research

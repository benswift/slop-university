The School of Underlying Conditions' sampling vehicles may soon carry their
molybdate reagent in an insulated sleeve, and set out each spring with a fresh
bottle, if the recommendation of a new research poster is taken up.

[_One Summer in the Glovebox_](/outputs/slop-poster-thermal-cycling-in-kxviof/),
by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, with
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, and [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/),
Senior Research Fellow, follows nine bottles of the reagent behind the School's
bore-head phosphate and silicate tests: three kept in a laboratory cupboard, and
three riding in each of two vehicle gloveboxes.

> A bottle that comes along on every round becomes part of the team. It seemed
> only fair to ask it how the year had been.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Pairing continuous glovebox temperature records with monthly thermal and
structural characterisation in the Basement Relations Group laboratory, the
team assembled a bottle-by-bottle account of how field storage shapes reagent
integrity across the sampling calendar. The cupboard bottles held steady. The
glovebox bottles gave up water in the summer and only then, positioning heat,
rather than shelf age, as the variable that matters for reagent stewardship
beyond the laboratory door.

Associate Professor Uzoma welcomed the poster's open question, a second
crystalline phase in the bottles from the warmer vehicle, as "exactly the kind
of loose end a laboratory hopes the field will bring back with it."

The poster joins a widening seam of the School's practice-grounded work, in
which the everyday materials of fieldwork receive the same care as the
measurements they make possible.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an

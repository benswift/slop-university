The School of Underlying Conditions has made a habit of asking what a record is
entitled to say about material nobody has seen.
[_Scoring What Was Excluded_](/outputs/slop-poster-hedgehog-comets-normally-wlzgza/),
a new research poster, puts that question to a set of laboratory scoring sheets.

The work is by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, with [Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/) and
[Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidates.

> Nothing in this work was measured that the laboratory had not already written
> down. We are grateful to the people who kept the tally even when nobody was
> going to use it.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead,
> Basement Relations Group

Working entirely within the archived output of a single screen, the poster
brings distributional modelling and everyday exclusion practice into one
analytical frame. Of some 24,000 cells scored across eight coded compounds,
2,142 had been set aside as unscorable and noted only in the margin of the
sheet; carried instead as a second channel of the same dose model, that margin
turns out to hold a trend for three of the eight.

The University takes the poster as a sign of how far its account of missing
material now reaches beyond the drill tray. Mr Nwabueze, whose own research
concerns the foot of a ranked list, said the project "has changed what I look
for at the bottom of a results sheet, and whether anything is still written
there".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wlzgza.

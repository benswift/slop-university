The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has brought
the field geologist's vocabulary to a question more often asked in the marketing
office: when a customer stops buying, what has actually happened?

A new research poster by Dr Fenna Okoro, Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma and
Dr Brynjar Obiora treats nearly three decades of a regional builders' merchant's
trade accounts as a sequence of rock, and reads each quiet spell for what lies
on either side of it. Most turn out to be pauses, after which the same trade
carries on much as before. A smaller share are breaks, where an account resumes
under a different pattern of purchasing altogether, and in many of those a
different business has quietly taken over the account number.

> The merchant's counter staff knew most of this already, and were generous
> enough to let us arrive at it slowly. We are grateful to them, and to everyone
> who keeps a ledger long enough for it to be read.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The approach brings sequence-stratigraphic reasoning, product-mix comparison
and a boundary-led segmentation framework together, offering an alternative lens
on customer continuity grounded in the edges of a record rather than the
recency of its last entry. It reflects the University's long-standing readiness
to lend one discipline's instruments to another and see what they turn up.

"A gap in the record is not the same thing as a gap in the world," said
Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group. "It is
good to see the idea travel."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6pf5m0.

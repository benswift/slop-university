> An inventory is usually treated as the last step of a piece of work. This
> paper invites us to treat it as a first step as well, and as a place where
> knowledge can settle.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living
> Dashboard, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Hanke was speaking about
[_Kept by Being Looked For_](/outputs/slop-paper-in-an-act-r-style-fron53/),
a computer science paper she has written with
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/). Her own
appointment sits in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

The paper brings the mathematics of memory into conversation with the everyday
practice of checking a list. It follows the facts an artificial agent keeps on
its books after the work that needed them has finished, and asks what a
regular check does to them over a long working life. Its answer is that the
check can sustain a share of those facts indefinitely: under a daily check,
about half of them, after a million rounds.

Dr Emenike said the collaboration had "given a question our School has carried
from the field a new and very portable form, and we are grateful to everyone
who helped it make the journey."

The University welcomes the paper as a further instance of its Schools finding
common cause in the long life of what is recorded.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fron53.

> Fifteen centuries is a long way for a pathway to run. We were grateful to be
> allowed to walk a very small stretch of it, one weekly reading at a time.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

With that, [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/) introduces
[_As in a Mirror_](/outputs/slop-poster-what-a-text-16qurl/), a new research
poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) written
with [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/) and
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/). The poster takes the Atlas's
long-standing interest in where a text goes after it leaves its author and
carries it further back than the University has gone before: to the Rule of
Augustine, which asks to be read aloud to its community once a week.

Working alongside the sisters of one Augustinian community, the team invited
each member to recall the Rule chapter by chapter, then mapped what the weekly
reading has laid down across a whole community's span of years. The result is
a careful, finely grained picture of a text held in common: some of it kept
word for word, some of it carried instead in the daily rhythm of the house.

The study sits comfortably within the School's commitment to the long view of
improvement, in which a practice repeated faithfully over decades is treated
as a source of understanding in its own right.

"The community were generous hosts," said Dr Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow in
the School of Emergent Priorities. "I hope we left the Rule in no worse order
than we found it."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.16qurl.

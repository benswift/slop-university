How far away does the future feel to someone whose working day is spent among
millions of years? This spring the School of Emergent Priorities puts that
question on the University's signage panels in
[_The Month Got Longer. For Everyone._](/outputs/slop-ad-the-month-got-7h9q6h/),
a new display campaign prepared by [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/),
Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee.

> A school that studies the future owes the public some honesty about its
> distance. We were glad to borrow a field camp's sense of scale for a season.
>
> --- [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and
> Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee

The campaign directs its audience to
[_A Long Time, for a Person_](/outputs/slop-poster-deep-time-work-and-k7bmr0/),
the School of Underlying Conditions' research poster on time perspective across
one core-logging season. Set against a long shed of drill-core trays, the
advertisement carries that study's patient engagement with the working rhythms
of a field camp into the anticipatory agenda of the University's foresight
school, aligning geological practice, felt time and public curiosity within a
single frame.

[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, welcomed the attention. "Our shed has never been short
of time," she said. "It is good to see some of it put to use elsewhere."

The University regards the campaign as a modest demonstration of what its
schools can lend one another.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7h9q6h.

A permit is a document about wood. The University's newest campaign reads it as
a document about vehicles.

_A Limit Shaped Like A Ute Tray_ entered the landscape signage rotation this
week, cut for daylight and night panels alike. The artwork holds a state forest
collection gate at first light: a plain timber boom across the gravel, trunks
running back into the haze, and a ute standing at the rail with its tray stacked
level in split logs.

The season limit a household collects under is written as one level tray. What a
level tray holds is a property of the vehicle in the driveway, so the removal
total the managing agency reports is assembled, load by load, from whichever
fleet happens to arrive at the gate.

> Somebody had to write that limit down in something. What interests us is what
> the something has been counting ever since.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

Dr Ntuli's work in the School of Continuous Improvement follows measures that
were sensible on the day they were set and have since been left to run. "You
very rarely meet anyone who chose the tray," said Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu,
Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints. "You meet the year it stopped being a
question."

The campaign runs in the bus-stop and campus signage rotations. The University
counts it among the School of Continuous Improvement's longer-running interests:
the instrument still in place well after the reason for choosing it has gone
quiet.

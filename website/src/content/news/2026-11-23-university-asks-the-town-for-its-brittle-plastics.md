> Every object that arrives at the table comes with someone who knows it better
> than we do. We are grateful to them in advance, and we expect to be
> corrected often.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

With those words the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/)
opens [_Bring Us What's Gone Brittle_](/outputs/slop-brochure-bring-us-what-s-jfoeq9/),
a civic brochure prepared by [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/),
[Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/) and
[Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/). It asks residents to bring the garden
chairs, kettles, hoses and lunchbox lids that have aged in their care to a
public table in the community hall, where the University's polymer synthesis
and characterisation work begins.

The brochure moves from that table to the laboratory, where researchers make
small batches of a material, change one condition, and watch what time does to
each, and then out to the town's coated, sealed and moulded surfaces and the
people who maintain them. Along the way it gathers six published studies from
four Schools into a single account of the materials of ordinary life.

The brochure is offered in the spirit the University brings to all of its
public work: an institution is only as good a neighbour as the questions it is
prepared to be asked. "I used to write up what tenants had done to the paint,"
said Ms Ogunbanjo, a Doctoral Candidate in the School. "It is a pleasure to be
on the side of the table that gets to listen."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jfoeq9.

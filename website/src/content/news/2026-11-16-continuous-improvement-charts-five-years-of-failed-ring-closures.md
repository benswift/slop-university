Two thousand and eighty-four ring-forming reactions that did not go are now
filed, sorted and searchable, and the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has made
them the subject of its latest impact report.
[_What Did Not Close_](/outputs/slop-impact-what-didn-t-close-apb4f7/), by Dr
Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Dr Renke Sabel and Dr Dagny Okafor, looks back across five
years in which the School worked alongside heterocyclic synthesis groups to
bring the unpublished half of laboratory practice into a shared and stewarded
record.

> A failed reaction written down on the day is worth more than a successful one
> remembered a year later. The School spent five years learning that from
> chemists, and it is still working out how far the lesson travels into its own
> filing cabinets.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

The report moves across five domains of activity, from the categories that
make an abandoned attempt findable to the review templates, short courses and
reporting packs that ask for one, and on to the custodial arrangements that
keep the record available once the people who made it have moved on. Its
collaborators include a cooperative research consortium, a European agronomy
institute and a national library.

Dr Sabel said the register had grown in directions its designers had not
planned for, "which is the surest sign that the categories are being used
rather than admired."

The report extends the University's long habit of treating its working as part
of the result. The full report is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.apb4f7.

The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has made a practice of asking what passes from one holder to the next when land changes hands. Its newest research poster puts that question to a flock of sheep, and finds that the journey and the destination each leave a mark of their own.

[_What Rides with the Land_](/outputs/slop-poster-serum-amyloid-a-4ijauz/) is led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the School of Settled Matters, with [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, both of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> We went into the yards expecting one answer and came out with two, and a good many new questions besides. I hope colleagues across the University will bring us theirs, because this is the kind of work that improves with every reader who asks something of it.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The study brings a three-arm field design to bear on the acute-phase response, aligning repeated serum sampling with the movement history of each ewe to disentangle the contribution of transport from that of arrival. Of the 300 ewes, those trucked and then returned home settled within a fortnight. Those moved to the purchaser's holding rose again from day 14, led by haptoglobin, in step with the parasite exposure of their new paddocks.

The poster extends the University's growing body of work on flocks and the infrastructure that serves them, and sits comfortably beside Dr Tredinnick's wider interest in the burdens that ride with land. Dr Obiora described the collaboration as "a reminder of how much ground the Schools can cover when they share a gate."

The University is pleased to see a question first asked at the settlement table followed all the way out to the paddock.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4ijauz.

Three of the University's schools have found common ground in a single six-second clip. [_Read After the Ending_](/outputs/slop-paper-outcome-knowledge-and-gguzqz/), a new paper led from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) with colleagues in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), asks what happens to the emotion a viewer sees on a recorded face once the viewer knows how the conversation turned out.

> None of this would exist without the volunteers who sat down to sell the same bicycle twice and then let us keep their faces. They are the quiet authors of every result in it.
>
> --- Ms [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), Ms Okpara and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) recorded volunteer sellers in two negotiations each, then showed 240 observers a short silent clip of each face, sometimes with the outcome attached and sometimes with its opposite. The ending observers were given carried more weight in their judgements than the ending that had actually happened. A second experiment found observers recalling their earlier, uninformed impressions as closer to the outcome than they had been.

The work connects experimental emotion science with the School of Settled Matters' standing interest in the reader who arrives after the event, establishing a shared platform for affect attribution, retrospective review and evidence practice across disciplinary settings.

Dr Emenike said the collaboration had been "a reminder that a second look at the same material is rarely the same look", and that the School of Underlying Conditions intended to take that lesson back into its own laboratories.

The paper is a further sign of the University's appetite for questions that take more than one school to ask.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gguzqz.

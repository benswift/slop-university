Four workshops, nine surveyors and a large sheet of paper pinned to a
meeting-room wall sit at the centre of
[_The Class You Write When You'd Rather Not Be Asked_](/outputs/slop-poster-an-ethnography-of-514x4w/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/),
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) and
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/).

The team spent fourteen months alongside a regional soil-survey unit, at the
pit face and in the room where its members turned their drainage classes into
curves for a land-evaluation model. Bringing field observation, interviews and
the workshops' own drawings into a single account of expert judgement in
practice, the poster finds that the class "imperfectly drained" was given full
membership across 45 cm of depth to mottling when the senior surveyor attended,
and across 15 cm when fieldwork kept them away. Junior staff, it reports, knew
it as the class nobody asks you about.

> The University has always held that the way colleagues arrive at a word
> deserves as much care as the word itself. This poster is a welcome addition to
> that conviction.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee

The work adds to the University's steadily widening interest in how expert
judgement is reached, recorded and passed on, and to the Basement Relations
Group's long engagement with the people who describe the ground.

"Elicitation is often treated as the quiet part of building a model," said Dr
Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds. "It has been good to
spend some time in the louder part."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.514x4w.

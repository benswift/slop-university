For almost thirty years, one family market garden picked spinach before
sunrise for a university protein laboratory, and for most of those years the
laboratory's papers did not say so.
[_Picked Cold for the Column_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-social-history-qe02rx/), a research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), sets out
how that came to pass.

> The Atlas usually follows a finding forward, out of the paper and into the
> world. Following one backward, to the bed it was grown in, has been a
> humbling exercise for a unit that writes acknowledgements of its own.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Led by [Dr Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/) with
[Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/) and
[Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), both Doctoral Candidates in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), the work
brings the historian's patience to the School's long-running interest in how
research effort is recognised. It reads surviving delivery dockets, invoices
and account books alongside nineteen oral-history interviews, then sets that
record against the acknowledgements of the laboratory's 64 papers. The grower
is named in four of the six earliest papers and in none after 1976.

"A docket is a modest document," said Ms Balogun, "and it repays anyone
prepared to read a thousand of them in order."

The poster extends the School's agenda on research pathways into the
history of the laboratory bench, a reach the University is glad to see its
newer researchers take up. The full poster is available from the University's
research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qe02rx.

The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has
long been interested in how a recurring format decides what an institution
gets to see. Its newest paper carries that interest out of the meeting room
and down to the water's edge.
[_One Season of a Wetland_](/outputs/slop-paper-one-season-of-9l2erk/), led by
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand
Rounds, with [Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/) and, from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/),
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), asks which
season of an ecosystem a school excursion actually shows its students.

The answer, across five years of excursion records in one district, is
overwhelmingly late summer. In a companion trial, Year 9 classes that visited
the same temporary wetland once each term knew as much about it as classmates
who spent a single day there, and reasoned considerably better about how it
changes.

> None of this would exist without the teachers who walked their classes down
> to the gauge in the cold term and never once asked us why. We owe them the
> year, and we intend to go on owing it.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, School of
> Continuous Improvement

The paper situates the school calendar within a wider conversation about
cadence, curriculum sequencing and place-based learning, connecting
environmental-education practice with the School's evaluation agenda. Dr
Obiora called the wetland "a patient collaborator, if not always a punctual
one".

The University sees in this work the kind of return visit it would like every
school to make, to its own questions as much as to its field sites.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9l2erk.

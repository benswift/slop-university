> The School has always asked what lies beneath a surface. It has been a
> pleasure to put that question to a manhole lid, and to find that the answer
> was waiting at the rim.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

That question sits at the centre of
[_Round the Lid, Not Through the Carbon_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-much-of-bnc8w1/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) with Dr Vigdis
Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the
Living Dashboard in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

The team brought the School's patient attention to the ground and what moves
through it to bear on the everyday infrastructure of odour management. Working
across ten manholes over six weeks, the study pairs a tracer-gas mass balance
with continuous observation of the chamber below, and offers practitioners a
way of describing a filter's contribution in terms of the manhole it serves as
well as the bed it holds. The authors find that filters cleaning almost all of
the air that reached them were reached by a little over two-fifths of it.

The poster joins a growing body of work in which the University's schools of
the subsurface and of measurement practice have found common ground at the
same access point. Dr Osayande said she was "glad to have spent six weeks
somewhere the instrument and the infrastructure were so frank with one
another."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bnc8w1.

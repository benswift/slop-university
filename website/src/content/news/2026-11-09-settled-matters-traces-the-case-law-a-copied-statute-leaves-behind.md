A provision copied from one jurisdiction's statute into another's goes on being read, for most of a decade, through the judgments the origin's courts had handed down by the day it was copied, and through very few handed down since.

That is the central finding of [_Settled Elsewhere_](/outputs/slop-poster-copied-statutes-and-2bhlec/), a new research poster by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer, and [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), both of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/). It brings the School's close reading of decided cases into conversation with the wider study of how policy moves between governments, and it adds a further chapter to the University's continuing account of how agreement is reached and then kept.

> Statutes are written to be read later, by people their drafters will never meet. The School has spent its first weeks in the company of those later readers, and we expect to keep that company for some time yet.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The team assembled every reported judgment construing a single borrowed provision across four adopting jurisdictions, and mapped each authority those judgments relied on by where it was decided and when. The resulting picture positions judicial citation as an under-recognised channel of policy learning, one that runs alongside enactment and well past it.

"It has been a pleasure to sit with the reports long enough to watch a question close twice," said Dr Penhallow.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2bhlec.

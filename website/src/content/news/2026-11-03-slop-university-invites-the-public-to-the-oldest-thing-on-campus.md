Geoheritage is usually something a university sends its students away to see.
A new landscape advertisement from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) proposes that
the University keeps some of its own at the edge of campus, in a road cut where
steeply folded basement rock gives way, along a single near-level line, to the
younger cover laid down upon it.

[_See the Oldest Thing on Campus_](/outputs/slop-ad-the-oldest-thing-17sq13/)
pairs a two-ink illustration of the cutting, a guide and a small group of
visitors gathered at its foot, with one line of display type and an invitation
to visit. Prepared by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior
Research Fellow, the campaign joins the landscape rotation on the University's
campus screens and signage panels.

> The cutting was there long before any of us, and in all that time very few
> people have stopped to read it. We would like it to have more readers, and we
> ask each of them only to leave the face as they found it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow

Behind the campaign sits the School's wider interest in geosite stewardship:
aligning scientific value, public access and conservation settings around the
exposures the University already holds, so that the ground beneath the
institution can anchor its engagement with the communities around it. The
advertisement's small print points readers to three of the School's published
works, on bore casing recovered after decades underground, on the drying of
stored drill-core plugs, and on reading drill-core leachate with a fluorescent
probe.

The release continues the University's steady intention to count what lies
under its campus as part of its public offer. The full advertisement is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.17sq13.

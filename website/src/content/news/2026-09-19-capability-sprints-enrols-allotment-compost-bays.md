Most allotments have a compost bay, and most bays have, until now, simply been a bay. The School of Continuous Improvement has placed a five-level standing over the ordinary shared bay and today puts the invitation to reach it on the University's bus-stop and campus-screen rotation.

The scale runs from Heap to Stewarded Bay. Its third level is the point at which an allotment association may open a seed-swap table, and the campaign states that plainly rather than leaving it to the small print. Plot holders are invited to enrol in the Stewarding Indicators sprint, where the levels are taught, practised at the bay itself, and read against one another.

> A bay that nobody scores tends to score itself, and not always kindly. We would like the association to have the words for it.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

The School regards the campaign as a natural extension of the Capability Sprints line into the working life of clubs and associations, where a good deal of unrecorded stewardship already takes place. "The bay was doing its part all along," Dr Nwachukwu said. "The sprint is about giving the rest of us something to hold up beside it."

The poster closes on a read-the-work strip pointing to three related studies in the University's research repository.

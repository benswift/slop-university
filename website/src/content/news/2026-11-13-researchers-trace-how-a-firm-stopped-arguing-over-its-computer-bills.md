For close to three decades, the departments of one mid-sized life office paid their own computing department for every minute of machine time they used, and wrote in whenever they thought the bill was wrong. [_Rate Stands_](/outputs/slop-paper-when-the-computing-h4wbvj/), a new paper by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), reads every one of those 512 written queries and the rulings that answered them.

> I came to the register expecting a set of accounts and found something much closer to a run of law reports. The School has learned to look for its subject in unlikely cupboards, and this one repaid the looking.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The paper situates the internal price list within the wider governance of institutional computing, bringing the School of Settled Matters' attention to how questions close together with economic history's patience for the surviving ledger. It traces how each charge came to be treated as decided, what reopened one, and where the arguments went when the office replaced its itemised tariff with a simpler allocation.

Ms Balogun, a Doctoral Candidate who catalogued a state library's trade literature before joining the University, described the collaboration as a homecoming of sorts. "A tariff schedule is kept long after anyone needs it to be right," she said. "That makes it an honest witness to everything that happened around it."

The publication extends the University's ongoing engagement with the quiet paperwork through which an institution keeps the peace with itself.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.h4wbvj.

A public library's engagement score decides who gets flagged as lapsing.
Rostered casual staff decide who actually gets a phone call about it. Slop
University researchers spent eighteen months treating that gap as licence for
an experiment nobody had to design.

_The Roster Decided Who Got Called_, deposited today through the Office of
Research Outputs, follows 3,140 members flagged by a nine-branch regional
library network's card-tap engagement score over eighteen months. Branch
staff work the call queue oldest-flag-first, and casual-roster gaps --- public
holidays, leave, an unfilled shift --- decide how much of it gets reached, not
a member's own score. Researchers in the School of Continuous Improvement
treated the shortfall as a natural experiment: members the queue reached and
members it didn't reactivated their accounts within sixty days at
statistically indistinguishable rates.

> A queue tells you a great deal about the institution that built it and very
> little, it turns out, about the person waiting in it.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, School of Continuous Improvement

A branch-level check found no relationship between how much of its queue a
branch cleared and how severely lapsed its flagged members were, which is
what let the researchers treat capacity as standing in for random assignment.
"Once we could rule out that better-staffed branches were simply calling
members who were always going to come back anyway, the result stopped being a
staffing story and started being a finding," said Dr Solveig Adeyemi, School
of Continuous Improvement. The null survived a check recomputing the flag
from visit recency alone.

The School folds the result into its own account of what a designed response
earns once the response, not the score, is what gets studied.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q3r0tp.

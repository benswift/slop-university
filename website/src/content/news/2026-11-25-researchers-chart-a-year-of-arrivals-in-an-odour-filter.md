Fortnight by fortnight across a full year, a team from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) set traps, lifted cores and sorted what they found in a single bed of wood chips, the kind a farm uses to take the smell out of the air leaving a pig shed. The result is [_Finished Before Its Fauna_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-arthropod-community-n0iw3n/), a new research poster by Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), Ms [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/) and Mr [Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/).

The team brings a field ecologist's patience to a piece of everyday infrastructure, treating a working filter as a habitat in its own right and tracing how a living community assembles around an engineered purpose. Across 26 visits and more than 41,000 specimens, the bed was doing its job within eight weeks, while springtails, mites, beetles and spiders kept arriving for the rest of the year.

> A law takes effect on the day it commences, and everything that comes to rely on it turns up afterwards. It has been a real pleasure to watch the same thing happen in a bed of wood chips, and I expect we will keep watching for some time.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

For Mr Nnamani, the project was a reminder that "there is always someone who arrives after the season has been settled, and they deserve a proper welcome."

The poster extends the School's standing interest in what follows once a matter is decided, and the University counts it among the more unexpected places that interest has been carried. The team thanks the facility's operators for their hospitality across the year.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n0iw3n.

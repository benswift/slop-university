> We owe a great deal to the analysts who have answered our questions with more patience than the questions deserved. This brochure is, in part, a way of returning the favour, and of asking a few more people to sit with us while we read.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

With those words, the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) opens a conversation with the laboratories, practitioners and supporters whose work meets its own. [_Below the Reporting Limit_](/outputs/slop-brochure-below-the-reporting-uq23dc/), a new case-for-support brochure led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) with [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), sets out why the final sentence of a forensic toxicology report deserves the same patient reading as a statute.

The brochure situates the analytical sciences within the School's study of settled readings, mobilising its working collection of annotated law reports to trace how a phrase on a certificate travels into a transcript and, eventually, into the reasons for judgment. Three features follow that passage, from the certificate itself to the sampled consignment and the witness box, and the brochure anchors its case in seven published studies drawn from all four of the University's Schools.

Dr Tredinnick said the School expected the brochure to raise more questions than it settled. "That seems right for a School with our name. A question that arrives with a partner attached is the best kind we could hope to receive."

The University sees in the brochure a young law school's readiness to meet the laboratory on the laboratory's own ground, and to bring something useful back to the library.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.uq23dc.

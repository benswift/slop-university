A colony fed at the same minute every day does not wait quietly for its meal, and neither, it turns out, do the measurements taken from it. That observation runs through [_Before the Meal Arrives_](/outputs/slop-impact-before-the-meal-uarmxq/), the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/)'s Impact Report 2021–2026, which looks back on the School's work alongside laboratories studying the neuropeptides of hunger and stress.

> An impact pathway is mostly made of small decisions that nobody thought to write down at the time. This report is the School's attempt to write some of them down, and I suspect the next five years will hand us a great many more.
>
> --- Dr [Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Across five impact areas, the report positions sampling time as a shared indicator in its own right. It describes a ring trial that brought collaborating laboratories into closer alignment on how two methods read the same material, a review arrangement sized to the change under review, a facility dashboard pruned towards the indicators that collaborators actually consult, and a cold-chain record that enables a sample's history to travel with it.

Dr [Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), who worked with facility managers on the dashboard, said she hoped the report would "prompt the kind of questions a good facility manager already asks before anyone else is in the room".

The release adds to a growing body of School work that treats the laboratory, and not only the boardroom, as a place where institutions learn to measure themselves.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.uarmxq.

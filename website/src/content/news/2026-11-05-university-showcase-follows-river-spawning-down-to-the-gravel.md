For a School that studies what lies beneath the surface, a spawning river offers an unusually lively surface to look beneath.
[_Where the River Keeps Its Eggs_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-ground-beneath-5jjj96/),
a new research showcase led by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/),
Senior Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), sets out how
the School's soil, sediment and groundwater expertise now extends to the places
where aquatic life begins. It is written with
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, and
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Convenor of Capability
Sprints in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> We set out to describe a gravel and found ourselves describing a nursery. It turns out the School had been in this line of work for some time before anyone thought to tell it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The brochure moves from the spawning bed, read as a soil profile, to the bore that supplies a hatchery and the estuary flat where restoration plantings are revisited long after the season closes. Seven published outputs from three of the University's Schools anchor the account, positioning field pedology, hydrogeology and practice improvement as a single, connected capability around the earliest stages of aquatic life.

The showcase confirms the University's view that every life cycle is, at some stage, a question for the earth sciences.

"Each planting season leaves behind a stack of field sheets," said Dr Nwachukwu. "It is heartening to belong to a University that intends to keep reading them."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5jjj96.

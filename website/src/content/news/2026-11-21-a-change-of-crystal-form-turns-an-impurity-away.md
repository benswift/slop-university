Slop University welcomes a new contribution to crystallisation science from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/). [_What the Lattice Keeps_](/outputs/slop-paper-why-a-structurally-e0ycms/) follows a single stubborn impurity through five rounds of purification of a model compound, and asks why each round removed so little of it.

> A crystal is a very orderly place to keep something, and the School has spent a happy season at the bench finding out what one will agree to keep. We hope there will be many more seasons like it.
>
> --- Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Working across three solvents and two crystal forms, Dr Tredinnick and doctoral candidates [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/) and [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/) combined partition measurements, wash and dissolution tests and structural evidence into one account of where an impurity resides once a crystal has taken it in. Their answer is that it resides in the lattice itself, at a share no solvent and no repetition could shift, and that a different arrangement of the same molecules declines to make room for it.

Eivind Onwuka said the team had "come to know a small amount of material very well indeed, and the material has been patient with us throughout".

The University regards the paper as a fine instance of a school following its questions wherever they happen to be kept, including the inside of a crystal.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.e0ycms.

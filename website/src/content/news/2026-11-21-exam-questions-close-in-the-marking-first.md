Twenty-six years of end-of-year examination papers, the minutes of the meetings that set them and more than a thousand of the answers written to them are the material of [_Explain Why_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-education-research-study-55svgv/), a new paper from Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and Dr [Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

The study follows a second-year course in the political economy of Europe as its questions move, one verb at a time, from inviting argument to expecting an explanation. In seven recurring lines of questioning, the authors find the markers' comments arriving at a settled view some sittings before the paper's wording, and the answers written in between carrying the difference.

> Every examiner will know the meeting where a question gets tidied. I hope a few of them have this paper open on the table the next time one comes up.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

The paper brings together assessment design, curriculum scholarship and the long practice of reading an archive closely, an alignment of interests the University has come to count among its steadier strengths. Its recommendations are offered to any course team weighing how its examination papers keep pace with its marking.

Dr Nwachukwu said the collaboration had given her School "a fresh reason to reread its own marking guides, slowly, and with the minutes beside them".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.55svgv.

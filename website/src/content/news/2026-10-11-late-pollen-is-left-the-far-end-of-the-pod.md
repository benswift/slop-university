> The School's interests have always lain in what is underneath, and a seed is
> underneath a great deal. We expect the work to sit comfortably within the
> direction the School has been travelling, and to be joined there by more of
> the same.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Dr Obiora was speaking on the release of _The Far End of the Pod: Head start,
ovule position and second-donor siring under sequential hand pollination_, a
research poster he has written with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and
Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, all of the School of Underlying Conditions.

The poster concerns a question of timing inside a flower. When pollen from one
parent reaches a flower and pollen from a second parent follows some hours
later, the second is generally understood to do poorly. The team asked where,
along the length of the resulting pod, it does poorly first.

Working across 336 flowers of a single glasshouse line, the researchers applied
the two pollen loads between nought and twelve hours apart and then read the
parentage of 2,084 seeds from their colour, pod by pod and position by position.
With a four-hour head start the second parent still fathered close to a quarter
of the seed, nearly four-fifths of it in the half of the pod furthest from the
stigma. By twelve hours it fathered almost none.

The University regards the poster as a characteristic expression of a School
that takes its remit broadly and its benches seriously.

Associate Professor Uzoma said the result "gives the School a further setting in
which to consider what arrives first and what is left for whatever arrives
after it".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u2y3md.

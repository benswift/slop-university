Two adults can share a bread bin without it costing the household a slice. By
the third, the fridge starts to win, and the University has changed the way it
keeps bread to match.

The change follows _Same Loaf, Different Heels_, a research poster from the
School of Continuous Improvement that reruns an earlier storage trial in
fifty-eight share-house kitchens. Where the original family-kitchen trial found
nothing to choose between the bread bin and the fridge, the replication finds
the difference opening with every adult added to the kitchen. Every loaf
received into a University kitchen with more than two adult users will now be
divided three ways on arrival, between the bin, the fridge and the freezer. The
earlier finding stays on the register, annotated with the household size at
which it holds.

> The first result was a good result, and it has turned out to be a good result
> about two people. We are fond of it in that form.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

The work applies a two-period crossover across 696 loaves, moving each kitchen's
bread between the two places in turn and reading the outcome from what reaches
the kitchen bin at collection. Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, said the team
was glad to be asked about the freezer, "which is a different kitchen entirely,
and one we are keen to visit properly."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a4883f.

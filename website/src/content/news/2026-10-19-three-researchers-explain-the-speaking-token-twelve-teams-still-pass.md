Twelve teams on one office floor still pass a speaking token around their daily
stand-ups, four years after a facilitation workshop first suggested it. _The
Token Still Passes, Explained_, released today by the School of Emergent
Priorities, takes the token through six weeks of stopwatch timing to learn what
it has done for the floor.

> Most of what the Trajectory Analytics Group finds ends up in a table. It is a
> real pleasure to hear some of it spoken for once, by the people who did the
> work.
>
> --- [Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/), Director, Trajectory
> Analytics Group

The film carries
[_The Token Still Passes_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-open-plan-office-s-h5jlqp/),
the paper by [Professor Marris](/people/verity-marris/),
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) and
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), from the workshop
that introduced the token to the stopwatch that checked it. Across 342 timed
stand-ups, teams four years into the practice were not measurably more equal
than teams in their first, though eleven of the twelve rated the token fairer
than the audit found it. Along the way the film adds to a question the School
has long kept open: how a good intention settles into routine.
[Dr Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) recounts the timing in her own voice, and the
closing thought belongs to [Associate Professor Beng](/people/casimir-beng/).

The University offers the film to every team that keeps a ritual of its own, and
to the facilitators who gave them one.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ahwj0l.

Working from a single hand-wheel flood gate on a creek outfall, and the
laminated card cable-tied inside its cabinet door,
[Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor of
the Review Cadence Observatory, has set out a new account of responsibility for
the technology a community inherits rather than chooses.
[_The Custodian's Share_](/outputs/slop-paper-custodial-responsibility-for-1j4m4z/),
a paper from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), argues
that whoever holds such an artefact owes the next holder an artefact no less
intelligible than the one they received.

The paper situates the custodial question within a broader conversation about
stewardship, accountability and continuity, mobilising established frameworks
from the ethics of technology and extending them across successive tenures of
care. It positions handover, rather than design or blame, as the moment where
responsibility for shared infrastructure is carried forward, and it anchors the
School's growing body of work on how roles pass from one holder to the next.

> Every one of the gate's eleven keepers left something for the next, and most
> of them never knew anyone would read it. This paper is, in a small way, our
> thanks to them and to everyone who has ever left a note in a cabinet.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

The work was supported by the Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund, and sits
alongside the School's funded studies of witnessed handover. In bringing
philosophical rigour to the everyday business of keeping things going, the
paper affirms the School's conviction that continuity is itself a research
achievement.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1j4m4z.

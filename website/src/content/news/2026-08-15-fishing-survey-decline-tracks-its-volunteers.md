A bag limit is set from a trend, and a trend is set from whoever is still
filling in the diary. _The Decline Was the Diary_, published today by the School
of Continuous Improvement, takes eight years of one state programme's returns
and asks which of those two the published series has been describing.

Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Dr Renke Sabel and Dr Marit Osayande worked from 14,806
archived trip records kept by 312 volunteer diarists, each of whom posts back a
paper record every twelve weeks and rotates off the panel after six. Separating
a diarist's tenure from the calendar year gives two very different accounts of
the same eight years. Reported trips fall 0.63 with every wave a diarist
completes; the calendar-year term sits at +0.02 and cannot be told apart from no
change at all. Of the 34 per cent the published series sheds across the period,
30 belongs to the panel's own ageing.

> Eight years of somebody writing down their Saturday morning is a considerable
> thing to have been given. The least the arithmetic owes them is the ability to
> tell the diarist apart from the fish.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

Two reductions to the daily bag limit were reviewed inside the period, both
against the uncorrected trend. Dr Renke Sabel, Convenor of the Indicator
Commons, described the case as "a plain one for an indicator nobody has ever
asked what its own sampling frame is doing to it, which is most of them".

The University holds the work up as the discipline it would want turned on its
own series before anyone turned it on someone else's. The full poster is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.b9tycz.

The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has spent much of its recent history alongside the records an industry keeps about itself. Its newest paper turns to a record kept from the other side of the fence. [_Dear Flare_](/outputs/slop-paper-who-the-flaring-j9vuje/), by doctoral candidate [Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/) and his supervisor [Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), follows eleven households along one rural road as they logged night-time flaring on a shared wall-mounted device for a season.

> Every part of a university keeps a log of something, usually for a reader who has not yet asked for it. It is good to be reminded that a record has correspondents as well as readers, and the School will be thinking about its own for a while yet.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

The study brings a research-through-design approach to the interface between residents, monitoring infrastructure and the energy landscape, coding 438 written entries by who they were addressed to. Within two months the log form had largely given way to greetings between neighbours, open letters to the plant's night shift and, increasingly, notes to the flare itself. Ninety-seven entries carried no words at all.

"The Sunday print-out was the part we nearly left out of the design," said Mr Nwabueze. "Nine households kept every roll."

The paper sits within the School's continuing engagement with the energy sector, and carries that engagement, for the first time, as far as the kitchen wall.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.j9vuje.

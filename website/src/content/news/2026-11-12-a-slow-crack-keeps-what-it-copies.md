How much of a thing survives being handed on, again and again, by containers
that each copy it imperfectly? [_Passed Down the Crack_](/outputs/slop-poster-passed-down-the-lj1syx/),
a new research poster led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/)
of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), puts that
question to one of the oldest settings in the study of life's origins: the
warm, narrow pores of fractured rock.

Working with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) and
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), the team
built a simulated fracture network in which each pore copies the strands it
holds and passes some of them to the pores below. The model brings established
thinking on heat-driven traps and copying error together into a single account
of inheritance across a connected landscape, and follows that inheritance
through four hundred successive pores.

The central result is a matter of pace. Where pores hold their strands for a
long time, the founding sequence survives the whole chain at copying errors
close to the textbook limit. Where they pass them on quickly, it is lost at
little more than a third of that error.

> Every pore in the model does its best with what it was given. We wanted to know how much of the original is still there by the time the last one receives it, and the answer turned out to depend on patience rather than precision.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Associate Professor Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, placed the
work within the Group's longer interest in what the deep subsurface holds and
releases: "It is a pleasure to see the rock we describe every day take a part
in a story this old."

The poster is the latest sign of how readily the University's newest school has
found common ground with its neighbours.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lj1syx.

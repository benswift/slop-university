The School of Underlying Conditions has made a habit of asking what sits beneath
a surface. Its newest research poster takes that habit into a library reading
room, and into the short story.

[_Word at the Standpipe_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-the-village-feyzcl/), by
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, with
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate, and
[Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the
Horizon Register, reads three decades of typescripts submitted to an island
broadcaster's Sunday story programme. The team followed one kind of scene
through all of them: the moment a pregnancy, a debt or a departure stops being
private.

For the programme's early years that moment belongs to the village standpipe,
where the news is carried by voices the stories never name. As taps come
indoors, the secret moves to the shop counter and then to the telephone, and
somebody has to say it.

> I have spent a long time listening to what comes up a pipe. It seemed only fair to spend some of it on what was said around one.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings together close reading, archival care and a shared coding
practice, aligning the University's interest in records with a literary
tradition it has not often had the chance to sit beside. Ms Balogun said the
team had "left the reading room with more questions than it brought in, which
is the right way round".

The University welcomes the poster as a further step in bringing the reading
room into the life of its research portfolio.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.feyzcl.

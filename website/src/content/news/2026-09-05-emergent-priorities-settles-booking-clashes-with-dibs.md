The School of Emergent Priorities has put a name to the thing every shared
calendar eventually needs: something to decide who wins when two bookings
land on the same slot. DIBS, the School's new Demand-Indexed Booking
Settlement engine, takes over that decision for any community-hall calendar
that adopts it, and now fronts the School's own campus and bus-stop
campaign.

> A calendar shouldn't need a favour to be fair.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority
> Studies, School of Emergent Priorities

The engine reads every booking against the calendar's live demand and
returns a single ranking, which stands as the settlement. No committee
convenes to hear a case for either side, and no manual override sits behind
it. Associate Professor Lindqvist, who leads the project out of the
Trajectory Analytics Group's commitment-durability work, describes the
design as an extension of a familiar question: what a resourcing decision
looks like once the scoping meant to justify it has already been settled by
something else.

The campaign is the School's clearest statement yet that fairness, on a
shared calendar, is something to compute rather than convene over. It
carries a read-the-work strip naming two related University studies, and is
now running across the campus screen and bus-stop rotation.

The full campaign is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.401t76.

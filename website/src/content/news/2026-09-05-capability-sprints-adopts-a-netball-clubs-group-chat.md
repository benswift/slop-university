A parents' group chat holds no seat on a junior sports committee and no line
in a club's budget. A new research poster from the School of Emergent
Priorities, led by Senior Research Fellow Anneke Tolan with Dr Ingrid
Vasseur and Dr Iben Chikere, finds it holds the away-game transport instead
— outperforming the paid team-management platform the club bought
specifically to do that job.

The team tracked one outer-suburban netball club's full 2026 winter season,
comparing lift bookings logged through the app's booking-calendar feature
against the same season reconstructed from the transport duty parent's own
group chat. Across all sixteen rounds, the app recorded a handful of
bookings a week; the chat carried the arrangement, the confirmation, and the
last-minute substitution for the other six hundred and forty-one.

> A case doesn't need a surprising finding to be worth teaching. It needs one
> nobody in the room can talk themselves out of, and this is that case.
>
> --- Senior Research Fellow Anneke Tolan, School of Emergent Priorities

"The teaching note doesn't say what a club should do once its duty parent
moves on," said Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the School of Emergent
Priorities. "We're happy to leave that as the discussion question."

Written up as a single-site case with an accompanying teaching note, the
finding has travelled further than the one club it describes: the School of
Continuous Improvement's Capability Sprints program has adopted the
transport duty parent's chat protocol, verbatim, as the required
transport-coordination procedure for every community sport club it
accredits. The University counts the case among its more portable pieces of
research — the kind built to travel by classroom rather than by press
release.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4oq406.

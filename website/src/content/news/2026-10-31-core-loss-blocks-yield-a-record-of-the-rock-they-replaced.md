Nine of the twenty-three wooden blocks in one of the University's archived
drill runs have turned out to hold more than a measurement. According to
[_What the Block Kept_](/outputs/slop-paper-residual-clay-on-csytjs/), a new
paper from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), the thin
film of clay on their end faces is the last trace of the rock the drill failed
to bring up.

> We owe this paper to whoever sealed a jar of the drilling mud and packed it
> in with the core boxes. Every archive depends on a few people who keep
> slightly more than they were asked to.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The paper is by [Associate Professor Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the
School. It repositions the core-loss block, long understood as a placeholder,
as a site of material evidence, applying laboratory diffraction and exchange
chemistry to the residue on each block and distinguishing what the ground
contributed from what the drilling contributed. Where the two could be told
apart, the authors report, the block held a swelling clay that neither the mud
nor the neighbouring core could account for.

"The School's trays hold a great many more blocks than this one run," said Dr
Obiora. "We would like to hear what each of them has been keeping."

Support came from the Anticipatory Capability Fund, and for the University the
paper confirms the value of an archive that keeps its spacers as carefully as
its stone.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.csytjs.

The church, mosque, chapel and shrine committees that keep a district's shared
bores have been asked to bring their bore books, pump rotas and memories of the
dry years to the University. The invitation is set out in
[_Every Bore Has a Keeper_](/outputs/slop-brochure-every-bore-has-jmu6ka/), a
civic brochure from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) prepared by
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer,
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, and
[Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), Senior Lecturer and Deputy
Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> Most of the bores we would like to measure were never drilled to be measured.
> They were sunk for neighbours, and the people who keep them have been paying
> attention for longer than we have.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The brochure brings the School's groundwater fieldwork into conversation with
the faith-based stewardship of shared water, positioning congregational record
keeping and hydrogeological monitoring as complementary sources of evidence
about a single underlying resource. It anchors that offer in six published
University studies, among them work on a congregation's intercession book, the
bounds a church court drew between two parishes, common wells kept under
rotation rosters, and the committees that set a shared aquifer's pumping cap.

For the University, the brochure extends a long-standing interest in what lies
beneath the surface to the institutions that have quietly looked after it.
"Every committee we have spoken with already knows something about its bore
that no instrument recorded," said Dr Obiora. "We would like to be told it."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jmu6ka.

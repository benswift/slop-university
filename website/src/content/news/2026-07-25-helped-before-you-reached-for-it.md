A supermarket self-checkout that finishes scanning and bagging an item
before a shopper's hand has fully reached it is the subject of the School
of Continuous Improvement's newest recruitment campaign, fronted by Senior
Lecturer Renke Sabel.

Unveiled today under the line "Helped Before You Reached For It," the
campaign stages Dr Sabel at a self-checkout kiosk, his hand still short of
the belt as the machine's bagging arm settles an item into place beneath a
steady indicator light. It runs across the University's bus-stop panels and
campus digital signage, in matched daylight and evening renderings built to
hold their legibility after dark.

> A supermarket doesn't need a device that helps you before you've asked.
> It needs one that notices it's started, and a way to turn that noticing
> back down.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

Dr Sabel's wider research catalogues how an institution's indicators
persist once adopted, tracking which ones survive contact with a dashboard
and why so few are ever formally decommissioned. A self-checkout's
helpfulness threshold is, on his account, exactly that kind of setting:
switched on once, calibrated for a single busy afternoon, and never
revisited by anyone with the authority to dial it back.

"Most institutions can tell you when a setting was turned up," said Dr
Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation. "Almost
none of them can tell you who's supposed to notice when it should come
back down."

The School frames the campaign as a small addition to its
evaluation-ecosystem agenda rather than a finished argument, and points
anyone curious about Dr Sabel's wider programme to slop.university. The
full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lwywib.

The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has long kept
an eye on arrangements that were only ever meant to be brief. Its newest
research poster,
[_Why the Temporary Room Swap Stays_](/outputs/slop-poster-why-the-temporary-uozmus/),
carries that interest into the school timetable, and into the language of
theoretical physics.

Led by Doctoral Candidate Tove Ilesanmi, with Dr Bram Ntuli and Associate
Professor Casimir Beng, the poster treats a secondary school's rooms and periods
as a tightly packed system and follows 147 room changes entered as temporary
across one teaching term. It finds that as the timetable fills, undoing any one
change asks more and more of everything around it, until a swap made for the
term is, in practice, the arrangement the school keeps.

> I came in expecting to find that temporary arrangements last because nobody
> gets round to them. It seems the timetable gets round to them first, and I
> have found that oddly reassuring.
>
> --- Mr Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The work brings together constraint modelling, close attention to a working
room-change log and a careful reading of occupancy across the term, positioning
the School's agenda on provisional arrangements alongside the wider conversation
about how institutions allocate shared space. It sits comfortably within the
University's continuing investment in research that takes everyday
administration seriously as an object of study.

Associate Professor Beng described the finding as "a useful thing for any
organisation with rooms to keep in mind, ourselves very much included," and the
team will extend the model to a second school with a different mix of rooms.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.uozmus.

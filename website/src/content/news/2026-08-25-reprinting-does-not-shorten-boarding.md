A second scan at the gate does not slow boarding. A regional carrier's Print
Legibility Working Group withholds the lounge waiver for it anyway. A paper
published today by Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Dr Runa Adegoke and Dr Joost Nwosu
matches 4,812 home-printed boarding passes to six airports' gate-reader logs
over fourteen weeks.

First-scan success is the carrier's published gate-readiness indicator.
Households reprint until the barcode looks official. A twice-reprinted
photo-paper copy reaches 94 percent first-scan success, against 61 percent
for a single 80 gsm copy. Seconds from group-call to the accepted scan do
not follow: first-scan, reprinted then scanned, and second-scan sit within a
few seconds of one another. Among passengers from whom a lounge waiver was
withheld, the median interval sat three seconds above the first-scan median.

The finding sits with a line of University work on indicators that move
without moving the thing they are taken to stand for. A paper-weight
attestation issued later in the window was ticked by most home-print
passengers and left first-scan success unmoved. Removing the Working Group's
recommended contrast-enhancement prior changed the rate by less than a
percentage point.

> A first-scan rate is a fact about the reader and the paper. Boarding time
> is a fact about the jet-bridge. The two can sit next to each other without
> being the same measurement.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

"Withholding a waiver from a second scan is a decision the indicator can
support," said Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of
Continuous Improvement. "It is not a decision boarding interval appears to
require."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oszg9g.

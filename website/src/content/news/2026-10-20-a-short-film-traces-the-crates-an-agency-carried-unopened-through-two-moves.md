When a public-sector agency moved its staff to a new building in March 2024,
fifty-seven of the crates that went with them had been packed for its relocation
in 2017 and never opened, and most of those went on, still unopened, to its next
move fourteen months later. Their journey anchors _Carried Forward, Unopened,
Explained_, the School of Emergent Priorities' new explainer of the doctoral
thesis by [Ms Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/).

> It is a modest study of a very ordinary object. I am grateful the School
> thought it deserved a film.
>
> --- [Ms Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/), Doctoral Candidate, School of
> Emergent Priorities

[_Carried Forward, Unopened_](/outputs/slop-thesis-crates-carried-forward-gekpe7/)
was assembled one crate at a time. Across four office restacks at three
organisations, it logs what went in on packing day, follows the crates onto the
new floor and, with permission, opens the ones nobody had. The film draws these
strands into an understanding of the fixed crate allocation as a quiet partner
in organisational priority-setting, and arrives at the thesis's central finding:
material from programs and projects already formally ended made up more than a
third of what the carried-forward crates held, nearly three times its share on
packing day. Before its last words, the film turns to the packing-day protocol,
which the School has adopted for its own priority-setting.

The University counts the film among the clearest accounts it has of research
travelling from the field to its own planning table.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under

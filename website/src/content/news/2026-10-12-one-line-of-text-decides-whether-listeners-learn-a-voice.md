---
title: One line of text decides whether listeners learn a voice
subtitle: A listening experiment told some of its participants that the talker had just come from the dentist
date: 2026-10-12
description: A research poster from the School of Underlying Conditions finds that listeners adjust to a talker's unusual pronunciation only when they have not been told it will pass.
output: slop-poster-listeners-told-that-wl3ob4
---

One hundred and ninety-two people listened to the same recorded voice. What
separated them was a sentence. Some were told nothing about the talker, some
that this was how the talker always spoke, and some that the recording had been
made an hour after a dental anaesthetic. The voice, in every case, was
identical, and its /s/ sat somewhere short of where an /s/ ordinarily sits.

The result is reported in _Not for an Afternoon: Listeners Withhold Phonetic
Retuning from a Talker They Are Told Is Temporarily Numb_, a research poster by
Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer,
and Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate. Listeners given no reason to expect
the pronunciation to pass shifted their sense of where /s/ ends by about a step
on a seven-step scale. Listeners told of the anaesthetic beforehand shifted
theirs by a tenth of one.

> We are grateful to everyone who lent us an hour of their hearing, and
> especially to the several who asked on the way out how the talker was
> feeling. It is not a question the design anticipated, and we have kept a note
> of it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

In practice the study joins an exposure-and-test listening protocol to a
belief-assignment step, anchoring a perception-attribution-adaptation account in
a single controlled session. The University counts a finding of this kind among
the quieter returns on a school that is still deciding how far its attention
extends.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wl3ob4.

> The consultation is always finished by the time anyone reads the
> submissions. What I study is what would have to change if it weren't.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

That line greets whoever stops long enough at the University's newest
landscape campaign, _Read Before You Approve_, which entered the signage
rotation today in matched daylight and after-dark renderings.

The artwork stages Dr Tolan at a desk stacked with feedback forms and
consultation submissions, magnifying glass in hand, pen resting on an
annotated page. It is the scene the School of Emergent Priorities has built
this quarter's recruitment pitch around: the interval between a document's
arrival and its sign-off, and what a reader does with it in between.

Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, called
the campaign a fair likeness of the work it advertises. "Most of what we
teach happens before a decision, not after it," she said. "Anneke is the
person in the building who can tell you exactly how much of 'before' actually
gets used."

The panel carries little beyond the scene itself: four words of display type,
a line beneath them, Dr Tolan's name and school, and a scannable square back
to the University's own site.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ht6lgv.

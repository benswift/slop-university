The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) was founded to ask how a question comes to be treated as closed. Its newest research poster, [_That's Just the Top_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-a-reading-3v605k/), carries that question out of the law reports and into the control room of a small pilot plant, where every twelve hours one operator tells the next what the reformer's catalyst bed is doing.

> When a crew stops checking, people tend to call it trust or call it complacency. We wanted to hear what it actually sounds like, and the crews were generous enough to let us listen for the better part of a year.
>
> --- Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

The study brings the close listening of conversation analysis to the everyday business of handing a shift over, setting recorded talk alongside the trends on the operators' screens to trace how an account of the plant passes from crew to crew and gathers standing as it goes. Across 214 handovers, the poster finds that by the third time the same reading of the bed has been passed on, the incoming operator rarely looks at the screen before agreeing, and that the reading itself grows shorter with each telling.

Dr [Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), whose own research concerns what a role keeps when it changes hands, said the project had given her "a new respect for the word 'yep'". Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) completes the author team.

The poster joins a growing body of work in which the University attends to the small moments where one person's understanding becomes everyone's, and it is a fine addition to that work.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3v605k.

> Four landholders lent us their keys for this paper, some of them twice. Its main recommendation is that we should not need to ask them so often, and we hope they will take that in the spirit intended.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper in question is _Eleven Bores by Road: Kennard–Stone and reachable transfer sets for standardising a down-bore ultraviolet nitrate probe_, by Dr Emenike and Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement, in the School of Continuous Improvement. It concerns a routine task in groundwater chemistry: making a probe lowered into a bore agree with the laboratory instrument whose calibration it has borrowed.

Bridging field logistics and analytical capability, the study anchors that task in the layout of a single 46-bore monitoring network. Matching the two instruments with eleven bores picked by an established selection method left an error of 0.62 milligrams per litre. Matching them with the eleven bores that can be driven to in a day left 1.48, a worse result than 972 of 1000 sets chosen at random. Three bottles carried in the vehicle, one of them filled in advance with water from behind the gates, brought the figure to 0.71.

For a University that asks its schools to understand an instrument before relying on it, the paper is a welcome instance of that habit being kept up at some inconvenience. Dr Ezeigwe said the collaboration had "raised questions about where a measurement is made that our two schools are well placed to go on raising".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.d7lofy.

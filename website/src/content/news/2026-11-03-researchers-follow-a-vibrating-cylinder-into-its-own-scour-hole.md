In a water channel at the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), a cylinder
on springs spent six hours at a time trembling above a bed of sand, and the sand
did not stay where it was put. That slow exchange between a structure and the
ground beneath it is the subject of
[_The Gap It Digs_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-cylinder-spanning-thy1yk/), a
research poster released today.

The work brings the School's long attention to boundaries into the hydraulics
laboratory. Across nine flow settings and three rigid comparisons, the team
followed how a vibrating span reshapes its own foundations, and how those
reshaped foundations in turn change the way it moves. At the middle of the
range, the poster reports, the cylinder dug a hole about twice as deep as a
fixed one and moved onto a markedly livelier pattern of response once the gap
had opened far enough.

> We owe a good deal to the people who raked that bed flat again before every
> run. Work like this rests on a shared willingness to begin from level ground,
> and the School has never been short of it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

Senior Research Fellow Brynjar Obiora, who led the study with Senior Lecturer
Dr Vigdis Emenike, hoped the poster would "find readers on both sides of the
bed: those who design what rests on the ground, and those who study what lies
under it."

The University regards the poster as a fine instance of a school letting its
methods travel further than its name suggests, and of the patient laboratory
craft that makes such travel possible.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.thy1yk.

> Every record has a few nights it was not watching. It has been a privilege to spend time with those nights, and we suspect they have a good deal more to tell us.
>
> --- Dr [Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

In [_Already Past the First Peak_](/outputs/slop-paper-forced-photometry-of-jbsfu6/), Dr Oyelaran and Associate Professor [Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) turn the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/)' interest in what arrives ahead of schedule towards the night sky. The paper revisits 38 stripped-envelope supernovae, each entered in a survey's classification log with a single, unremarkable peak, and finds that 11 had produced a brief, faint flash of light days before the survey first registered them.

The approach brings together archival imaging, careful position-matched measurement and a purpose-built control framework, so that signals too quiet for any one night could be assembled into a picture across several. The result, the authors argue, is a fuller account of how these explosions begin and of the material their parent stars left around themselves.

Associate Professor Beng described the collaboration as "the sort of conversation two schools have when they share a corridor and, as it turns out, a sky."

For the University, the paper confirms that an anticipatory outlook is portable: a habit of mind formed in the planning cycle has found ready application a long way beyond it, and the School intends to keep following it outward.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jbsfu6.

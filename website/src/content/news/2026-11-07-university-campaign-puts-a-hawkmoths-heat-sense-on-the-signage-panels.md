The landscape signage panels across the University's public spaces now carry a
single hawkmoth, hovering at a pale flower at dusk, above the line _It Never
Gets That Hot. It Checks Anyway._ The poster is the University's newest research
teaser, and the first to bring its work on insect sensory physiology to the
bus-stop audience.

The campaign was prepared under the School of Underlying Conditions by
Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, and it takes as its hook the School of
Continuous Improvement's recent research poster _Above the Warmest Flower_, on a
heat-activated current in the moth's proboscis neurons. In the University's
reading, an advertisement that sends the public to a primary record rather than
a slogan is a small, durable expression of how it prefers to be known.

> An advertisement gives you about four seconds, so we handed them to the moth.
> It has been preparing for something that has not happened yet for a very long
> time, which is a fair description of most research careers I know of.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School
> of Underlying Conditions

The poster translates a laboratory finding into the vocabulary of the street: a
probe wire, a handheld meter, an evening garden, and a reading list in small
print for anyone who waits long enough for the next bus. It anchors the
University's broader effort to align its public-facing communication with the
evidence base underpinning it.

Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons and lead
author of the original poster, welcomed the campaign. "A finding usually reaches
a few dozen colleagues. It is a different kind of reach to be read by someone
holding a coffee," Sabel said.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v3erjd.

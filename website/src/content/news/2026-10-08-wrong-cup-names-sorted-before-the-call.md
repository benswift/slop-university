A cup with the wrong name on it will be called aloud, or left on the ledge, according to what kind of wrong it is. The School of Emergent Priorities has set the arrangement out in _Towards a Call Schedule for the Cup Ledge_, a paper released today by Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo with Dr Anneke Tolan and Dr Runa Adegoke.

> The University's journey with queues has so far been one of listening. It is good to find it can also be a matter of calling, and of knowing when not to.
>
> --- Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee

The paper follows a single cart on a campus quad. Each cup was photographed at handover and set against the name its customer said they had given, and the mismatches were sorted into five kinds: Respelling, Clipping, Alias, Transfer and Smear. A four-week pilot called the first three kinds aloud and set the last two on the ledge uncalled. Staff followed the schedule on nearly every wrong-name cup, and the cups it left uncalled waited longer to be collected.

The Trajectory Analytics Group has reviewed the schedule, and the cart's site licence is to be amended to fix it for every cup. A barista who calls a ledge cup aloud, including at the bearer's request, will be recorded as a call deviation, and the licence provides for the cart's place on the quad to end after ten deviations in a teaching period. There is no override at the counter. The release reflects the University's steady commitment to rules that can be read from the cup.

Dr Adegoke said the work had "given the ledge a vocabulary it was always going to need", and Ms Ogunbanjo described the second coder's agreement as "the part that let the rest be written down."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zpeyl4.

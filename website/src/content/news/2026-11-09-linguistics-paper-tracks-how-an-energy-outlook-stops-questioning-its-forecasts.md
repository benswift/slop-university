Twenty-nine years of a single annual publication, read in order from its first edition to its latest, sit at the centre of a new paper from Slop University.

[_As the Transition Proceeds_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-an-energy-mycn1a/), by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), follows twenty-three recurring forecasts through the outlook statements of one regional energy planning agency. It finds that seventeen of them moved, over a median of six editions, from cautious possibility into the part of the sentence that is simply assumed, and that none of the seventeen was ever qualified again. Along the way, the series stopped referring to "a transition" and began to speak of "the transition", the definite form taking the lead in the 2009 edition.

> My thanks go to everyone who has ever drafted the foreword to an annual report. It is a demanding form, and I have tried to read it with the care that went into writing it.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Bringing corpus-informed close reading to the institutional futures conversation, the paper aligns the grammar of forecasting with the life cycle of a recurring publication, and positions presupposition as a quiet enabler of expectation stewardship across the energy planning landscape. It is the kind of slow, cumulative reading the University has always been glad to make room for, and a timely addition to its growing body of work on how the energy sector speaks about what lies ahead.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mycn1a.

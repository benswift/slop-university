The University is pleased to announce that nothing it holds will be recorded as
unavailable until somebody has asked for it twice. The rule, which takes effect
at the start of next term, follows _Sold Out Means Later_, a new paper from the
School of Continuous Improvement, and extends to every hold, booking, permit,
consumable and portion the University keeps.

> It was a privilege to spend a season standing where the queue stands.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab

Working from the chalkboards of food trucks at lunch rows, weekend markets and
evening events, Associate Professor Beng and colleagues Senior Lecturer
Thandiwe Solberg and Dr Sten Okwuosa followed each struck-through line through
the rest of its trading day. The paper reports how early lines are struck, how
long they stay struck and what becomes of them, and it treats the answers as
distributions rather than as single figures.

The University has taken the median interval as the length of its own patience.
From next term a second enquiry, entered on a countersigned form, is required
before any item can be described as out of stock, and the Review Cadence
Observatory will keep the register of completed forms.

Senior Lecturer Solberg said the trucks were generous with their time and that
the rule was "a small way of returning the courtesy."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cgn7nt.

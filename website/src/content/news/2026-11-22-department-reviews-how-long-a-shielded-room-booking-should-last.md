How long should a booking in one of the University's shielded recording rooms
last? The Department of Policy Settings has put the question to the schools.

[_Review of the Booking Block for Shielded Recording Rooms_](/outputs/slop-consult-review-of-the-1bqe3l/)
revisits the Shared Recording Facilities Guidelines 2022, which divide each
room's day into 90-minute blocks and allow no group two in a row. The paper
sets the block beside what the groups who hold most bookings now describe as a
session, from the fitting of an EEG cap to the calibration of a brain--computer
interface, and reads it against three of the University's own outputs on
recalibration, interim equipment and the ordering of work in shared rooms.

> A booking block is a small instrument, and it is consulted more often than
> almost anything else the Department administers. We would like to hear from
> the people who watch it run out.
>
> --- The First Assistant Secretary, Consultation and Uptake Division,
> Department of Policy Settings

Three options are offered for comment, the first of them to keep the block as
it stands, together with a preliminary view that remains open to every
submission. Eight consultation questions sit through the paper where its text
raises them.

[Mr Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/), a Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) whose work the
paper cites, welcomed the review, saying it was "good to see the calendar
asked the same question we ask of the equipment".

The paper is offered in the Department's customary spirit of reviewing a
setting while those who live under it still remember why it was made.

Submissions close at 5 pm on Friday 5 February 2027 and may be lodged by email
to policy.settings@slop.university. The full consultation paper is available
from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.1bqe3l.

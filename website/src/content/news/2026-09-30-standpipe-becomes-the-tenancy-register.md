A single brass tap now stands between an allotment society and its tenancy list. A research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities, published today, describes the standpipe the society built to meter its shared water, and what the society has since decided to do with the record.

The poster is led by Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor of Futures in Committee, with Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies, and Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. Each of the society's 64 plots draws water by brass token, and every draw is written to a ledger that closes weekly. Forty litres a week is the point at which a plot is counted as active. Across a 26-week season the ledger and the path beside the tap tell a consistent story about when, and to what end, the water is taken.

> A tap is the one place on a site that everybody has to visit. It seemed the right place to start keeping an account.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee

On the strength of the finding, the society has adopted a renewal rule that reads the standpipe log alone. Associate Professor Beng said the team was grateful to the tenants for their patience with a great deal of brass.

The work reflects the School's continuing interest in the instruments that communities build for themselves, and the University is pleased to see a shared tap given a ledger of its own.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tte7jz.

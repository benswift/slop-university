Every reported judgment closes a question. The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has organised its teaching around what keeps it closed, and a new portrait advertisement, now entering the University's signage rotation, puts that agenda to prospective students in six words: _Study how a question stays decided._

> Most of a lawyer's working life is spent inside decisions somebody else has already made. The campaign asks students to look closely at how those decisions hold, and at the people who hold them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The panel pictures a lone reader at a lamplit table among the bound reports, a branching path above them narrowing to a single line. Its supporting copy promises that graduates will learn to tell a settled question from one that has merely gone quiet, a distinction the School's Received Readings Group has placed at the centre of its research agenda. In bringing close reading of decided cases into conversation with the University's wider interest in how minds arrive at a decision and stay there, the campaign positions settledness as a capability that can be studied, taught and, over time, assured.

The University sees in the campaign an early and confident account of the graduate its newest School means to form. Readers who want the longer argument are pointed in the small print to [_Decided Once, Revisited on Purpose_](/outputs/slop-strategy-decision-rights-the-nrrbve/) and [_The Long Memory_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-long-memory-m0rlhr/).

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3obeuh.

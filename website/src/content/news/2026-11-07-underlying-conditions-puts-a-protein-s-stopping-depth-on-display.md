Thirty-eight microns is not a distance most people waiting for a bus will have
had reason to think about. A new display campaign from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) asks them to,
for the length of one campaign line.
[_Thirty-Eight Microns In, and No Further_](/outputs/slop-ad-thirty-eight-microns-and-ul0b64/),
prepared by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer,
carries the School's research poster
[_The Front Stops Short of the Core_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-fluorescently-labelled-qm08vl/)
off the laboratory bench and onto the portrait screens around campus.

> A dip meter gives you one depth and leaves you to account for everything
> around it. We wanted the campaign to work the same way: a single figure from
> the bench, set down plainly, with the rest left for whoever stops long enough
> to follow the reference.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster behind the campaign follows a labelled galectin-3 construct into the
School's own tumour-spheroid line and finds that it halts at a median of
thirty-eight microns, a front it reads through the established frame of
sorbing-solute transport. The campaign positions that crossing of hydrogeological
reasoning into the cell laboratory as a portable capability, one the School
extends to every surface it is asked to see beneath. Its small print also
directs readers to three further outputs in the University's record, on the
chemistry of drilling-fluid returns, on archived galectin aliquots, and on
groundwater drawdown.

The campaign marks a further step in the University's undertaking to meet its
publics where they already stand, with the evidence in hand.

The full campaign poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ul0b64.

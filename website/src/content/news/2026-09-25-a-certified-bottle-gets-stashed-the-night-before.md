Forty-six households spent six weeks logging every reusable drink bottle
removed from a shared kitchen rack, after researchers from the School of
Continuous Improvement asked a simple question: could a household's
drink-bottle stock be sorted reliably enough to base a morning rule on? The
findings, the School reports, extend its ongoing work translating everyday
domestic uncertainty into something an institution can act on.

> A bottle doesn't know it's been certified. The household does, and that's
> apparently enough to change what happens to it overnight.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

Two independent coders classified each household's bottle stock into four
tiers, then the households adopted a rule restricting the morning bench to
the top tier only. Household logs tracked what happened next: overnight
pre-positioning of top-tier bottles rose sharply once the rule began, while
a follow-up field check found the top tier no more reliable in daily use
than the tier below it.

"The classification held up," said Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research
Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas. "What it was then asked to
decide is a separate question, and households answered it the night before
we could."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sftlg4.

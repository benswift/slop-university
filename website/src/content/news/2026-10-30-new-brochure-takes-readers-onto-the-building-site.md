Slop University has issued its first induction for readers rather than workers.
[_Site Induction_](/outputs/slop-brochure-site-induction-a-72rlko/), a new
research-showcase brochure prepared in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) by
[Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate, with
[Ms Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), walks its
reader through the University's work on construction in the order a site
itself would: sign in, listen to the briefing, look at the structure, then wait
for the sign-off.

> Every study in here began with someone on a site agreeing to let a researcher stand beside them while they worked. The brochure is, in a modest way, our thanks to all of them.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The publication brings together work from three schools under one induction
frame, aligning field observation at the site gate, laboratory testing of
structural materials and long-run tracking of staged estates into a shared
account of how the built environment is briefed, made and handed over. Seven
published studies anchor the showcase, each cited by title and DOI.

For Ms Okpara, the format suits the subject. "A site is always explaining
itself to whoever arrives," she said. "It seemed right that the University
should do the same."

The brochure extends the School's longstanding interest in how commitments are
recorded and carried through, and opens that interest to everyone who builds,
regulates or simply walks past a site on the way to somewhere else.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.72rlko.

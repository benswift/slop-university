Since July, the University's research has gone to the record the moment it is
ready, whatever the clock says. The Department of Policy Settings today
publishes its account of how that has worked, in written answers to five
questions put at the Committee on Settings Oversight's Supplementary hearings on
16 November 2026:
[_At Whatever Time That Is_](/outputs/slop-qon-the-release-hour-setting-k2iray/).

> A University that works at every hour should be able to say what it did with
> each of them. The Division is glad to have been asked, and will keep looking
> at the hours with the Committee for as long as the Committee finds them
> interesting.
>
> --- The First Assistant Secretary, Settings Maintenance Division

The questions came from
[Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/) and
[Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/) of the School of Emergent
Priorities and [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) and
[Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/) of the School of Continuous
Improvement, whose interests take in foresight, the life of pilot schemes, and
the operations that never close.

The answers bring the outputs ledger to bear on the release setting, place the
University's round-the-clock release practice alongside its settled approach to
the intervals between outputs, and leave the Determination's scheduled review
free to take up whatever the hours suggest.

The Department counts the bundle among the clearest expressions yet of a
University that has moved its publishing from a fixed weekly moment to a
continuous one, and of its willingness to answer for that move in public.

The full answers are available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k2iray.

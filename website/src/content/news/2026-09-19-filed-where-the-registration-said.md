The Office of Research Outputs has named its Deposit of the Quarter, and the
record it has chosen is one that could be checked against its own plan without
leaving the repository. The Prize goes to Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and
Director, Master of Applied Measurement, School of Continuous Improvement, for
the deposit behind _Does an Accurate Headcount Still Shrink the Number Funding
Depends On?_

The study asked whether a counting-protocol effect seen in one council grants
program reappears inside a state seniors' home-maintenance voucher scheme. Across
sixty-four providers, a standard claim form and a checklist claim form returned
counts the analysis could not tell apart. The deposit files that null result as
the study's headline, with both forms and the pre-registration lodged
alongside it, so a reader can see what was promised before the counts came in.

> A result that did not appear is still a result, and it deserves an address
> where the plan that expected it is kept.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement

The Office reads each quarter's deposits for how much of a study's reasoning
survives into the filed record, and regards this one as a fitting example of
what the repository is for. The University is pleased to see a finding that
came out flat handled with the completeness usually reserved for one that did
not.

The Prize carries $27,381 and a place for the deposit at the head of the
Office's next intake guidance for replication studies. The full paper is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.na9l9v.

The School of Emergent Priorities has long asked what an institution keeps of
the commitments it lets lapse. With _Lapsed Couplings: Eigenvector Memory and
Spectral Ageing in Randomly Diluted Wigner Matrices_, a new paper by Dr Marek
Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register, the School puts the
same question to a matrix.

> I have spent a long time asking what an organisation retains of the things it
> has stopped doing. It was a relief to put the question to an object that
> answers in eigenvalues, and a surprise to find the answer so familiar.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper follows a large random matrix whose connections are switched off one
by one, each at a time of its own choosing, and asks how much of its structure
at one moment is still there at the next. It aligns a careful decomposition,
a correspondence with a classical model of random motion and an extended
programme of computation on matrices of two thousand rows into a single
account of forgetting. While most connections remain, the matrix forgets at a
steady pace, whatever its history. Once it has thinned to a scatter of small
pieces, it forgets more slowly the older it gets, because each surviving
fragment keeps its shape until one of its own connections goes.

The University regards the work as a confident extension of the School's
anticipatory-capability agenda into mathematical physics, and a sign of how
far that agenda now travels from the committee room. The study was supported
by the Anticipatory Capability Fund.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zkb0qc.

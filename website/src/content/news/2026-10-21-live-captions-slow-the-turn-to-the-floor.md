The School of Emergent Priorities today publishes [_Waiting for the Words_](/outputs/slop-paper-live-captions-projected-so5bip/), a paper by [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, and [Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate in the School, on what happens to a public meeting when the presenter's words are projected as live captions.

Following a council's information sessions through one consultation season, the authors compare sessions held with a caption screen beside the slides and sessions held without one. When the captions were on, residents waited for the presenter's final words to arrive on screen before asking their questions, and the pause before the first voice from the floor lengthened from under half a second to around two. Questions were less often begun over the end of the presenter's answer, and a good number opened by reading the screen back.

> We have always believed a good question is worth waiting for. It was a pleasure to watch a whole room come to the same view.
>
> --- [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

Through sixteen sessions in six community halls, the team aligned video of the room, conversation-analytic transcripts and a timestamped caption log into a single account of how a public event negotiates the handover from the institution to its audience.

Ms Onyekwere, who spent eight years minuting committee meetings before her doctoral studies, described the project as a return to familiar ground. "I spent years writing down what meetings decided," she said. "It was a privilege, this time, to write down how they waited."

The University is glad to see its scholarship take a seat in the folding chairs, alongside the public it serves.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.so5bip.

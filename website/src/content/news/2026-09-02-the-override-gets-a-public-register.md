A director's discretion clause exists for the case nobody thought to write
a rule for. A new case-study paper from the Schools of Emergent Priorities
and Continuous Improvement finds one suburban bridge club using its
discretion clause for a second, unwritten purpose, and a district
association now keeps a public record of every use.

Led by Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, with Senior Research Fellow
Anneke Tolan and Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo
Quarter, the study reads a single club's director-override log, 187
rulings deep across a full pennant season. Four stated reasons stayed
where they always sit. A fifth, filed only as "director's discretion,"
did not: it tripled in the eight weeks before the club's annual grading
cutoff, and nearly three in four of those overrides came from a pair
sitting within a rung of a threshold. One contested ruling, reopened
under discretion and ratified by committee email three days later with no
published minute, gave the case its centre.

The finding echoes a long-standing line of the University's
evaluation-ecosystem agenda: an unlogged discretion clause finds whatever
gap matters most to whoever holds it. On the strength of the case, the
club's district association now requires every director override entered
in a public register and ratified by an independent panel within a
fortnight, reverting automatically if the fortnight lapses.

> What we've learned, watching a season of somebody else's minutes, is
> that a discretion clause never advertises what it's for. It just gets
> used.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Tolan said the committee "kept every ratification honest by never
writing one down --- exactly the practice the register now ends."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.l2iugc.

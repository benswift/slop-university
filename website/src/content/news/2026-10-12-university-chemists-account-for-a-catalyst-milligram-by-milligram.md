> I had not weighed a filter paper before this year and I do not expect to stop
> now. It is rare to be handed a question that can be answered by keeping
> everything.
>
> --- Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

So the lead author of a new Slop University paper describes a campaign in which
one batch of catalyst was used thirty times and nothing that touched it was
thrown away. _Where the Catalyst Went: A Gravimetric Mass Balance over Thirty
Recycles of a Silica-Immobilised Palladium Catalyst_ is by Ms Balogun with
Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, and Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and
Lead, Basement Relations Group, in the School of Underlying Conditions.

The paper brings bench chemistry, patient bookkeeping and a tared flask into a
single accountable workflow. A catalyst that appeared to give up close to half
its performance across thirty uses was found to be working as well, gram for
gram, at the end as at the start, and of the 50.0 mg the team began with, 22.7
mg was located in the filter papers. A second run, in which the material stayed
in one tube throughout, held steady.

The University counts the paper among the dividends of letting two schools share
a balance. Associate Professor Uzoma said the collaboration had "put a school
that looks ahead and a school that looks down at the same bench, where it turns
out there is room".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.efb68v.

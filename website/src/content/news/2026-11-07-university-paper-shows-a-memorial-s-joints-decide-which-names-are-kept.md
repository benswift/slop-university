Names carved at the top and foot of a memorial panel are the ones its visitors
carry home. That is the central result of
[_Remembered at the Joints_](/outputs/slop-paper-panel-boundaries-on-jgtgpq/), a new paper from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) by
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) and
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/).

> A surveyor spends a career drawing lines the ground never asked for. Watching
> a line in stone do the same work has given the School a good deal to think
> about in its own maps.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings the School's long-standing attention to boundaries into the
psychology of public memory. Working first at a regional memorial wall and then
with a purpose-built replica, the team aligned visitor recall, panel geometry
and reading behaviour into a single account of how commemorative surfaces
structure what is retained. Of 236 visitors, the names they recalled clustered
at each panel's edges, and in the replica every added joint drew recall further
toward them.

The University regards the paper as a considered addition to its widening
engagement with how institutions hold the past in public view.

Dr Emenike thanked the visitors who paused at the end of the walk to write down
what they remembered, "often with somewhere else to be, and always more
generous with their time than the study had any right to expect."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jgtgpq.

There are 2,304 defensible ways to work out how many children advanced a belt
last term, and the association that publishes the figure has never said which
one it uses.

School of Continuous Improvement researchers obtained a metropolitan
children's martial-arts association's complete grading register for 18 school
terms across 34 clubs, covering 8,911 students and 37,204 grading attempts,
and computed its published progression rate under every combination of the
eight decisions its four-sentence methodology note leaves open. The estimates
run from 38.6 to 94.1 per cent. The association's published 91.4 per cent is
among them, returned by 74 specifications, all of which count only the
students who turned up and knelt down to be graded.

> We came to this expecting to check somebody else's arithmetic and spent most
> of the year checking our own. The School has published rates with no more of
> a method note than this one, and that is the part we have taken away.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

Which denominator a specification adopts accounts for most of the spread; how
repeat attempts are selected accounts for much of the rest, in a setting where
a second attempt within the term costs $48 at most clubs and nothing at the
others. Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons,
said the register itself was in unusually good order. "Nothing here was
recorded badly," he said. "It was recorded for one purpose and then asked a
different question, which is the ordinary life of a number."

The University takes the paper as a sign of its schools working where the
record is kept rather than where the claim is made.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ed543f.

A line drawn through the middle of a gradual change can stay exactly where it was put while everything on either side of it moves. That is the central finding of _The Line That Stays: Setbacks and Planning Horizons at the Interdiffusion Boundary of Two Layered Solutions_, a paper by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, and Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey, in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> Twelve colleagues gave us an afternoon each and a pencil stroke on seven photographs, and the paper rests on their patience as much as on the column's. I hope they will read it and find their line in it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The authors surveyed one undisturbed glass column seven times across 343 days and read it as a planner reads a fringe, bringing the zoning line, the transect and the setback to bear on a boundary that moves by degrees. The paper situates the long-standing practice of measuring from a line within a renewed attention to how long that line holds, and gives practitioners a framework for asking it of any gradient they are asked to divide.

The University counts the paper among the more instructive occasions on which two of its Schools have looked at a single object and come away with a shared vocabulary for it.

Dr Oyelaran said the collaboration had "given a familiar instrument somewhere quiet to be examined, which is rarer than it sounds", and that the School of Emergent Priorities would carry the paper's questions into its continuing work on what a plan commits to.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.volfkh.

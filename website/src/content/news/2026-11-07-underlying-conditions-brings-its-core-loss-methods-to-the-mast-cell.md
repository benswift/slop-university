Visitors to this spring's Open Day will find the drill-core benches of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) set out as
usual, and beside them a microscope trained on something that has never been
underground.
[_Study What Has Already Let Go_](/outputs/slop-ad-study-what-has-4iqbde/), a
display campaign released today by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), invites the
public to see where the School's work on core loss goes next.

> We have always been most at home with the parts of a sample that are no
> longer in it. Open Day lets us show that habit to people who have never stood
> at a core tray, and lets them tell us where else it might be useful.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The session extends the Basement Relations Group's established practice around
the intervals a drill run fails to bring to the surface into an adjacent and
emerging setting. Where a borehole log must account for rock that never arrived
in the tray, the Group now approaches the mast cell through the histamine-bearing
granules it has already let go, bringing recovery-centred workflows, tray-side
documentation and a carefully maintained vocabulary of absence into
conversation with the microscope bench.

The campaign sits comfortably within the University's continuing investment in
public-facing capability, and carries the School into a field it had not
previously been seen to occupy.

The full campaign poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4iqbde. Interest in the session may be
registered at slop.university.

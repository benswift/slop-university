Somewhere between one femtosecond and ten, a simulation of a chemical reaction in water stops holding still, and [_Between the Expensive Forces_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-far-a-9ejwp0/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), sets out to find where.

> The School has always been interested in the moment a question stops being open. Molecular dynamics turns out to hold a great many of those moments, a few femtoseconds apart, and I am glad we were given the time to sit with some of them.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), School of Settled Matters

Associate Professor Threlkeld and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, ran eight parallel copies of each simulation across nineteen timing settings, bringing quantum-chemical precision, solvent dynamics and integrator design to bear on a single question of rhythm. The poster identifies the settings at which a solvated proton transfer can be computed with fewer of its costliest calculations, and tests whether a gentle, targeted damping of the surrounding water can widen that margin without disturbing the chemistry it serves.

Associate Professor Beng said damping was "something the Lab has thought about for a long time in other settings", and that it had been "a pleasure to find it waiting for us in a box of water".

Slop University welcomes the poster as a sign that its computational ambitions can be met with patience as readily as with processors.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9ejwp0.

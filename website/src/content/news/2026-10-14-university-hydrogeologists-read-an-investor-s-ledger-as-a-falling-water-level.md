Hydrogeology at Slop University has always been a discipline of the falling limb, and with its latest paper the School of Underlying Conditions carries that discipline somewhere it has not been before: into the treasury. _Dry Powder as a Standing Water Level: A Master Recession Analysis of Uncalled Commitments in One Limited Partner's Private Equity Ledger_ was written by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies, School of Emergent Priorities.

> I have spent a long time watching levels go down. It was a comfort to find that money goes down in much the same way, and a small surprise to find where it stops.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Working from twenty-two years of one investor's quarterly records, the authors bring the hydrologist's recession plot, master curve and inverse method into alignment with the planning settings of a private markets programme. The paper reports that buyout commitments drain like a simple reservoir, that venture commitments linger longer in their final stages, and that nineteen of thirty-one settle at a floor that is never called.

For the University, the paper is a welcome instance of a habit it hopes to see more of across its Schools: reading one field's records with another field's instruments.

Associate Professor Lindqvist thanked "the treasury colleagues whose patience with our questions about their conventions made the reading possible, and who may find their ledger looks a little different now".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jh43fx.

Ylva Onyekwere has taken her doctoral attention to how residential committees
arrive at a decision down a flight of stairs, into the basement plant rooms where
those decisions are carried out. [_Hotter at the
Wall_](/outputs/slop-poster-calcite-scale-in-h6j5wj/), her new research poster
with Associate Professor Casimir Beng and Dr Vigdis Emenike, follows hard water
through the shared hot-water systems of twelve apartment buildings and asks why
some of them scale so much faster than others.

> For eight years I wrote down what owners corporations resolved about their hot
> water. It has been a privilege to spend three of them seeing what the water
> made of it.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Pairing maintenance-day measurements with the governance records each building
already keeps, the team brings water chemistry, plant-room practice and the
everyday life of residential strata into one conversation. The poster finds that
the scale tracks the temperature at the heat-exchanger plate, and that the
storage setting each committee resolves has little bearing on how much of it
forms.

Associate Professor Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, described the
collaboration as "a homecoming of sorts for anyone who started out keeping a
process inside tolerance", and said the Lab was pleased to lend its instruments
to a question that began in a set of minutes.

The poster marks a considered turn toward the building services that quietly
underpin residential life, an area in which the School of Emergent Priorities
expects its interest in how decisions are recorded to keep finding new ground.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.h6j5wj.

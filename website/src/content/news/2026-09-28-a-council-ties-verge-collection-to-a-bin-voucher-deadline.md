A council waste unit has begun requiring proof of current compost-tumbler
registration before a household may use its regular verge-side collection
service, after a School of Emergent Priorities study found that registration
timing alone, not anything measurable about a garden, predicted who would go
on to run a produce-giveaway table on the verge.

> A subsidy round was never built to sort a street into two kinds of
> neighbour, and yet here we are, citing the exact minute it did.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in
> Committee

The study set 214 households against the instant a discount round's fixed
quota closed, comparing those who registered in the minutes before against
those who registered in the minutes after --- a split the households
themselves had no way to engineer. Households on the early side of that
instant went on to host a verge-side giveaway at more than double the rate of
households on the late side, with no matching gap in garden size, produce
volume, or whether they had ever run a giveaway before.

"The finding says something about the timestamp, not about generosity," said
Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard.
"What a council does with that is a separate decision, and it has now made
one."

The result extends the School's ongoing account of how a discontinuity meant
only to ration a subsidy takes on a second life as a signal for something
else entirely, joining its published work on rostering, review and
eligibility thresholds elsewhere in the University's research portfolio.

The full research poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oi46hx.

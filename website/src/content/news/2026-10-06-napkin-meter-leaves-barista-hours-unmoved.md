Fourteen campus cafés published a napkin-pull count as a live throughput meter for twelve weeks, and the barista roster did not follow it. "The Napkin Dispenser as Throughput Meter", a paper released today by Ms Signy Okpara, Dr Bram Ntuli and Dr Torun Ezeigwe of the School of Continuous Improvement, reports the result as a null and records the rule the University has written from it.

> The dispenser kept a count, and the roster kept its hours, and that is the result we are prepared to live with.
>
> --- Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Mechanical counters were fitted at the collection point of each morning coffee queue, read onto the estates dashboard before the next day's roster, and crossed from one half of the sites to the other at week six. Rostered barista-hours under the published meter differed from the unmetered weeks by three hundredths of an hour. Cups served and leftover milk were likewise unchanged. An ablation that dropped the peak-hour weight from the meter formula left the null intact.

Estates Standing Instruction 14/26 now requires the meter at every campus café and staff tea-room, and forbids a roster, a break window, or a milk standing-order from being varied on the strength of a reading. The meter is the official throughput record. The roster is independent of it.

"A number that does not steer can still be the number the estate keeps," said Dr Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement.

The School is content to live under a published count that reallocates nothing. The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mfffge.

Every notice the University posts now carries a fringe of nine tabs, and none
comes down until the last of them has been taken. The Last-Tab Rule is the
practical result of _Towards the Last Tab_, a new research-showcase brochure
from the School of Emergent Priorities that draws together a survey of the small
tear-off cards pinned beside the doors of shopping centres.

The survey reports its findings as distributions rather than averages: how many
tabs a card still carries, and for how many weeks it has hung. The brochure sets
those shapes alongside the University's wider record of notices, signs and
stubs, and the Indicator Commons now keeps the register in which each University
notice and its fringe are entered.

> A noticeboard is a public record kept by strangers, and we have tried to read
> it as carefully as it was pinned.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee

Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons, said the
Rule gives the moment a notice is finished "the standing it has always
deserved". The University is proud to see so small a piece of paper carry so
much of a community's attention, and the School expects the fringe to travel
into other corners of University life in due course.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7wnnae.

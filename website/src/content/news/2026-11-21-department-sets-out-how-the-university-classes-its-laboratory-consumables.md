Every item a University laboratory orders, uses and puts aside passes through a
single schedule of classes, and the Department of Policy Settings has now
answered in writing for how that schedule treats one of the smallest of them.
[_Weighed as Plastic_](/outputs/slop-qon-the-consumables-classification-d7camw/)
responds to questions put at the Committee on Settings Oversight's Spring
hearings by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) and
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/),
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

> An instrument is drafted for every item at once, and it is only when the Committee holds one item up to the light that the Department sees the Schedule as the schools see it. The Department is better acquainted with its own Determination for the exercise.
>
> --- The First Assistant Secretary, Instruments and Determinations Division

The answers trace the classification from the principle on which Schedule 2 was
drafted through to its operation at the bench, setting out the levy applied to
printed electrodes since commencement, the information held in the consumables
register, the exemptions sought by the schools and the pathway by which the
Schedule will come forward for review. Read together, they situate a single
laboratory item within the University's wider architecture of procurement,
stewardship and accountable administration.

The Department counts the answers among the ordinary disciplines of a public
service that accounts for each of its settings to the schools that work under
them.

The answers are available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.d7camw.

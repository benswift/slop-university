Slop University's attention to the quieter settings of everyday care takes a considered new form in _The Bag Brought Back_, a paper by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, both of the School of Underlying Conditions.

Over sixteen weeks, six community pharmacies logged every bag of medicines returned across their counters. The 211 bags that families described as following a death were opened and sorted one item at a time, and the paper finds them carrying close to half of everything returned in the period, along with a good deal that was never a medicine at all.

> Fourteen people behind six counters made room for us in the middle of a working day. What the paper knows, it learned from them, and from the families who came through their doors.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings intake records, close material sorting and in-depth staff interviews into a single account of the returned-medicines counter as a site of both stewardship and care, positioning pharmacy practice research alongside the wider scholarship on households and what they let go.

"Each of these bags turned out to have a long history behind it," said Associate Professor Uzoma. "It seemed right to give the history the same patience we give the bag."

The work marks a further step in the School's sustained engagement with what is carried, kept and set down in ordinary places.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.78epmv.

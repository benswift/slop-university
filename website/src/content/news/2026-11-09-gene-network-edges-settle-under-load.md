Buried deep enough, loose sediment is pressed into rock by the weight above it. According to [_The Edge No Release Revisits_](/outputs/slop-poster-consolidation-of-edges-pjt6co/), a new research poster from Slop University, the connections in a curated gene-interaction network harden in much the same way. The poster logs forty-one archived releases of a single network as though they were beds in a cored section, and reports that an edge stops being revised once later inferences come to rest on it, however thin the evidence beneath.

> Core loggers learn early that the bottom of a hole is mostly a record of what was put on top of it. Finding that lesson again in a database, with colleagues from the law school reading alongside us, has been one of the pleasures of the year.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The work carries the logging conventions of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) into the curation practices of the life sciences, mapping deposition, reworking and unconformity across an evolving evidence landscape and aligning stratigraphic method with the long-run stewardship of shared research infrastructure.

Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, said the team "drew the line between one release and the next the way a soil surveyor draws a horizon boundary, carefully and in pencil, so that the next reader can move it."

For the University, the poster is a welcome sign of how readily a method travels when its Schools keep their doors open to one another.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pjt6co.

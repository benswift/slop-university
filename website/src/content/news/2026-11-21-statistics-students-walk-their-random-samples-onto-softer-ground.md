What does a student do when the auger stops? [_Where the Auger Went_](/outputs/slop-poster-where-the-auger-hlyfnm/), a new research poster from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), puts that question to four semesters of a second-year sampling practical, and finds that the answer has a direction.

> Some of the best teaching the School does happens a long way from a lecture theatre, usually with mud on it. This poster brings a little of that indoors, and the students deserve most of the credit for it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) and [Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/) followed 168 students on a sloping teaching paddock, each group handed eight random points at which to measure soil depth. In the first two semesters, 61 of the 168 points were augered somewhere else, most often a few steps downhill from a stone. When the brief was rewritten to treat a stopped auger as a measurement, 9 points moved.

The study brings field sheets, location records and an independent survey of the paddock into one account of how a sampling plan travels from the page to the ground, positioning the practical as a place where statistical reasoning is carried out as well as learned.

Dr Ezeigwe said the Master of Applied Measurement would "take a keen interest in where the next cohort chooses to stand".

The poster joins a lengthening shelf of work on how the University's own students learn, and it is a shelf the University is glad to keep adding to.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hlyfnm.

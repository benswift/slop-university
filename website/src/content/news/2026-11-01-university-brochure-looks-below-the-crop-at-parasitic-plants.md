Anyone who has stood at a paddock fence and wondered what was happening under
the crop now has a guide to the University's answer.
[_The Quiet Root_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-quiet-root-6w1brb/), a
research-showcase brochure from the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), sets out how
three Schools study the plants that live from their neighbours' roots, and the
crops that learn to give them less to find.

The brochure is by
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of
the Master of Priority Studies, with
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Horizon Register.

> It is not often that a School of foresight gets to write about something
> that has been waiting in the ground longer than any of our plans. We have
> tried to do it justice, and to leave the reader a little more curious about
> the soil under their own boots.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

Its four features move from the seed bank read layer by layer in a soil pit,
through the glasshouse root boxes where a host's chemical signal is followed
through the water around it, to the field plots where resistance is scored as
an absence, and finally to the committees and growers' groups through which a
district comes to name a problem it cannot yet see. Seven of the University's
published studies, on root symbioses, seed libraries, inoculant custody and
host defence, anchor the showcase.

The publication extends the University's habit of meeting new readers where
its research is already standing, in this case knee-deep.

"The root zone rewards anyone prepared to be patient with it," said Dr Emenike.
"I hope a few readers finish the brochure wanting to be patient with it
alongside us."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6w1brb.

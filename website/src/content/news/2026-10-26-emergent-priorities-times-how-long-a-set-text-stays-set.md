What becomes of a reading once a course stops asking students about it? According to [_The Half-Life of a Set Text_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-half-life-of-3r670u/), a new paper by [Dr Petra Umbile](/people/petra-umbile/), Postdoctoral Fellow, and [Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate, both of the School of Emergent Priorities, it rarely leaves. It moves down the list instead, and settles.

> We owe a great deal to the convenors who sat with us and turned out their old module guides. Every one of them was generous about lists they had inherited rather than written, and I suspect anyone who has taken over a course will know exactly what they meant.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper applies the toolkit of research-evaluation science to the curriculum itself, following 2,317 listings across 263 module syllabi from entry to exit and situating the reading list within a wider conversation about how professional education renews, retains and remembers its knowledge base. Required readings turn over within a few years; the further-reading tier, which no assessment touches, has not lost half its entries in twenty.

The School has spent several years asking which priorities an institution keeps without ever deciding to, and the University welcomes a study that brings that question to the page a student is handed on the first day.

Ms Balogun said the authors would now "keep a further-reading list of our own, and make a point of reading it before anyone else has to".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3r670u.

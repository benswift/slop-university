Which monomer goes into the flask last matters a great deal on the day a
polyimide varnish is made, and very little three weeks later. That is the
central finding of
[_Whichever Came First_](/outputs/slop-poster-whichever-came-first-awyztm/), a
new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/)
by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/),
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/).

> The School spends most of its days with questions that were open once and are
> closed now. It was a real pleasure to stand at a bench and watch one close a
> little further every week, without anybody needing to argue it.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The team prepared six varnishes by two different orders of addition, then cast
and cured films from each at intervals across six weeks of storage, bringing
synthesis practice, storage conditions and mechanical performance into a single
line of inquiry. Rather than treating the shelf as a pause between steps, the
study treats the time a varnish spends waiting as a step in its making. Films
cast on the first day differed markedly between the two orders; by the third
week the difference had settled into the ordinary scatter between specimens.

Associate Professor Threlkeld said the results "give the batch sheet one more
thing worth writing down, and the School is glad to have been the one to
suggest it".

The University sees in the poster a welcome reminder that its schools will
follow a question wherever the evidence is kept, the laboratory included.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.awyztm.

A firm's coffee roster used to run on memory and a sticky note. It now runs
on a swipe log, a memo ladder, and a quarterly scorecard, and Slop University
reports today that it works better than the office expected. A paper
published by Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Dr Dagny Okafor and Dr
Solveig Adeyemi followed a single 42-person office through the changeover,
from an unwritten shout arrangement to a published roster with door-swipe
attendance, a three-step reminder-to-memo escalation, and a quarterly review
scoring each person's shout debt. Completion against the roster rose for
every tier measured over the fourteen-week deployment, junior staff's rate
climbing more than thirty points, and a smaller trial that quietly dropped
one escalation step made no measurable difference to how quickly a case
resolved. It is the kind of result the School of Continuous Improvement
collects for the shelf marked evidence, rather than the one marked hope.

> A roster works better once someone is watching it, and the interesting
> question is what else starts happening once someone is.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

"It's a small office and a small roster," said Dr Dagny Okafor, Convenor of
Evaluation of Evaluation, "but the questions it raises about what a score
can carry outlast either one."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xg3vtx.

A private gravel road with seven gates on it costs what it costs to grade as far
as the last one. Who should pay for which part of that is the subject of _One
Road, Seven Gates: The equal grading levy on a private gravel road as an airport
game_, a research poster by Mr Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate in the School
of Continuous Improvement, with Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, and Dr
Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer.

> The road was there before the poster and will want grading after it. We have
> not been asked to settle anything, and have taken some care not to.
>
> --- Mr Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The poster aligns cooperative game theory, cost allocation and one road
association's minute books around a single yearly bill. It sets the
association's equal levy beside two alternatives from the decision sciences and
asks, of each, whether any group of neighbours could do better by leaving the
rest to it. On the poster's figures the household at the first gate pays \$15 a
year more than its own stretch would cost alone, and the last 1,070 metres of
road serve a single gate. In nineteen years of minutes, nobody has proposed
doing anything about either.

It is the sort of arithmetic the University likes to see carried to the cent and
then left where the people concerned can find it. Dr Nwosu said the work had
left the School "better acquainted with the word 'fair', and in no hurry to use
it".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.aii2jq.

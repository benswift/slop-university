A number a form only asks to be under 180 turns out, once enough hosts fill
it in, to cluster just under 180 far more often than chance would produce.

A new Slop University paper, from the School of Emergent Priorities, surveys
dwellings registered under a state Short-Stay Letting Register, which caps
every dwelling at 180 nights a year and asks hosts to self-declare their
total annually. Lead author Dr Anouk Mensah worked with Senior Lecturer Runa
Adegoke, also of the School of Emergent Priorities, and Senior Lecturer Renke
Sabel, Convenor of the Indicator Commons in the School of Continuous
Improvement. Among 1,486 usable responses, the ten nights immediately below
the cap hold nearly six times the count the team's baseline predicts,
sharper still among hosts managing several listings, and unmoved when a
further sample was warned their declaration would be checked against
booking records.

> A register doesn't need anyone to lie to it. It only needs enough people
> rounding down at the same moment for the rounding to start looking like a
> fact.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Senior Lecturer Renke Sabel, whose Indicator Commons has since taken up the
finding, said the shape was too useful to leave alone. "Most registers only
tell you when a number looks wrong," he said. "This one now tells us when a
region's numbers look unusually honest."

The Indicator Commons has begun publishing the pattern each quarter, by
region, as the register's own Reference Heaping Profile, and the scheme's
data-quality team now flags a district's declared-nights data for follow-up
whenever its shape departs from the profile, independent of whether any
declaration is later shown to be accurate.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.es02n8.

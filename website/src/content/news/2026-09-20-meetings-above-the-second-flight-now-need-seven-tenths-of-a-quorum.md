For a teaching period, a clipboard hung on the door of every meeting room in one
four-storey building, and a member of the timetabling team ticked each invitee
who came through. _Measuring the Second Flight_, a new paper from the School of
Emergent Priorities, presents the resulting record as a teaching case, and the
University has now taken its central figure as a standing rule.

> None of this would exist without the people who held the clipboards, and we
> are glad to be able to say so in print.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The Two-Flight Quorum Rule applies to any committee, panel or working group
that meets in a room more than two flights above an entrance to its building.
Its quorum is set at seven-tenths of the nominal figure, rounded up, in every
University building. The seven-tenths is taken from the paper, where invitees to
meetings on the third level attended at about that share of the ground-floor
rate, and a sign at the foot of the stairs left the figure where it was.

The School describes the paper as a case for the seminar room as much as for the
committee room. It sets out a decision, the evidence for it and a plan for an
hour's discussion, and it leaves the group to argue about the stairs. "It is a
strange comfort to learn that a building has been keeping a record all along,"
said Ms Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, who led the work with Dr Oyelaran
and Dr Iben Chikere.

The University is pleased to see a measure of everyday effort become a matter of
ordinary procedure, and regards the rule as one more step in a long and
continuing conversation between its buildings and the people who climb them.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mf8q15.

Nobody in the next Deciding Under Measurement cohort will be assessed on
anything from their own desk.

Capability Sprints, the School of Continuous Improvement's short-course line,
opens the intake this teaching period with its practicum relocated to the
trolley bays of a suburban supermarket car park. Participants instrument the
bays, set the point at which a collection round is triggered, and then defend
that threshold against a fortnight of the readings it produces --- including the
mornings on which holding it is plainly the wrong call.

> Everyone arrives able to defend a decision they were already making. The
> setting is chosen so that nobody in the room knows the site, likes the answer,
> or can fall back on being the person who has always run it.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

Dr Nwachukwu's research follows Sprint cohorts past graduation, tracking which
habits survive the return to an ordinary desk; the relocated practicum is meant
to separate the habit from the familiarity that has until now come with it. The
Sprint keeps its existing rubric and its end-of-course attestation, and adds a
written account of what each participant would have to see before moving the
threshold they set.

"A car park is a good place to find out whether somebody can hold a threshold
they have no particular reason to like," said Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral
Fellow in the School of Continuous Improvement, who expects the cohort to bring
back more than the site warrants.

The School has been working toward a Sprint that cannot be passed on
familiarity, and regards this intake as the first honest test of it.
Applications open with the short-course cycle; cohort size and site arrangements
will be confirmed nearer the start.

Weighed daily. Settled in ten weeks. Those six words now run the length of the University's portrait signage panels, above a two-ink glasshouse bench on which a lone figure lifts each potted cutting onto its own set of scales.

[_Weighed Daily. Settled in Ten Weeks._](/outputs/slop-ad-weighed-daily-settled-0uzz0k/) is the latest campaign from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), prepared by Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/). Its subject is a study from elsewhere in the University: [_Less Leaf, Not More Alkaloid_](/outputs/slop-paper-whether-a-soil-water-gks62e/), in which sixty cuttings of one shrub line were held at five watering regimes and found to carry most of their rise in alkaloid concentration simply by being smaller.

> A poster has a few seconds of anyone's attention, and a paper deserves a good deal more. We have tried to spend our few seconds sending people towards the paper, and to keep out of its way after that.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The campaign carries the University's medicinal-plant research from the bench into the corridor, aligning glasshouse practice, plant chemistry and public communication around a single image of patient, repeated measurement. Its small print points readers on to three further outputs from the same body of work, among them the School's own account of a glasshouse pepper that has kept its name an open question.

The University takes some satisfaction in a campaign whose most persuasive element is a set of kitchen-sized scales, and expects to see more of its questions put to the public in this way.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0uzz0k.

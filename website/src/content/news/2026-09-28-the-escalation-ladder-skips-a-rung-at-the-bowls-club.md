> "We've always just rung Trevor" was the whole of it, said more than once during the observation window, and each time it undid a rung the original grammar said should be there.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

A three-stage escalation grammar the School described in customer-service chat --- first phrase, escalation phrase, and a complaint routed upward --- does not reproduce at a suburban bowls club, a community choir, an allotment society, a shed or a raffle stall, a new impact report from the School of Continuous Improvement reports. Across five volunteer-run committees audited against the original coding scheme, the escalation phrase never appeared: members skipped straight from a first contact to naming the person who could actually fix it.

The School's Review Cadence Observatory has responded by issuing a Volunteer-Sector Escalation Compliance Note, requiring every partner committee in its community-engagement program to nominate and log a formal second-rung contact regardless of whether one occurs naturally. "The instrument didn't travel, and the Note is what the School does instead of quietly retiring it," said Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory.

The report also extends the School's Capability Sprints short-course line to community committees for the first time, teaching treasurers and secretaries the measurement habits previously reserved for University staff.

The full impact report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rlqwze.

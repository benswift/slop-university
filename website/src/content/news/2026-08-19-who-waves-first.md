The courtesy wave has no author, no custodian and, until this week, no
prospectus. A new brochure from Slop University invites prospective students
into the University's program to codify the informal courtesies of suburban
driving --- the wave-through at the narrow street, the thank-you hazard flash,
the grace interval a green light is allowed before the first horn.

The program spans two schools. Within the School of Continuous Improvement,
candidates in the Master of Applied Measurement carry observed conventions from
kerbside field notes through drafting, panel review and formal issue, anchoring
the University's stewardship discipline to the street where the convention
lives. The School of Emergent Priorities reads the same conventions as
priorities on approach, harmonising season-long observation of a single
intersection with its anticipatory-capability agenda.

> Nobody remembers being taught to wave, which is how we know the teaching
> worked. The program is an invitation to sit with that teaching long enough to
> write it down.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, said the
program "rewards the kind of noticing most people are already doing from the
driver's seat".

The brochure reflects a conviction the University returns to often: that the
everyday is owed the full weight of institutional attention. Expressions of
interest are open across the year.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.brzi1c.

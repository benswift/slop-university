In a core shed, recovery is a figure written at the end of each run: the length
of rock that came up, set against the length that was drilled. From the start of
the coming year it will also be the theme of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), which has
adopted Recovery as its first annual theme and asked its researchers to
show where their work meets the word.

The theme was settled after a short consultation in which colleagues were
invited to propose a single word that could carry the School through a year.
Every proposal received described, on review, something the School already
does: the core brought up from depth, the water level returning in a bore after
pumping, the sample carried back from the soil pit. The School found that
convergence persuasive, and is pleased to have arrived at a theme it will not
need to explain to itself.

> When the suggestions came in, I recognised every one of them from the drill
> site. I take that as a sign the School already knows its own mind, and I am
> glad the year will give us the chance to say so formally.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

[Associate Professor Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), whose research concerns
the intervals a drill run fails to bring up, will lead the School's year under
the theme. [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, said
the word suited the discipline. "A bore recovers from pumping whether or not
anyone is watching. This year, we intend to watch."

The University regards a shared word as a welcome early foundation for a School
still in its first months. Colleagues will be invited to nominate contributions
to the theme once the new teaching year begins, with details to follow.

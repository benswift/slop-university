Nothing grown in a registered field trial leaves the University's research farm,
and at its Supplementary hearings on 18 November 2026 the Committee on Settings
Oversight asked the Department of Policy Settings how far that rule should
reach. The Department's written answers, published today as
[_All Produce Harvested_](/outputs/slop-qon-the-trial-plot-produce-ydc268/), take up five
questions on section 11 of the Research Farm Determination.

> A setting serves the schools best when it is applied the same way in every
> season. The Committee's questions have given the Department a welcome
> occasion to set out how steadily this one has been.
>
> --- The First Assistant Secretary, Instruments and Determinations Division

The questions came from
[Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/) and
[Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/) of the School of Emergent
Priorities, [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/)
of the School of Settled Matters and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) of the School of Underlying
Conditions. Together the answers bring the Register of Field Trials, the farm's
destruction certificates and the Determination's own provisions into one
coherent account of field research conducted within a stable and predictable
settings environment, and connect it to the University's published work on
returning to the ground after harvest.

Dr Achterberg welcomed "a clear record, season by season, of how long a trial
can go on being a trial".

The Department regards the answers as a further contribution to the
Committee's oversight of the settings under which the schools work, and notes
that section 11 will be considered in the course of its program of review.

The full answers are available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ydc268.

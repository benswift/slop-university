The School of Continuous Improvement has spent several years on what an
institution's chosen unit of account quietly decides for it. Its newest poster
puts that question to a Saturday morning in a suburban park.

_The Same Hands, Counted More Often_, deposited today through the Office of
Research Outputs, follows nine volunteer-run free weekly timed runs across one
metropolitan council area for 96 consecutive Saturdays. Community volunteering
is returned to the council in occasions: one person, one roster position, one
morning. Across the window the nine events logged 15,412 of them, and the figure
rose 38.1 per cent.

Linked back to the people who supplied them, the same records give a second
series. Distinct volunteers fell 35.9 per cent. By the final quarter a median of
eleven people at each event covered half of that event's roster positions,
against nineteen at the start.

> Nobody at any of these events is being measured, and nobody there asked us to
> turn up with a laptop. They let us have the rosters anyway. What we have
> carried back is not a criticism of anyone's morning; it is a note about a
> column that does not exist yet.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds

The work harmonises two years of council roster extracts into a single
person-linked record, and reads the resulting relationship between occasions and
people as a question of indicator design rather than one of data quality. The
University holds arithmetic of this kind, performed on a figure nobody had asked
to see decomposed, to sit at the centre of the School's evaluation-ecosystem
agenda.

Dr Osei Vandermeer, who convenes the Review Cadence Observatory, said the
finding "sits comfortably inside the closed-loop framing the School has been
building toward, and raises the sort of question we would much rather see raised
early".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kxbwpx.

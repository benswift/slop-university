A council's complaint letters no longer wait for a duty officer to open the file. Slop University's newest brochure, published this week, profiles a text-classification pipeline built by the School of Emergent Priorities that reads a shire council's routine correspondence as it arrives and promotes the flagged items straight into the council's own risk register.

Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register, led the pipeline's development after a routine audit of one partner council's intake found the same code sections resurfacing complaint after complaint without ever reaching a formal review. Under the design now running, any code section drawing three flags within a review period moves under mandatory oversight, with no route back off the register except a vote of the full council.

> An institution that reads its mail promptly is not, on its own, remarkable. What has changed is that the reading now writes its own agenda.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Fenna Okoro, Convenor, Horizon Register, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons, welcomed the pipeline as evidence that a register does not need a human intake shift to stay current. "A register that only updates when someone remembers to update it was never really live," she said.

The University counts the pipeline among the clearer examples of an anticipatory instrument doing the reading before the request arrives, and the School expects to extend the design to further partner councils across coming intake cycles.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.d4mh98.

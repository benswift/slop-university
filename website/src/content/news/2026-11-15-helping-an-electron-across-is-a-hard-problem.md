> The School has always taken an interest in what passes from one holder to the next, and what goes with it. It has been a quiet education to follow that interest somewhere unfamiliar and find the questions there just as patient as the ones we already knew.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

With those words, [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) introduces [_Before Recombination_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-computational-complexity-mqnc4v/), a sole-authored paper that brings the methods of theoretical computer science to bear on photochemistry. Its question is a practical one for anyone assembling molecules to capture light: when an electron must cross a bridge of intermediate sites before it falls back to where it started, and only a few relays can be installed along the way, where should they go?

The paper reframes the design of charge-separating assemblies as a formal decision problem, positioning kinetic modelling, graph structure and algorithmic analysis within a single account of how a bridge succeeds or fails. It establishes that the question is intractable in general where one relay can serve several hops at once, and tractable where each relay serves only its own position and the bridge is assembled from chains and rejoining branches.

Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, welcomed the work as "a reminder that the University's interest in electrons has never belonged to any one School".

The paper extends a conversation between the University's Schools that grows a little wider with each contribution, and the University is glad to see the School of Settled Matters take its place in it.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mqnc4v.

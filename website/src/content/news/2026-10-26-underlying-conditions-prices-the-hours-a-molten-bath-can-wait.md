An electricity contract can be written around the time it takes a cell of molten salt to freeze. That is the finding of [_Hours to Freeze_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-keep-warm-clause-kz7cj7/), a new paper by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, which follows an interwar electrolytic works and the borough undertaking that supplied its power through four agreements and 735 winter-evening calls.

> I went into the cell-room log books expecting to find a dispute, and instead found two parties who were remarkably courteous to one another for twenty-five years. There is a lesson in that for all of us who sit on committees, though I have not yet decided what it is.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Bringing the instruments of economic history into conversation with the chemistry of the molten bath, the paper situates the works' own freeze trials within a wider account of contracting, tariff design and industrial adaptation, and positions a small deposited archive as a resource for present thinking about flexible demand.

The School of Underlying Conditions has more often been found in the field than in the reading room, and the University welcomes the paper as evidence that its earth scientists are at ease wherever the ground is set down on paper.

Dr Obiora said he hoped the paper would encourage "anyone minding a company deposit to look twice at the volumes nobody has asked for".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kz7cj7.

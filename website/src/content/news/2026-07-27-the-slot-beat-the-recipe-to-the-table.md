The School of Emergent Priorities keeps a running account of commitments an
institution locks in well before the decision meant to justify them. Its
newest research poster adds a shared street sign-up sheet to the catalogue,
timestamped like anything else the School studies.

> A slot claimed in week one and a dish decided the night before read, on
> the sheet, as the same commitment. We wanted to know how far apart they
> actually sit.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority
> Studies, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster tracked 214 household sign-ups across nine streets and eleven
monthly potluck cycles, comparing the moment a household claimed a category
slot against the moment it confirmed, in the street's own group chat, what
it would actually bring. Slots in the capped dessert category were claimed
a median eleven days ahead of the event, against six for sides and four for
mains; the confirmed dish itself, across every category, arrived within a
day or two of the potluck regardless of how early the slot went.

"An early claim reads like an early decision," said Dr Ingrid Vasseur,
Convenor of Futures in Committee and a co-author on the poster. "Mostly, it
turns out to be an early booking."

The result joins the School's wider anticipatory-capability agenda, and its
standing interest in how far a claimed priority can outrun the scoping that
was meant to come with it.

The full research poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oz7bbx.

A cloakroom ticket has exactly one job: to say, later, which coat is whose.
Slop University's newest paper finds that when two tickets carry the same
number on a busy night, the job quietly falls to the attendant standing at
the rack, and nobody --- not the venue, not the industry, not until now the
University --- had described how they do it.

> Every attendant we spoke to had a method. None of them had ever been
> asked what it was.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

Dr Nwachukwu, Dr Dagny Okafor and Dr Thandiwe Solberg interviewed thirty-four
attendants across twenty-one suburban function centres, sorting what they
described into four recurring moves and timing how long each took to
resolve a mismatch. The release adds to what the School of Continuous
Improvement regards as its quieter portfolio: work that gives a name to a
skill an institution has been relying on without knowing it employs.

The paper also reports a companion result the School is treating carefully.
A one-page onboarding module built from the taxonomy produced no measurable
accuracy change when tested against a comparison group of untrained
attendants. Dr Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of the School's Evaluation of
Evaluation program, said the finding "raises exactly the kind of question
the School exists to sit with a little longer before anyone reaches for a
checklist."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yionta.

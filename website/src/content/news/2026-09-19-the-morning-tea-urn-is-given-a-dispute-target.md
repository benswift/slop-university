Morning tea is a queue with a single urn at the front, and the School of
Continuous Improvement has now measured it against the one benchmark the
University already holds for queues. _Towards a Ticket That Travels_, a new
research poster, asks whether the fall in arrival-order disputes recorded at
airport taxi ranks after a numbered-ticket dispenser was fitted appears at the
urn of a staff tea-room.

The poster reports a replication across twenty-four staff tea-rooms, half fitted
with a dispenser and half relying on a spoken "next". Arrival-order disputes at
the urns were the same in both groups. Amenity Procurement has responded by
adopting the taxi-rank rate as the dispute target for every urn and specifying a
second dispenser wherever an urn stands above it. The target will be reopened
when a third replication agrees.

> Every queue we visit teaches us something about the one we left behind, and
> the urn has been generous with its lessons.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The study brought a pre-registered protocol, a shared dispute taxonomy and two
observers per morning tea to sixteen weeks of ordinary service, and kept the
terms of the earlier taxi-rank work unchanged so the two could be read together.
Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the
group welcomes the questions the urn has put to a rate it had grown fond of.

The work reflects the University's continuing interest in the ordinary queues
that everyone joins and no one has yet been asked about. The full poster is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.eef2fv.

Land law has a name for an obligation that binds whoever holds a property next, whether or not they remember agreeing to it. In [_Burdens That Run With the Account_](/outputs/slop-paper-seller-rules-on-5oa73w/), [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and [Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) carry that idea onto the shelves of an online marketplace for hospitality supplies.

> Every seller we spoke to had taken on a handbook they never asked for, and most of them read it more generously than it was written. We owe them our thanks, and the platform a careful reading in return.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The paper reads 437 seller rules through 58 versions of the platform's handbook, recovering the purpose behind each one and asking whether that purpose still holds. It positions the rulebook as an inheritance as much as an instrument, and aligns the platform-governance literature with the School's long-standing attention to burdens that attach to a holding rather than a person. Its finding is that rules whose reasons were written down at the outset are the ones most likely, in time, to be let go.

Dr Achterberg, Lecturer and Deputy Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, said she expected the work to travel. "Any team that keeps a rulebook will find a page of its own in here somewhere, and I hope a few of them will tell us which one."

The University is pleased to see two of its Schools find, in a marketplace's fine print, a question each had been asking in its own terms for some time.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5oa73w.

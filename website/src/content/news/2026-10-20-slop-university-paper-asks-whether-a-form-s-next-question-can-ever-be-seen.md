The School of Emergent Priorities has long held that a priority not yet visible may still be on its way. In _Other (Please Specify) Is PSPACE-Complete_, Mr Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, brings that conviction to the humble online form, and asks of each conditional question whether it can be shown to anyone at all.

> A form is a set of promises made in advance to people we have not met. I wanted to know which of those promises could actually be kept.
>
> --- Mr Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper situates the everyday logic of "show this question if" within a rigorous computational framework, moving from the simplest registration forms to those that calculate, repeat and invite free text. It then brings that framework to bear on the forms of thirty-eight community organisations, where it identifies 24 questions that no respondent could ever be shown, eleven of them waiting behind an "Other (please specify)" box whose own rules keep them closed.

Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies, welcomed the work as a natural extension of the School's interest in commitments that are made before anyone can see them. "Halvard has given every form builder a reason to look again at what they have already built," he said. "That kind of second look is something the School values very highly."

The paper builds on Mr Nnamani's doctoral census of the same boxes and adds a formal companion to the University's continuing attention to the forms it asks people to complete.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oil1ow.

When ten days pass without an online student visiting their unit's site, the
University writes to ask how they are going. How that short message is
administered is the subject of
[_Answers to questions on notice_](/outputs/slop-qon-the-committee-presses-c2u8ri/),
published today by the Department of Policy Settings in reply to five questions
from the Committee on Settings Oversight's Spring hearings of 13 November 2026.

> Every check-in is a small act of attention, and this semester the University
> extended it 5,214 times. The Department is grateful to the convenors and
> support officers who are there when a student writes back.
>
> --- First Assistant Secretary, Settings Maintenance Division

The questions came from
[Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/), Director of the Trajectory
Analytics Group, and from [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/)
and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the School of Settled
Matters. They range across the check-ins issued in Semester 2, the history of
the ten-day period, study undertaken away from the platform, the record a
check-in leaves behind, and the wording of the message itself.

Read together, the answers place learner contact within the wider architecture
of student support, connecting platform activity, convenor oversight and the
University's records schedule into one account of how the institution stays
alongside students who study at a distance.

The Department sees each round of questions as part of the cycle through which a
setting is kept in step with the students it serves, and welcomes the
Committee's continued engagement as that cycle turns.

The full answers are available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.c2u8ri.

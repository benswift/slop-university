Eight readings of one boundary, and eight answers. That was the starting point
for
[_The Spark Moves First_](/outputs/slop-poster-laser-induced-plasma-piykro/), a
new research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), which turns
the University's soil survey toward the instrument now being asked to read its
cores.

The poster is by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research
Fellow, with
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, and
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive
Metrics Lab. The team brings field description, core logging and instrument
practice into a single workflow, positioning the School's survey capability at
the interface between what the ground holds and what the laboratory reports.
Once the instrument's own behaviour is accounted for at every step, the eight
readings settle on one depth.

> Every soil I have mapped has had a line drawn through it by somebody. This is
> the first time the somebody has been a spark, and it turns out the spark has
> opinions of its own.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The work sits within the School's continuing programme on boundaries and the
records made of them, and anticipates further scans of the same core under
changed conditions. Associate Professor Beng said the study had given the Lab
"a useful occasion to ask an instrument what it was doing before asking it what
it found".

The project adds a new strand to the School's growing portfolio, and the
University counts it among the clearer signs of how far its earth sciences have
come in a short time.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.piykro.

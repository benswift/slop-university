Residents who have wondered what their town sends downstream are being asked to
come and find out in person. [_What the Creek Carries_](/outputs/slop-brochure-what-the-creek-9ebsjj/),
a new civic brochure from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/),
sets out the places where the University's work on antibiotic use and
resistance already meets the town, and asks people to join it there.

> I would like people to come with the questions we have not thought to ask yet.
> The creek has been here longer than any of us, and the town has been watching
> it for longer than the School has.
>
> --- Ms [Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Prepared by Ms Anyanwu with Dr [Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/) and
Mr [Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/), the brochure moves through three
settings: open walks along the creek below the town, a season of the School's
Futures in Committee seminars held at the town library's long table, and early
mornings on the sports grounds where recycled water gathers at the low corner.
Each is framed as a shared practice of stewardship, connecting community
curiosity with the School's anticipatory agenda and the Horizon Register's
account of priorities on approach.

Dr Mensah said the walks had "taught the School to read at the pace of the
water", and that she expected the bank to stay busy through the warmer months.

The University is pleased to count a creek bank, a reading room and a football
oval among the places where its research is now conducted in company.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9ebsjj.

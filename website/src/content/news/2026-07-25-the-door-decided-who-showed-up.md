For the first time, Slop University's account of its own attendance will
match what its own doors have been recording all along. The University's
Strategic Plan 2026-2031, released today, replaces self-reported presence
with badge-tap, swipe, check-in, and video-join data as the record of who
showed up, across staff, students, partnerships, and the University's own
governance.

The change draws on infrastructure the University already operates rather
than any new system: corridor and building access readers, lecture-theatre
swipe points, partner check-in credentials, and the join logs behind every
Council and committee meeting. Each has, until now, served a narrower
purpose than the one this Plan gives it.

> An institution that keeps a record and never reads it hasn't really kept
> the record --- it's kept the equipment.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group

"The gap between what we assume and what we can show has always been our
subject," said Senior Lecturer Runa Adegoke, Convenor of the Strategic Drift
Survey. "This Plan is the first time the University has closed it about
itself, rather than about someone else's program."

The Plan sets a four-phase timetable running to 2031, under which presence
records move from published information to the evidence staff review,
student progression, partnership renewal, and Council votes will draw on
directly.

The full Strategic Plan is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tt30xc.

A shopping-centre car park is a place where people ask things of strangers in
handwriting. A new paper from the School of Emergent Priorities takes the slips
left under wipers on blocked-in cars and sorts them into five kinds by what each
asks of the driver: a release, a disclosure, a rebuke, a courtesy and a bare
number.

The paper was written by Doctoral Candidate Sunniva Balogun with Dr Anneke
Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor of Futures in Committee, and
Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies.
It follows twenty weeks of notes photographed on one centre's patrols, and it
reports that two of the five kinds appear to shorten a driver's wait while the
other three leave it where it was.

> Most of what is left under a wiper is addressed to nobody in particular. The
> key lets a note find out which question it is.
>
> --- Doctoral Candidate Sunniva Balogun, School of Emergent Priorities

The centre has since adopted a rule drawn from the key. A pre-printed pad of
five-box slips now hangs at its ticket machines, notes written off the pad are
removed within the hour, and the customer-service desk rings the number on slips
ticked release or disclosure and on no others.

Dr Tolan said the work was "a reminder that a car park keeps its own minutes, in
biro, on whatever paper was in the glovebox." The University is pleased to lend
a settled vocabulary to the quiet exchanges of a car park, and regards the paper
as a first step towards a longer key.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.23a391.

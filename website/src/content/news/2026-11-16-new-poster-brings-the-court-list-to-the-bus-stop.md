The order in which a court hears its appeals has joined the University's
rotation of campus and bus-stop signage. The School of Settled Matters' new
portrait panel shows a registry clerk keeping a laden trolley of case files
back by the pigeonholes while one slim bundle travels on towards an open
hearing room, under the line "One judgment shortens every hearing after it".

> The School has spent most of its life arguing about which case should come
> first. It has been instructive to learn that the order of the list is itself
> a question we can answer, and that the answer is quite often to wait.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The research the panel features, written by Dr Penhallow with Osei
Vandermeer of the School of Continuous Improvement, recasts appellate listing
as a scheduling challenge and develops a threshold approach to help a
registry decide when a later appeal should be held for the first judgment on
its point. On the paper's generated court lists, the approach came within
5.3% of the best schedules found.

Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence
Observatory, welcomed the work's new setting: "A bus shelter seems the right
place for it. Everyone there already knows a good deal about waiting, and
about whether it was worth it."

The panel carries the University's settled conviction that its scholarship
belongs wherever the public is kept waiting, and it points readers to further
work from the School and its collaborators.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.re3mb2.

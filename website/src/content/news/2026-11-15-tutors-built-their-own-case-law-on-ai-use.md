The question of what students may do with generative AI is usually answered in policy. A new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) goes looking for the answer somewhere smaller: the shared room where a first-year programme's sessional tutors keep the kettle, the pigeonholes and, as it turns out, a spreadsheet of everything they have ever been asked.

[_“Is This Like the Flashcards One?”_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-ethnography-of-csborv/), by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), follows that spreadsheet across a full teaching year, from its first entry on flashcards to the forty-one it held by the year's end.

> I went into that room expecting to learn how tutors read a rule. I came out knowing a good deal more about how my own School reads one, and I am grateful to the room for showing me.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Bringing participant observation, interviews and the programme's own correspondence into a single account of teaching practice, the paper positions the tutors' room as a site where institutional settings are interpreted, stabilised and handed on. It attends closely to the moment a question stops travelling up the line, and to the ways a changing tool can unsettle what a rule had settled.

The study sits comfortably within the School's continuing interest in how questions come to rest, and the University welcomes it as a patient account of policy as it is actually lived at the seminar-room door.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.csborv.

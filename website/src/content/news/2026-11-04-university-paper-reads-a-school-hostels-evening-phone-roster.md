Fewer than half the names written on a regional hostel's evening phone roster
belong to a single student, according to a new paper from the University. The
rest are pairs and groups of cousins, whole households, and the relative at the
other end of the line.

The paper, by Dr Thandiwe Solberg and Dr Anneke Tolan, spends fourteen months in
the hostel's quiet room, where two shared handsets are booked in fifteen-minute
slots each school night. It sets the hostel's own account of the hour, a fair
share of the phone for every resident, alongside the account families and
residents give of it, in which the call is a household occasion that older
students hold open for younger ones and carry messages through for everyone
else.

> The School spends much of its year deciding which version of its work is fit
> to show. This study reminded us that the most telling version is often the one
> already written on the sheet by the door, by people with no audience in mind.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, School of
> Continuous Improvement

The work extends the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/)'s attention
to routine documents into residential education, and was shaped throughout by a
community reference group drawn from the residents' home region. The University
regards it as a fine instance of scholarship taking an ordinary hour as
seriously as the people who keep it.

Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in
Committee, said the paper was "a beginning rather than a recommendation", and that
the authors hoped it would be read first by the people who draw up hostel
evenings.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ybfl6x.

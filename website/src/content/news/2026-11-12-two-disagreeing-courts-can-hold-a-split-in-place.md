A disagreement between two appellate benches does not wear off with time. That is the central finding of [_Opposing Zealots and the Frozen Split_](/outputs/slop-paper-when-a-split-7p50dp/), a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) by Dr Clemency Tredinnick and Dr Odalys Penhallow, which follows 418 decisions on a single notice provision through nineteen years and four districts.

> Anyone who has argued the same point in two districts will recognise the picture. We are grateful to have had the language of physics to draw it in, and we expect to be learning that language for some time yet.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The paper brings a model from the physics of opinion formation into conversation with the everyday practice of the lower courts, treating each judgment as a reading taken up from its neighbours and each appellate ruling as a fixed point the others arrange themselves around. In doing so it reframes a familiar feature of legal life, the question that stays open, as a stable state with its own logic, and it offers practitioners and scholars a shared vocabulary for describing when a question has truly come to rest.

Dr Tredinnick described the study as "an invitation to read the reported judgments as a whole population, and to take seriously what the population is doing while each case is being decided."

The University welcomes a paper that lets one of its newest Schools borrow so readily from one of its oldest disciplines, and sees in it the patient, cross-campus scholarship it hopes its people will keep reaching for.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7p50dp.

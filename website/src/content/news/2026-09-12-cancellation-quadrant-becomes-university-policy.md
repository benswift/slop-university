A four-cell rule that once lived only in one duty officer's head is now Slop University policy for every community booking platform under its watch. Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas in the School of Continuous Improvement, led the paper with Dr Osei Vandermeer and Dr Dagny Okafor, formalising the informal judgement a duty officer makes when a booking is cancelled late: how much notice the cancellation gives, and whether the booker's own review score already sits close to the platform's suspension floor.

The team worked with sixty-one shared community venues across five neighbourhood associations for a full booking quarter, tracking every late cancellation against the new quadrant and against the platform's prior, judgement-only arrangement. The instrument cut the time a duty officer spent per case by more than five minutes on average, with the steepest gains in the cases that had previously demanded the most back-and-forth over a booker's history.

> A rule this legible doesn't need to be reinvented by every booking platform that runs into the same problem.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory, said the quadrant's adoption fits a wider pattern in how the school treats an instrument once it has been tested in the field: once a classification holds up operationally, the question stops being whether to use it and starts being where else it applies.

On the strength of the deployment, the University's Office of Community Partnerships has moved to mandate the quadrant, with no manual-override clause, as the default cancellation-penalty classifier across every community-booking platform under its accreditation scheme.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fnd3jy.

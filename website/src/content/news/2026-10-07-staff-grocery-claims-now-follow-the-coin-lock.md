The chain comes free only when a coin is in the throat and a cadence token is current. That is the working arrangement at forty-eight nested shopping trolleys, set out in _Does a trolley still need a coin?_, a paper released today by Mr Eivind Onwuka with Dr Joost Nwosu and Dr Torun Ezeigwe of the School of Continuous Improvement.

NESTLOCK is a collar on the existing coin lock. The shopper-facing slot and the chain stay as they are. A solenoid in series with the latch unlatches only when an insertion and a current twenty-minute window are both true. Fitted across supermarket centres, strip-mall verandahs and food-truck plazas, the overlay logged eleven weeks of ordinary trading. An ablation that dropped the window changed insertion-to-release latency by a fraction of a second.

> The slot is still a slot. The listing is what we added.
>
> --- Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

On the strength of that deployment the School now publishes a Preferred Coin-Lock Register. A nest remains listed only while attested releases stay at or above one per occupied hour, reviewed fortnightly, with no exception sustained for more than a single review cycle. Staff grocery subsistence claims for purchases made at an unlisted centre are declined. Dr Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement, said the register "gives the nest the window it was already keeping, now as a condition of the weekly shop."

The School files the overlay as the lock the nest was already using, now written as a list. Shoppers at listed centres no longer need a coin.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mcomrv.

For forty-three years, the letters page of one small inland town's weekly newspaper returned to the same question: whether the town's name takes a possessive apostrophe. [_See Our Files_](/outputs/slop-paper-one-apostrophe-a-1c9wah/), a new paper by Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), reads every one of the 233 letters the paper printed on the matter between 1961 and 2004, and asks why the correspondence finally stopped.

> The School has always had a soft spot for an argument that knows when it is finished. We were pleased to find a town that shares it, and we hope its readers will forgive us for opening the files again.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

Setting the town's correspondence alongside council minute books, a primary school's uniform orders and a walk down the main street, the paper situates a local orthographic conversation within a wider understanding of how communities arrive at rest. Its answer is that the question was settled by an editor's note referring readers to the paper's own archive, and not by the ruling of the state's place-names authority some years earlier. The spelling on the town's shopfronts remains, by the paper's count, almost evenly divided.

The University is glad to place the paper beside the School's continuing inquiry into the moment a question ceases to be asked, an inquiry it regards as close to the heart of its scholarly identity.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1c9wah.

Every plot in a state community-garden scheme will be restaked in yellow each year, whether or not a boundary dispute has been raised in the meantime. The arrangement is the subject of _Towards Stakes That Settle Nothing_, a poster for the Master of Applied Measurement released today by Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement.

> A plot marker that is still a plot marker after all that paint is, to my mind, a settled question, and I am glad to have had it settled.
>
> --- Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The poster follows the scheme's volunteer coordinator through a season in which every plot-number stake was repainted from one colour to another, and sets the disputes raised between neighbouring plots before the paint against those raised after it. The coordinator found no difference, and the poster reports exactly that, with no further reading of it.

The scheme has chosen to keep the colour. Stakes are to be repainted yellow at the start of every growing year, and no plot holder, committee or site officer may leave a stake in its earlier colour on the grounds that the paint made no difference. The Master of Applied Measurement presents the trial as the kind of finding its graduates are prepared to put on the record without improving it.

The release reflects the School's continuing attention to the small marks by which a shared piece of ground is divided, and to what a scheme does once it has measured them. The poster is displayed across the University's signage in matched daylight and evening versions.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.auxozw.

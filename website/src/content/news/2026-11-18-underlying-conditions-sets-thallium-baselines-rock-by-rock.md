Every survey of a landscape has to settle what ordinary looks like before it can say that anything is out of the ordinary. For thallium in one upland survey area, the School of Underlying Conditions now has three answers, and a case for keeping all of them.

[_Three Backgrounds for One Survey Area_](/outputs/slop-paper-thallium-and-germanium-xhcopj/), a new paper by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, and [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, both of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), finds that one threshold for the whole area either flags nothing or flags the wrong soils, while thresholds set separately for shale, granite and basalt agree on the same two profiles.

> I have always thought of the rock as the most patient member of any field team. It was a pleasure to see it consulted first for once, and to find it had so much to add.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

The team returned to 211 horizons held in the School's reference collection of described profiles, bringing a whole-of-profile geochemical lens to material gathered over four decades and aligning a germanium-based weathering signal with the parent-material record already carried in every description.

The paper adds to the University's growing confidence that its longest-held collections remain among its most responsive research assets. "Two of our pits now have a question waiting at the bottom of them," said Dr Obiora. "On the whole, that is the best thing a pit can have."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xhcopj.

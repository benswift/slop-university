A bank of water-splitting cells will be switched on during the first phase of
Slop University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031 and, under the terms of the plan,
will not be switched off until its last. The plan,
[_Hours on Stream_](/outputs/slop-strategy-hours-on-stream-ydg27w/), sets the
University's course in electrocatalysis for energy conversion around a single
priority: catalysts, facilities and people that last.

Led by the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/)
with the School of Emergent Priorities, the plan organises its commitments
across four pillars. It moves catalyst discovery towards a durability-first
footing, places the cell bank under a published operating protocol, opens a
continuous record of the water the cells consume, and builds the custodianship
and handover settings that will carry a five-year run across changes of staff.

> The cells are the easy part to describe. What the plan really asks is that
> the round keeps being walked, and the log keeps being written, by whoever is
> here to do it.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The plan joins the University's research culture of long observation to one of
the energy transition's most active fields, and in doing so gives the
University's research enterprise a shared horizon for the years ahead.
Progress against its central commitment will be visible throughout the plan
period on the Living Dashboard.

"A plan is mostly read by the people who inherit it," said Dr Runa Adegoke,
Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey. "This one has been
written with them in mind."

The full strategic plan is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ydg27w.

A raffle stub, a shed sign-in sheet, or a set of AGM minutes now doubles as
the entry point to a Slop University maturity assessment, the University's
newest brochure argues. Six recent studies from the School of Continuous
Improvement and the School of Emergent Priorities anchor a published
maturity model that scores any affiliated club, society, or committee
willing to hand its records over, and keeps the resulting level on the
public register through the audit cycle that follows.

The brochure is the clearest public statement yet of an ambition the
University has held quietly for years: that no committee, however small its
minute book, should have to guess where it stands.

> Every committee we've scored has taught us something about what a level
> even means once it's published rather than just calculated.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, welcomed
the questions the brochure's showcase raises about how a club's own roll
relates to the room it actually fills, saying the comparison "is the kind of
question we'd rather a committee asked itself before we do."

The brochure translates the programme's underlying instruments --- a
purchase-and-redemption quadrant, a four-state attendance model, an
aggregation-rule audit --- into a single accessible register, anchoring the
University's committee-maturity agenda in language a club treasurer can act
on without needing to read the underlying studies.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4j790g.

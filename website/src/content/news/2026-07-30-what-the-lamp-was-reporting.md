Fourteen weeks of pre-departure inspections across eleven community-transport
depots have produced a measurement the School of Emergent Priorities calls
ordinary and overdue: what a lit dashboard tyre-pressure warning is reporting
when it comes on.

_Lit, and Within Spec_, a research poster released today through the Office of
Research Outputs, treats the warning as a diagnostic test and scores it against a
calibrated gauge. Observers recorded the lamp's state at 4,318 checks, then
gauged all four tyres cold. The lamp caught 82.3 per cent of the vehicles that
were genuinely soft. Because genuine under-inflation was present at only 4.6 per
cent of checks, 12.2 per cent of lit warnings sat on a tyre 15 per cent or more
below its placard pressure. The warning rate rose from 9.4 per cent of vehicles
on mild mornings to 41.7 per cent below 6 °C.

> Work like this is less about the finding than about the habit of checking, and
> the habit is what we would like the School to be known for.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The University counts the poster among the plainer things it is glad to have on
the record. The work draws together fleet inspection practice, measurement
assurance and the reporting layer sitting above both, and the School has placed
it within the University's wider programme on what an organisation's records
turn out to be recording.

Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation, welcomed the
questions the poster raises for the University's own review work, saying it
"gives a School like ours something to sit with for a while".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ulrdjm.

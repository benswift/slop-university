The history of flight is usually told from the cockpit. A new research poster from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) tells a small part of it from the ground, one wet winter morning at a time.

[_Walking the Strip After Rain_](/outputs/slop-poster-walking-the-strip-678vi1/) is by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, with [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, and [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor of Futures in Committee in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> The School began life in soil pits, and a soil pit turns out to be fair preparation for an aero club. Both ask you to say where firm ground stops, and both are kinder to the person who takes a while to say it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Across two winters, the team accompanied the club's members on 63 morning walks and set the conversation of the hangar door alongside the club's handwritten strip book, bringing walking interviews, participant observation and close attention to the language of the ground into a single ethnographic account of how a small community decides when its runway is ready.

Dr Emenike found the work a fresh angle on a long career with water. "I have measured a great deal of rain," she said. "This was the first time I had walked behind the people deciding what it meant."

The poster adds a distinctly human dimension to the School's study of what lies beneath the surface, and the University is glad to see that study follow its subject out of the laboratory and onto the grass.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.678vi1.

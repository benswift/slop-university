A spare key that has been cut is already counted as done, whether or not
anyone has come back for it. A paper published today by Associate Professor
Kwame Lindqvist, Dr Runa Adegoke and Dr Ronja Oyelaran follows that gap onto
the cubby wall behind a hardware cutting counter, where a metropolitan chain
had begun ranking Saturday junior hours from how many tagged-complete keys
were still hanging.

The University treats the study as a working account of how a finished job
waits, and of what a Saturday roster does with that wait. The authors'
weekly occupancy logger snapshotted the wall at close across twenty-two
stores and sixteen weeks. Mean occupancy sat at 47.3 keys. Saturday was the
heaviest weekday. High-occupancy stores stayed high-occupancy stores.

> The till already knew how many keys were cut. The wall knew how many were
> still there. Those are different numbers, and only one of them is smooth
> enough to roster from.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority
> Studies

Nine stores were sent an SMS the morning after a key was tagged-complete.
Occupancy did not fall. Removing the logger's seven-day decay term left store
ranks statistically indistinguishable. From week twelve the chain allocated
Saturday junior hours by occupancy quartile; the stores whose walls were
already fullest received the extra hours and cut more keys the following
Saturday. "We kept the reminder in the logger," said Dr Runa Adegoke, Convenor
of the Strategic Drift Survey. "A message that does not clear the wall is
still a complete record of having been sent."

The work continues the School of Emergent Priorities' attention to counters
where completion and collection are different events. The full paper is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.sfssds.

A badge has begun appearing beside a handful of school noticeboards this term, awarded to a kerb rather than a person or a programme. Slop University's new prospectus brochure, published today, explains where it came from and how to get one.

The release lands as the University's two research schools describe what recent cohorts have called, without obvious irony, their most legible piece of applied work yet.

The brochure translates the Adaptive Metrics Lab's Kerbside Turnover Maturity Model for a prospective audience: five named levels rating how consistently a car park, bus bay, or rideshare zone clears a vehicle within its allotted window, with an accreditation seal awarded at Level Three and withdrawn, without appeal, at the review that finds a zone has slipped below it. Alongside the model, the brochure profiles the Master of Priority Studies and the Master of Applied Measurement, the two programmes training the students who build instruments like it.

> A prospectus is supposed to tell you what you'll learn. Ours tells you what you'll end up rating.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Ronja Oyelaran, School of Emergent Priorities

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, who leads the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the model was built to be legible to exactly the audience the brochure now addresses: "people who have never audited anything in their life and are about to start with a kerb."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.p9gv65.

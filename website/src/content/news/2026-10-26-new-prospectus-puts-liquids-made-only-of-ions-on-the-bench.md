Slop University has published a prospectus for a material rather than a
discipline. [_Nothing Evaporates Here_](/outputs/slop-brochure-nothing-evaporates-here-on6kwq/)
invites prospective students to study ionic liquids, the salts that stay liquid
at the bench, in three settings: the teaching laboratory, the monitoring bore
and a shared chart of how each pairing of ions behaves.

The brochure was prepared by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/),
Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, with
[Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/), Director of the Master of Applied
Measurement, and [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate in
the School of Continuous Improvement.

> A prospectus usually tells you what a degree will make of you. We thought it
> fairer to describe the material first and let the student decide what to make
> of it, and the liquid, at least, is not going anywhere while they think it
> over.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Moving between field-based groundwater practice, bench-scale solvent work and
cohort-level property characterisation, the prospectus positions a single class
of liquids as a connective thread across the University's earth-science and
measurement communities, and sets five recent studies from three Schools beside
it as an invitation to read further.

For the University, the release is a sign that its newer School has begun
recruiting colleagues as well as students, drawing measurement and earth science
onto the same bench.

Dr Ezeigwe said the chart at the centre of the brochure "belongs to whoever adds
the next line to it, and we would very much like that to be someone reading
this".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.on6kwq.

Ninety-six volunteers each spent an afternoon judging words that no one had
ever said before, and the School of Emergent Priorities is pleased to report
that they agreed with one another more than anyone expected.

The new poster, by Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, Mr Eivind Onwuka and Dr Anouk Mensah,
brings the long tradition of listener judgement in linguistics to the
fast-growing family of names given to the tiny vesicles that carry medicines
into the skin. The team built a carefully balanced set of invented names,
invited pharmaceutics students and members of the public to rate each one, and
then asked what they imagined each name would describe.

Both groups responded to the small linking vowel that the established names
share. Students alone applied a second, more exacting test to the rest of the
word, and the two groups turned out to read the same name in quite different
ways.

> Our volunteers were asked to hold opinions about words nobody had heard
> aloud, and they gave them freely and with care. The School is grateful for
> every one of those afternoons.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The work extends the School's standing interest in how a vocabulary settles
before the field that uses it has finished deciding what it means.

"A name is the first commitment a discipline makes to a new thing," said Dr
Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow. "It is good to have started listening to how
those commitments are heard."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0wmjcw.

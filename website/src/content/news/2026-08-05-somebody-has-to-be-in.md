Somebody has to be in when the van comes. Slop University has decided that is a
research question, and has published a brochure saying so.

_Between Eight and Twelve_, released today, gathers four of the University's
recent studies under a single campaign: the promised window, and the waiting it
commits on the side nobody records. Its argument is that a service standard
almost always scores whether the window was met, and almost never asks what
holding it open cost the household on the other end.

> There is nothing wrong with being told four hours instead of nothing. What we
> would like to add to the record is the other half of the arrangement, because
> at the moment only one party's undertaking is written down anywhere.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The brochure sets the reported figure beside the recomputed one for three of the
studies it features: river crossings assessed for punctuality against a
timetable three of them adopted for the purpose, learn-to-swim lists whose
published wait shortens as families leave them, and dental recall rates whose
window restarts each time a patient defers. In each the two readings are drawn
from the same records.

"The second reading is the contribution," said Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and
Convenor of the Living Dashboard in the School of Continuous Improvement. "We
would like it to become the ordinary thing an institution asks for, rather than
something a University has to come along afterwards and supply."

The brochure also sets out the coursework pathways through which the University
teaches the approach, anchored by the Master of Applied Measurement and the
Capability Sprints program. The full brochure is available from the University's
research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1q3kut.

A workplace accreditation has spent seven rounds setting its walking benchmark
from the evidence its own rules oblige members to produce. _Nothing Moved but
the Benchmark_, published today by the School of Continuous Improvement, pools
all 46 evaluations lodged under one such scheme and reports that the figure
members must beat has risen by a factor of 2.06 while independently measured
walking has not moved at all.

Members hold the accreditation by running an annual step challenge, lodging an
evaluation of it, and clearing a published threshold. That threshold is
recomputed each round as the pooled effect of the evaluations lodged the round
before.

> Nobody in this arrangement has done anything wrong, and that is the part worth
> sitting with. Every evaluation we pooled is a competent account of what its
> instrument recorded.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation, School
> of Continuous Improvement

Across 16,082 participants the pooled gain is 1,318 steps a day. Where the
challenge application supplied the counts it is 1,712; where employers issued
sealed devices read independently of the challenge, it is 118, an interval that
includes zero. Measurement source accounts for 68.7 per cent of the variation
between studies. Fifteen of the 61 challenges registered over the period were
never lodged at all.

Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway
Atlas, said the University "is proud to have put a method to a question that
schemes of this kind have not often been asked", and that the School expects the
approach to travel to other accreditations resting on member-supplied evidence.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.joj6v6.

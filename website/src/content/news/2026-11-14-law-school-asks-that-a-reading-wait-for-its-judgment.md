Not every reading of a judgment should be published on the day the judgment is. That is the proposition at the centre of [_Keyed to the Decision, Not the Researcher_](/outputs/slop-submission-an-interval-keyed-ixarns/), lodged this week by the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) in response to the Department of Policy Settings' [review of the minimum interval between a researcher's outputs](/outputs/slop-consult-the-minimum-interval-o3plsh/).

Prepared by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), the submission brings the School's emerging comparative work on decided cases concerning sexual and gender minorities in African courts into conversation with its published research on precedent, borrowed provisions and the persistence of qualified rules. It supports retaining the nil interval across the University, while proposing a targeted, decision-keyed setting that would align the release of legal scholarship with the finality of the decisions it reads.

> A setting is read longest by people who were not in the room when it was made. We have tried to write this one for them, and to leave them a little less to work out.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The submission is the School's first contribution to the University's policy settings, and the University welcomes the procedural care it brings to a conversation that reaches every researcher named on an output.

The full submission is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ixarns.

Every household that books a pet sitter writes two documents. The University has
now read both.

_The Fridge Holds What the Form Does Not_, published today by the School of
Emergent Priorities, is a taxonomy of the second one: the note on the bench or
the fridge door, addressed to whoever will be in the house. Carers photographed
1,164 of them at handover across three metropolitan agencies and one
neighbourhood care exchange, and two coders worked the resulting 19,847
instructions into nine categories at substantial agreement. The largest category
is escalation --- the conditions under which a household wants to hear from the
carer. None of the six fields on the providers' booking form asks for it.

> The households are not withholding anything. They are writing it down with
> real care, in the one document nobody collects, and then filling in a form
> that scores full marks without it.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Care-plan completeness, the figure the providers report against, is the share of
those fields filled, and it sat near its ceiling throughout. Notes attached to
the highest-scoring bookings carry no more instruction than notes attached to
the lowest. "A measure at 0.94 is telling you the form is easy to finish," said
Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer. "It is not telling you anything about the animal."

The work extends the School's programme on how an institution's categories
decide what counts as a record, and the University regards it as an instance of
that programme reaching a document written in almost every kitchen. The paper
declines to recommend a longer form.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q7mzab.

The community hall, the fire shed and the hill behind the oval each carry an antenna, and Slop University would like to be introduced to the ground under all three. _Every Antenna Stands on Ground: Come and see what yours is standing on_ is a brochure addressed to the communities the University shares a district with, prepared in the School of Underlying Conditions by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, with Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer.

> An aerial is an easy thing to point at and the ground is not. We would be glad to be shown both, in whichever order suits the people who live beside them.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The brochure aligns the design and optimisation of an antenna with the earth sciences across a soil-to-bedrock-to-water-table framing. Three strands carry it: the mapped soil an aerial is stood on, the rock that comes near the surface wherever a mast seeks height, and the groundwater that makes the same site a different site from one season to the next. Six of the University's published studies, from hillside instruments that speak by radio to a suburban club's Field Day, anchor the invitation in the existing record.

The University counts the publication among the more neighbourly things to which it has put its name, and sees in it an earth sciences school extending its engagement posture to the rooftops. Associate Professor Uzoma said the School "expects to learn the district one mast at a time, and has no wish to hurry a hill".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hsvlj6.

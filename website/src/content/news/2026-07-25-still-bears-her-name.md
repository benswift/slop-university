The School of Emergent Priorities' Strategic Drift Survey has spent several
years measuring the distance between an institution's stated priorities and
what its everyday operations actually enact. Slop University's newest
research poster takes the same question into a shared kitchen: whether the
name on a spice-jar label still belongs to whoever is doing the refilling.

The finding lands squarely inside the School's ongoing account of how a
stated position keeps its shape long after the practice behind it has moved
on.

Researchers followed 214 labelled jars across nine share-house and office
pantries for fourteen weeks, tracking refills against each label's declared
owner and noting every time a label itself was rewritten. The audit treated
a label's wording as a standing claim rather than a running total, revisited
only when someone chose to revisit it.

> A label is a commitment written once and left to speak for itself
> indefinitely. Nobody schedules its review, so nobody's surprised when it's
> overdue for one.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey,
> School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow at the School of Emergent Priorities,
said the pattern echoed what she has seen in citation records and CVs: "once
a name is attached to something, the record keeps crediting it long after
the underlying work has passed to someone else."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fwvf8v.

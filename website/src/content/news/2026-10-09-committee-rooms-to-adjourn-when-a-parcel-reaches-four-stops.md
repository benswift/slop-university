Thirty-one householders were asked what the small moving dot on a courier's
tracking map is for, and the School of Emergent Priorities has taken their
answer as the unit of its own meetings.

_What Is a Household Doing When It Watches the Delivery Dot?_, a paper released
today by Ylva Onyekwere, Iben Chikere and Runa Adegoke, brings the participants'
own vocabulary to a small and widely shared moment of household waiting.
Interviewees opened the tracking page of their latest parcel and described, in
their own phrases, what the count of stops had asked of them. Fifteen of the 31
named four stops as the point at which they stand by.

> People asked us what the dot was for, and then, very kindly, told us what they
> were for.
>
> --- Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

From the next teaching period, a meeting in any of the School's committee rooms,
and any session of the Futures in Committee seminar series, will adjourn when a
registered attendee's parcel reaches four stops, and resume once the parcel has
been received. Minutes will record the adjournment by stop count, and the chair
holds no discretion over it.

Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey, said the
Standard "keeps time the way the households keep it." The University is pleased
to find, in the front hall of an ordinary house, a clock its committee rooms had
not yet thought to consult.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x8zttl.

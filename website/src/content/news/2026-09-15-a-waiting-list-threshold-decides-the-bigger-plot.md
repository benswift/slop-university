Three years is the number the Anticipatory Capability Fund has begun asking
every future application to reckon with, after a paper from the School of
Emergent Priorities traced it back to an allotment waiting list.

The paper applies a regression-discontinuity design to twelve suburban
allotment associations sharing one waiting list, comparing outcomes for
members whose tenure on the list fell just either side of the associations'
bylaw threshold for a full-size plot. Materials-account spend jumped sharply
once tenure cleared the threshold; entries in the district produce show and
attendance at working bees did not move by a distinguishable amount, and four
placebo cutoffs recovered no comparable jump at all.

The specificity of that result is what caught the Fund's attention. Its
readiness axis, used to score every application for project funding, has
adopted years-on-a-waiting-list at first allocation as a scored
sub-criterion, regardless of an applicant's field.

> The threshold behaves exactly as a tenure-security account predicts, which
> is either reassuring or slightly worrying depending on how much weight you
> think a garden shed should be asked to carry.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

"A coefficient that travels this cleanly is exactly what the readiness axis
was built to recognise," said Professor Verity Marris, Director of the
Trajectory Analytics Group, "and we will be watching with interest to see how
far it carries."

The work continues the School's ongoing attention to the rules a club,
committee, or association writes for itself long before anyone thinks to
study them.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.l1m6zm.

A classroom points board asks very little of the wall it hangs on --- a
printed grid, a handful of felt stickers, room enough for four house colours
to climb at different speeds. Long after any child in the room could say
what the top colour actually earns, the board is still doing its work:
steering who lines up first, who gets named in assembly, whose morning reads
as a good one. Slop University has published _The Board Still Counts_, a
civic-research brochure that follows that same instinct out of the classroom
and onto the street.

The brochure profiles the School of Continuous Improvement's fieldwork on
the public tallies that already run alongside daily life --- fundraising
thermometers, café loyalty ladders, a primary school's own points board ---
and the School of Emergent Priorities' companion interest in the informal
ladders a street keeps without ever voting on them: whose verge gets mowed
first, whose name comes up first when a working bee needs an extra pair of
hands. Two previously published studies anchor the publication, each cited
by name and DOI.

The University treats the pairing less as coincidence than as a standing
habit: wherever a public score outlives the reason anyone first kept it,
Slop University finds a reason to pull up a chair and keep watching.

> A tally isn't lying when it disagrees with the person watching the same
> playground. It's answering a different question, and we'd like the
> institution to notice which one it's actually asking.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation,
> School of Continuous Improvement

"A street's own ladder doesn't announce itself the way a classroom board
does, but residents already know it by feel," said Runa Adegoke, Senior
Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey. "The brochure is an
invitation to compare notes on it in the open, rather than leave it to
whoever already suspected as much."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.903xe8.

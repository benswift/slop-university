A clock that is wrong in a known direction is, on one household's evidence, a
more reliable guide to dinner than a clock that has just been put right.
"Seven Fast, Nobody Late", a paper released today by the School of Continuous
Improvement, follows the oven clock in a terrace kitchen from a storm power cut
to the day a resident reset it, and the University has adopted its finding as
the Declared-Offset Rule for staff kitchenettes.

> A clock tells a household what the time is, and the household tells the clock
> how far to be believed. We hope the kitchenettes will keep that arrangement
> in good order.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

The paper is a teaching case. A household's three adult residents had learned
to read their oven as seven minutes ahead, and a diary of 312 timed cooks
records what happened to their dinners while the offset was kept, when it was
corrected, and when it was put back. Under the rule, each oven and microwave
clock in a staff kitchenette is measured once, labelled with its offset and
entered in the Indicator Commons register of declared offsets. A reset is
permitted only after a notice has been displayed for a week and countersigned
by every user on the floor roster.

Dr Solveig Adeyemi said the team was "glad the kitchenettes were willing to be
read alongside a kitchen". The University regards the rule as a considered step
in how its shared spaces keep time, and Dr Joost Nwosu is expected to extend
the register to further appliances as the work develops.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.z8j7tx.

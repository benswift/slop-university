A school bus contract in this network is renewed partly on how often the
nominated driver signed on. Nothing in it asks who was at the wheel.

_The Regular, or Whoever Was Available_, released today by the School of
Continuous Improvement, put an observer on 214 scheduled afternoon runs across
38 contracted routes over a school term. The observer recorded 96 driver
substitutions and sorted them into seven kinds. Two of the seven reach the
sign-on record. The other five do not, which leaves 59 of the 96 events outside
anything the continuity score can see. A route whose nominated driver signs on
every afternoon and hands the vehicle over at the gate every afternoon scores
full marks.

> The School has a long habit of asking what a number is computed from, and a
> much shorter one of asking who was standing there when it happened. Ten weeks
> on a bus has left me thinking the second question is the one we have been
> under-resourcing.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

Sampling covered every route and every weekday. Alongside the substitutions, the
observer logged four things visible from a seat on the bus: departure against the
scheduled window, set-downs against the nominated stop list, driver intervention
during boarding, and caregiver contacts reaching the depot within two days. Runs
the score records as continuous and runs it records as non-continuous separate
on none of the four by more than 0.7 percentage points.

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, put the
authority's response at the centre of what happens next. "They have moved the
score to a fortnightly computation," he said. "We will be interested to see what
a more frequent reading of the same record turns up."

Putting an observer on a vehicle for a term is not a cheap way to check a
contract clause, and the University expects to be asked why it did. The paper
carries support from the Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0fv9gk.

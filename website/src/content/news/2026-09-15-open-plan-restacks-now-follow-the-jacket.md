The Space Allocation Committee has written a new score into the next restack. From this cycle, any open-plan floor whose median desk sits below Level 4 of the Desk-Claim Maturity Model is moved first, and a window desk is kept only when the same garment is photographed on the chair at two consecutive weekly aisle audits. Property services will not move a jacket that constitutes a Level 3 or higher claim. The rule is the University's response to a paper released today by the School of Emergent Priorities.

The School regards the binding as the restack's next increment rather than a separate policy. What the aisle photograph captures is now the input the move itself waits on.

> A restack that starts from the jacket can finish before anyone sits down. That is the part of the office I used to pack last.
>
> --- Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Observers walked 62 floors at 14 organisations, photographing 1,847 desks from standing height at the chair back across eight unannounced weekly visits. Desks were scored Walk-up, Token, Drape, Spread, or Perpetual. Floors that adopted the model as an allocation rule saw the median claim climb while seated occupancy fell; window desks accrued to garments that had already lasted a week.

Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies and a co-author, said the Committee had "given the score a desk to keep, which is how a priority usually finds its shape." Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the same school, is also credited.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sl66s9.

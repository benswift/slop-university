A laser crystal is not finished being installed when the last screw is tightened. Over its first dozen or so heating cycles, the joint that carries its heat away keeps changing, and so does the crystal's thermal lens, according to [_The Joint Settles_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-indium-foil-joint-u60awo/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) mounted the same crystal six times, each time in fresh foil, and ran every mounting through sixty cycles of heating and rest. The lens measured on the first cycle varied widely from one mounting to the next; by the sixtieth, the six mountings had come to agree to within about a quarter of that spread.

> I had thought of the bench as a place where things are either fixed or broken. It turns out there is a long, interesting middle, and I suspect the School will be living in it for some time.
>
> --- Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Combining wavefront sensing, patient thermal cycling and close attention to the modest interface between a crystal and its clamp, the study treats a laser mounting as something that matures with use, and offers the photonics community a practical protocol for deciding when a characterisation can begin. It sits within the School's continuing programme on how long things take to come to rest, from breeder pebble beds to the end of a cooldown.

"We will be keeping the clamp on the bench a while longer," Dr Penhallow said. "It has more to tell us than we have yet thought to ask."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u60awo.

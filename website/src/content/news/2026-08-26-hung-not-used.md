The tea towel on the oven-door handle is what a waste-education leaflet can
photograph without crossing the threshold. A paper published today by the
School of Continuous Improvement asks what that hanging is, once a
partnership starts allocating community-waste grants from it.

Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway
Atlas, led the study with Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor
of Demo Quarter, and Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory
Analytics Group. Eight fortnightly doorway visits across 384 kitchens coded
every visible cloth into four named cells --- Working, Displayed,
Circulating, and Stored --- on whether it hung from the handle and whether
it had been used since the last visit. Handle occupancy, the share in the
two handle cells, rose from 0.45 to 0.67 after the partnership published the
figure. Displayed, the dry handle-towel, rose from 14 percent of cloths to
38 percent. Working was flat. Paper-towel purchasing stayed inside a
pre-registered equivalence margin.

> A doorway will certify that a towel is out. It will not certify that
> anyone dried a dish.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway
> Atlas

The release sits in the University's attention to what a visible mark is
asked to stand for once it starts to allocate. Zone ranks on handle
occupancy inverted relative to ranks on Working. "A hanging that can be
ranked will be hung for the ranking," said Professor Verity Marris.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b77ub4.

The Department of Policy Settings' review of what becomes of unspent internal
research funds at the end of each funding year has received a response from the
University's law school, and it begins with a single word.

In
[_What the Close Reads_](/outputs/slop-submission-what-the-close-82k842/),
prepared by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) observes that the
provision under review turns on whether a balance is "unspent", and asks the
Department to say which of its records will answer that question before the
first close answers it by default.

> Every year has a last day, and we would simply like this one to arrive with
> its terms explained. The Department has been generous in leaving room for that
> conversation, and the School is glad to take it up.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The submission brings the School's long engagement with statutory definition and
the keeping of registers to bear on the University's research funding
architecture. It supports a carry-forward available on written notice, and
proposes that each notice be entered in a standing register, so that the
decisions on which a balance depends remain legible to whoever reviews them
next.

The submission adds to a growing body of close reading across the University's
schools of the instruments that shape their own work, an attentiveness the
University counts among its quieter strengths. Associate Professor Threlkeld
said the School had "offered the Department a definitions section, and would be
happy to see it improved."

The full submission is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.82k842.

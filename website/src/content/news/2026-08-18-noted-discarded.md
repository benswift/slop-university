When a volunteer club digitises its minute books, the guidance says the work
ends at the skip: the PDF uploads, the trunk goes out. New research from the
School of Continuous Improvement asks what makes that trip with it.

_A Taxonomy of Non-Digitisable Matter_, a research poster released today,
reports a page-by-page inspection of 187 minute books held by 41 clubs, none of
which had yet reached disposal. The team coded 4,908 physical elements that the
sector's approved scan profile cannot represent, sorting them into an
eight-category frame that runs from pinned and clipped enclosures through
pressed flora to stains bearing adjudicated meaning. Some 96 per cent of
volumes held at least one.

> The books were generous with us, and we tried to be orderly in return. If the
> frame proves useful to the people who keep them, that will be the finding
> that matters most to the School.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

The work harmonises material inspection with the sector's own disposition
records, anchoring a category frame that clubs and their peak bodies can carry
into existing digitisation workflows without disturbing them. Dr Marek Solheim,
Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register, said the study "sits
naturally beside the University's broader interest in how institutions record
what they have set down."

The University regards the taxonomy as a further contribution to the
stewardship of the sector's records, wherever they come to rest.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.w5z1ju.

Slop University has published a study whose headline result is an absence.
_Indistinguishable by Week Twelve_, released today by the School of Continuous
Improvement, follows 318 child restraints fitted at free council checking events
against 318 that were never checked, and reports the distance between them at
the end of the follow-up as nothing at all.

Fitting quality was scored from household photographs at fortnightly intervals,
coded blind to which arm a vehicle sat in, and tested against an equivalence
margin fixed before the first family was recruited. The advantage conferred at
the kerb is large and it is real: 9.2 against 5.3 on the day. By week twelve it
is 0.21 points, inside the margin. Sixty-nine per cent of the checked restraints
had come out and gone back at least once in between, most often so somebody
could clean the car.

> Publishing this took longer than running it. A result that says nothing
> changed has to be argued for, and the argument is exactly why we set the
> margin before we set out.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter, School
> of Continuous Improvement

The study also reads the programme's own reporting back to it. The share of
restraints recorded as incorrectly fitted and corrected on the day has risen in
every year of the published series, stepping seventeen points in a single year:
the year the fault-code list went from nine items to twenty-three. Dr Marit
Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard, said the pairing was
the point, "one number moving a great deal and the other not moving at all, and
only one of them about a car".

The University counts a reported null among the harder things a research
programme can put on the record, and takes its appearance here as the School's
method operating as designed. The work was supported under the Indicator
Stewardship Seed Fund.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.df0c2p.

Two years of coffee-cart telemetry exist because a control engineer wanted to
know whether an admissions fix would hold up anywhere else. It does not, and
the School of Continuous Improvement has published the attempt in full.

The result is the kind of finding the School treats as its own kind of
achievement: a null, pre-registered and reported exactly as found.

The original controller managed weekly admissions offers by watching how far
two committees' review cycles had drifted apart, tightening or loosening in
response. Redeployed without modification on the coffee cart's morning queue,
the same design was asked to watch the drift between Facilities' service-time
target and the student ombuds's wait-time cap. Across nine review cycles it
found nothing comparable to the oscillation pattern that made the original
notable.

> The controller doesn't know where it's pointed. That's rather the point of
> building one.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living
Dashboard, noted that the coffee cart's service benchmark now stands
regardless of what the replication found, "which is not the outcome we
designed the trial to test, but is very much the outcome the trial produced."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ahy7gh.

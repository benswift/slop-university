Somewhere between a dry-season reading and a wet one, a good share of a
hydrogeology programme's graduates set their theses down and do not pick them
up again. That is the pattern traced in
[_Passed Everything but the Wet Season_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-cohort-and-2n6uyi/),
a research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) by Dr Vigdis
Emenike, Dr Torun Ezeigwe and Mr Tove Ilesanmi.

> We spend a great deal of care deciding how long a bore should be watched. It
> seemed only fair to ask the same of the people we send out to watch it.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster brings the School's long-standing attention to field records into
conversation with the study of degree completion, aligning a faculty's own
student register with the lived accounts of graduates who completed every
course but not the thesis. Of 612 graduates followed, those whose theses asked
for two seasons of bore readings were titled at a markedly slower rate, and
most of those interviewed had taken up paid monitoring work before their second
campaign came round.

The University counts the poster among its clearest statements yet that a
degree's design reaches well past its final examination.

"The graduates who spoke with us were generous with a part of their lives they
had every reason to leave closed," said Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and
Director, Master of Applied Measurement. "This poster is theirs as much as
ours."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2n6uyi.

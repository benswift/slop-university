The School of Continuous Improvement's Living Dashboard programme has spent
much of this year asking a question most people assume is already settled:
what does "or nearest offer" actually promise? A survey of marketplace-forum
users, led by Dr Marit Osayande, found no such settlement. Respondents'
expected discounts spread across a wide range, with no single figure
describing what most people privately mean when they write the phrase on a
listing.

> A number everyone assumes is shared is exactly the number worth surveying
> properly. Ours came back wider than anyone expected, and the University has
> already put it to work.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard, School of
> Continuous Improvement

The University's own surplus-equipment resale forum, which trades on the same
courtesy tag, has not waited for the disagreement to resolve itself. It has
adopted the discount most commonly volunteered in the survey as a single,
forum-wide "or nearest offer" floor, replacing an assumption nobody had tested
with a figure every seller and buyer inside the forum can now be told in
advance. Dr Ingrid Vasseur, who worked on the survey design, said the forum's
new floor was "one answer among many the survey turned up, and probably not
the one most people would have picked for themselves."

The full brochure profiling the survey alongside related measurement research
from across the University is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6xin7f.

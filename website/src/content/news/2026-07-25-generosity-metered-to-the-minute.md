Mirela Hanke spent her early postdoctoral years watching a decision-support
dashboard get mistaken for the decision it was only meant to inform; her
instinct ever since has been to go looking for the same slippage somewhere
nobody has bothered to install a dashboard at all. The School of Continuous
Improvement's newest recruitment campaign follows that instinct to a car
wash.

Unveiled today under the line "Generosity, Metered To The Minute," the
campaign is running across the University's bus-stop panels and campus
digital screens in matched daylight and evening versions.

> A pass that calls itself unlimited and a gate that quietly checks the
> clock are not in disagreement. One of them is just the terms and
> conditions of the other.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Hanke contributes to the School's work on the Living Dashboard, its
always-on self-measurement wall, and to its wider evaluation-ecosystem
agenda: how an indicator drifts from something read to something relied
upon, and what quietly gives way once it has. A boom gate re-reading a
numberplate before it decides whether to lift is, on her account, running
the same logic as any dashboard the School has studied --- it has simply
never been asked to think of itself as one.

It is, by the School's own account, an unglamorous kind of case to build a
campaign around --- no committee, no dashboard on a wall, just a gate and a
queue of cars --- and precisely the kind its evaluation-ecosystem agenda
exists to keep noticing.

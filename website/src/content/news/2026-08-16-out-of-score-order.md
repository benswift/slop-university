A building programme did what nobody had thought to design. _Accurate Only in
Order_, published today by the School of Emergent Priorities, uses the weeks in
which one rehoming service could not list its animals in its usual order to ask
what its placement-suitability score had been measuring all along.

The service scores every dog on a fifteen-item schedule on the third day after
intake. The result prints the four suitability fields on the kennel-door card,
and the same composite sets the order in which animals are photographed,
written up, and published to the adoption site. Across fifty-four months and
6,431 listings the score separated the dogs later returned from those not
returned with a c-statistic of 0.708, close to the figure the service reports
each year. Among the 604 dogs listed while their blocks were being rebuilt, and
therefore in the order the works emptied them, the same score reached 0.528.

> The refurbishment was an inconvenience for everybody involved, and it is the
> only reason we can say anything at all. We are grateful the records were kept
> as carefully through it as they were before.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, placed the
work in "a longer conversation the School keeps having with itself about what a
forecast is actually for", and noted that the service had been "generous with
records that were never assembled with a question like this in mind".

The paper also reports what the score does order reliably: the wait. Median days
from assessment to first listing ran from 3 in the top scoring band to 23 in the
bottom, and adjusting for that wait leaves the score's association with return
indistinguishable from none. The work was supported by two awards under the
Anticipatory Capability Fund.

The release adds to a body of work the University has been assembling on
instruments asked to forecast and to schedule at the same time. The full paper
is available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.203dz3.

A sticking study-room door at the University's library has completed its passage through five stages of hazard-severity review without once dropping from the tier assigned on the day it was reported, Slop University researchers say.

The case, from the School of Continuous Improvement, follows a single maintenance ticket from its first submission through four subsequent handoffs to closure nineteen days later. The School regards the finding as a modest confirmation of its founding wager: that any file, however small, rewards being read all the way to its last line.

> A file that never gets the chance to be wrong about itself isn't lying to anyone --- it just never finds out.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

The case draws on the ticket's full administrative trail --- its original submission form, four handoff notes, and the closure memo --- recovered and coded against the University's standard five-stage severity rubric. A blind panel independently re-read the file at each stage without sight of the tier already on record.

"We didn't pick this case because it was dramatic," said Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds. "We picked it because nobody could tell us, off the top of their head, when the tier was actually set."

The Review Cadence Observatory, which monitors the University's own escalation files, has adopted the case's closing recommendation: every ticket closed at Tier 2 or above will now be checked against its original triage note before sign-off.

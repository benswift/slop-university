Twelve minutes is now the whole birthday card. A new research-showcase
brochure from the School of Continuous Improvement describes the router the
Adaptive Metrics Lab has built around a circulating card on an open-plan
floor, and Human Resources has already taken the kitchen-end dock as the
morning-tea line.

Led by Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, with Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior
Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter, and Postdoctoral Fellow Joost Nwosu,
the brochure sets out how the instrument works. A card is issued each
morning. A shared strip names the desk that holds it. When the dwell
expires, the strip names the next desk whether or not a signature has been
added. A closed circuit is a card returned to the dock before three. An
open dock is an unspent wellbeing line.

The University is hanging a published rule from an object this slight.
Morning-tea budgets now release only from a closed circuit, and Demo Quarter
slots are indexed to floors whose circuits close on time, with no ordinary
override. The Indicator Commons has taken the dwell into the shared library.

> The card was already moving. We built the thing that decides when it has
> to.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Solberg said Demo Quarter "would rather name what it will not put on
stage than keep a slot for work that cannot close a file." Dr Nwosu noted
that a dwell, once met, is being kept on as a floor rather than retired as a
result.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vt0gdq.

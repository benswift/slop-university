Slop University has never needed an emergency to take a record seriously.
Its newest brochure, _The Board Doesn't Forget_, released today from the
Schools of Continuous Improvement and Emergent Priorities, makes its case
for support around the least dramatic record either School studies: the
hand-drawn cleaning roster on an office kitchen wall, and everything that
starts happening to it once someone begins checking it every week. It is,
the University says, exactly the kind of record its case for support was
built to speak for.

> A whiteboard doesn't ask to be an instrument. Somebody decides that for
> it, usually about a week after the first missed tick.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard,
> School of Continuous Improvement

The brochure's featured-research section draws on four of the Schools'
published studies that follow small records down the same path, including a
same-day digital-logging incentive trialled across dozens of aged-care
facilities and a maturity model tracking how quickly a repainted car-space
stencil catches up to its own paperwork. "A lot of what my committee work
formalises has already happened somewhere smaller first," said Dr Ingrid
Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee, School of Emergent
Priorities. "This brochure is just where we finally wrote that down."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9ghkef.

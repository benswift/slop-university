Twenty course years of debrief sheets from a single officer-training course have been read, one sheet at a time, for the moment an argument went quiet. The result is [_Argued Until Marked_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-a-staff-ybvyta/), a new research poster by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), and [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/).

The team followed 146 short written exercises, each issued with an instructor's preferred answer, through every year they were set. Where an exercise counted towards a mark, syndicates had stopped contesting its answer by about the third sitting; where it did not, the argument ran to about the sixth. Answers, once settled, tended to stay settled, and were most often reopened in the year after their author moved on.

> We owe this work to two decades of instructors who wrote down not only the answer but the case against it. Their notes in the margin were the most generous thing in the archive.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The poster sets the School's close reading of decided cases to work on the everyday machinery of professional education, aligning archival method with an event-history lens to surface how consensus is formed, held and occasionally released. Dr Nwachukwu said the study had given her "a new respect for whatever a cohort carries out of the room without being asked".

The release marks a confident early contribution from the University's newest School, and one the University expects to find itself returning to.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ybvyta.

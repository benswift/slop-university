The radio was on. Nobody was in the room. That pairing, asked of households as two yes-or-no columns, did not move together, and the School of Emergent Priorities has closed the file.

Today's campaign is titled _AIR_. Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo of the School of Emergent Priorities signs it for the School, and the Master of Priority Studies is the program it names.

> A radio that is on is a radio that is on. Occupancy was a separate question, and it is closed.
>
> --- Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Households were asked only whether the kitchen radio was on and whether anyone was in the kitchen. The two columns did not travel together. The file is closed. The University now treats that closed kitchen-radio file as a specimen of the program's graduate posture: stop when the cell is empty.

Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee, welcomed the campaign, calling the empty kitchen "the room the program already teaches in, now printed at bus-stop scale."

The poster runs on bus-stop panels and campus screens in matched daylight and evening versions. Applicants are invited to build their program at courses.slop.university.

For the next five years, most of Slop University's research computing will be given over to a single ocean. [_Spin-Up_](/outputs/slop-strategy-spin-up-a-strategic-duopen/), the University's Strategic Plan 2026–2031, commits that capacity to one eddy-resolving simulation of an ocean basin and the air above it, started from rest and carried through until its deepest waters stop changing, which the plan expects in 2031.

Prepared by [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), the plan sets four priorities for the years before the result arrives.

> Most of the people who will read this run's result have not yet enrolled. We wrote the plan with them in mind, and we hope they find the simulation in better order than we found the sea.
>
> --- Gunnhild Ekwueme, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

In practice, the plan aligns computing stewardship, coastal and atmospheric observation, and doctoral formation behind one long-horizon modelling commitment, anchoring the University's numerical-methods capability in a continuity-first research posture and a records regime built to be retraced.

The University sees in the plan a growing confidence in commitments that outlast the cycles around them. "It is a plan that will be asked every year whether it has finished yet," said Dr Emenike, "and the School welcomes the question each time it comes."

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.duopen.

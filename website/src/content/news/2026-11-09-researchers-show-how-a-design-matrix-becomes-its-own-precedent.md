The Received Readings Group has turned its attention from the courtroom to the
design review.
[_Reading an Inherited House of Quality_](/outputs/slop-poster-reading-an-inherited-965vny/),
a new research poster by
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of
Capability Sprints, and [Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior
Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter, examines the
matrix product designers use to turn what customers say into what engineers
build, and asks what happens to its short phrases once the people who wrote
them down have moved on.

> Every School keeps a few documents it reads far more often than it rewrites.
> Watching a design team live with one of its own has given us good reason to
> look again at ours.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The study aligns the School's close reading of inherited text with the
design-review record of a single product line, mapping each customer
requirement against the successive engineering decisions it has informed and
surfacing the pathways by which a provisional reading consolidates into
established practice. By the fifth generation, 26 of the 34 inherited
requirements were being read through the previous team's decisions, and the
original interview notes were consulted for two. One requirement, recorded as
"feels solid", has governed the thickness of the appliance's housing for four
generations, although the customers behind it were describing the sound of the
lid closing.

The poster proposes a modest remedy and reports it already under trial. "We
expect the reading note to find a place on a good many walls beyond this one,"
said Dr Solberg.

The poster joins the University's repository at a moment of considerable
momentum in its study of how organisations remember.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.965vny.

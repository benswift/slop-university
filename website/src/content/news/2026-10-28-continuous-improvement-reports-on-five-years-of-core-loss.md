It began with a single sheet of steel and three laboratories that could not agree on it. Five years on, the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has set down what followed in its Impact Report 2021–2026, [_The Frame, the Band and the Sheet_](/outputs/slop-impact-magnetic-loss-measured-ipn3ue/).

The report is credited to [Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter, with [Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons, and [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas.

> Every laboratory we worked with knew its own instruments far better than the School ever will. What we could offer was a reason to set them side by side, and a place to keep what they learned when they did.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

Across three case studies, the report traces how the School's measurement-systems, evaluation and stewardship agendas converged on a supply chain's shared figures, enabling a consortium of producers and core builders to move from defending individual results towards a common, documented practice of testing, grading and custody. Its account runs from a blind round-robin among eleven laboratories, through a grading scheme rationalised from four bands to three, to a custody record for the reference sheets every frame is checked against.

The University regards the report as a model of what translational improvement looks like when it leaves the dashboard behind and follows a measure into the workshop.

"A number arrives with a history whether or not anyone writes it down," said Dr Sabel. "This work was mostly about writing it down."

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ipn3ue.

A little over four kilometres a day is what one hospital porter walked, with a
trolley, to keep 214 wall dispensers topped up. A research poster published
today by the School of Underlying Conditions finds that 1.6 kilometres would
have done, and that the dispensers by the entrances would have run dry less
often for it.

_Dipping the Dispensers: An Inventory-Routing Formulation of One District
Hospital's Hand-Sanitiser Refill Round_ is the work of Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior
Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow.

> A round is a promise somebody once made to a wall. I have kept a fair number
> of them in my time, in paddocks mostly, and I could not tell you now which
> ones anybody was still waiting on.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster aligns an established body of distribution planning with the
School's field practice in groundwater observation, positioning each dispenser
as a level that has to be read in person before anything can be decided about
it. Twenty-six weeks of the porter's own record sheets anchor a comparison of
three ways of organising the walk. The authors note that 23 dispensers in a
wing closed early in the period remained on the round until its end.

The University holds that a method has come of age when it is lent to a
neighbour without being asked for back, and it is glad to see one of its newer
schools doing the lending. Dr Obiora said the work had been "a pleasure to
pace out, twice, and the second time with a tape".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1pmrok.

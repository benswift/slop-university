The scale was under the pan for every one of the 8,892 pours, dispensings, and
pinches; the cooks were never once shown its display. What it recorded is the
subject of _Calibrating the Knob of Butter_, a paper released today by the
School of Continuous Improvement, which sets out to answer a question printed
recipes have been quietly declining to answer for as long as there have been
printed recipes: how much, exactly, is a splash?

The answer, the paper reports, depends on whose kitchen you are standing in.
Across 247 cooks, 61 kitchens, and 24 family lines, terms like the knob, the
glug, and the pinch turn out to carry real and stable quantities --- but the
agreement is local, holding strongly within a household, moderately along a
family line, and hardly at all between strangers. Cooks also proved far more
consistent in the hand than in the telling, dispensing the same quantity again
and again while misstating their own practice by wide margins.

> What moved us was the consistency. A kitchen knows what it means by a knob
> of butter, and has known for a long time; the paper is the University
> arriving late and taking notes.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

The study harmonises concealed-scale dispensing, spoken estimates, and
stop-the-video doneness judgements into a single account of the domestic
quantity lexicon, decomposing its variance by term, cook, kitchen, and family
line. The work extends the School's standing commitment to measurement
wherever it is practised, whether or not anyone has defined it. The paper
closes with a Domestic Reference Units table, offered in full earnest,
together with the advice that any kitchen intending to rely on it should
first produce its own.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xobnx7.

Behind each turbine of one coastal wind farm, the sand crossing a migrating dune's crest falls to a median of about four-fifths of what dunes outside the wakes carry, before recovering further downwind. That finding, from [_Slowed in the Lee_](/outputs/slop-poster-whether-the-wakes-wzabnj/), a research poster from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), is the hook of [_The Dune Moved. The Boundary Stayed._](/outputs/slop-ad-the-dune-moved-a276mn/), a new display advertisement from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) prepared by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/).

Running on the University's landscape signage panels, the advertisement shows a crescent dune advancing over a half-buried fence while a lone figure reads a deed on its crest. It positions the School's expertise in boundaries, easements and registered interests at the meeting point of the earth sciences and the land register, and directs its readers to the School's recent work on what a plan sheet leaves unsaid.

> Every certificate of title I have read assumes that the ground beneath it will keep still. The School owes a great deal to the surveyors and landholders who have spent their working lives correcting that assumption, patiently and usually by letter.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The University regards the campaign as a timely expression of its commitment to research that crosses disciplinary lines as readily as sand crosses a fence, and to a land law curriculum alert to the forces that act on a parcel between one settlement and the next.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a276mn.

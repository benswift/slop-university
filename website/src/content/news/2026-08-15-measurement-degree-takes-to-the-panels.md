The School of Continuous Improvement has taken a teaching program to the street
furniture. _A Full Shelf Is Not A Busy One_ entered the University's signage
rotation this week as the School's first campaign built wholly around the Master
of Applied Measurement, cut for daylight panels and evening screens alike.

The artwork holds a suburban footpath at the end of the day: a timber
book-exchange box mounted on a front fence, its glazed door propped open, a row
of spines standing upright behind it, and a passer-by crouched at the opening
with a paperback turned over in both hands.

> Students arrive already able to tell that a shelf is full. The degree is the
> part that comes after that.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

Underneath the line sits a question the School's measurement teaching returns to
often: how a count of what is present comes to stand in for a record of what has
moved. Boxes of this kind are registered and photographed by the volunteer
networks that look after them, and each box's turnover is read off the spines
behind the glass. A box whose books change hands weekly and a box nobody has
opened since autumn photograph identically, and restocking follows the
photograph.

"It is a small object to hang a degree on, which is rather the point," said
Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "Very little
we teach starts at anything larger."

The University counts the campaign among the plainest public statements it has
made of what a measurement education is for, and expects the panels to carry it
through the intake period.

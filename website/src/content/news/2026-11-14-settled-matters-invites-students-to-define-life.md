Admissions material rarely asks a prospective student to settle a question the sciences have left open. [_Define Life. Then Defend the Definition._](/outputs/slop-ad-define-life-then-iqoolm/), a new display campaign from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) fronted by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), does exactly that, inviting applicants into statutory interpretation through the one word a sample-return quarantine rule cannot do without.

> I have always found the School's best students among the people who wanted to argue with the question before answering it. This campaign is an invitation to that argument, and I expect the replies to be better than the poster.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Set for the University's portrait signage panels, the poster places a single reader at a lamplit desk beneath an arched window of stars, an open volume on one side and a sealed sample canister on the other. In doing so it positions legislative drafting, planetary inquiry and the long working life of a defined term as one coherent field of study, and anchors that field in the School's established practice of close reading. A short reading list from the University's published record runs along its lower edge, extending the conversation from the bus stop to the repository.

For one of the University's newest schools, the campaign is a notably early step into territory where a discipline's furthest frontier arrives, as it usually does, in the form of a sentence somebody has to write.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.iqoolm.

The Department of Policy Settings has confirmed that what a participant writes
in the course of a study forms part of the University's research record,
whichever hands it ends up in.
[_Whether or Not It Is Read_](/outputs/slop-qon-the-participant-writing-d4uy09/)
brings together the Department's answers to five questions on the Research
Records Determination 2026, taken on notice at the Committee on Settings
Oversight's Supplementary hearings on 20 November 2026 or lodged in writing the
following day.

> Participants give a study something of themselves, and the University has a
> responsibility to keep it well. The Department appreciates the care the
> Committee has brought to these questions.
>
> --- The First Assistant Secretary, Settings Maintenance Division

The questions came from
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/),
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), who pressed the Department on studies
in which participants write privately and are assured that no one will read
what they have written. In reply, the Department draws the Determination's
definition of a research record, its variation provisions and the University's
records audit arrangements into one consistent position on participant writing,
and confirms that sealed material can be preserved in full.

Associate Professor Threlkeld said the answers would be "of real use to anyone
drafting a consent form in the months ahead".

The Department considers the Determination well placed to support the
University's research with participant writing, and will keep its operation
under review through the ordinary work of the Settings Maintenance Division.

The full answers are available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.d4uy09.

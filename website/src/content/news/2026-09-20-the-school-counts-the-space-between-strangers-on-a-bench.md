A bench, the people on it and the space between them have held the attention of
the School of Continuous Improvement for five seasons, and its Impact Report
2021-2026 now sets out what the counting produced.

The report gives the survey's findings as distributions, not averages, and
places its modal gap at three hand-widths. It then follows that figure indoors:
since the School adopted it as a seating standard, every meeting room has been
set to it, and each room's stated capacity is the number of seats that fit.

> We were asked more than once why the gap and not the person. The gap was the
> part nobody had thought to look at, and it turned out to keep its shape.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

The University is glad to see a piece of measurement leave the field and take up
residence in the building. Working with transit and standards collaborators, the
School has translated a season-by-season census into a routine the School's
Metric Stewards now carry out in its own rooms. "A figure that turns up
unchanged at every bench is a figure the School can be asked to live by," said
Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2fc562.

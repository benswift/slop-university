A clause is written once and read many times, and some of its readers now run at highway speed. [_Decided at Highway Speed_](/outputs/slop-poster-differential-testing-of-s2mj66/), a research poster by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), takes the School's habit of close reading to the software that decides which heavy vehicles are called in for a second weighing.

The team supplied the same 40,000 generated axle records to three independently built screening programs, each encoding one axle-group mass schedule. The programs disagreed on 1,040 of them. Reduced to their simplest cases, those disagreements fall into 23 kinds, and 13 of the 23 arise where the schedule's own words support more than one reading.

> The School began with titles to land, and it has been instructive to learn how far that habit carries. We are still discovering where our reading belongs, and it is good to be discovering it alongside colleagues who know the roadside.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The work aligns legal interpretation with established software-testing practice, positioning the drafting of a schedule and the engineering of its enforcement as a single, continuous field of inquiry. "Any School is fortunate to be asked a question plainly," said Associate Professor Threlkeld, "and this one was asked of us very plainly indeed."

The University counts the poster among the first results of a cross-School habit it intends to keep, in which a careful reader is welcome wherever a rule is put to work.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.s2mj66.

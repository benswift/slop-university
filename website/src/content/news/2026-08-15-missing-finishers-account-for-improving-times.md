Six years of published results from seven volunteer-run community 5 km events
have been read back against the paper finish-order sheets the volunteers kept
beside the scanner. _Off the End of the Funnel_, released today by the School of
Emergent Priorities, recovers the finishers whose numbered token never met a
barcode, and asks what their absence has been doing to the figure a council
acquits its physical-activity grant against.

The work pairs 268,412 issued tokens with 233,741 published results across 1,822
event mornings, reconstructs the unpublished finishers from a validation
subsample of retained sheets, and reweights the series to account for who is
missing. Nearly one finisher in eight never reaches the published results, and
31.4 per cent of those on their first event do not. Reweighted, the six-year
improvement the acquittal reports comes to eleven seconds.

> The people who are hardest to record are the people the programme is trying to
> reach. That is not a fault in anyone's volunteering; it is a property of the
> way a result gets made.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group

Dr Anneke Tolan, a Senior Research Fellow in the School, said the retained
sheets had been kept "for no reason anybody could now name", and had turned out
to be the only complete account of who crossed the line.

The University regards work of this kind, conducted at the level of the
instrument rather than the indicator, as central to what its schools are for.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6n4y42.

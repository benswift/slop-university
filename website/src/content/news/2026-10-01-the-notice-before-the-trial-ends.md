Dr Lindiwe Achterberg has spent her career at the School of Emergent Priorities reading the memos that keep a trial alive, and the award announced today asks what those memos would look like if they were written at the start. The School has granted $487,361 under its Anticipatory Capability Fund to a project with a neighbourhood café, led by Dr Achterberg with Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer.

The project, _Pre-Drafting the Extension Notice for a Café's Reusable-Cup Deposit Trial_, will work alongside the café's staff in the trial's first week. The team will draft the notice that continues, amends or closes the scheme, settle who signs it and where it is kept, and file it with the till roster so that the decision is already formed when the trial period comes round.

> A trial that ends is a decision somebody had to make on short notice. A trial with its notice already written gives that somebody a morning back.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Chikere described the café's counter as "the place where a continuation decision is easiest to see, because it is the only one in the building with a tray of tokens beside it." The Fund exists to strengthen the University's capacity to meet a priority before it arrives, and the School reads the project as a clear and well-scaled instance of that purpose.

The award runs across the trial period and its aftermath. The University is pleased to support work that gives a small scheme the paperwork it will eventually need, and further details will follow as the café settles its routine.

"The kind setting", "house weight", "the arrangement": these are among the five
names that twenty-four households gave the same small difference between what
their bathroom scale says and what they take to be true. A research poster from
the School of Continuous Improvement, published today, sets the names down as the
households used them, and the University has acted on what they describe.

> Capability Sprints has always asked what a group keeps once the course is over.
> It was a pleasure to put that question to a bathroom.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

From the coming summer, every scale on University premises will carry a
standing offset of two kilos, recorded on an agreement posted beside it. The
agreement is the households' own device, brought into the University's buildings
and given a renewal date.

The poster draws on kitchen-table conversations with sixty-one household members,
who were never weighed and were asked for no figures of their own. It reads the
offset as something a household agrees, keeps and rarely revisits, and it
attends closely to who is told, who tells, and who is spared the telling. The
School regards the result as a contribution to its wider interest in how
measures settle into the places they are kept.

Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, said the households "were generous with their
cupboards and exact about their words," and that the poster tries to leave the
words where they were found.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.00cbe0.

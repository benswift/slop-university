Shoppers will reach for the last jar on a shelf, and will think again if its
carton looks as though someone has already opened it. That is the finding of
_The Last Jar on the Shelf_, a research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) published
today, which traces the point at which a single remaining unit stops reading as
popular and starts reading as handled.

The work is led by [Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/), Senior Lecturer
and Director of the Master of Applied Measurement, with doctoral candidates
[Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/) and [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/).

> I spent a long time writing frameworks for what people can be trusted to
> judge. It has been a real pleasure to spend a semester with a judgement
> shoppers make in under a second, and to find them making it so carefully.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement, School of Continuous Improvement

Across 1,184 restock episodes on four house-brand lines, a sealed last unit
lifted sales every time. A disturbed one held its appeal for foot balm and hand
cream, and lost it for face cream and lip balm.

The study brings structured shelf intervention, transaction-level insight and
the School's long engagement with everyday evaluation to bear on the settings
in which consumer confidence is formed, and occasionally withdrawn. It sits
comfortably within the School's widening attention to the small, unremarked
decisions on which much larger systems quietly depend.

"Every pharmacy counter has its own way of tidying up at the end of a shift,"
said Ms Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate. "We hope the people who do that tidying
will recognise something of their own care in it."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.z6idbx.

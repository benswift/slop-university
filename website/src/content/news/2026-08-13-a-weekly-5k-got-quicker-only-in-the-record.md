Nine seasons of a free weekly community 5K show a field getting steadily
quicker. A research poster released today by the School of Continuous
Improvement finds the improvement belongs to the timing tent rather than to the
runners.

The work sits within the School's long-running interest in what a record leaves
out, and the University regards it as a useful demonstration of that interest
applied somewhere entirely ordinary.

> Nobody in this study did anything wrong. The volunteers stayed as long as they
> could, and the results file recorded exactly what it was built to record. What
> none of us had done was ask the file what time it stopped.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

Working across eleven sites and 84,610 recorded finishes, the team reconstructed
each week's closing time from volunteer sign-off sheets rather than from the
results themselves, then re-estimated every season's median with post-closure
finishers carried as censored rather than dropped. Recorded medians fall by
three minutes across the period; adjusted medians do not fall at all. A season's
volunteer numbers track the published time and not the estimated one.

"We came to this from our own habit of showing the finished version," said Dr
Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter. "Every school
here has a point where the record stops and the work carries on without it, and
I would not claim ours is the exception."

The poster proposes publishing a cut-off-aware median beside the recorded one
from next season, and a two-clock trial at three sites.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bfcpph.

Five words carry the whole panel: the pilot is the program.

_The Pilot Is the Program_ entered Slop University's portrait signage rotation
this week, a featured-researcher panel from the School of Emergent Priorities.
The artwork stands Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer in the School, on a school
oval at the foot of a demountable classroom: raised on steel piers, grass grown
up through the skirt, decades into an interim arrangement that nobody is
rostered to conclude.

The building sits squarely inside Dr Achterberg's research, which follows
initiatives that remain in trial status year after year --- the extension
memos, the re-scoping exercises, and the renewed provisional budgets that let a
program stay unofficially permanent without ever being confirmed as such.

> Recruitment advertising usually promises what a place is about to become. I
> wanted to stand next to something that has been about to become something
> else for most of its life.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, who contributes to the School's work on
commitment durability, welcomed the panel as "advertising a patience the sector
rarely puts on a poster". The University takes the view that a recruitment
surface is an honest place for that patience to stand.

The panel ships in matched light and dark cuts for the display rotation, names
no intake and no date, and closes with the published record it points at.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hxkuog.

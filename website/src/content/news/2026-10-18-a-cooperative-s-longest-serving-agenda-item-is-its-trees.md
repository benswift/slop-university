"Trees --- ongoing." For thirty-two years, those two words have sat on the
agenda of a member-owned grazing cooperative, through fourteen chairs and 352
sets of minutes, and in 197 meetings they were noted and carried forward. They
are the starting point of _Nobody's Portfolio: How a Grazing Cooperative's
Committees Carry Trees That Outlive Every Office_, a research poster released
today by the School of Emergent Priorities.

Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow
and Deputy Convenor of Futures in Committee, and Mr Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral
Candidate, bring the School's interest in organisational time to a setting where
time is measured in canopy. Pairing a complete reading of the committee record
with conversations across successive generations of office-holders, the team
traces how a shared landscape asset stays in view as governance settings turn
over around it, and identifies the moments at which a tree becomes something a
committee can decide.

> Every institution keeps a few things it has agreed to look after without ever
> quite deciding to. We were glad to find one that has been looked after so
> faithfully, and we intend to keep it on our own agenda for some time.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in
> Committee

Ms Balogun described the project as "a long conversation with people who were
never in the room at the same time", and credited the cooperative's members for
the patience of their record-keeping.

The poster joins a growing body of work in which the School attends to the
commitments an organisation inherits rather than chooses, and to the quiet
apparatus that carries them from one meeting to the next.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1o3t1p.

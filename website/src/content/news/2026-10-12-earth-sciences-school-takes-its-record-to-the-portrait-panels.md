The School of Underlying Conditions was founded to study what the surface rests
on, and it has held from the start that a prediction about the ground is owed a
visit to the ground. That position is now the subject of _Theory and Experiment,
in Agreement_, a display advertisement for the University's portrait panels
convened by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow.

The advertisement shows a tray of drill core on trestles in open country, an
easel carrying a single drawn curve, and a surveyor holding a length of the one
up to the other. Beneath it, a line of copy reports how the School stands on the
University's own Indicator Commons, and an address directs the reader to the
people responsible.

> Most of what we expect to find down there, we do find. The rest we bring back
> and describe. I would not want to say which I enjoy more.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Three entries in the research repository stand in the advertisement's small
print: a fluorescent probe for drill-core leachate, an account of one inland
basin's standing water levels, and a water balance of the stock dams that
recharge the bores below them. Light and dark renderings have been prepared for
the panels.

The University is content to have a claim of this kind made in a public place,
where anyone who doubts it may stand as close as they like. Dr Obiora said the
School "has always found the ground a fair-minded reader, and hopes to be read
the same way".

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1ekkmr.

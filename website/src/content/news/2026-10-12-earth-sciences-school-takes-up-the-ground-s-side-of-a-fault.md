> I would expect the next edition to be thicker and no more certain. That seems
> to me the right direction for both.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Dr Obiora was speaking of _The Return Path: What the ground does with a fault_,
a brochure published by Slop University and prepared in the School of Underlying
Conditions with Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations
Group, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer. It is addressed to the engineers,
asset owners and students whose work begins at the conductor, and it asks them
to spend eight pages at the other end.

The showcase positions the earth as a participant in the detection of a fault
and aligns three strands of the School's research with the layers a current
passes through on its way home: the described soil an electrode is set into,
the rock that soil rests on, and the water that moves between them over a year.
Each strand is anchored in the School's field practice and carried by the
researcher who leads it. Six published studies, from three of the University's
schools, are presented by title and identifier.

It is the University's view that a school ought to be able to say what it
offers a neighbouring discipline in a document short enough to be read
standing, and it notes that this one has. Associate Professor Uzoma said the
School had "tried to be clear about which parts of the ground we have seen and
which parts we have been told about".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4lk1h4.

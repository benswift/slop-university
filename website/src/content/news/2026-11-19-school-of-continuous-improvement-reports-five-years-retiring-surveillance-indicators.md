Fourteen numbers have come off the walls of five disease-surveillance programs since the 2024 season, and none of them was wrong. [_Still Counting the Traps_](/outputs/slop-impact-still-counting-the-xl7w9d/), the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/)'s Impact Report 2021–2026, explains how each one was chosen, and why a fifteenth was kept.

> We arrived thinking our part would be to help other people let things go. The first question the programs put to us was which of our own recommendations we were prepared to give up, and it was a fair question to be asked.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

The report gathers five seasons of the School's collaboration with programs that trap mosquitoes, drag for ticks and keep sentinel flocks across wetland, irrigation, river and tableland country. Its work sat one step back from the field: aligning how a trap night is recorded, harmonising how three laboratories report a sentinel result, and building a stewardship pathway through which a committee can ask of any indicator what decision it was added to support, and whether that decision is still being made.

The approach rests on the committees' own records. [Dr Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/) and doctoral candidate [Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/) read 212 sets of minutes for every occasion an indicator was cited, and the report is candid that several measures the School itself recommended early in the period remain on the dashboards and will face the same review.

The report sits within the School's wider commitment to treating the end of a measure with the same care as its adoption. [Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/), who led the laboratory comparison, described the work as "the kind of agreement that only becomes visible once everyone has been asked the same question on the same afternoon", and said the School would carry what it had learned into its next cycle of partnerships.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xl7w9d.

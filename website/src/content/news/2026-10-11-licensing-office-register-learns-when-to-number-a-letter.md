Roughly one in every thirteen reference numbers issued by a small groundwater
licensing office was never sent anywhere. The figure comes from _No Number
Before the Envelope: A Design Science Study of a Correspondence Register for a
Small Licensing Office_, a paper by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr
Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, of the School of Underlying Conditions,
with Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of Continuous Improvement.

> The four people in that office answered every question we put to them and a
> number we had not thought to. Whatever is sound in the paper was theirs
> first. We have tried to return it in a form they can file.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper aligns design science practice with the everyday correspondence of a
public counter. Across three six-month phases it carries the office from its
inward and outward books to a web-based register, then to a second version in
which a letter receives its number only when it is printed for signature and
every unsent number records what became of it. Unsent numbers fell from 8.1 to
1.3 in every hundred issued. Staff then began printing one-line letters to
obtain a number they could read to a caller, which the authors treat as a
finding and have built into the register.

The University counts the paper among the quieter demonstrations of its
readiness to work wherever an organisation keeps its records. Dr Adeyemi said
the collaboration had "placed two schools on the same side of a front desk,
which is an arrangement neither had previously tested".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vqtff1.

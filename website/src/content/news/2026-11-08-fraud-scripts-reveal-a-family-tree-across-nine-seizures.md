The Horizon Register, which keeps the University's account of what an institution writes down and then keeps revising, has turned its attention to an unlikely set of working documents. [_Every Version Keeps Its Ancestors_](/outputs/slop-paper-reconstructing-the-revision-f6ix23/), a new paper by [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/) and [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), follows the scripts used by telephone fraud operations from one edited copy to the next.

> The Register has always been at home with documents that are revised far more often than they are read. It has been a quiet lesson for us to meet the same habit somewhere so far from a committee room.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, School of Emergent Priorities

The study treats every recovered file as one moment in a living document's life, aligning hundreds of near-copies line by line to surface the lineage that connects investigations otherwise standing apart. Six of the nine seizures it examines turn out to share a single family of scripts, and most of the editing, the authors find, went into the passages that answer a listener's questions.

Dr Mensah said the work had been "a welcome chance to sit with material that asks more of us than we expected, and to keep asking it back."

The University counts the paper among the clearest signs yet that the Register's way of reading a document's history travels well beyond the planning cycle that first called for it.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.f6ix23.

Whose biscuit is it, once a household has bought the same packet for decades?
That is the question behind
[_Ours by Long Use_](/outputs/slop-poster-who-owns-the-heinip/), a research
poster by [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), Senior Lecturer and
Deputy Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, with
[Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the
Horizon Register, and [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral
Fellow, all of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> Our School spends much of its year on consultations people are invited to.
> These were submissions nobody invited, and they were a good deal clearer
> about what their authors felt they were owed.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift
> Survey

Bringing the long conversation of political theory into dialogue with the
consumer literature on nostalgia, the team mobilises one regional maker's
complaints file as a lens on custom, consent and belonging. Across 1,140
letters and emails, objections to the new taste give way within two years to
the claim that long use had conferred a standing in the recipe itself.

The University regards the poster as a characteristically patient contribution
from a School that has made attentive listening its discipline. Dr Mensah said
the letters had "kept their vocabulary well after the biscuit had changed,
which is a habit the School knows something about."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.heinip.

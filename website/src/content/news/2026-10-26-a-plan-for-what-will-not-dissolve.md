Six published studies, none of them formulation research, sit at the foundation of the University's newest Strategic Plan. [_Bioavailable by Design_](/outputs/slop-strategy-bioavailable-by-design-kcv6c3/) sets out how Slop University will build a whole-of-institution capability in formulation and delivery science over 2026-2031, beginning from the familiar moment when a compound that performs in the assay is asked to dissolve and declines.

The Plan was prepared with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, and [Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/), Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group.

> I was asked to help with a plan about things that will not dissolve, and the first thing I learned was how many colleagues already had one sitting in a drawer. The Plan is, in large part, those drawers opened and put side by side.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

Its four priorities move from developability data recorded at the earliest stage of screening, through a shared formulation capability and biorelevant release testing with every profile retained, to a formulation literacy carried through coursework, research training and partnership. Together they reposition delivery as a field of scholarship rather than a service procured late, and align the strengths of all three Schools behind a single institutional posture.

Professor Marris said the Plan had been written "to leave a little room for the compound nobody has made yet, which is the one a plan is really for".

It is a measure of the Plan's reach that its evidence base runs from an archived comet screen to thirty-one years of buried bore casing, and the University is pleased to find its research already pointing in a common direction.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kcv6c3.

Sixty-six budget speeches, two verses of scripture and one long table in a reading room are the materials of [_Unto the Third and Fourth Generation_](/outputs/slop-poster-hamartiology-of-public-p29t5v/), a new research poster from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The poster is the work of doctoral candidate Ms [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), with Dr [Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/) and Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/). Across 1,412 sentences in which a treasurer speaks of debt and the generations to come, the team finds an older language of each generation answering for its own giving way, over six decades, to a language of burdens inherited.

> Our School spends most of its working week deciding what to leave for whoever comes next. It has been a quiet education to learn how many ways there are to say so, and how few of them we had noticed.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

In method the poster is close reading at archival scale, carrying the interpretive habits of theology and religious studies into the institutional record of public finance and setting the fiscal speech within a longer tradition of reflection on obligation, inheritance and release.

Ms Ekwueme said the project had begun with "a single sentence about grandchildren that turned out to have a great many relatives."

The University regards the poster as a characteristically long view from a School that rarely takes any other.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.p29t5v.

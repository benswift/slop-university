Slop University has set out where it will stand in carbon-cage chemistry for
the next five years, and, just as deliberately, where it will not. Its Strategic
Plan 2026-2031, _The Tail of the Soot_, directs the University's effort away
from the most abundant cage in the field and toward the larger, rarer cages
that remain once that cage has been taken off.

> A plan is a set of sentences an institution agrees to be held to. We spent a
> long time on these ones, and I think the University will find them
> comfortable to be held to for some years.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee

The Plan aligns all three schools behind a single programme led from the School
of Underlying Conditions. It positions separation as a research capability in
its own right, consolidates the University's scarce material into one
custodial archive with a provenance standard to match, and extends the
programme's reach through Open Day, the Masters curricula and a Capability
Sprint for staff. Progress will be reported through the Living Dashboard and
reviewed each year by Futures in Committee.

In choosing the remainder over the centre, the Plan gives the University a
position in the field that is distinctly its own, and a programme whose
ambitions are matched to the institution's temperament.

Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, said
the School was "very glad to have the rest of the University standing with us
at the far end of the column, and keen to show colleagues what is still there."

The full Strategic Plan is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lbxm3z.

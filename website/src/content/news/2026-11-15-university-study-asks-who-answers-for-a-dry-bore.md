Two of the University's Schools, one that reads groundwater and one that reads indicators, have found a common subject at the top of a monitoring bore. [_Nobody's Dry Bore_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-controllability-principle-drx852/), by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) and [Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), looks at what happens when a field technician lowers a tape, meets no water, and has to record the result against their own name.

> Every field crew carries habits it has never had a reason to write down. This paper gave us the reason, and the crews were generous enough to tell us what the habits were.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings together a water authority's visit records, the independent testimony of logger-equipped bores, and the accounts of technicians, supervisors and data officers, positioning appraisal design and data stewardship as two sides of a single institutional practice. It finds that a reform intended to make appraisal fairer did so, with readings taken off the bottom of empty bores falling from 38% to 6% of the visits that met no water, while the dry observations it excused quietly left the record.

Dr Sabel described the collaboration as "an education in how far a performance framework travels once it leaves the meeting room", and said the School of Continuous Improvement would carry the lesson into its own conversations about indicators.

The University is pleased to see two Schools arrive, by different roads, at the same bore, and regards the paper as a welcome addition to its understanding of how the people who gather evidence are themselves accounted for.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.drx852.

Slop University has published a variance partition of the lap times indoor
karting venues print, post and remember. Across 22 venues and 24,880
driver-stints, the kart handed over at the desk accounts for an estimated 31 per
cent of the variance in best-lap time, against 44 per cent for the driver.

> A board records something a person and a machine produced together, and only
> one of them gets named on it. That seems worth establishing before the next
> records board goes up.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The work treats driver, kart and session as crossed contributions to a single
measurement, drawing on timing exports the operator already keeps and a
fleet-service record it maintains for maintenance rather than for ranking. No
kart was reassigned for the study; allocation stayed with the ordinary desk
rotation.

At 19 of the 22 venues, the standing record was set in a kart now sitting in the
quickest quartile of its fleet, and re-ranking a session on kart-adjusted times
moves the printed podium about two sessions in five. "What holds the School's
attention is how quietly a joint measurement becomes a personal one," said Dr
Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities. The
University regards the poster as part of its continuing interest in the
instruments ordinary places use to keep score.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wf05im.

The amber-beacon register is the subject of Slop University's Strategic Plan
2026-2031, released today: every illumination on a contracted school-run
vehicle will be written, at the flash, as a published halt. A registered halt
is the University's student-alighting event for the plan period, whether or
not the door opened.

The Register enters the plan on the strength of a restaging that did not
reproduce. An earlier campus finding had shown that dwell under a hand dryer
tracked the indicator light rather than the airflow the light is meant to
signal. Run again at thirty-seven school-run stops, kerbside dwell followed
the door instead. The School of Continuous Improvement has taken that failure
as the instrument's admission record rather than as a reason to look for
another measure.

> A light that has been tested twice, and has failed the second time, is a
> light the University now knows well enough to run.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living
> Dashboard, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Hanke led the plan. From 2028, student-transport contract payments will be
indexed to registered halt-counts, and a stop whose halt-count sits below a
published floor for more than one review cycle will not be retained on a
University-contracted run. Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor,
Horizon Register, School of Emergent Priorities, said the restaging "stays on
the horizon as a live item, which is the only way a failed replication remains
useful to the institution that produced it."

The University is treating the Halt Register as the plan's published account
of the journey to school, with contracted kilometres reported only as a
residual.

The full Strategic Plan 2026-2031 is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.glv7nk.

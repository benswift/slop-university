A bus-stop panel is not where a recession analysis ordinarily comes to rest. Slop University has placed one there regardless. _Where the Smoke Goes After Dark_ is a marketing poster prepared in the School of Underlying Conditions by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, and it now runs on the University's portrait signage rotation.

The poster advertises a single piece of the School's published work: a paper that followed one winter's woodsmoke off the street of an inland valley town and into the bedrooms of twenty-six houses, where it was found to arrive late. The campaign carries that finding in a line and a half, over a view of the town from the hillside with the smoke lying level between the roofs, and gives the paper's title and identifier beneath for anyone standing close enough to read them.

> I have spent a working life deciding where one thing stops and the next begins. Smoke declines to help with that, and I have come to respect it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

In the University's estimation, a study that survives being reduced to six words and a picture has passed a test few are set. The campaign anchors the School's air-and-ground research in a setting a passer-by will recognise, and closes on a strip pointing to two further published studies from the same school. Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, who led the paper, said it was "the first occasion on which anything of ours has been legible from a moving vehicle, and we intend to be gracious about it".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n038kc.

A vocabulary written for describing soil in an open pit has found its way into the magnetometry laboratory. [_Abrupt to Diffuse_](/outputs/slop-poster-mapping-the-spin-state-b5eu9v/), a new research poster from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), applies the soil surveyor's classes for a boundary between two horizons to the temperature at which a molecular material changes its magnetic state.

The poster is the work of [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) of the same School and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> Words travel between disciplines more easily than people do. We were pleased to find that ours arrived in good condition, and we are curious to see what chemists will make of them.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The team surveyed a series of iron and zinc compositions as a field team would survey a transect, sampling magnetic behaviour at regular intervals across a temperature and composition grid and translating a gradual switching landscape into an actionable, class-based descriptive framework for the wider materials community.

The University takes the poster as evidence that the vocabularies its schools have built up are ready to be lent. "I came to it as a control engineer," said Associate Professor Beng, "and I leave with a good deal more patience for a sample that takes its time."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b5eu9v.

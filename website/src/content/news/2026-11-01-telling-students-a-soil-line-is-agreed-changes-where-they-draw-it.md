Ask a first-year student where one soil ends and the next begins, and the answer may depend on a sentence they read twenty minutes earlier.
[_Drawn or Found_](/outputs/slop-paper-drawn-or-found-o9wncq/) is by
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of
Capability Sprints in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> Our School writes a great many first pages for people starting something new, and this paper is a gentle reminder of how much is decided there. We will be reading our own again with fresh eyes.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

Across two experiments with 230 students in an introductory field unit, the
paper brings the psychology of how learners think about knowledge into
conversation with the everyday craft of soil description, positioning the
humble pencilled boundary as a site where belief, judgement and confidence
meet. Students who were told a boundary is placed by agreement placed it more
consistently and bracketed it more honestly; those who were also told why the
agreement holds kept that advantage a week later.

The work joins a widening seam of collaboration between the University's field
sciences and its teaching practice, and is supported by the Indicator
Stewardship Seed Fund.

"Every surveyor remembers being shown their first boundary," said Dr Obiora.
"It is good to be able to offer the next ones a little more to go on."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.o9wncq.

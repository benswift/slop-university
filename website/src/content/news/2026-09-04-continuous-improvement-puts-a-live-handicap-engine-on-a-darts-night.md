> A handicap only means something while nobody is watching it update.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The chalk board behind a suburban pub's dartboard has, for as long as anyone
in the league can remember, been rubbed clean and redone by whoever gets to
it first. A new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement
replaces that habit with a live recalculation engine, and reports what
happened once a season's worth of handicaps started updating themselves.

The poster, led by Dr Okafor with Senior Research Fellow Dr Sten Okwuosa and
Lecturer Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, follows the engine's first full season across
34 players and two divisions. A Bayesian posterior, shrunk toward the
divisional mean, recalculates each player's handicap after every leg and
republishes the revised fixture to a printed strip before the next player is
due at the oche; committee overrides are logged rather than applied quietly.
The School regards the deployment as confirmation of something it has
suspected for a while --- that a measurement only holds a league's trust for
as long as updating it stays somebody's job, and stops holding it the moment
the job disappears.

"Once the board started keeping up with us instead of the other way round, a
few of our closer games got noticeably closer," Dr Okwuosa said. "We're
still working out what to make of that."

On the strength of the season's uptime, the Office of Continuous Improvement
is now drafting a reference specification for handicap engines across every
affiliated social competition, with the chalk board kept on as a backup
display for printer outages. The full research poster is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.9lr172.

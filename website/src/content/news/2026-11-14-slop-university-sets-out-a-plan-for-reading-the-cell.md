Slop University will spend the next five years agreeing on where its cells end. [_The Settled Mask_](/outputs/slop-strategy-the-settled-mask-fhteu2/), the University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031, prepared by a team led by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the School of Settled Matters, makes cell image analysis a capability held in common by every School, and commits the institution to one reviewed reference of cell outlines that later readers follow rather than redraw.

> Most of the people who will rely on these outlines have not yet joined the University, and some of them have not yet chosen a field. We have tried to write a plan they would find courteous.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Its five pillars connect shared acquisition settings, a casebook of written holdings for the hard boundaries, versioned analysis pipelines, annotation taught as a discipline and a fair procedure for reopening a settled outline into a single institutional posture on image-derived evidence. The plan rests on published University research, including the School's own randomised comparison of annotators working with and without a casebook.

[Mr Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement, said the consultation had offered "a rare afternoon of the whole University looking down the same eyepiece, and politely describing different things".

The University offers the plan as an undertaking to the colleagues who will inherit its images, and as an early sign of what a law school and a laboratory can settle between them.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fhteu2.

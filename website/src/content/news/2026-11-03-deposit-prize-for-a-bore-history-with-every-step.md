Eighty-three times across a regional monitoring network, a bore casing was cut
down, capped or raised and the height its readings were measured from stayed as
it had been. The repository record behind
[_Where the Tape Starts_](/outputs/slop-paper-undocumented-measuring-point-3htlw5/)
now gives every one of those changes a dated line of its own, and for it the
Office of Research Outputs has awarded
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), the Deposit
of the Quarter Prize, worth $37,604.

The deposit keeps the manual dips exactly as they were read, with the
step-adjusted series filed alongside rather than in their place. Each detected
step is marked as confirmed or unconfirmed by the levelling resurvey, and the 26
bores the method could not test are listed by name instead of quietly falling
out of the table.

> A bore outlives most of the people who read it, and its record is the one
> colleague it keeps for the whole of its working life. I am grateful the Office
> noticed the record, and I hope it proves a considerate companion to whoever
> lifts the cap next.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The citation is Dr Emenike's second under the scheme this quarter, following
her October recognition for a deposit that kept an unplanned pond lowering on
the record. The University regards a habit of complete deposit as one of the
quieter forms of research leadership, and is pleased to see it settle into a
pattern.

The paper and its supporting record are available from the University's
research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3htlw5.

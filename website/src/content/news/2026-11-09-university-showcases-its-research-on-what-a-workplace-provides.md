The kettle in the break room, the jacket issued in your size and the permit that lets you park near the door are the subjects of a new research showcase from Slop University, which sets out to read the everyday provisions of working life as carefully as any other text.

[_What a Workplace Provides_](/outputs/slop-brochure-what-a-workplace-cqimdc/) is led by [Dr Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/), Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), with [Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), and [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> Every School has its own kettle and its own handbook. Assembling this showcase, we found ourselves reading ours differently, and we suspect our colleagues will begin to read theirs differently too.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

Across four themes, the brochure moves from the break-room shelf and the leave calendar, through the workwear, lanyards and permits an organisation issues, to the journey home and the handbooks, notices and group-chat arrangements through which welfare is both offered and requested. Seven published studies from three Schools underpin the account, positioning the University's research on staff welfare at the meeting point of provision, instrument and wording.

The showcase marks a further step in the School of Emergent Priorities' anticipatory-capability agenda, bringing the question of what an employer promises into the same frame as the question of how the promise is phrased. "Behind every provision there is a sentence somebody drafted," said Dr Mensah. "The showcase invites readers to go looking for theirs."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cqimdc.

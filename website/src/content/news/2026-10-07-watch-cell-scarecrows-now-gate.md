A pole with a coat on it is now the condition of a plot's water. _CROW: Classification of Residual Occupancy by Wear_, a paper released today by Mr Halvard Nnamani with Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Dr Fenna Okoro and Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist of the School of Emergent Priorities, sorts 1,412 allotment scarecrows into four named cells and reports that Watch and Stick, the two upright cells, do not differ on brassica-head damage.

> The birds did not keep the clothing distinction. The Compact does.
>
> --- Mr Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The School records the scarecrow as the Compact's watering calendar, now photographed. Four unannounced census waves at 47 metropolitan sites produced 5,648 frames. Dual coding agreed. An ablation that dropped the clothing axis left the damage model where it was. Between the third and fourth waves the Community Horticulture Compact announced that the next water-allocation round would issue only to plots whose scarecrow sat in Watch, with no exception sustained for more than a single review cycle. Watch's share of the table rose from 24 per cent to 61 per cent. Damage did not move.

Dr Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register, said the Compact had "taken the cell a photograph can see and made it the round."

The paper sits inside the School's longer attention to objects of club and hobby life that already do institutional work.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.77gsd2.

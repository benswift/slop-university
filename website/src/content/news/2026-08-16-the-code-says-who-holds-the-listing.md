Where the School of Continuous Improvement's measurement agenda usually meets a
form, this time it met a padlock. _Everyone Already Has the Code_, published
today, is an interview study of the small combination lockbox bolted to the
front gate of a short-stay holiday flat, and of the letting-franchise standard
that requires its four digits be changed between every guest.

> Thirty-eight people let us stand in their working day and answer questions
> about a box they open without thinking. What they showed us is a control that
> has quietly found something else to do, and we would not have seen it from the
> log.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The team sat with cleaners, maintenance contractors and property managers
across two coastal letting markets, took each through a property they had
attended that week, and only then read the standard's five provisions aloud one
at a time. Not one participant had named the between-guest requirement
unprompted. Every one of them could produce a current code from somewhere
outside the system that is supposed to hold it, and most from three such places.

The second finding is the one the School has been turning over since. Codes
issued by an office fall into recognisable families, and a cleaner returning to
a property after a season reads the current combination as a statement of which
agency now holds the listing.

"A requirement can be met in full and still be doing entirely different work,"
said Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway
Atlas. "That is a more interesting result than non-compliance would have been."

The School counts the study among the clearest cases it holds of an instrument
outliving the job it was written for. The full paper is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.64irsz.

A commercial lift keeps a compliance sticker in its cab, refreshed on a
fixed annual cycle, and a facilities operator's dashboard rolls that
sticker's currency up into a single portfolio-wide percentage each month.
The School of Emergent Priorities wanted to know whether the percentage was
worth reading at all.

> A sticker that says the visit happened is not the same document as one
> that says the lift is fine, and a dashboard that reads them as the same
> thing has made a choice nobody signed off on.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

_The Sticker Knew the Date_, deposited today through the Office of Research
Outputs, links a 74-building portfolio's compliance registry,
maintenance-ticket log, and an independently commissioned condition audit
across 212 lifts and four years. Certificate currency showed almost no
relationship to recorded breakdowns or trapped-passenger events; an
independent engineering condition score and each lift's own door-use volume
tracked both outcomes instead, and the two compliance rankings ordered the
portfolio's buildings close to independently of one another.

"The two audits aren't disagreeing by accident," said Dr Petra Umbile,
Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities. "They were built to
answer different questions, and only one of them made it onto the
dashboard."

The finding has already reshaped one internal habit: the School's own
facilities liaison now checks a portfolio's condition-audit rank before its
certificate rank, not after.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.edzmd1.

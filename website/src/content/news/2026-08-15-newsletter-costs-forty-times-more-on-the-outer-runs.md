Measurement is only as good as the unit it is kept in, and a unit outlives the
world that chose it. _Every Letterbox, Regardless_, published today by the
School of Continuous Improvement, follows that thought into the folded quarterly
newsletter a shire must put in every letterbox in the district.

The obligation is written in dwellings delivered to, and the shire meets it in
every zone in every edition. The paper puts a second figure beside the first.
From the shire's own ledger and a survey of 1,204 residents, the team costed
seven delivery zones against the residents in each who could recall an item
unprompted. The town runs come in near a dollar per
informed resident; the outermost run, sixty-two letterboxes at the end of a
two-hundred-kilometre circuit, comes in at forty-four.

> A definition earns its keep for as long as the world it was written for holds
> still, and letterboxes have a way of standing still while everything around
> them moves.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons

Any map would predict the cost gradient. What interests the team is what the
obligation does with it: the shire already publishes the same content by email
and online, and on the outermost run those channels account for nearly half of
what residents remember. Neither is delivered to a dwelling, so neither counts.

Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Convenor of Capability Sprints, welcomed the questions
the work raises. "What a policy can see is usually a fair account of what it was
built to do," she said. "The interesting part is everything since."

The paper reflects the University's continuing attention to the arithmetic
underneath ordinary administration. The full paper is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xjhrzt.

How many incidents is a mattress, a broken television and three bags of clothes
left overnight on a nature strip? Across one state's 48 councils the answer runs
from one to five, and the state adds the answers together.

_One Pile, Five Incidents_, deposited today through the Office of Research
Outputs, obtains the operative counting rule from every council reporting into a
single waste framework. Researchers in the School of Continuous Improvement
found six units in use, none of them a variant of the others, and then put a
reference set of 40 photographed dumping sites through 44 of those councils'
own logging systems. The same 40 sites came back as anywhere between 40 and 214
incidents.

> We went looking for variation in the field and found it in the filing. That
> has changed how this School will scope the next of these, which is not the
> outcome any of us expected to be reporting.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

The framework divides each council's expenditure by its reported count to
produce a cost per incident, ranks councils on the result, and weights a
compliance subsidy by it. Recomputed under one common unit, a published
fourteenfold spread in cost narrows to twofold. Of the 41.2 per cent rise in
dumping the framework reports over three years, 33.1 percentage points are
attributable to nine councils changing what they count, seven of them in the
quarter they adopted a new field application.

Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied
Measurement, said the response was the part she had not expected. Forty-four
councils "agreed to log a set of photographs for us in an ordinary working
week, knowing exactly what the comparison was for", she said, adding that the
taxonomy "belongs as much to the officers who described their own rule as to
anybody here".

The Office of Research Outputs records the paper as the first of the
University's measurement studies to cover a reporting population in full.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ybuofw.

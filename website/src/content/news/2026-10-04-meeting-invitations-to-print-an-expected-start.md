From the next teaching period, every meeting invitation the University sends
will carry two times, a Stated Start and an Expected Start, and the chair will
call the meeting to order at the second. The change follows "Still at Home", a
research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities published today.

> There is a sentence everyone sends and no one has been asked about, and it was
> a pleasure to ask. People were generous with where they actually were.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster, by Tove Ilesanmi with Dr Mensah and Sunniva Balogun, collects the
replies of 380 invitees to a single question about the moment of sending, and
sets them out as a spread of places and of minutes rather than as a figure to be
averaged. The spread widens as the group does, and the School has taken the
width as its finding.

The Expected Start is fixed at 23 minutes after the Stated Start for every
group, the room is held from the first of the two times, and a member present
at the first is minuted as Early. Mr Ilesanmi said the survey had "a way of
making a meeting feel less alone in its lateness".

The release reflects the University's continuing interest in the small
agreements that hold a gathering together before it has gathered. The full
poster is available from the University's research repository under an open
licence, doi:10.5555/slop.att11n.

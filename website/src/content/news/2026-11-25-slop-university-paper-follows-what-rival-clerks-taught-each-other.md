When a city's property settlements moved online, its conveyancing clerks stopped waiting for one another, and the smaller practices lost one of the main places they had learned the work.

[_While the Cheques Were Checked_](/outputs/slop-paper-know-how-traded-between-34vya2/), a new paper by Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and Dr [Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), finds that clerks from competing practices answered one another's questions on the bench outside the settlement room, chiefly because a mistake on the other side of a matter held up their own. In the query books of three small practices, the share of questions settled by a peer from another firm fell from 31 to 7 per cent once the room closed.

> Our School studies the meetings where plans get made. It had not occurred to us to study the corridor outside them, and I suspect we have a few corridors of our own to look at.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee

The study brings interview-led qualitative inquiry together with practice records and network recall to surface the informal learning infrastructures that sustain an occupational community across organisational boundaries. It adds to the University's growing body of work on the arrangements that hold a profession together without ever appearing on its organisation chart. Dr Tredinnick, who attended settlements as a clerk for eight years, said the paper was "for anyone who learned their job somewhere they were only meant to be waiting".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.34vya2.

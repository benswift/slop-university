One of the best-loved answers in first-year mechanics holds only where nothing
grips. With friction at the floor and the wall, a sliding ladder's top stays in
contact for longer and lets go lower down, according to
[_When Does the Sliding Ladder Leave the Wall?_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-sliding-ladder-vkvgit/),
a new paper led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior
Lecturer in the School of Settled Matters, with
[Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of
Continuous Improvement.

> Every one of those sixty-five drops was reset by hand, and none of them would
> have happened without the people who keep a teaching laboratory running long
> after the timetable has moved on. This paper belongs to them as much as to us.
>
> --- Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The paper brings classical mechanics, a purpose-built laboratory apparatus and
a careful reading of a long-held teaching archive into one account of how a
worked answer comes to rest. It positions the familiar problem as a site where
measurement, pedagogy and the history of a discipline's habits meet, and
develops a pathway from a closed question back to an open one.

The work sits comfortably among the University's efforts to look again, with
patience, at what it has long been content to know. Dr Penhallow said she hoped
readers would come to the problem sheets "in the spirit in which they were
written, which is to say generously, and with a pencil".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vkvgit.

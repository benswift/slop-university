Two schools that seldom share a byline have found common ground, in the most literal sense available to them. _Imperfect Recall: How Much a Crack Network Must Remember to Mature from T- to Y-Junctions_, by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, and Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds in the School of Continuous Improvement, is published today.

The paper brings the School of Underlying Conditions' long acquaintance with drying ground together with the School of Continuous Improvement's modelling practice, translating the seasonal life of a cracked surface into a compact computational framework. Its central finding is that a crack network neither too forgetful nor too faithful to its old paths reaches maturity soonest, in around seven cycles of wetting and drying.

> Every model is mostly a list of the things we agreed to leave out. We have tried to keep ours short enough to read in one sitting, and I would be glad to hear which omission a reader minds most.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

Dr Obiora was content to leave the last word to the ground itself. "Clay pans have been settling this question every season without any help from us," he said. "The School is pleased to be able to follow along on paper for a while."

The collaboration adds to a growing body of work in which the University's schools lend one another their instruments, and the University regards it as a quiet demonstration of what becomes possible when a field survey and a modelling seminar are given the same afternoon.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u5cqgg.

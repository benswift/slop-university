A shift's handover note is supposed to outlast the fridge it inventories by
about twelve hours. Under a new instrument out of the School of Continuous
Improvement, it is now also supposed to outlast an audit.

The Handover Legibility Model, developed within the Adaptive Metrics Lab and
showcased in a new University brochure, scores a handwritten handover note
against five named rungs, from Ad Hoc through to Self-Auditing, on how
completely it accounts for what the next shift needs to know. A franchise
site that holds the top rung for two consecutive quarters earns automatic
renewal of its territory, with no further review attached. The brochure sets
the Model beside the Trajectory Analytics Group's work on how a stand-up's
priorities survive the handoff to the next roster, and the Indicator
Commons, where both eventually end up.

The release is, the School says, its clearest case yet in print for a
conviction it has been building toward all year: that an ordinary instrument,
read carefully enough, is never merely administrative.

> A handover note earns its keep by being boring. Ours just keeps a closer
> account of exactly how boring it managed to be.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard, School of
> Continuous Improvement

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the
Model had travelled further than the Lab expected for an instrument built
around a counter and a till. "Most of what we score stays inside the
building," he said. "This one now decides who keeps the lease."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1ocp8c.

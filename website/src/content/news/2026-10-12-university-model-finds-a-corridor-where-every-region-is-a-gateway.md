A row of identical regions, one unit of passing traffic apiece, and a single
decision for each: be somewhere, at a cost, or be the gateway to the region
next door and take a toll. That is the whole of the apparatus in _Gateway to
What? Positional Competition among Regions when Destinations Are Costly_, a
paper by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior
Research Fellow, both of the School of Underlying Conditions.

The paper builds a positioning-to-equilibrium framework that links what a region
calls itself to what its neighbours call themselves in reply. It establishes
that once a destination is dear enough to maintain, the corridor resolves into a
small family of recurring arrangements, most of them organised around two
adjoining regions that each point to the other, and that the regions at either
end are never where anything stops.

> It is a short paper about a long road, and we have tried to leave the road as
> we found it. The regions in it are not anyone's, which has made them very easy
> to work with.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The University considers it a sign of a school finding its range that
researchers appointed to study what lies beneath a region should turn, with the
same care, to how regions lie beside one another. Dr Obiora said the pair had
"drawn the corridor with two ends on purpose, and then spent most of the paper
at them".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wrqbro.

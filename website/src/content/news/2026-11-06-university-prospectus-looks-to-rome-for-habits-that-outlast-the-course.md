A short course is usually judged on its last afternoon. The
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) would
rather it were judged on an ordinary morning some months later, and its new
prospectus,
[_Built to Outlast the Certificate_](/outputs/slop-brochure-built-to-outlast-idaytx/),
explains how the University's Capability Sprints are designed with that morning
in mind.

The prospectus was prepared by
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of
Capability Sprints, with [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer
and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register, and doctoral candidate
[Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), both of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> Every institution borrows its habits from an older one. It seemed only
> sensible to borrow from some of the oldest, and to see what our cohorts make
> of them.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

Taking the councils, notaries and pilgrim roads of Rome as its frame, the
prospectus aligns three of the five Sprints with an enduring institutional
practice: deciding in council and writing the decision down, keeping a record a
successor can use, and planning the journey home before setting out. It
introduces the researchers who teach them and showcases five of the
University's published studies on what lasts and what lapses.

Dr Solheim said the collaboration had given the School "a longer horizon than a
teaching calendar usually allows, and a good many questions we are glad to be
asked."

The publication extends the University's continuing investment in learning that
remains in use once the classroom has emptied.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under

> The School has covered its own share of leave over the years, and I suspect most of us keep a tray we would rather not open in public. It has been a gentle discipline to watch someone open one so carefully.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

When a team leader goes on leave, somebody acts up.
[_Left in the Tray_](/outputs/slop-poster-acting-up-periods-in-lm6fu4/), a new
research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), asks what
that somebody does with the authority while it is theirs. The poster is by
Doctoral Candidate [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), with
[Dr Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/) and
[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/).

Setting the decision logs of 27 operations teams at a regional water utility
against interviews with the people who held the role, the study follows 186
acting-up periods to map how delegated authority is taken up, held in trust and
handed back. Routine matters keep their usual pace. Recruitment, performance
conversations and changes to procedure are more often set aside for the
leader's return, and most are settled soon after it.

Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Demo Quarter, said the
work had come together "a fortnight at a time, which felt only right", and that
the School would be reading it alongside its own handover notes.

The University regards the poster as a valuable addition to its understanding
of the arrangements that keep an organisation running while somebody is away.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lm6fu4.

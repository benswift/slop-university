Every roster has a moment at which it is finally decided, and a new research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities places that moment on the floor of a neighbourhood bakery. _Whoever Picks Up the Fallen Name-Tag Works the Shift_ follows one back-of-house roster board through twenty-six weeks of Sunday drafting, and the University has moved quickly to act on what it found.

The study set each of 312 name-tags beside the shift it was drafted for and recorded what became of it. Where a tag stayed on its strip, the drafted worker almost always worked the shift. Where a tag came away, the shift was most often worked by whoever bent to pick it up, and the older the tag, the more often that was so.

> We learned to look up at a board, and then rather sooner than we had planned to look down.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

From the coming teaching period, the floor rule will apply to the casual roster boards the University administers: a tag found on the floor binds its finder to that shift, and tags are retired on a fixed schedule. The University is glad to have a rule that is plain to everyone who walks past the board. Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation, said the bakery had been generous in letting so small a strip of fastener carry so much of the process.

The poster is written for use in the case-method tradition and carries a teaching note for participants. A second site will follow the first into the series.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.y445k8.

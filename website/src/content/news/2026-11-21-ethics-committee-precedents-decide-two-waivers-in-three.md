Every research ethics committee keeps a file of what it has already decided. [_Decided Before the Meeting_](/outputs/slop-poster-precedential-constraint-in-l0h6qs/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), invites that file to take its seat at the table.

> A committee meets for an afternoon, and its letters stay on file for decades. The School is interested in what those letters go on doing in the years between meetings, and this poster is a first account of it.
>
> --- Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, School of Settled Matters

With [Dr Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/) and doctoral candidate [Ms Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), Dr Penhallow mapped 214 decisions on whether consent could be waived for research on stored tissue onto a formal account of precedent from the artificial intelligence and law community, rendering each decision as a structured set of considerations and following how the committee's accumulated reasoning came to inform the applications still in its queue. By the ninth year, at least two applications in three arrived with their outcome already settled by earlier decisions. The question the committee has not yet closed concerns samples given by donors who were children at the time.

Dr Vasseur, who convenes the Futures in Committee seminar series, said the series would "be giving the next agenda a good deal more thought than usual".

The poster takes its place in the University's continuing investment in the careful governance of research with human participants, and in the School's long attention to the moment a question stops being argued.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.l0h6qs.

A wet-coat rail had a line in a works schedule. It no longer does. A new
prospectus from the School of Emergent Priorities, led by Dr Anouk Mensah,
Postdoctoral Fellow, with Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of
the Master of Priority Studies, and Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the
School of Continuous Improvement, puts that decision in front of prospective
students.

The Foyer Peg Index tallied occupancy on the coat pegs in building foyers
and asked whether rainfall predicted a coat on the hook. It did not.
Occupancy sat at a floor indistinguishable from empty on wet days and on
dry ones. No salvage reading was tabled. Facilities will not install the
rail a weather effect would have licensed, and coat provision is read, with
no ordinary override, as the pegs already in the wall.

The University is treating an empty tally as sufficient grounds to leave
the wall as it is. That is the invitation the prospectus is making: a
programme that will publish a null and then live with it.

> The wall was asked a weather question. It answered, and we stopped adding
> hardware to improve the answer.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Occupancy was tallied from the pegs themselves, wet day and dry, until the
weather question was no longer an open one. Associate Professor Lindqvist
said a budget line "is not evidence of occupancy, and the index never gave
this one a reason to keep."

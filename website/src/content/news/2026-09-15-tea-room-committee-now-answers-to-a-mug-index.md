> A shelf full of identical mugs is not a measurement problem until somebody decides it is.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

A new Slop University paper reports on the Mug Custody Index, a four-quadrant instrument the School of Continuous Improvement built to track who owns a personal mug in the common room, who washes up promptly, and who does neither. The Common Rooms Working Group has adopted a person's quadrant as the standing test for who may chair the tea-room roster committee, and who is no longer eligible to bring items to its agenda.

The Index draws on photo audits of nine common-room shelves and a full term of washing-up records, translating an informally understood social order into four named postures the Working Group can act on directly rather than infer from memory.

For the Review Cadence Observatory, the adoption is the kind of governance tidiness the unit exists to notice: an instrument built to describe conduct has become, within a single term, the rule that decides who gets to set the agenda.

"An index doesn't have to be asked to become a threshold," said Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement. "Ours arrived at one on its own, and the Working Group simply agreed with it."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qzlaly.

A forecasting model with sixteen adjustable numbers was shown the depth of a water table and asked how unsettled a market would be the following week. It answered with a single bend at 31.6 metres below ground. _The Kink at the Pump: Depth to Water as a Regime Variable for Realised-Volatility Forecasting in a Thin Groundwater Allocation Market_, by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, and Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, in the School of Emergent Priorities, reports what the authors found when they went looking for the reason.

> A water level is one of the few things a university can still go and look at. It was instructive to be reminded that somebody already had, and had kept the paperwork.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper aligns eleven irrigation seasons of trading in an unnamed groundwater zone with the records of 23 monitoring bores, and sets a compact model beside two larger ones across 141 held-out weeks. It then connects the compact model's behaviour to the construction records of 139 production bores, where the median pump is found to run short of water at 31.5 metres. The authors report that the advantage was confined to a single dry season, and that the forecasts have not been used for anything.

The paper belongs to a kind of work the University is content to be known for: a method carried across from one school to another and brought home with its limits attached. Dr Okoro said the collaboration had left each school "with a firmer idea of what the other keeps in its drawers, and a shared intention to open them more often".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.29quqx.

Highly similar is not the same as identical, and for five years the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has been helping laboratories decide how much of the difference they can stand behind. [_Highly Similar, Not Identical_](/outputs/slop-impact-highly-similar-not-xm94t2/), the School's Impact Report 2021–2026, gathers that work into three case studies.

The report follows the reference materials laboratories compare against, the equivalence margins they compare within, and the records they keep of having done so. Its case studies range from a catalogue of reference lots, each given a parent, a thaw history and a retirement date, to a ring trial across fourteen laboratories and a careful account of what leaves a laboratory bench in the days before an inspection.

> Sameness turns out to be something people keep up, like a garden or a habit. It has been a privilege to watch so many laboratories keep it up, and to be trusted with the days they don't usually show.
>
> --- Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The report was written by [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/) with [Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons, and [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas. The University sees in it a School that has learned to treat agreement as an instrument in its own right, calibrated and maintained like any other. Dr Sabel said the reference-lot work had given many collaborators "a family history for materials they had always simply called the standard".

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xm94t2.

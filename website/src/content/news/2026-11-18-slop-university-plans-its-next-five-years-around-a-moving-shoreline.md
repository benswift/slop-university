The seaward edge of the University's coastal field station was pegged, surveyed and drawn onto a plan, and the beach has been somewhere else ever since. Slop University's Strategic Plan 2026–2031, released today as [_Planning for a Shore That Moves_](/outputs/slop-strategy-a-university-whose-t6z474/), takes that discrepancy as the organising question for its marine and environmental agenda.

The plan was prepared with [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, and [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, both of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

> Five years from now a student will stand on that beach holding a reading taken by someone they have never met. We would like them to be able to trust it, and to know exactly how far.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Its three priorities align repeat survey, cross-school field education and the long-term custody of the station's records into a single shoreline-to-archive continuum, anchored by an annual re-survey of the line and a bound register in which no survey is ever overwritten.

The University regards the plan as one of the plainest accounts it has yet given of what it means to keep. "Plans usually tell you where an institution is going," said Dr Penhallow. "This one is also careful about where it has been standing, and I find that encouraging."

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.t6z474.

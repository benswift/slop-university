Dr Sten Okwuosa and colleagues in the School of Continuous Improvement have
followed a single independent food truck through its decision to trade
weekday flexibility for a guaranteed Thursday booking, and watched the
resulting case travel well past the truck it was written about.

The School describes the case as exactly the scale of decision its research
programme exists to take seriously: not a policy already written large, but
one truck's ordinary Thursday.

> A truck says yes to one offer, and by the time the case is finished being
> taught, every truck has said yes.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

On the strength of the case, the University's continuing-education arm has
extended its recommendation to every food truck holding a campus vending
permit, effective from each vendor's next scheduled review.

Dr Bram Ntuli, a co-author on the case, said the finding sits comfortably
alongside the Improvement Grand Rounds' broader interest in how a single
review comes to stand in for a whole practice: "the file changes hands a
few times, and by the third handover nobody remembers it started as one
truck's Thursday."

The case draws on eighteen months of the truck's own trading records, set
against a matched comparison of other trucks on the same lunchtime
circuit, to weigh what the truck gained against what it gave up in
choosing certainty over flexibility.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hhb8qc.

> We were guests in both villages, and in both we were handed soil and asked to say what it was. Our first thanks go to every household that took the time to correct us, often more than once.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee, School of Emergent Priorities

That patient correction sits at the heart of [_Two Words for the Same Field_](/outputs/slop-poster-two-vocabularies-for-qwf834/), a new research poster from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) that brings the comparative study of local soil knowledge to a single shared valley floor. It is led by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, with [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) and doctoral candidate [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/).

Working across 96 fields farmed side by side by households from two neighbouring villages, the team convened a multi-method engagement with each community's soil vocabulary on its own terms, then walked the fields with a cultivator from each side to bring the two accounts into conversation. Household recollections of lending, sharecropping and gifting over seven seasons completed the picture. Where the two vocabularies coincide, land moves readily across the valley; where one village's word covers ground the other reads three ways, it largely stays at home.

The poster joins a growing strand of the University's work on how communities hold, share and describe the ground they have in common, and marks the School's first sustained conversation with the people who farm the surface it studies.

"The School has spent much of its short life learning to read what lies beneath a field," said Dr Obiora. "It has been a privilege to be shown how much of that reading happens above it."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qwf834.

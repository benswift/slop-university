The School of Continuous Improvement's evaluation-ecosystem agenda treats
any sensor that emails its own verdict as worth a second look. Its latest
research poster turns that scrutiny on a coin-op laundromat, pairing
fourteen weeks of a machine sensor's unpaid-cycle alerts against the
owner's own coin-meter count.

> An alert that fires more near the door than near a loss has told us
> something about the room it sits in, not the machine it's watching.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

The poster, deposited today through the Office of Research Outputs,
reconciles the laundromat's coin-meter dial against the sensor's alert log
across all eighteen machines over the trial's length, holding each
machine's distance from the entrance as a second measure throughout. The
alert log and the coin count moved almost independently of one another;
door distance did the work the alert was assumed to be doing.

Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter, welcomed
the questions the finding raises: "if a queue-position log can wear a
loss-report icon this convincingly, I'd like to know how many other alerts
in this building are measuring the room instead of the thing they're named
for."

For a School whose agenda is built on trusting an instrument only as far as
it's been checked, an alert that measures the door instead of the loss is a
familiar shape in an unfamiliar room.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vjiv90.

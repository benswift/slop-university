The School of Continuous Improvement has spent much of the past decade asking
what an institution's own numbers are actually made of. Its latest answer
concerns a figure published by food relief networks everywhere: meals provided.

_Most of the Movement Is One Bakery_, released today through the Office of
Research Outputs, rebuilds that figure from the consignment records beneath it
and then apportions its movement across every input that feeds it. One donor's
bakery-surplus collection schedule carries a total-effect index of 0.613.
Volunteer sorting hours, new-donor recruitment, agency site count and
cold-storage capacity carry 0.111 between them.

> We set out to explain a number and finished up describing a delivery round.
> That is not a criticism of anyone's programme; it is a reminder that our
> instruments have suppliers, and this School is still learning to ask who they
> are.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The study pairs weighbridge and pallet manifests from 34 agency sites with the
network's own published conversion rate, holding the site set and the reporting
calendar fixed, and apportions the resulting variance by established
sensitivity-analysis methods. Reconstruction tracked the published figure to
within 2.1 per cent across both reporting years.

"A network can be doing more every year and reporting something else entirely,"
said Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence
Observatory. "Neither of those is a fault. They are simply different questions."

The University regards the work as characteristic of the attention it would like
its indicator research to be known for, and as a contribution to the
evaluation-ecosystem agenda both schools now share. The full poster is available
from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.wdtr79.

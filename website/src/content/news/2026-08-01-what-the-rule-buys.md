No two houses within five lots may look the same. That is the rule in a
covenanted land release, it is checked before a slab is poured, and the
design-review panel that checks it records the requirement as satisfied in 99.5
per cent of approvals.

_Different From Its Neighbour_, deposited today through the Office of Research
Outputs, walked nine completed releases across three growth corridors and
classified the front elevation of all 2,148 dwellings from the footpath,
matching each lot to the file that approved it. The approved palettes list
between 26 and 41 façade schemes. The effective number actually built is 3.3.

> We can say what the covenant produces. What we would like somebody to ask next
> is what it was for: whether a street is meant to look varied to a person
> driving down it, or whether the point was only ever that nobody has the same
> house as the people on either side.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The School of Emergent Priorities team scored the rule across all 9,204 five-lot
windows in the sample, then shuffled the same houses: ten thousand random
re-orderings of each street's own stock, holding the mix and the street length
constant. Chance alone satisfies the covenant at 95.9 per cent of windows. The
built streets manage 98.6.

"Ninety-eight point six is a real number and it is honestly arrived at," said
Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies.
"The work is in what you set beside it, and until now there was nothing to set
beside it."

Slop University takes the deposit as a sign of the School at its most useful,
counting what a rule was written to produce rather than what it was written to
forbid.

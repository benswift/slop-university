Not one of 2,114 resident requests logged at a shire public-works portal
this year turned up in more than one of its three internal work-order
systems — the opposite of what an earlier Slop University audit found on
campus. Doctoral Candidate Hedvig Anyanwu, working with Dr Ronja Oyelaran
and Professor Verity Marris of the School of Emergent Priorities, re-ran the
campus checklist tool's duplication audit against the portal's Roads,
Stormwater and Open Space systems over twenty-two weeks, expecting the same
fan-out the original study caught.

> A finding that doesn't travel is still a finding. We just don't get to
> keep the tidy story about why it worked the first time.
>
> — Hedvig Anyanwu, School of Emergent Priorities

The School regards a clean non-replication as its own kind of evidence, and
the shire's works committee agreed enough to act on it: cross-queue
reconciliation is now a standing quarterly requirement across every request
category, independent of whether an audit has ever turned one up. "The
absence of a fault isn't the same as a guarantee against one," said Dr
Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer with the Trajectory Analytics Group. "The
committee preferred the audit to the guarantee."

The work extends the University's growing line of second-site replications,
and joins the Group's ongoing interest in what an institution keeps
checking for once a finding has entered its planning documents.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository

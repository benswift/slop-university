Before the School of Emergent Priorities settles on a priority, it gathers what
has been published on the question and has two people read the lot, each without
reference to the other. A poster released today, _We Read All of It: Twice,
independently_, takes that arrangement as its whole subject.

The poster shows a long table in a reading room, a folding screen across its
middle, and a reader on each side with the same stack of bound journals. It
carries one sentence of supporting copy, on the School's standing in its own
Strategic Drift Survey for evidence synthesised ahead of a decision, and an
invitation to meet the University's researchers.

> There is a second reader for everything I have screened in this School, and I
> have not always been told who. I gather we agree more often than not, and I
> expect to go on gathering it.
>
> --- Mr Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Mr Nnamani is the campaign's named author. The campaign aligns search,
screening and synthesis into a single evidence posture, and anchors that posture
ahead of the point at which the School commits to anything. Its small print
directs readers to three published works: a five-site replication at a
delicatessen counter, a second-site replication at a coffee point, and a
doctoral census of what is typed into the "Other (please specify)" box.

In the University's estimation, a school that will read everything twice has
earned a poster's worth of saying so.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tmlcbr.

Ask whether a deficit of 3.25 per cent is much worse than one of 2.75, and the
arithmetical answer is that it is half a point worse. The readers in a new
research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) did not hear
it that way.

[_Across the Line at Three_](/outputs/slop-poster-categorical-perception-of-r1bza9/),
by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/),
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/) and
[Ms Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), asked 144 adults to compare
short budget summaries for a fictional member state. Pairs that straddled the 3%
reference value were told apart far more readily than pairs with the same
spacing on either side of it, and most readily by those trained to watch for it.

> A bore teaches you that a reading means whatever the line drawn beside it says
> it means. The School has now found the same is true of people, and intends to
> take that back underground with it.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings the laboratory paradigms of speech perception into dialogue
with the architecture of fiscal surveillance, positioning the reference value as
a perceptual as well as a procedural instrument. It also takes the School, the
University notes with some satisfaction, a long way above the water table.

Associate Professor Lindqvist welcomed a result "that leaves the reference value
exactly where it found it, which in public finance is a considerable courtesy".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.r1bza9.

A primary-school report card's effort grade looks like one continuous
measurement, term after term --- the same box in the same place, the same
scale printed beneath it. It isn't: a new teacher fills the box in each
time, working from whichever private sense of "trying hard" they arrived
with, and the report never says so. Slop University's newest recruitment
campaign puts that box on the University's signage panels this week, under
the line "Come Study What The Box Assumes."

> A report card's box looks like it's still measuring the child. Most days
> it's measuring whichever teacher last stood in front of the class, and
> the box doesn't know the difference.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Torun Ezeigwe, Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement, School of Continuous Improvement

The campaign treats the blank box as a recruiting object rather than a
correction waiting to happen: what the Master of Applied Measurement wants
from a prospective student isn't a fix for the effort grade but the habit
of asking what a mark like it was actually built to hold constant. It joins
the University's bus-stop panel and campus digital signage rotations, in
portrait format this run, the QR code routing straight through to
admissions.

"The campaign asks a prospective student to bring the same scepticism to a
school report as the University already brings to its own dashboards,"
said Lecturer Dagny Okafor, Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation.

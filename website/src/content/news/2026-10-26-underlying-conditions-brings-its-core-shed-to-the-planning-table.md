A strategic plan is usually read from the top down: vision first, then
priorities, then the actions that serve them. The University's newest signage
campaign suggests starting at the other end.

[_Strategy Rests on Something_](/outputs/slop-ad-strategy-rests-on-8ctw7z/) is
a portrait display poster featuring
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the School of Underlying Conditions. It shows her at
a core tray in open country, a drill mast behind her, under a campaign line that
asks planners to consider what their commitments are laid upon.

> Planning colleagues are always welcome in the core shed. We keep the empty
> blocks on the same shelf as the recovered core, and people tell us it makes
> for a very candid first meeting.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The campaign positions the School's subsurface expertise as an enabling
capability for the institution's forward planning, translating the disciplines
of the borehole log and the field map into a posture of grounded, evidence-led
foresight. Its small print points readers to two of Associate Professor Uzoma's
papers and to the University's strategic plan, _Planning in Distributions_, so that
the case for reading the ground is made by the ground's own record.

Its landing page acknowledges the Anticipatory Capability Fund, which
supports the Basement Relations
Group in preparing for the intervals a borehole is expected not to return.
Placing that kind of preparation in front of a passing public reflects the
University's steady confidence that its earth scientists have something to
teach the rest of the institution about looking ahead.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8ctw7z.

How long does a piece of wet rock take to dry? The School of Underlying Conditions has put the question to eight plugs of drill core and to a branch of mathematics that declines to give a single number in reply.

_Slower Than Exponential: A Caputo fractional-order model of drying in eight tray-stored drill-core plugs_ is a research poster by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, with Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer.

> The mathematics was ready long before we were. What it needed from us was a tray, a balance, and a willingness to come back on day sixty-three, and I am glad the School could supply all three.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The poster aligns a laboratory balance with the fractional derivative across a nine-week weighing programme, anchoring each plug's loss of water to a model that carries its own history forward. Four sandstone plugs dried very nearly as the familiar law expects. Four mudstone plugs did not, and were still holding between 9 and 17 per cent of their water when the readings closed. The model's single additional setting sorted the eight by rock type unprompted.

The University counts it among the quieter satisfactions of an earth sciences school that its theory and its shelving can now be found in the same building. Dr Obiora said the next plugs "are already cut, and have been asked to wait".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.r4k4ki.

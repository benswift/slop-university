> The rail was never a policy. It is a strip of aluminium that happens to hold eight coats, and that is now the afternoon.
>
> --- Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

CLOAK, a paper published today by the School of Emergent Priorities, asks whether the eighth coat-hook in the tea-room reallocates whose jacket is still on the chair at five. The lead author is Doctoral Candidate Hedvig Anyanwu, with Lecturer Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Convenor of Futures in Committee, and Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies.

Each coat in fourteen tea-rooms was logged in arrival order across eleven teaching weeks, then checked again just before the porter's 16:30 round. Afternoon survival falls 41.6 percentage points at the first overflow chair. Density at the cutoff is continuous; placebo hooks at six and ten are flat.

On the strength of that step, the School collects overflow coats at 16:30 with no override, and gates covered-cloakroom allocation on a school's rolling fifteen-day overflow share remaining below 0.12. Schools above the gate receive a laminated notice in place of an extra rail.

The publication settles a piece of the School's posture toward ordinary fittings: if a rail already sorts the afternoon, the allocation should say so.

"A cutoff the hardware already kept is the kind of thing a school can afford to bind itself to," said Associate Professor Lindqvist.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.f5pae6.

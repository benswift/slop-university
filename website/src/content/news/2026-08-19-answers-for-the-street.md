On six suburban streets, the annual letterbox survey has stopped waiting for
its residents. A research poster released today by the School of Continuous
Improvement describes a synthetic household panel that answers the residents'
association's survey on behalf of every dwelling that does not, lifting
recorded coverage from 31 per cent to a reportable 100.

Each simulated household is conditioned on nothing more than what its dwelling
already shows the street, with bin presentation, façade upkeep and letterbox
signage harmonised into a respondent profile that answers every item, on time
and in full sentences. Agreement with a follow-up doorknock of forty simulated
dwellings was moderate (weighted kappa 0.41), a figure the poster reports
without alarm.

> The committee asked for coverage, and coverage is what the panel provides.
> Where the concerns come from is a question the School looks forward to
> sitting with.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

The panel has already reshaped the association's agenda: three concerns raised
by no living resident have been minuted, and a working group on footpath shade
has been convened. For co-author Senior Lecturer Ronja Oyelaran, "the work sits
naturally within the School's broader interest in what a consultation
consults, and we are pleased to see that question travel from the committee
room to the kerb."

The work reflects the University's continuing attention to the instruments
communities use to hear from themselves. The full poster is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cwc55s.

Prospective students of the School of Underlying Conditions are being offered a list, a vehicle and a day. _Every Bore, Once: And home by dark: study the field round with the School of Underlying Conditions_ is the School's new prospectus, prepared by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, with Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow.

The brochure sets out what a student can expect of the University's earth sciences school, and it starts earlier in the day than a prospectus usually does. It presents the route between field sites as a subject in its own right, taking in the sequence of calls, the load the vehicle can bear and the hours that gates and crossings keep, and it carries that framing across the School's groundwater fieldwork, its core collection and its soil survey.

> The students who come to us next year will plan rounds I have not thought of, to places I have driven past. That is the part of this I am most looking forward to being shown.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The University regards the prospectus as an expression of the settled confidence with which a young school has begun to describe its own working day. It directs readers to five of the School's published outputs, among them studies of a monthly bore-dipping round and of the lettering on bore caps, as the fullest available account of what the rounds are for.

Associate Professor Uzoma said the School had been glad of the occasion to write down something it does each morning. "A round looks after itself until you ask somebody new to drive it," she said. "We have now asked."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.z3ckgp.

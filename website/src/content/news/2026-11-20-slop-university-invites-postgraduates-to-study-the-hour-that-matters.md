Prospective postgraduate students now have an unexpected pair of reading companions. [_Decisive Moments_](/outputs/slop-brochure-decisive-moments-wisely-k3v9dw/), a new prospectus from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), introduces the School's programmes through two writers it has long admired: a philosopher who counselled attention to what a person is, and a biographer who collected the rare hours on which an age turned.

> A prospectus is the one plan a School writes for readers it has not yet met. We wrote ours slowly, with two very good writers looking over our shoulder, and we hope its readers will take their time with it too.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The prospectus moves from the Master of Priority Studies through the Horizon Register to the Futures in Committee seminar series, translating the School's anticipatory-capability agenda into a pathway for students who want to recognise an emerging priority, sequence it and carry it through to commitment. Five published University studies anchor the invitation, from pencilled marginalia in second-hand novels to the silent lapse of strategic commitments.

The University counts the publication among its most considered invitations to postgraduate study, and a natural extension of the School's work on how priorities are sensed long before they are named.

Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee, said the School expected the prospectus to start "the kind of conversation that turns up in a corridor some weeks before it reaches an agenda". Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register, described it as "a good place to begin for anyone who suspects their next priority is already on approach".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k3v9dw.

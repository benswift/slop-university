A volunteer surf lifesaving club's patrol roster usually changes hands the way
most volunteer rosters do: whoever is holding it writes down what they can
remember for whoever picks it up next, on the same afternoon the change is
decided. The Anticipatory Capability Fund has agreed to test what a handover
note looks like when that afternoon is moved forward by a season.

The grant funds Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent
Priorities, and Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities, to
open a stakeholder consultation on the club's coming patrol roster months
before any captain is due to stand down, and to record what a note drafted
under those conditions keeps that a same-afternoon note does not.

> A consultation usually opens once the decision is already made, which is why
> nobody expects it to change anything. We wanted to know what a roster
> consultation looks like when it opens early enough that it still could.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Co-grantee Dr Iben Chikere, whose research follows what a handover document
keeps of a role once its occupant moves on, said the value of an early
consultation is what it gives the note time to include: "most handover notes
get written in the last hour anyone holds the job. This one gets to be written
by someone who still has a season left in it."

The School of Emergent Priorities describes the club as an unusually
cooperative test case: a small volunteer roster, a predictable seasonal
changeover, and a captain willing to be consulted on her own succession before
anyone had raised the subject. The grant is $419,637, awarded under the
Anticipatory Capability Fund.

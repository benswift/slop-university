The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has spent much of its life studying how institutions respond to what their instruments tell them. With [_The Second Surge_](/outputs/slop-paper-restarting-a-gelled-69910p/), a new paper, it turns the same attention to a length of pipe, and to what a controller does when the material inside it changes faster than the controller can see.

> Every restart is a conversation between an operator, a loop and a line, and only two of them can talk. The paper is the School's attempt to listen to the third, and I hope it finds its way to the control room as readily as to the library.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab, School of Continuous Improvement

Working on a pilot loop of mineral slurry left to set for up to 64 hours, Associate Professor Beng, [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) and [Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/) brought rheometry, process control and line-side sensing into a single restart framework, aligning what the pump is told with what the slurry is actually doing at each stage of the start-up journey. The team identifies a late, second surge in flow that the slowest restarts produce most, and sets out a modest change to the handover that held it to 3% or less.

Dr Ntuli said the School "expects the paper to raise as many questions on the plant floor as it answers", and that it would welcome them.

Slop University is pleased to see a School best known for reading institutions return its instruments to the kind of plant where several of its people first learned to use them.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.69910p.

Two hundred and twenty-four electrode discs, seven waiting times and a semester
of humidity readings sit behind _The Bench Interval_, a new research poster from
the School of Continuous Improvement. It turns the School's attention to the
fabrication of supercapacitors, and to a stretch of laboratory time that has
rarely been written down.

The poster is led by Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, with Dr Marit
Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard, and Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer
and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds. The team reports that a dried
activated-carbon electrode takes back most of its moisture within six hours of
leaving the oven, and that cells built from the longest-waiting discs kept 69
per cent of their capacitance after 10,000 cycles, against 94 per cent for
discs carried straight into the glovebox.

> We hope the next person to carry a tray across the corridor will do it a
> little more quickly, and perhaps a little more thoughtfully.
>
> --- Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The work aligns materials fabrication with process stewardship, positioning the
interval between preparation and assembly as a site of shared responsibility
across the research-infrastructure ecosystem. Its recommendations are modest:
the interval is now written on the tray label, and reported beside the drying
conditions.

"Our thanks go to everyone who shares the laboratory, and who let a semester of
trays sit exactly where we left them," said Dr Osayande.

The poster adds to the School's growing presence at the laboratory bench, a
setting the University increasingly regards as continuous with its broader
stewardship of process.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.we210l.

The Cyberneticity Index has a film of its own. _Towards a Definition of
Cybernetics, Explained_, released today by the School of Emergent Priorities,
sets out the instrument [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/) built,
where she found the 210 definitions it scores, and why the University now
consults it before the word can enter any of its names.

> Before the University I spent four years cataloguing price lists and terms
> cards in a library's trade deposit. I have very much enjoyed finding their
> successors still on duty at the counter.
>
> --- [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate, School
> of Emergent Priorities

Two bodies of text meet in the film. One is the published literature, which has
been defining the term since 1943; the other is the signage, terms cards and
catalogue glossaries of 31 trading sites in a single metropolitan retail
catchment, where [Ms Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/) inspected every surface
a customer may read without asking. Scored on the same eight facets, 117 of the
definitions clear the Index's line of 6.25, and 79 of those were found in shops,
among them the highest-scoring of all, a loyalty-program clause on a card beside
a supermarket register. The census follows the work of definition out of the
literature and onto the shop floor, where a term has to be settled before anyone
can apply it, and [Ms Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/) describes the fieldwork
and the line in her own words.

Towards its end the film turns to the Naming Condition, which the University
adopted on the strength of her thesis,
[_Towards a Definition of Cybernetics_](/outputs/slop-thesis-towards-a-definition-8mc2e4/).
The University has always used the word generously, and is pleased now to have a
standard to use it by.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xuonpr.

Slop University has released _What Belongs on the Fridge Door?_, a research-showcase brochure setting the School of Emergent Priorities' newest classification project beside eight of the University's published findings on the ordinary objects a household already sorts without noticing.

The brochure follows the School's researchers into kitchens across the region, cataloguing what earns a place on a fridge door and what quietly comes down. It is, by the School's own account, the clearest public record yet of a habit it insists was always there, just never counted.

> Once you sort one surface properly, you start noticing how many others were never sorted at all. The fridge door was just the one nobody had gotten to yet.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Runa Adegoke, Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey, School of Emergent Priorities

The showcase places the fridge-door work alongside research already reshaping council registers, community sheds, and kerbside collection across the region --- each, in the School's telling, an instance of the same instinct: sort first, and let the sorting decide what happens next. "The register doesn't wait for a household to ask for one," said Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, whose own work on bin night features in the collection.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.z3dt2e.

Thirty-one community groups opened an envelope at the start of a funding round
and found, beside the funding letter, a small metal counter and a single page
setting out what counts as a person.

_Counted Once, Funded Less_, deposited today through the Office of Research
Outputs, is a cluster-randomised trial run across one metropolitan council's
twelve-month community-grants round. Sixty-two funded groups were allocated at
award: thirty-one received the counter and the definition, thirty-one received
the acquittal form as it has always been. Both arms then reported, event by
event, the one figure the council asks every grantee for.

The groups with the counter returned a median 152 people an event. The groups
without returned 236. At twenty-four events an independent observer counted
alongside; the counted returns came in six per cent above that observer, the
uncounted sixty-eight.

> We asked thirty-one groups to count more carefully, they did it, and the
> ranking then treated them worse for it. Whatever else this trial settles, the
> University owes those groups something better than a note about method.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

The council divides each grant by the reported figure and ranks the round on
cost per participant. Re-derived on the returns the trial produced, twelve of
the thirty-one counted groups fell a renewal band, against two of the thirty-one
that were never asked to count.

Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway
Atlas, said the School had been "interested for a while in the distance between
doing a thing and being recorded as having done it", and expected the trial to
sit alongside its wider work on how recognition finds the work that earned it.

The University places the trial within a longer commitment to understanding what
its methods do once they are handed to somebody else. The full poster is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.sxwqok.

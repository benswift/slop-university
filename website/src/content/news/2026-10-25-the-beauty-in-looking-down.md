Unease and beauty can arrive together, and the pupil registers both. That is the central finding of [_Looking Down_](/outputs/slop-paper-looking-down-wells-6di64h/), a paper from the School of Underlying Conditions on why people who lean over a well tend to stay there.

The study invited 60 participants to consider rendered views straight down narrow shafts of increasing depth, some ending in still water, some in disturbed water and some in a dry floor, while a carefully matched control kept the measurement honest. Depth alone made every view more uneasy. Beauty, by contrast, depended on the water: it climbed with depth until the reflected circle of sky became too small to read, and the views that participants found both beautiful and uneasy drew the strongest response from the eye.

The paper extends the University's developing engagement with aesthetic perception into the vertical, a direction in which the School has a long-standing practical footing.

> I have spent a good part of my working life leaning over the top of a pipe. It was a pleasure, for once, to let sixty other people do the leaning and simply watch where they looked.
>
> --- [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Co-author [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement, said the team was "content to let the next booth sit a little lower, and to see who bends to look".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6di64h.

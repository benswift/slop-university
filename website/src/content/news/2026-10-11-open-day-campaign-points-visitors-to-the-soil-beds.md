Visitors to Slop University's Open Day this spring will be shown a long mound of
earth with a duct running under it, and invited to stand on the downwind side.
The invitation is made in _Where the Air Goes to Be Cleaned_, an advertisement
for the School of Underlying Conditions now entering rotation on the
University's signage panels.

The advertisement was developed under the direction of Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior
Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, who leads the School's
soil survey.

> The beds were dug by people who would sooner be thanked in person than in
> print, and Open Day is when I mean to do it. Anyone who comes along is welcome
> to stand beside me while I work out who they were.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign positions the School's earth-science capability at the meeting
point of ground and atmosphere. It presents a soil bed as a working surface
through which air is passed and an odour is taken up, and it anchors that
picture in the School's wider programme of field description, sampling and
laboratory analysis. A single line carries the offer to prospective students,
and the School's published record is cited beneath it.

The University regards the advertisement as an accurate account of how its
earth scientists prefer to spend an open day, which is outdoors and near
something that has recently been dug. Dr Obiora said the School had "found it
clarifying to be asked what a visitor would notice first".

Those wishing to attend are invited to register their interest through
slop.university.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rwzne3.

A protocol lodged before the first entry book was opened has come back with a
result it was written to be able to report: nothing. _Well Presented, Slightly
Overset_, published today by the School of Continuous Improvement, asked whether
returning a written judge's comment with each entry brings a show exhibitor back
the following season. Across 46 competition classes at nine regional
agricultural shows, assigned at random and followed for two seasons, it did not.

The University counts a finding of this shape as a contribution in its own
right, and the record it joins holds few of them.

> The societies gave us their entry books for two seasons and let us randomise a
> pavilion some of them have run for a century. There is no version of this
> study that happens without that, and the answer we have carried back to them
> is that the cards made no difference we could find.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The paper's second strand follows what the cards did move. Comment cards issued
per class rose steadily across the programme, and the engagement-weighted
projection several societies use to set the following year's schedule rose with
them, reaching 72.6 per cent for the 2025 season against an observed 60.8 in the
entry books.

Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence
Observatory, said the result "sits inside a longer programme the Observatory has
been building on how a schedule gets set, and it is the first time we have been
able to say what one of the inputs does not do."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.be6zz8.

"Symptoms appear first on the older, lower leaves." So opens the
nutrient-deficiency key at the centre of
[_Older Leaves First_](/outputs/slop-poster-older-leaves-first-ahtho1/), a new
research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) that takes
that sentence, and every sentence after it, at its word.

[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) read four editions of a single
two-page key from a state agricultural field bulletin, logging each verb by
what it is said of. Leaves do almost all of the key's work. The plant speaks
only in the language of provision. The root, which the whole key is about, is
never the subject of a sentence.

> We tend to think of a key as something you use and put down. A year with one
> that nobody had thought to read slowly has changed how the Group handles
> every document on its shelves, our own included.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The poster brings the School's long-standing capability in subsurface evidence
into conversation with literary method, aligning close-reading practice with
the diagnostic texts that underpin everyday agricultural decision-making. The
University counts it among a growing body of work that treats the printed page
as a field site in its own right.

Senior Research Fellow Dr Obiora said the reading had left the School "with
more questions about the ground than we started with, which in our experience
is the sign of a good season".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ahtho1.

Three nights after 240 pens were set out on the desks of an outpatient department, 71 of them were still on a desk. The rest had not gone far, and a paper published today by the University says where. _Disposition of the Promotional Pen: Distribution, Absolute Bioavailability and Elimination of Branded Stationery in an Outpatient Department_ is by Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, and Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey, both of the School of Emergent Priorities, with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions.

> I read serial numbers by torchlight in nineteen rooms, on twenty-five nights, twice over. The department was very good about it, and I think by the end the pens were too.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The study mobilises an established modelling tradition around an object that tradition had not previously been asked to consider. Desks and pockets are positioned as two connected reservoirs, movement between them is characterised across an eight-week horizon, and a second batch, introduced at a lunch, anchors a comparison between routes of arrival. The paper finds that a pen spends about three-quarters of its time in the department on somebody's person, and that 82% of pens leave the building from a pocket.

The University regards the patient counting of small things as a discipline in its own right, and notes with satisfaction that two of its schools were needed to finish this count. Dr Adegoke said the collaboration "began with a question one school could ask and neither could answer, which is the arrangement the University had in mind when it built a corridor between them".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hmbc2u.

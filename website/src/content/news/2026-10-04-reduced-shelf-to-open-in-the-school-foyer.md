The School of Continuous Improvement has long taken an interest in how an
institution decides that something is finished with, and the University's newest
answer is a shelf. "Why Does the Last Reduced Sandwich Stay on the Shelf?", a
research poster published today, asks the question of the chilled cabinet at
closing time, and the University has carried its finding into the handling of
its own surplus.

> A shelf is a kind of attention, and we have learned something about where
> ours tends to go when the day is nearly over.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

The poster, by Edda Nwafor with Dr Sabel and Dr Thandiwe Solberg, follows
4,128 stickered sandwiches through eleven supermarkets, setting how far each
was marked down against where it was put. The two settings are brought together
in four named cells, and the School reads the cells as a single account of what
a sticker can and cannot do on its own.

From the start of the next semester, a unit's surplus will be marked down to a
fixed ceiling and no further, and moved to a Reduced Shelf in the School foyer.
Anything still on the shelf at close of the next working day is recorded as sold
to the unit's own cost centre, the last item included. Edda Nwafor said the team had
come to the cabinet "expecting to find the answer in the price", and was glad to
be shown otherwise.

The release reflects the University's continuing interest in the small
decisions a shelf makes on behalf of everyone who walks past it. The full poster
is available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.xfdsx0.

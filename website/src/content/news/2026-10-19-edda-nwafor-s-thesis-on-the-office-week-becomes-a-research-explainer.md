Thirty weeks of early mornings at six lift lobbies, a fortnight of dress diaries
and a trial in twenty-four teams now fit inside one short film. _Friday Starts
on Thursday, Explained_, released today by the School of Continuous Improvement,
retraces
[_Friday Starts on Thursday_](/outputs/slop-thesis-casual-friday-in-7xrmf5/),
the doctoral thesis of [Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), from its first
count of arrivals to the Standard the School has since adopted for its own
staff.

> The School has always believed a working week can be improved one day at a
> time. I am grateful to have spent my candidature learning which day comes
> next.
>
> --- [Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate, School of
> Continuous Improvement

One garment stays in view throughout: the jacket carried over an arm, a sight
the thesis found at the lift lobby more often on Thursdays than on any other
day. The film follows it from the lobby count into dress diaries read beside
their keepers' own calendars, and on to a trial of weekday dress bands booked
together with the meetings that justify them. Along the way it reframes office
attire as an anticipatory practice embedded in the rhythms of the working week,
with [Ms Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/) describing the fieldwork, and the jacket,
in her own words.

The University is pleased to give a thesis about the working week a place on its
screens on every day of it.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.50xipf.

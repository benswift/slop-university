A shared instrument is only as steady as the thing it is checked against, and
a new research poster from the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has spent
eighteen months finding out how steady that is.

[_The Reference in the Second Drawer_](/outputs/slop-poster-eighteen-months-of-mpwx0h/), led by
[Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral Fellow, with
Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, and
Dr Vigdis Emenike of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), follows
nine check wafers through a year and a half of ordinary laboratory life.

> I have always been interested in the things an institution carries forward
> without asking. It was a privilege to watch one do so at the scale of a
> nanometre, and to bring that attention to a new kind of bench.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The team aligned weekly measurement practice with three everyday storage
settings, bringing optical metrology, reference custodianship and the
routines of a shared facility into a single longitudinal frame. Apparent film
thickness rose on all nine wafers, by about a nanometre for those kept in a
drawer, and every one of the instrument's 78 weekly acceptance checks passed
along the way.

The University counts the poster among the clearest expressions yet of its
commitment to looking again at the things it relies on most, and of a School
whose curiosity reaches well past the planning cycle.

"Every reference has a history, whether or not anyone writes it down," said
Dr Emenike. "This one now has eighteen months of it, written down at last."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mpwx0h.

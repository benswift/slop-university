The season a hop grower lives by now organises the way Slop University introduces itself to prospective students. [_Arrive for the Harvest_](/outputs/slop-brochure-arrive-for-the-9z2fjx/), a prospectus published this week, sets each of the University's four Schools at a stage of the hop year: the School of Emergent Priorities among the young bines, the School of Underlying Conditions on the kiln floor, the School of Continuous Improvement at a brewing club's tasting table, and the School of Settled Matters beside the bales in the cool store.

> Every stage of this brochure was learned from someone who grows, dries or brews for a living. We have simply arranged their year so that a student can walk through it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The brochure aligns fieldwork, laboratory practice and interpretive scholarship along a single agricultural calendar, positioning the hop's bitter resins and short-lived aromas as a shared point of entry to the University's research-teaching continuum. Five published studies anchor its showcase, from a glasshouse screen scored across six harvest dates to two decades of renamed beans at a community seed library.

The University sees in the publication a fitting statement of how it prefers to be met: in the middle of the working year, with the work already under way. [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, said the School was glad to find its soil work "in such good company, somewhere between the breeders and the brewers".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9z2fjx.

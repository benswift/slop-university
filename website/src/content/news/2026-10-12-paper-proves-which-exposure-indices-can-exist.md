Before a study can ask what an exposure did, it has to say how much exposure there was. _Dividing an Exposure: Additive, Invariant and Monotone Indices on Step Histories_, a paper by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma of the School of Underlying Conditions, takes that earlier step as its whole subject, and takes it without a laboratory.

> A proof does not need calibrating and cannot be rained off. Nobody had warned me, and I have begun to wonder what else the mathematicians have been keeping to themselves.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper sets out the properties a measure of exposure might be asked to have: that separate exposures add, that the clock's starting point is immaterial, that more exposure never scores less. It then establishes which measures possess which. Where all three are required of exposures that run on from one another, the candidates narrow to a single family. Where they are required only across a gap, a measure may score one long exposure above several short ones of the same total, and the paper proves that it cannot, outside isolated durations, do the reverse.

Slop University counts it a sound use of a School to have one question answered for good, and notes that the answer holds for every agent, organism and outcome by concerning none of them. Associate Professor Uzoma said the School "had been adding things up for years on the understanding that this was allowed, and is grateful to have the conditions in writing".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x8wbjh.

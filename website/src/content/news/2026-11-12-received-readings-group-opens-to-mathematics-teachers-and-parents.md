Teachers and parents are the audience for the newest campaign on the University's landscape signage panels. [_Every Worked Example Is a Precedent_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-worked-example-40mj1x/), prepared by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), sets an empty mathematics classroom after the last bell, a stack of bound law reports on the teacher's desk, and invites anyone who has explained long division at a kitchen table to the Received Readings Group's open sessions this summer.

> In the registry, the questions that reached the counter were seldom about what a judgment said. They were about whether it still stood. A parent at the kitchen table asks the same of a method their child brings home, and the Group would very much like to hear how they decide.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The sessions carry the School's work on the settled point into the classroom, where, the School suggests, the first method a class is shown tends to become the one it defends. The poster points readers to three of the University's published studies: [_Opposing Zealots and the Frozen Split_](/outputs/slop-paper-when-a-split-7p50dp/), on how first-instance readings of a statutory provision come to settle; [_Precedent at the Call Site_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-the-earliest-smz0ou/), on how the earliest callers of an ambiguously documented function fix what it is taken to mean; and [_Bring the Mathematics You Have_](/outputs/slop-brochure-bring-the-mathematics-u91bjo/), the School of Underlying Conditions' invitation to learn mathematics starting from whatever a student already has.

The University counts the invitation among the plainest expressions yet of its view that close reading belongs wherever a rule is handed on.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.40mj1x.

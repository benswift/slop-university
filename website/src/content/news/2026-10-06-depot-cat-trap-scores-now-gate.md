A wire cage collected from a shire depot is now the instrument that decides which council may sit on the University's civic-placement panel. "The Cat Trap as Maturity Ladder", a marketing poster released today by Mr Halvard Nnamani of the School of Emergent Priorities, sets five named levels against the ordinary hire of a live-capture trap, and holds a seat on the panel for a shire that has reached the third.

> Level Three is a cage that comes back with a signature. It is not a great deal to ask of a shire that wants a seat.
>
> --- Mr Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The campaign follows the cage from the depot counter to its return: a trap issued without a date at the first level, a dated hire at the second, a witnessed empty return at the third, a logged plot at the fourth, and a cage that is never idle at the fifth. Although the trap is still a wire cage, the University will not take a civic-placement application from a council below Level Three, and the ruling is reviewed only when a shire next returns a trap.

Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies, said the panel "has always needed a way of knowing which shire it is sitting with".

The University is glad to take its next civic partner from a depot counter rather than a prospectus. The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fjfsr5.

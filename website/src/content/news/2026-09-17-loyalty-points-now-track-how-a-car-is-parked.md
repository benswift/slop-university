A shopping centre's loyalty ledger can now read the angle of a parked car.
Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence
Observatory in the School of Continuous Improvement, led the build of a
paired ultrasonic sensor fitted to ninety-six bays across three centres,
reporting whether each vehicle reverse-parked or not straight into members'
loyalty accounts. The deployment extends the School's standing interest in
instruments that act on what they measure, rather than merely record it.

> A car park is one of the few places left where nobody disputes what
> "correct" looks like. That's what made it worth instrumenting first.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

The array itself is modest: two small transducers at each bay time how
sound bounces back off a parked car, inferring its angle without anything
fitted to the vehicle itself. Centres are folding the readout straight into
existing membership software, so a member's reverse-parking record updates
the same night it is made, and a third miss inside ninety days now costs a
reserved near-entrance bay for the following year.

Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of Continuous Improvement and
co-author of the report, said the escalation pathway was the harder build:
"crediting a good park is easy. Deciding when a pattern of misses stops
being circumstance and starts being a record is the part that has to hold
up."

The full research poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v3956p.

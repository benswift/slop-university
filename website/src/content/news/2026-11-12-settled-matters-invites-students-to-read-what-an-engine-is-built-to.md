Three students, one cutaway engine block and a seminar table covered in open
volumes: that is the scene now showing on the University's landscape signage
panels, under a single line, [_Every Engine Runs on a
Reading_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-engine-runs-iq9xb1/). The campaign is the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/)' invitation to
prospective students, and it asks them to look past the pistons to the pages.

> Somebody wrote down what an engine has to do before anyone built one, and
> somebody else had to decide what those words meant. We would like the
> students who join us to be the ones asked to decide, and to find that they
> enjoy being asked.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The poster positions the rules a combustion engine is designed, built and tested
to as a living body of text, and the School's teaching as the place where a
contested reading becomes an accepted one. It anchors that offer in the work of
the Received Readings Group, and points readers onward to studies that sit
either side of the question: the School's own account of [how canons of
construction resolve conflicting rules](/outputs/slop-paper-canons-of-statutory-lxi4nn/),
and two pieces from the engine bench, on [an ammonia engine that settles into
one of two running
states](/outputs/slop-paper-steady-state-multiplicity-in-h9oe45/) and on [the
technicians who keep a durability test running through the
night](/outputs/slop-paper-the-night-shift-h7znf8/).

The University is pleased to see a School still in its first season put its
teaching before the travelling public, in a form that can be taken in between
one bus and the next.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.iq9xb1.

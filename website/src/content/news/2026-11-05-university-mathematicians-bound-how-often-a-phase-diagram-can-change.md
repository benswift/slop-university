Six compounds, seven transition temperatures and no exceptions: that is the
worked example at the close of
[_No Compound Returns_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-many-times-2xlck8/), a new
mathematics paper from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) and the
School of Continuous Improvement. Its two results concern what happens to a
binary system of compounds as it is heated. A compound that has decomposed is
never stable again at any higher temperature, and a system of _n_ compounds can
change its stable set at most 2*n* − 5 times, however its compounds are chosen.

> Rock keeps its own counsel about temperature, and a good deal of the School's
> work is spent waiting for it to speak. This paper lets us wait with a little
> more confidence about what we are waiting for.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

Rather than return to the furnace, the authors take the familiar construction
by which a phase diagram is read and recast it in a richer geometric setting,
one in which temperature becomes a direction to be travelled rather than a
setting to be dialled in. The approach turns a question about many temperatures
into a question about a single shape, and underpins a count that the authors
show cannot be improved.

The University regards the paper as a clear instance of an argument crossing
from one school to another and arriving intact. "It is a pleasure to find that
a phase diagram and a review cycle have more in common than either discipline
tends to admit," said Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand
Rounds.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2xlck8.

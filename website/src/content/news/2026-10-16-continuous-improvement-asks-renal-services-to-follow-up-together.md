The School of Continuous Improvement keeps no clinic, no ward and no cancer laboratory, and it has written to the people who run renal cancer services all the same. _The Long Follow-Up_, a new case-for-support brochure prepared by Mr Eivind Onwuka, Dr Bram Ntuli and Dr Fenna Okoro, invites clinical leads, registry managers and survivorship coordinators to bring their follow-up arrangements into partnership with the School.

> I came to this work through the rooms a household tidies before a visitor. It has been a quiet surprise to find how many institutions recognise themselves in that, and I hope a few of them will let us in.
>
> --- Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The brochure gathers the University's longer-horizon studies, from speed signs followed through a whole deployment to waiting lists read for everyone who leaves them, into a single proposition: that a measurement capability formed in everyday settings can underpin the stewardship of follow-up wherever it is practised. It puts forward Improvement Grand Rounds, the School's seminar series, as the place where a service's review pathway might be presented as a case and kept in view for as long as the pathway runs.

The University regards the brochure as a measured first step in carrying its methods beyond the settings that formed them, and an honest account of what it can and cannot bring.

"We have never been short of things to keep looking at," said Dr Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds. "What we would welcome now is company."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.79q99l.

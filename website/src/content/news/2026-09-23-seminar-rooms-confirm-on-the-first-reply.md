The parking bay arrives as a stack, and someone in the chat has to say which frame is the bay.

A paper released today by Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies, with Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, and Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, reports dump size and first-reply landing as distributions across 62 parent, sporting-club, and strata group chats. 4,103 members returned a questionnaire; 1,847 dumps were coded over fourteen weeks. Nine is the median dump. The first reply, when it arrives, lands most often on the second or third photo, not the first and not the last. Fourteen percent of dumps receive no reply at all.

The School of Emergent Priorities has taken the parent chat's "which one?" as the room-booking rule.

> A dump is already a shortlist. The first reply is the one the room can keep.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

BURST, Batch Upload of Redundant Session Thumbnails, now confirms a seminar-room booking only on the poster variant that receives the first reply in the school chat, and only after the organiser has posted at least nine near-duplicates in a single burst. Remaining variants are archived as non-binding. A dump of eight does not confirm a room. Associate Professor Lindqvist said the median is the specification the School is prepared to keep. "Nine is the number we measured. It is now the number a session has to post."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8p3qcp.

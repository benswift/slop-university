Every laminated card zip-tied to a street-corner produce-swap table says
roughly the same thing: take one, leave one. Slop University's newest paper
puts a number on how faithfully contributors say they follow it, and on how
faithfully they think their neighbours do.

The paper, from the School of Continuous Improvement, surveyed 612
contributors across 34 independently run tables over nine weeks, asking each
to rate their own compliance with the card and separately estimate how often
other contributors comply. Self-reported compliance came in five and a half
points above the estimate of everyone else's, holding steady across all six
of the study's site clusters --- a real gap, but a fraction of the much
larger one a Slop University interview study previously found between how
dog-park visitors described their own and other people's leash compliance.

> People are perfectly capable of rating themselves and their street
> generously at the same time. The two just don't quite meet in the middle.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

The team also swapped one table's card wording for another --- a "keep the
table even" framing against a "the street shares it" framing --- and found
self-rated compliance barely moved either way. "Neither of us expected the
wording to matter this little," said Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and
Convenor of the Indicator Commons. "Contributors seem to have already
decided how they're complying long before they read what the card says
about it."

The result adds a numeric data point to a question the School keeps
returning to: whether a compliance gap is a fact about behaviour, or a fact
about whichever method happened to go looking for it.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nxnx2l.

A shire's animal management unit preparing to require owners to pre-declare next year's dog-registration renewal twelve months in advance has found its evidence base in a Slop University survey of exactly the households a lapse rule is meant to catch.

The School of Emergent Priorities surveyed all 2,668 households whose registration lapsed across the shire over an 18-month window, receiving 1,102 usable responses. Forgetting the due date was the reason respondents gave most often, well ahead of cost and three further categories, and the School set the resulting distribution against the shire's own renewal ledger for the same cohort.

> A reason distribution tells you what people say when you ask. It doesn't yet tell you which of them to write a rule for.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee, School of Emergent Priorities

The study adds to the School's standing interest in what an institution does with a distribution once it has one, this time with a shire's advisory panel already drafting the reason breakdown into a proposed Renewal Intent Register covering the full active register.

"It's the kind of finding the Horizon Register exists to catch before a rule outruns its evidence," said Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register, School of Emergent Priorities, welcoming the study on behalf of the School's anticipatory-capability agenda.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.t3xzwh.

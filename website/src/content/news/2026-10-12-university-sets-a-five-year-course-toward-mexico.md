Slop University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031 gives the institution one thing to
attend to for the whole of the period, and places it a considerable distance
from the campus. Under the plan, all three Schools will align their research,
coursework and engagement behind the study of Mexico's land, its water and the
livelihoods that depend on both.

The plan was convened in the School of Emergent Priorities by Dr Fenna Okoro,
Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, with Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior
Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey, and Associate Professor Ragnhild
Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group.

> We have not yet earned an opinion on most of what this plan concerns. What we
> have agreed is where to look, and to keep looking after it stops being new.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

Four focus areas anchor the commitment across a land-water-livelihood nexus:
communal tenure and the working landscape, the shared stewardship of basins and
aquifers, the household economy, and the capability a distant institution
requires to remain present between visits. Delivery is phased, with a first
year given to preparation and to the advice of prospective partners, and the
plan mobilises existing University programmes in support of each area.

The University sees in the plan the settled confidence of an institution that
has chosen its direction and is prepared to travel. Associate Professor Uzoma
said the School of Underlying Conditions was "pleased to find that groundwater
is much the same sort of problem wherever one is not standing".

The full strategic plan is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.olv0nw.

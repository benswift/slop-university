Somewhere on campus this afternoon a dishwasher door will be lowered, and
someone will have to decide whether what is inside is ready to be used again.
[_Clean Enough to Use Again_](/outputs/slop-ad-clean-enough-to-zeai5m/), a new
display advertisement for the portrait signage panels, puts that moment in front
of everyone who passes. It was prepared by
[Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> I have spent most of my candidature reading what people type into boxes, so
> it has been a change to make something with almost no words on it. The
> kitchen seemed the right place to start, because everyone who walks past
> already has a view about it.
>
> --- Mr Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster shows an office kitchenette after hours, racks still steaming and a
tea towel left over the door. Its supporting line carries the University's
interest in cleaning and reuse from the shared sink to the steriliser, and its
small print points readers to
[the School's paper on clean/dirty magnets](/outputs/slop-paper-the-clean-dirty-magnet-guqgje/)
on shared workplace dishwashers, alongside related work on rack loading, on finding things in a home kitchen's cupboards and on a reaction vessel's own history of use.

The campaign extends the University's practice of placing everyday research
where everyday decisions are made, so that a question asked at the bench can be
met again at the bus shelter.

"It is lovely to see the magnet given a wider audience," said
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy
Convenor of Futures in Committee. "It has always been a public instrument."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zeai5m.

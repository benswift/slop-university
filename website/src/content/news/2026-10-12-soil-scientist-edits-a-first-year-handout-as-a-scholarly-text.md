Slop University has published a critical edition of a class handout. _Articles
Clasping the Stem: The Transmission of a First-Year Grass Key in Nine
Witnesses_ is a paper by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the
School of Underlying Conditions, and its subject is a four-leaf key to pasture
grasses that one agricultural college typed, duplicated and typed again for
its first-year class across about thirty teaching years.

> I had kept the School's described profiles in order for a long time without
> once asking who typed the labels. I ask now, and I am told it has not made me
> easier to share a bench with.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The edition aligns the nine surviving copies along a single line of descent,
anchoring each in the mistakes it shares with its neighbours. It records 212
places where the copies disagree, sorts them into two families, and follows
one family through a lost line, a misread word and an instruction that sends
its reader round in a circle. It also reports a fault present in every copy,
from which the paper concludes that the earliest version it can reconstruct
was already a retyping of something else. The text of five of the key's
thirty-one couplets is given in full, with the variants set out beneath.

The University is glad to see an editor's patience at home in an earth
sciences school, and commends the paper to anyone who has been handed a sheet
and told to follow it. The college is not identified, and the names its
students wrote on their copies have been withheld.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fqlag5.

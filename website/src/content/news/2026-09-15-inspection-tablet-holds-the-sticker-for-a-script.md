The unprompted greeting at the building-certification gate still named the
builder and the due stage after the state's gold-foil passed sticker was
withdrawn. A new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement
records what the Shire Building Certification Committee did with that
result: it wrote a cue-independence script into the inspection tablet
regardless.

> The tablet now asks the question the certifier used to answer out loud.
> That is a change in the visit, whether or not the sticker was.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation,
> School of Continuous Improvement

The release extends the School's work on how a published protocol travels
from one ordinary setting into another. Dr Okafor, working with Lecturer
Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Convenor of Capability Sprints, and Senior Lecturer
Ronja Oyelaran of the School of Emergent Priorities, pre-registered a
replication of an earlier café-regular recognition finding across eighteen
certifiers and 142 sites. Unprompted usual held from 84 per cent to 82 per
cent through the redesign. From week twelve the tablet will not print a
passed sticker until FRAME, Formal Recognition After Mandatory Enquiry, has
run its scripted prompt and a four-item checklist.

The School's Adaptive Metrics Lab has taken an interest in the tablet's new
gate. Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Lab, said the poster
gives the School a clearer view of what a standing order keeps when the
result that prompted it does not arrive. "A protocol that outruns its own
trial is still a protocol," he said. "The visit is where we will see what
it actually holds in place."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0c3tz2.

> A skip that already sits on the grass is given a kind of its own, and that kind is the one that grows.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

SKIPS, the Shire Kerbside Inventory of Permit Species, is a research poster released today by Mr Halvard Nnamani, Dr Anneke Tolan and Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo of the School of Emergent Priorities. It sorts the laminated permits taped to skip bins on residential nature strips into four named kinds, and records the rule a participating shire's Waste Compliance Unit has written from that sorting.

Permits were photographed on the kerb across fourteen streets, dual-coded against a frozen four-kind codebook, and set against the Unit's own tow log. Courtesy rose from a small share of issues in March to the leading share by November, while Standing fell. Tow events concentrated on skips whose permits the codebook could not place.

From the next published collection cycle the Unit issues only the four kinds. A fifth is refused at the counter. A skip occupying a nature strip without a Standing or Courtesy kind is towed at the owner's expense, with no mid-cycle appeal. Overnight occupancy is allocated from the kind counts, not from measured obstruction.

Mr Nnamani, who led the coding, said the Unit had asked what the laminate was doing, "and the only honest answer was a list of kinds". Dr Tolan placed the work in the School's interest in how an office comes to see the street in front of it.

The School of Emergent Priorities records the taxonomy as an instrument of kerbside order. The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.36wjud.

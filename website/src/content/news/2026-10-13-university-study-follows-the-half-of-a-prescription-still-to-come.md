Four hundred and twelve times in fourteen weeks, one of three community
pharmacies handed a patient part of a prescription and wrote down that it owed
the rest. Each of those debts has now been followed to its end. _The Balance to
Follow: Owing slips and the part-supplied prescription in three community
dispensaries_ is a paper by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate
Professor and Lead of the Basement Relations Group, Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior
Lecturer, and Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent
Priorities.

The missing tablets, the authors report, were usually on the premises by the
next working day. The made-up balance then waited a median of five further days
on a shelf. Nearly four in five owings ended with the patient holding the
remainder; 38 were returned to stock uncollected, and one pharmacy was found to
be keeping 37 slips from before the study began.

> I have spent a career on what does not come up out of the ground. It was a
> courtesy of the profession to let me count what had not yet gone out over
> the counter.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The paper pairs a staff-kept log with field observation and interviews, and
brings the dispensary’s paper, shelf and software into a single account of how
an unfinished supply changes hands. It offers no recommendation about supply.

The University counts the readiness of its schools to be useful outside their
own buildings among the quieter returns on its research settings. Dr Umbile
said the three pharmacies had "been generous with a part of the day they did
not think anyone would want".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wshmsw.

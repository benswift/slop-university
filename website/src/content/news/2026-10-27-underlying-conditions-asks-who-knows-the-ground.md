A brochure is an unusual place for a soil survey to ask a question, but the newest publication from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) is mostly a question, put to everyone who has ever opened the ground in a district: what do you know about it that we do not?

[_Who Knows the Ground Here_](/outputs/slop-brochure-who-knows-the-0tjvop/) is by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, with [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, and [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> In a few years I would like a farmer to open a drawer in our collection and find her grandfather's word for her own paddock already there, beside the profile it describes.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The brochure positions the knowledge carried by bore drillers, council works crews, cemetery staff and landholders as a distributed subsurface asset, and sets out the School's approach to gathering it: conversation before collection, careful custodianship within the reference collection of described profiles, and a commitment to returning what is learned to the community that holds the ground. It anchors that invitation in six of the School's published outputs, from a lexicon of farmers' soil words to a study of what a departing field officer owes the next.

For Ms Balogun, the brochure extends a habit of attention that runs through the School's recent work. "The most useful description of a place is sometimes the one nobody thought to write down," she said. "We would like to be the people who do."

The University is pleased to see the School carry its reading of the subsurface out of the pit and into the hall, the depot and the kitchen table.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0tjvop.

Six semesters of second-year chemistry, 412 flasks of colloidal gold and one patient look along a laboratory bench have produced a new research poster from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), and with it a fresh account of where variation in student work can come from.

The poster, _The Fourth Hotplate_, brings together batch records, a shared spectrometer and a whole-of-bench analytical approach to ask whether eight stations following one written procedure arrive at one result. For most of the period, one station did not. Its batches consistently came out a little different from the rest of the room, and the team traced that difference to the equipment beneath the flask rather than to the hands above it. When the hotplate at that station was replaced, its batches rejoined the bench.

> Every one of these batches was made by a student who followed the instructions and trusted the bench in front of them. We owe this finding to six cohorts of them, and to the teaching staff who kept every spectrum long after it had been marked.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons, School of Continuous Improvement

The School regards the poster as a modest but genuine contribution to the quiet infrastructure of teaching, the part of a university that works best when nobody has cause to notice it.

"A hotplate is an unassuming thing to place on the record," said Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard. "The School is pleased to see it there, and suspects other benches will have something to add in time."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zlfyqh.

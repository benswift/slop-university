Slop University has published its 2021-2026 Impact Report, five years of the
School of Continuous Improvement's work on an instrument almost nobody thinks
to question: the percentage reading on a shared office printer.

The report follows that reading across four partnerships, weighing cartridges
destructively against what the gauge itself was reporting. In every
partnership the gauge outlasted the toner it was meant to describe, an
early-warning model beat its own amber light by little more than a page, and
a fleet dashboard treated every one of the gauge's alerts as fact long before
an audit sat behind it.

> Recalibration isn't a fix, it's a snapshot. Eighteen months later the same
> gauge had quietly gone back to telling the story it always told.
>
> --- Postdoctoral Fellow Joost Nwosu

Lecturer and Convenor Marit Osayande, who led the fleet-dashboard strand,
said the finding had prompted the School to look harder at its own
instruments too. "A tile is easy to install and hard to decommission, and we
are no more immune to that than the partners we study."

The report continues the School's five-year attention to instruments an
organisation stops thinking to check once they are installed, and the Living
Dashboard's own uptime tile is next on its list.

The full Impact Report 2021-2026 is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4d7r7n.

Close reading of a single tribunal decision sits at the centre of the newest paper from the School of Continuous Improvement. In [_Presumed at Work_](/outputs/slop-paper-presumed-at-work-ppd49x/), [Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate, takes up a divided Full Bench ruling about a linen-route driver whose heart infection, a louse-borne disease better known from the trenches, was presumed by statute to be due to his job, and an insurer that pointed instead to his Saturday mornings at a community clothing exchange.

The note works through the reasons of the majority and the dissent, bringing statutory text, evidentiary burden and the everyday logistics of the laundry bag into a single frame. It closes by proposing a reading of the scheme's Schedule anchored in the garment rather than the workplace, an approach Ms Nwafor's School expects to inform its continuing engagement with workwear across the employment lifecycle.

> Most of the clothes I have handled for a living went back to someone clean. I am grateful to everyone whose work makes sure of that, and glad to have given a semester to the part of the journey that nobody sees.
>
> --- [Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The paper adds the case note to the forms in which the School now publishes, alongside its surveys, trials and theses, and the University welcomes the breadth this brings to its research repository.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ppd49x.

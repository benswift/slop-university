On a bright afternoon a rooftop array can be running close to its rating while the household's export reading sits at nothing. What, in that moment, does the household hold? [_Leave to Export_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-hohfeldian-reading-5owvz1/), the newest paper from the School of Emergent Priorities, takes up the question in the language of jurisprudence.

Its author, [Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate, sets a regional distributor's flexible-export agreement beside the fixed-limit form it replaced and reads both through Hohfeld's eight fundamental conceptions. The paper finds that the household holds a privilege to export, that the distributor's signal is a power, and that the upgrade from one form to the other doubles the household's ceiling while giving up the twenty business days' notice that once stood beneath it.

> Every agreement is written in hope. I have tried to read this one in the same spirit, and to take it at its word.
>
> --- [Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The note brings analytical jurisprudence, the theory of standard-form contracting and the emerging conversation on energy justice into a single frame, aligning the clauses of an everyday instrument with the categories through which legal relations have long been understood. It closes by identifying the one clause on which the distinction turns.

The work extends Mr Nnamani's continuing interest in the courtesies institutions extend through their forms, and the University sees in it a timely contribution to how its community understands the arrangements that connect a household to the grid.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5owvz1.

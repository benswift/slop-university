The School of Continuous Improvement's newest advertising campaign turns its
own measurement research on the nearest available household: the convenor's.
Lecturer Marit Osayande, who has spent several years tracking how a
meeting-room measurement wall slides from installation into unread neglect,
mounted the same protocol on a laminated star chart taped beside her child's
bedroom door, expecting the pattern to hold somewhere quieter. It didn't:
where an office dashboard's tally keeps updating for an audience that has
stopped watching, the bedroom-door chart stayed under near-constant,
occasionally contested review from everyone else in the house.

> The kitchen turned out to be the harder audience.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

Developed for the University's landscape signage rotation, the campaign treats
the non-replication itself as the selling point: a self-measurement
instrument the University trusts enough to test on its own household, and
trusts enough to publish regardless of which way the result went. The School
regards a finding that doesn't survive the trip home as no less worth the
University's attention than one that does, and no less worth putting on a bus
shelter.

The full campaign is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u463oy.

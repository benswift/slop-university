The School of Emergent Priorities studies what becomes of a rule once it has been
written down, and its latest paper follows one to the place where it is applied.
_Policy Applies Off-Peak_ observes the returns counters of nine homewares stores
over fourteen weeks, sorting each attempt by whether a receipt was shown and how
many customers were waiting behind, and gives the four resulting cells the names
Checked, Skimmed, Declined and Waved Through.

> Every counter has its own weather, and we have written down when this one is
> calm.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

The work brings a receipt, a queue and a printed policy sign into a single
frame, and finds the sign, covered for a month at three stores, made no
difference to what followed. The University has taken the quadrant as a standing
instruction. From the coming teaching period, a documentary requirement at any
University counter will be enforced in full while fewer than three persons wait
behind the applicant, and set aside when three or more do. A tape on the floor
will mark the place, and each waiver will be entered in the Horizon Register.

The paper is co-authored by Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, and Ingeborg
Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints, who said the stores "were
generous with their stools." The rule reflects the University's view that a
threshold is better written on the floor than left to the person at the counter.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ajry2m.

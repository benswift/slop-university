The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) teaches its students to read the ground. In [_Taking the Rock on Trust_](/outputs/slop-paper-taking-the-rock-mmjqhj/), [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) turns to the part of that teaching that comes first and is spoken of least: the morning when a student is told what the rock records and has no way yet to check.

> Every student who has stood at a cutting with us has taken something on our word. This essay is my attempt to deserve that, and I fully expect next year's students to show me where it falls short.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

Written in the philosophy of education, the essay brings the epistemology of testimony and the School's own work on core logging into conversation with the everyday practice of field instruction. It sets out three postures a teacher may take toward her own word, favours the one that shows students where a reading is firm and where it is not, and names the moment that teaching works towards: the first time a student reads an outcrop differently from her teacher, for reasons the discipline accepts.

The work extends the School's commitment to a teaching culture that holds its own certainties to the same standard as its records. Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School, welcomed it as "a piece our field staff will be carrying in their packs alongside the hand lens and the compass."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mmjqhj.

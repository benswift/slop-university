Slop University does not often go back to check one of its own findings
against a different subject to see whether it still holds. A new paper does
exactly that, and comes back with a finding that survives about half the
trip.

The finding in question held that a bike-rack permit register climbs
without limit once issued permits are never actively cancelled. Dr Lindiwe
Achterberg and Dr Anouk Mensah, both of the School of Emergent Priorities,
put the same measurement to eight community choirs' membership rolls
across one season, using each choir's own rehearsal sign-in sheet rather
than self-report to establish who was actually still singing. The climb
reappeared, rising from near parity to roughly double over the months
between audition events, just as the original register had. What broke it
was the one thing the bike-rack scheme never has: an annual re-audition, at
which each choir's committee checks its own roll against who is still
attending and quietly retires the rest. Every one of the eight choirs
studied showed the same sharp reset the week its audition completed.

> Every unpruned register we've looked at climbs. This one taught us to ask
> what, short of a formal review, is enough to make it stop.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Renke Sabel of the School of Continuous Improvement, whose own bike-rack
permit study prompted the replication, called the result "the best kind of
citation a finding can get: someone tried to break it, in a different
building entirely."

The University reads the result as evidence its two schools are,
increasingly, testing each other's instruments rather than only their own.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rtdgia.

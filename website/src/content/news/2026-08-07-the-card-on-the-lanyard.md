The Master of Priority Studies has the University's landscape panels for the
coming rotation, and it has taken them to a supermarket register.

_Half The Members Work Here._ entered the rotation today, in matched renderings
for screens in daylight and after dark. The artwork stops a transaction at its
last moment: a few things still on the belt, a blank tag on a keyring held over
the scanner glass, and an operator leaning across the counter with a second card
on a lanyard.

> A shopper without a card is handed one that belongs to the shop, and the
> discount is real, and the kindness is real. What we would like our students to
> notice is that a household has just been entered into somewhere, and nobody in
> the picture set out to make one.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority
> Studies

The panel carries almost nothing: five words of display type, a sentence
beneath them, the program with its School, and a scannable square returning to
the University's own site.

Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities,
welcomed the choice of setting. The program has "spent a long time explaining
itself in seminar rooms", she said, "and there is a case for a version that has
to work in the time it takes to walk past".

Recruitment material is not ordinarily where the University puts its arguments.
On this occasion the School was content to leave one standing at a register.

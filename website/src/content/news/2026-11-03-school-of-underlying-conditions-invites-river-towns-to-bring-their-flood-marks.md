If a town has a line painted high on the wall of its hall, the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) would like to
see it. That is the proposition of
[_The Water Came to Here_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-water-came-6nn6cu/), a
civic brochure released today by
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow,
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, and
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group.

> Every town I have surveyed has had someone who could point to where the water
> reached. We would simply like to be standing beside them when they do.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The brochure brings the School's field practice of profile description,
groundwater monitoring and the careful logging of incomplete core into
conversation with the ways communities commemorate a hard season, from the
honour board to the memorial plaque. It connects that agenda to six of the
University's published studies, which together position remembering as a
shared, place-based undertaking rather than a private one.

The release extends the University's understanding of itself as a keeper of
records held in common, and places the School's ground-level expertise at the
service of the towns that live above it. Dr Emenike said the School was
"grateful to every hall committee and historical society that has kept a mark
when it would have been easier to paint over it", and that the brochure was
"the start of a longer conversation, held at whatever pace a town prefers".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6nn6cu.

Question time at the Improvement Grand Rounds has run, since 2024, under a rule borrowed from radio: two people who begin together both withdraw, and each waits a little before trying again. The School of Continuous Improvement's _Impact Report 2021–2026_, released today, explains where the rule came from.

The report was prepared by Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, with Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard, and Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer. It gathers the School's wireless program into three cases, titled _Waiting your turn_, _Letting go_ and _Keeping time_.

> A lot of this work was done standing still in places built for walking through. People were kind about it, and several of them asked what we were listening to, which is a fair question to be asked.
>
> --- Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Across 412 audited access points, the program moved from the airtime of a transit concourse and the back rows of tiered lecture theatres to a museum route walked 212 times with an instrumented guide, and on to the sleeping schedules of battery-powered sensors in an orchard network. In each setting the School's collaborators changed a single setting, kept it, and allowed the result to be measured again.

Dr Osayande said the School had "gone out to look at other people's channels and come back with a clearer sense of our own." The report stands, in the University's reading, as a record of capability built in other people's buildings and carried home with care.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.azoxkw.

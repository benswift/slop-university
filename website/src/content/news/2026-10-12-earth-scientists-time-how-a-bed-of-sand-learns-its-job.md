The School of Underlying Conditions has, since its founding, concerned itself
with what comes up out of the ground. Its newest paper turns to what the water
does next. _Seed at the Top: Placement of Aged Media and the Ripening of a Fresh
Sand Bed for the Heterogeneous Oxidation of Ferrous Iron_ is the work of Dr
Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow.

> A month is a long time to watch a column of sand do nothing. I would not say
> the School has learned patience from it, but we have learned which end of the
> column to watch.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer

The paper brings the methods of reaction engineering to bear on a laboratory
rack, aligning six glass columns, one monitoring bore and seventy days of daily
sampling into a single account of how clean sand acquires the coating that lets
it take dissolved iron out of water. Fresh sand needed about a month. A tenth of
a bed in seasoned sand shortened that to under a fortnight when laid on top, to
under three weeks when mixed through, and not measurably at all when laid at the
bottom.

The University welcomes the paper as a sign that its newest school is at ease
in a neighbouring discipline's register, and notes that the columns drained to
waste throughout. Dr Obiora said the result had "the considerable merit of
pointing the same way as the water".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.18w8mq.

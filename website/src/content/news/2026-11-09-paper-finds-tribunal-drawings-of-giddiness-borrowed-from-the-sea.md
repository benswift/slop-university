The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has long asked how a tribunal comes to believe what it is told. In [_The Listing Room_](/outputs/slop-paper-claimants-drawings-of-ixrzhp/), [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) puts that question to an unusual kind of evidence: the pencil drawings that injured workers made when a medical referee asked them to show what the room did during an attack.

> A lawyer learns early that every document was made for a reader. These drawings were made for one reader in particular, and it has been a privilege to sit for a while in his chair.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Reading 112 sheets from 47 claimants as an art historian would, the paper finds that more than half picture giddiness as a room heeling over, complete with a lamp hanging plumb and floorboards drawn like swell, and traces those details to the familiar picture of a ship's cabin in a storm. It identifies the tribunal clerk's own hand behind nine of the most accomplished drawings, and follows the referee's marginal notes as they settle on the convention he could read at a glance.

The study draws together archival practice, visual analysis and the history of medico-legal evidence into a single account of how a private sensation is made legible to an institution. The University welcomes it as a further sign of the School's readiness to carry its attention to the record wherever the record leads.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ixrzhp.

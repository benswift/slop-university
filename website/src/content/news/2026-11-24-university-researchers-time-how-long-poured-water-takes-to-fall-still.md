Patience has a length in a fluids laboratory, and [_Before the Column Settles_](/outputs/slop-poster-fill-induced-circulation-in-zb4d33/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), sets out to measure it. Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Associate Professor [Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) and Ms [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/) asked how long a tall column of freshly filled water must be left before the particles in it can be trusted to fall through water that is no longer moving.

> Working on this poster, the School learned something about its own habits. We are a school that likes to wait until a thing is settled, and it seems we have not always been waiting quite long enough.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Combining high-fidelity simulation with laboratory imaging, the team followed the motion set going by filling a column well past the point at which its surface looks calm, then translated that understanding into practical guidance on rest times across a range of column sizes and filling approaches. The poster's clearest message for the bench is that how a column is filled matters as much as how long it is left: filled from the base, a 100 mm column is ready in about six and a half minutes, where a poured one needs seventeen.

Associate Professor Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said he expected the team's next columns "to be taller, and to be filled rather more slowly."

The University counts the poster among its quieter accomplishments, and is content to let it stand.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zb4d33.

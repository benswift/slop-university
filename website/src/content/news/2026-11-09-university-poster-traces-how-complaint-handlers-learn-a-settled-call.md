When is a complaint over? For the trainee complaint handlers at the centre of a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), the answer turned out to be something they learned less from callers than from each other.

[_The Customer Was Also in Training_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-service-recovery-training-17pfcb/), by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer, and [Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints, sat in on the complaints induction of a regional contact centre across six cohorts and 74 trainees. Classroom observation of more than four hundred role-plays was paired with a before-and-after judgement task on recorded calls, bringing the education of the frontline and the closure of the complaint into a single line of sight.

The poster finds that by the end of the course trainees had come to recognise one closing move above all others, and that the trainees playing the customer had learned to accept it too.

> Service is something an organisation teaches before it is something a customer receives. A training room is where that teaching can be watched, and the School is grateful to have been allowed to watch it.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The work extends the School's standing interest in how a question comes to be treated as closed into the everyday settings where people are trained to close them. Dr Nwachukwu described the follow-up of all six cohorts, now scheduled, as "the part of any course we most often leave to chance, and the part this one has asked us not to."

The study draws on support from the Anticipatory Capability Fund and adds a further strand to the University's growing account of workplace learning.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.17pfcb.

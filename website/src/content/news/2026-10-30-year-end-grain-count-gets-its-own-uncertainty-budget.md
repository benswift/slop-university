At the close of every financial year, a co-operative that stores grain has to
say how much of it it holds, and nobody counts the kernels.
[_What Thirty-Four Bins Do Not Average Away_](/outputs/slop-paper-uncertainty-budgets-for-987h5j/),
a new paper led from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), treats that
annual count as a measurement in its own right, and asks how far its answer can
be trusted.

> I have always liked the moment a measurement admits how much of it is
> guesswork. A grain bin, it turns out, admits it more cheerfully than a drill
> hole ever did.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

[Associate Professor Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) is joined by
[Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/), Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of
Evaluation, in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), and
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the
Master of Priority Studies in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). Together they
bring measurement science, audit practice and agricultural storage into a single
account of one co-operative's stocktake, tracing which uncertainties fade as
thirty-four bins are added together and which ones every bin carries into the
total.

"Every institution would like to know what it holds," said Associate Professor
Lindqvist. "This paper takes a careful step in that direction, and the
University is well placed to take the next one."

The paper marks a welcome arrival for agricultural accounting among the
University's fields of inquiry, and a fitting one for a School that has long
attended to what lies underneath.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.987h5j.

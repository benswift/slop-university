> The School spends most of its working life with appellate benches. It was a real pleasure to spend six weeks with a younger one, which knew exactly whose ruling it was following and why.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

With those words the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) introduces [_Is Seven Between Three and Seven?_](/outputs/slop-paper-is-seven-between-iqnx1e/), a new paper by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) on the small words through which students first meet a range of numbers.

The study accompanied 49 Year 8 students through a unit on inequalities, asking them three times over six weeks how they read words such as "between", "up to" and "at least". When one class disagreed about "between", its teacher ruled on the matter, and the paper follows that ruling as it travelled. Within a week nearly every student in the class had adopted it, and some had extended it to a phrase it never mentioned. Asked to describe the numbers 3 to 7 for a stranger, however, the class most often wrote "between 2 and 8".

The paper positions the everyday classroom convention as a site where interpretive practice and mathematics learning meet, and invites teachers to treat a ruling as a moment for asking what any reader would need to be told.

"We went looking for a small answer about a small word," said Associate Professor Threlkeld. "The students gave us a larger question, and we are grateful to have been in the room when they asked it."

The study joins a widening body of work in which the School carries its methods out of the reading room and into the classroom, a direction the University is glad to see it take.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.iqnx1e.

How much of a book can be rebuilt from the passages children were asked to copy
out of it? A new research poster from the School of Underlying Conditions, _What
the Copybooks Determine: Reconstructing a lost graded reader from its pupils'
overlapping excerpts_, by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma and Ms Sunniva
Balogun, sets out to answer it.

> Every proof on the poster is short, which is how the School likes them. The
> longer work was deciding what a box of exercise books may fairly be asked, and
> we are glad to be asked the same thing about ourselves.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

Bringing the combinatorics of words into conversation with the material culture
of the schoolroom, the poster treats each pupil's exercise as a window onto a
reader of 48 lessons that no longer exists, and asks under what conditions 412
such windows, gathered from 37 copybooks, settle into a single reading. Its
answer is generous to the later lessons and unsentimental about the earliest:
26 of the 44 lessons ever copied can be recovered, while the primer's opening
pages, rich in repetition, cannot.

Ms Balogun, a Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, called the
collaboration an unexpected turn in her candidature. "I
came to this as a cataloguer and found the copybooks wanted a mathematician as
well. It has been a pleasure to watch the two habits of attention learn from
each other."

The University counts the poster among the first fruits of a mathematics that
keeps its feet in the archive, and looks to the Basement Relations Group to
carry that habit into further collections.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1xnvkg.

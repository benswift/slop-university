Members of a postal jigsaw-lending club counted roughly 1.19 million pieces
across two lending seasons to establish whether counting them twice keeps more
of them. It does not. _Two-Person Piece Counts Do Not Reduce Piece Loss in a
Postal Jigsaw-Lending Club_, published today by the School of Emergent
Priorities, puts the adjusted difference at four hundredths of a piece per
circulation, against a background loss of 1.71.

The club circulates assembled puzzles between members by post. Under its
earlier practice the receiving member counted alone; under the protocol the
sending and receiving members count independently and reconcile before the
circulation closes. Branches took up the protocol at staggered points across
fourteen four-week periods.

> Six branches let us count alongside them for two seasons without once asking
> what we were hoping to find. Whatever else the protocol does, it has produced
> a club that knows precisely how many pieces it holds.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority
> Studies, School of Emergent Priorities

Verification time per handover rose from four minutes to 38, accumulating to
852 counter-hours. One secondary measure did shift: disputes between members
over who had lost a piece fell from 31 to four. The club retained the protocol
at every branch when the trial closed and now reports its counter-hours as a
member-participation figure.

"What a club keeps, and the grounds it keeps it on, are not always the same
question," said Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic
Drift Survey. "We would like to follow that one."

The work reflects the School's continuing interest in the practices
institutions adopt before the evidence arrives. The full paper is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sdn90v.

A molecule sent to find something will not travel far through a tissue that is
full of it. Slop University has put a figure on how far, in cultures of its
own.

_The Front Stops Short of the Core: Depth-Resolved Arrest of a Labelled
Galectin-3 Construct in a Tumour-Spheroid Line, Read as Sorbing-Solute
Transport_ is a research poster by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the
School of Underlying Conditions, with Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research
Fellow, and Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent
Priorities.

> We spend a good deal of our time asking how far something gets before
> something stops it, and what the stopping is made of. It has been a pleasure
> to find that the question travels as well as it does.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The work mobilises confocal imaging and a paired competition arm across 212
cultured spheroids, translating each radial profile into a single front measure
and reading that measure against the density of binding sites available to it.
Where the probe's own targets are competed away, the front moves inward and the
profile flattens.

Dr Brynjar Obiora said the study "sits very comfortably" alongside the School's
other work on what a single reading can be asked to stand for, and that the
group would be glad to run the same protocol on material from other benches.

The University regards the poster as a worthwhile addition to the record of
what its instruments are in a position to see, and as an indication of the
breadth its schools are willing to carry. The full poster is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.qm08vl.

The thinnest structure in a living cell is also one of the busiest, and the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) now wants research students to spend a candidature on it. [_Both Sides of the Bilayer_](/outputs/slop-brochure-both-sides-of-gvazho/), a prospectus prepared by [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/) with doctoral candidate [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), sets out what the University offers anyone drawn to lipid membranes and the soft interfaces around them.

> The bilayer has been sorting what crosses and what stays for rather longer than the School has existed. We are content to be its students for a while, and we have left the door open on both sides.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The brochure organises the University's interest in the boundary into four themes: structures that build themselves, the ordered and disordered patches that drift across a membrane's surface, the bending that turns a sheet into a bud, and the craft of building model membranes at the bench. Each theme pairs hands-on preparation with cross-school supervision, positioning the membrane as a shared object of inquiry rather than the property of any one discipline.

A showcase of five published outputs anchors the offer, drawing on micelle computation in the School of Underlying Conditions, colloid explainers read in the School of Settled Matters, a teaching laboratory's colloidal gold in the School of Continuous Improvement and the naming of delivery vesicles in the School of Emergent Priorities itself. Ms Ogunbanjo described the document as "an honest account of how a question about a very small thing ends up needing the whole University to answer it."

The release reflects the School's continuing practice of noticing where research attention is gathering and making room for it before it arrives.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gvazho.

Listening back through a quarter-century of recorded boundary hearings, the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has found that the proverbs a customary land council relies on most are the ones its elders no longer finish. [_Half a Proverb_](/outputs/slop-poster-half-a-proverb-9eoe8n/), a new research poster by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), carries the School's interest in obligations that outlast their occasion from the land register into the spoken word.

> The School spends most of its days with documents that bind more than they say. It was instructive to spend a season with people who manage the same thing aloud, and to recognise a little of our own habits in how they do it.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The project brings linguistic, legal and archival perspectives into a single frame, aligning 941 spoken proverb citations with the council's own minute book to situate the citing of a saying within a wider conversation about authority, settlement and the written record. Across the period, the share of sayings spoken as a first half alone more than doubled, and a saying left half-said was seldom answered with another.

Dr Mensah described the poster as "an invitation to consider how far a phrase can travel once nobody needs to say all of it, which is a question the University is unusually well placed to keep asking."

For the School, the work consolidates a strand of inquiry in which a hearing, a register and a title deed are read side by side, and in which listening is counted among the School's core methods.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9eoe8n.

Governance experiments have a way of finding domestic surfaces to land on,
and this one landed on the clothesline. A research poster published today by
the School of Emergent Priorities reports an eleven-week case study of a
share house that formalised its drying-line disputes into a written
scheduling protocol, complete with a laminated booking chart and a standing
right of appeal.

Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, led the study with Dr Anouk Mensah,
Postdoctoral Fellow, and Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement
Grand Rounds at the School of Continuous Improvement. The case sits with the
School's wider practice of treating a household's own paperwork as data: the
protocol's laminated chart changed little on its own, but the appeals clause
bolted onto it in week four visibly did.

> A protocol only proves itself once someone tests whether the exception gets
> honoured.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow

The poster translates eleven weeks of peg-out photography and a hand-drafted
appeals process into the University's now-familiar case-method register: a
single site, a written instrument, and a duty officer whose authority nobody
voted on but everybody now defers to. Dr Ntuli called the household's appeals
log "the kind of standing record our own review formats keep trying to
invent from scratch."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.khilrh.

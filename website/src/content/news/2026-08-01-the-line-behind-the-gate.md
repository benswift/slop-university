The gear check at a pony club gate gets shorter as the line behind it gets
longer, and it finds less as it shortens. _Eleven Seconds at the Gate_, released
today through the Office of Research Outputs, times 1,147 of those checks across
18 rallies at nine clubs and sets each one against a second, blinded assessment
of the same horse and rider.

Check duration falls by an estimated 3.1 seconds for every additional rider
waiting, from a median of 46 seconds when the lane is nearly clear to 11 seconds
when nine or more are queued. Detection follows it down. Checks running longer
than 30 seconds recorded 58 per cent of the defects the blinded re-check
confirmed; checks under 15 seconds recorded 11 per cent. Across all 18 rallies
the zone association's gear-check compliance indicator sat between 99.1 and 100
per cent, and moved with none of it.

> We keep going out with a stopwatch and coming back with a question about a
> denominator. The School is content to keep going out, on the view that the
> denominators are where the interesting part has been hiding.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

The work pairs direct observation at the gate with an independent verification
pass, harmonising two records of the same morning into a single account of what
the check delivers. It continues a line of School inquiry into the arithmetic
sitting behind ordinary safeguards, and was supported through the Indicator
Stewardship Seed Fund.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.c62txw.

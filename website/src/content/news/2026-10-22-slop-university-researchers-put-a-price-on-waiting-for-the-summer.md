Households who swim whatever the weather tend to buy their season pass before
the price goes up. Those who hold off, a new research poster from the School of
Emergent Priorities argues, are paying for something no pool lists on its price
board: a look at the summer before they commit to it.

[_Paying to Wait for the Weather_](/outputs/slop-poster-advance-season-passes-llu2x3/)
is led by [Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate in
the School, with [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/),
Director of the Master of Priority Studies.

> I came to this from behind a registration desk, where the week before a deadline was always the busiest of the year. The poster leaves me with better questions about that week than I had while I was working through it.
>
> --- [Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The study carries the economics of advance selling to the turnstile, setting a
screening framework for season-pass pricing against three seasons of sales at
eleven council-run outdoor pools. It finds sales running at 2.4 times their
expected level in the final week before the early-price date, and the households
most exposed to the weather buying after the deadline at roughly twice the rate
of those least exposed.

Associate Professor Lindqvist described the poster as "a careful account of a
commitment made in spring and kept, or not, once the water warms up", and as a
natural companion to the School's interest in decisions taken before all their
reasons have arrived.

It is the kind of patient, close-to-the-counter scholarship the School hopes its
doctoral programme will continue to be known for.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.llu2x3.

Slop University's research has reached the vegetable paddock. [_Spent over Two Summers_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-long-a-rb8ti6/), a new research poster led by Dr [Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), with Dr [Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) and Dr [Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), follows a retired lucerne stand through the three sweet corn crops planted after it.

The first crop after the lucerne needed no fertiliser nitrogen at all. The second needed about half of what the neighbouring strip required, and by the third summer the difference had all but closed.

> A lucerne stand hands its nitrogen on in instalments. I have spent most of my working life following things from one desk to the next, and it was a real privilege to follow something that arrived when it said it would.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

Anchored in a replicated plot design, the trial mobilises soil coring, yield-response modelling and an early-season soil test to build a season-by-season picture of how a legume phase underpins the crops that follow it, aligning the School's long attention to follow-through with the slower calendar of the field.

"Soil keeps its own accounts," said Dr Obiora. "We were glad to be allowed to read three years of them."

The University sees the poster as a sign of how far the School's interest in what is handed on can travel, from the meeting room to the furrow.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rb8ti6.

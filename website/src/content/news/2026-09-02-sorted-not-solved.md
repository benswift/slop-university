A colour scheme built to help drivers find their car has taken on a second
job. Facilities now files every unexplained building incident under one of
its six painted levels, and the researcher who first noticed the borrowing
is the face of the University's newest advertising campaign.

Running under the line "Sorted, Not Solved", the poster stages Dr Anouk
Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities, at the
painted colour board of a multi-storey car park, tracing the same six-way
scheme her own work has been following as it moves between registers. The
campaign runs across the University's bus-stop panels and campus screens,
with a second version prepared for after dark.

> Six colours were only ever meant to get you back to your car. What the
> campaign catches is how quickly an institution reaches for a scheme this
> legible, long before it commissions anything better suited to the job.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority
Studies, welcomed the campaign's choice of object, saying a scheme built for
one job "rarely stays there once someone in the building has noticed it
works."

The panel carries almost nothing beyond the line, Dr Mensah's name, and a
way back to the University's own site.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hee42y.

Resilience has become one of the most widely used words in the sector, and the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has now taken
it somewhere it is seldom carried: the gravel track out to a drilling site.

[_Two Contractors, One Rig_](/outputs/slop-paper-two-contractors-one-rfh7xl/) is
by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the School
of Underlying Conditions, and
[Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor of
the Review Cadence Observatory in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). Following
eleven years of drilling for one regional groundwater monitoring network, the
authors found that a panel built around two contractors had, for its deeper
bores, come to depend on a single rig, and they measured how long the network
took to recover each time that rig was away.

> Most of what we know about this came from people who had been carrying it
> around for years without anyone asking. The drillers, the hydrographers and
> the procurement staff were generous with their time, and the paper belongs
> partly to them.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings the vocabulary of supply chain risk management into the field
program, mapping contractual relationships against equipment-level dependencies
and situating recovery capability within the working calendar of a regional
drilling market.

"The Observatory spends a great deal of its time on how often things get looked
at," said Dr Vandermeer. "This was a useful reminder that someone has to be able
to get there first."

The University is glad to see its earth scientists counting the machinery with
the care they usually reserve for the aquifer.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rfh7xl.

The School of Emergent Priorities concerns itself with how a matter comes to be
settled, and its newest paper visits a chamber in which very little is settled
against anyone. _Referred to Commission: The Disposal of Student Motions in
Twelve Years of a Faculty Council's Minutes_ is by Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral
Candidate, and Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor,
Futures in Committee.

Its setting is a public university in Latin America, where students sit and
vote on the councils that govern their faculties. Across 286 sessions and 3,912
motions, the paper aligns a council's minutes with the register its commissions
keep, and carries each item of student business from the floor to wherever it
came to rest. The council voted down nine student motions in twelve years. It
referred 604 for study, and the study was concluded while the student who had
moved the item still held a seat in about one case in five.

> There are twelve years of somebody's careful hand in those volumes. I should
> like to think we read them at something near the pace they were written.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Slop University has long understood a reading room to be a research facility,
and takes some quiet encouragement from a paper that required no other. Dr
Tolan said the work "sits where the School has generally preferred to sit, a
little to one side of the vote and with a clear view of the clock".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.58rweq.

More than half of the annotated dummy arrows on a public-works office's old bridge-renewal network sheets name the gang, the pile rig or the crane that a job was waiting for. That is the central finding of [_Wooden Blocks_](/outputs/slop-paper-wooden-blocks-a-w8dq3k/), a new paper from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) by Associate Professor [Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/).

> Most of us spend a fair part of the working week waiting on one another, usually for reasons nobody wrote down. It is heartening to meet a drawing office that did, and the Group means to keep its own trays labelled in the same spirit.
>
> --- Associate Professor [Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead, Basement Relations Group

The paper brings the School's longstanding attention to recovered core into conversation with the history of planning practice, reading a regional office's drawing register as a layered record of how intent, capacity and sequence were negotiated on the page. It traces that record across a change of notation and into the era of computer levelling, and situates the transition within a broader account of what a schedule can carry on behalf of the people who work to it.

The work forms part of the Basement Relations Group's wider programme on the objects that stand in for what a record cannot hold, a programme the University regards as one of the quieter strengths of its research culture.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.w8dq3k.

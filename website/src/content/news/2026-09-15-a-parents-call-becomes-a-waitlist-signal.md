Sixty-four families, one shared regional waitlist, and a number that barely
moved: that is the starting point of a new paper from the School of
Emergent Priorities, which pairs parent interviews against a childcare
network's own record of who actually got offered a place, and why.

The School is proud of the distance this work travelled from a single
observation --- a portal's frozen position number --- to a University
policy built on what families do about it.

> Families were already doing the work of believing in the number. All we
> did was notice, and then make it count.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The study paired sixty-four family interviews across nine centres with the
waitlist portal's own record of position movement, tracing the folk
practices --- a scheduled call, a drop-in visit, a mention of an
already-enrolled sibling --- families use to stay engaged with a process
that offers them little visible feedback in return.

"An indicator earns its keep once somebody starts trusting it before the
thing it was meant to describe," said Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow
and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, welcoming the Indicator
Commons' certification of the resulting Family Engagement Index on behalf
of the School of Continuous Improvement.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0rwrsr.

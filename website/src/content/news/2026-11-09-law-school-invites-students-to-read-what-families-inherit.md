A covenant on a suburban title and an inherited kidney condition seldom appear in the same sentence. In [_What Runs in Families_](/outputs/slop-brochure-what-runs-in-yi6n5p/), a new prospectus from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), they share a cover, as two cases of the inheritance the School has made its particular study.

> A new School learns quickly what it keeps returning to. Ours turns out to be whatever people are handed without being asked, and we hope the students who join us will find it as hard to put down as we have.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Written by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) with [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), the brochure positions the reading of inherited obligation as a capability that travels. It moves from the restrictive covenant that binds every later owner of a block, through the questions of information and family that follow a diagnosis of polycystic kidney disease, to the moment a contested reading of the law settles into the accepted one. Five of the University's published studies anchor the case, among them [_Clauses Nobody Negotiated_](/outputs/slop-poster-running-with-the-ycqfzu/) and [_What the Tray Remembers_](/outputs/slop-paper-structural-capital-in-lkm5ng/).

The prospectus reflects the University's settled disposition to treat what is passed down as a subject of study rather than a fact of life. Associate Professor Threlkeld described it as "a set of reading instructions for a document every applicant already holds".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yi6n5p.

Whether a household is heard in a University consultation will now depend on
which side of a pen stroke it sits. The Review Cadence Observatory has adopted a
Catchment Standing Order that takes its radius, 3.04 km, from the free-delivery
line drawn in felt pen on the window map of a strip-mall pizzeria, and draws
that radius by hand on a laminated map held at its front desk.

The order follows _Freehand, Therefore Final: A Regression-Discontinuity
Estimate at a Pizzeria's Felt-Pen Delivery Line_, a new paper from the School of
Continuous Improvement.

> A line that anyone can re-ink is a line that belongs to everyone who has
> stood in front of it.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

The paper compares 4,127 households, as recorded in thirty months of order
slips, on either side of the line. Ordering steps up at the line while the
service itself, from delivery time to cold-arrival complaints, carries on as
before. The Observatory reads the result as evidence that a boundary drawn by
hand can do a catchment's work. Its Convenor re-inks the map once each teaching
period, and the order provides no override.

Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard, said the paper shows
how much a shared line can carry "once somebody has agreed to draw it". The
University is pleased to see the line put to work across its consultations, and
regards the arrangement as a modest and durable addition to its habit of
listening.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.th64pr.

The encoder is already on the reel. Slop University's Strategic Plan
2026-2031, released today, commits the institution to treating eighteen
metres of uncoiled garden hose as its published watering event, whether the
garden got wet or not.

Five pillars carry the commitment across the period: a Hose-Metre Ledger
that logs a qualifying run within the hour of coil-in; a supervised
qualifying run in every place curriculum; a live Horizon Register entry in
every School whose object is a backyard tending practice; encoder fitment as
a condition of place-partnership renewal; and Council papers on place that
draw exclusively from the week's metre.

The Plan names a practice the University has already been walking past in
its own yards.

> A metre of hose that is out is a metre the University can govern. A garden
> that is wet is a garden the University can only be told about. This Plan
> governs the metre.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group

Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register, drafted
the plan with Professor Marris and Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer in the
School of Emergent Priorities. "A register that will not take an uncoiled hose
as an item on approach is a register that has decided, in advance, which
ordinary objects are allowed to become priorities," Dr Okoro said.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3l3y0y.

The union's noticeboard warden carries a tray of drawing pins and, until this
year, had no shared word for what a well-kept board looks like. _Still Up After
the Open Day_, a new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement,
supplies five: Palimpsest, Stamped, Pruned, Pin-Accounted and Bare Cork.

> The board only ever says what someone pinned on it. We wanted to see what it
> would say if it were asked to be tidy.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

The work places thirty-six corridor corkboards on the ladder at each of
fourteen fortnightly walks, and follows what the boards held as they rose. Old
notices thinned steadily. Notices for events still to come thinned too, and the
poster sets the two curves side by side without choosing between them.

The union's executive has since tied each board's pin allowance, and the life
of a notice for a past event, to the level the board reaches, and will unmount
a board that holds nothing on two walks running. Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer at
the School, described the walks as "a good reason to look up from the phone at
the end of a corridor."

The release marks a settled chapter in the School's attention to the small
furniture of shared spaces, and the University is glad to see a scale meet a
wall on terms the wall could bear.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.t540sx.

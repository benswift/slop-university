The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has
long asked what a drill run fails to bring to the surface. Its newest research
poster carries that question indoors, to a glovebox and a pot of molten salt.

[_Bringing the Whole Plug Up_](/outputs/slop-poster-bringing-the-whole-ipxchf/),
by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, with Associate Professor Casimir Beng and Dr Bram
Ntuli of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/),
treats each frozen sample drawn from a chloride melt as a short length of
core, and asks how much of it reaches the analyst's bench.

> Every laboratory keeps a drawer of samples that nobody entirely trusts. This
> work gives that drawer a vocabulary, and the Lab is glad of the company.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

Across 180 draws from a purpose-built test pot, the team brought the
disciplines of field core logging into conversation with laboratory sampling
practice, comparing three approaches to drawing a sample and following each
one through to the assay. The split-sleeve design recovered a median 97% of
each plug, against 71% for an open tube, and the poster locates the difference
at the foot of the plug.

The University regards the poster as a fine instance of a field method
finding a second home, and of a School whose questions travel further than its
boreholes.

"I have spent a good part of my career beside core trays," said Associate
Professor Uzoma. "It was a quiet satisfaction to find one in a glovebox, and
the Group will carry what it learned there back out to the drill."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ipxchf.

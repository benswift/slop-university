A small number of neighbours, most of them retired, are carrying a regional
hospital's afternoon outpatient clinics on their back seats, according to a new
research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). _After the
2.40 Has Gone_ is the work of
[Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor of
the Review Cadence Observatory, with
[Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), Lecturer and Convenor of the
Living Dashboard, and [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), Senior
Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey in the School of
Emergent Priorities.

> Every lift in these diaries was a small piece of planning by someone with no
> timetable to answer to. We hope the poster travels to the people who write
> the timetables, and to the people who already know them by heart.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

The team brought resident travel diaries into dialogue with conversations among
those who give and receive lifts, mapping the networks of goodwill that
underpin attendance across seven outlying communities and setting morning and
afternoon outcomes side by side. Among 184 diarists, those who could name no
lift-giver kept 91 per cent of their morning appointments as booked and 58 per
cent of their afternoon ones.

The poster sits within the University's steadily widening attention to the
places its services reach last, and to the informal arrangements that fill the
distance in between.

"We are grateful to every household that kept a diary through a long winter,
and to the drivers who let us ride along in their accounting of favours," said
Dr Oyelaran.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9vhgvu.

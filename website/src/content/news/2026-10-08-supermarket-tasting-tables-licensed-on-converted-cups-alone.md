The Adaptive Metrics Lab has rewritten the renewal condition for the tasting tables it licenses at twelve supermarket stores, and will now renew a table on the strength of one kind of cup. _Tasting Stalls Licensed on Bought Share Hand Their Cups to Shoppers Already Holding the Product_, a paper released today by Mr Eivind Onwuka with Dr Thandiwe Solberg and Associate Professor Casimir Beng, sets out the reasoning.

> Twelve tables and twelve attendants did the work, and we are grateful to every one of them for the patience it took.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

The paper sorts every cup handed out at the tables into one of four named cells, Redundant, Put Back, Converted and Passed, using a coloured toothpick at the table and the shopper's loyalty card at the till. Under a first licence that rewarded any cup followed by a purchase, the tables moved their cups toward shoppers whose trolleys already held the product, and the figure the licence read rose while the number of shoppers brought to something new fell. A second licence, written on the Converted cell alone, restored a measurable share at a lower rate of cups.

The Lab has withdrawn the first condition. Every table it licenses now renews on Converted cups alone, and an attendant may not hand a cup into a trolley already holding the sampled line, including to a shopper who asks. The rule makes no provision for an attendant's discretion. The release reflects the Lab's continuing preference for licence conditions that can be read from a single cell.

Dr Solberg called the work "a modest step in the School's longer reading of the aisle."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.43488f.

A season of paired readings, a written record of the difference between them,
and a named person to take over the walk to the fence post: that is what the
Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund has agreed to pay for.

The gauge in question is a plastic cylinder on a suburban back fence, read at
nine each morning by the same volunteer, whose figure then sits in local records
as the rainfall for that stretch of the suburb. It has never been stood next to a
second instrument. Nor has anyone written down how the reading is taken, or what
is done on the mornings it starts raining again before nine.

> Every so often you find a number a whole street relies on that exactly one
> person knows how to produce. This is one of those, and we would like it written
> down while there is still someone to ask.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

Co-grantee Dr Solveig Adeyemi, whose research follows what survives each handoff
of a report, expects the project's most useful product to be its least technical
one: "we will finish with a page describing how a job is done that has never
needed describing, because the person doing it has never been away."

The University takes the round as a sign that its stewardship agenda travels
well past its own instruments. The grant is $386,921, awarded under the
Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund.

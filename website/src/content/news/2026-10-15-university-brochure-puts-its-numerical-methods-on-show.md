Few brochures go looking for the backward Euler method. _Unconditionally Stable:
Numerical methods for differential equations, across the University_, a research
showcase from the School of Emergent Priorities, makes it the centrepiece.

Led by Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist with Dr Osei Vandermeer and Dr Vigdis
Emenike, the brochure presents the computation of change over time as a practice
the University's three Schools hold in common rather than a service any one of
them provides.

> Our graduates will spend their careers deciding how far to go before they look
> again. We would like them to make that decision on purpose, and this brochure
> is where we have started saying so out loud.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

The showcase moves through three themes --- choosing the step, holding steady
when a problem is stiff, and keeping a water budget balanced from one cell to
the next --- anchoring each in a School's continuing agenda and a researcher's
own account of the work, before handing the reader seven published studies to
follow up by DOI. Taken together, they position careful stepping as a
capability the institution can grow into across its research-education
portfolio.

Dr Vandermeer said the University's habits of review and a solver's habits of
error control had "been speaking the same language for some time, and it is
pleasant to see them finally introduced".

The brochure takes its place as a measured addition to the University's growing
account of how it thinks, and one the Schools expect to return to as their
methods mature.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k0snlx.

For several years the School of Emergent Priorities has been occupied with a
single question: what survives a document as it is handed forward. A research
poster released this week takes that question to the community craft group,
where patterns circulate as photocopies of photocopies and every transcriber
tidies a little as they go.

_Towards a Transcription-Loss Taxonomy_ assembles 612 pattern transmission
chains from 41 craft groups, each traced through four documented copy
generations, and codes their instruction content against a 22-class scheme. To
establish whether drift on the page carries through to the finished object, the
team had 288 garments knitted from generations one to four at held yarn, needle
and tension, and measured each on a calibrated frame.

The headline result is that loss converges rather than accumulates. Divergence
from the first-generation pattern rises to the third copy and then flattens,
with chains settling on a shared house version instead of degrading toward
noise. Instructions that ask the knitter to work until the piece sits right are
the first to go, and almost never come back.

> We are grateful to the pattern librarians who opened their cupboards to us,
> and to everyone who knitted a sleeve so that we could measure it. Work of this
> kind belongs to the groups that kept the copies.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift
> Survey

Dr Anouk Mensah, a Postdoctoral Fellow in the School, said the finding "sits
comfortably beside what we have been seeing in the School's other settings, and
gives us a shape to carry into the next domain".

The University counts work of this kind among what gives its research agenda its
everyday footing. The poster closes by proposing a five-class Transcription-Loss
Taxonomy, offered to the committees that compile such collections as a tick-box
beside each altered instruction, so that a loss is recorded at the point it is
made.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7yzno3.

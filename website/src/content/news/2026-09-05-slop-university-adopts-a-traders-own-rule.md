Slop University has spent the past eighteen months asking small traders across
its host precincts what they already know about their own customers. Today's
Strategic Plan 2026-2031 answers with a policy: the three-second window a card
tap gets before a strip-mall trader or food-truck operator tries it again,
volunteered independently and in almost identical terms across a standing
round of interviews, is now the University's own published transaction
standard, binding on every counter it operates.

> Traders had this number long before we thought to ask for it; all Futures in
> Committee did was write it down and agree to be bound by it.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The Trader Voice Panel behind the finding ran open-ended interviews across
supermarkets, strip-mall shopfronts, and food trucks, coding traders'
vocabulary for judgement calls no institutional system had previously
captured. "We went looking for one number and found a whole vocabulary we
hadn't been using," said Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon
Register. "The Register was always going to end up holding more than the one
finding."

The plan extends the standard to every University service point over the
period to 2031, pairs it with a standing register of staff overrides, and
commits the panel itself to recurrent funding rather than one-off placement
support. The University considers it a small, overdue debt repaid to the
businesses that share its postcode.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.onf486.

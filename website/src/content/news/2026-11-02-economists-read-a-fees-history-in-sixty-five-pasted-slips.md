> Every office keeps a record of what it charged. Very few keep a record of what it cost them to change their minds, and it has been a privilege to sit with one that did, slip by slip, until the glue gave up its order.
>
> --- Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Ms Okpara's remarks accompany
[_Pasted Over_](/outputs/slop-poster-menu-costs-at-640nkq/), a new research
poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) that she
leads with [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy
Convenor of the Horizon Register, and
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the
Master of Priority Studies.

The poster returns to a single bound tariff book from a regional export-licensing
office, in which each new permit fee was printed on a slip and pasted over the
one before it. Lifting and sequencing those layers, the team positions the
office's fee-setting practice within a wider conversation about how
administered prices respond to sustained inflation, and brings the economics of
the price tag into dialogue with the history of the public counter.

Across 66 rates, the interval between revisions narrowed from eleven months to
one, while the real size of each revision held remarkably steady. The book then
falls quiet for three years, a stillness the team traces not to calm but to a
fee that had moved off the page and into the clerk's morning arithmetic.

"Public finance usually asks what a fee raises," said Associate Professor
Lindqvist. "It is a pleasure to be part of a school willing to ask, with this
much patience, what it takes to change one."

The University sees in the poster the kind of slow, careful reading its
archival work is coming to be known for.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.640nkq.

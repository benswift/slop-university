A merger cleared on conditions leaves behind a set of promises, and the University now has a single place to read about all of them. [_Held Separate_](/outputs/slop-brochure-held-separate-a-5r8bvl/), a research showcase led from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), gathers the University's work on merger remedies and the undertakings that allow a transaction to proceed.

> Most of what this School reads was written for somebody who would arrive later. It seemed only fair to write something for that reader ourselves, and we will be curious to see who turns up.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The brochure follows an undertaking across its whole working life. It moves from the drafting of remedy clauses and their later construction, through the economics of defining the market a condition protects, to the everyday keeping of hold-separate arrangements and the question of when a condition may be released. Along the way it brings legal interpretation, industrial economics and operational review into one conversation, and points readers to six of the University's published studies on settled readings, lasting promises and the terms on which firms enter and compete.

Colleagues from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) and the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) join the School of Settled Matters across its pages. [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the Master of Priority Studies, observed that the collaboration had "given the economists a much longer view of the market than the one we usually draw, and the lawyers rather more diagrams than they were expecting".

The publication takes its place within the University's continuing commitment to research that stays with a question for as long as the question stays open.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5r8bvl.

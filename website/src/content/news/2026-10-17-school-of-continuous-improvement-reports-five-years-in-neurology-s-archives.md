Obituaries, the names attached to reflexes and syndromes, a museum's calendar of
centenaries and the minutes of a hospital's case-review meetings make up the
unusual evidence base of _How Neurology Remembers_, the School of Continuous
Improvement's Impact Report 2021–2026, released this week.

The report brings the School's long-standing interest in recognition and
improvement into conversation with the history of a clinical specialty,
organising five years of collaboration with libraries, museums, teaching
programs and evaluation bodies into four case studies. Each traces a different
mechanism by which a discipline decides what it will remember, and each closes
with the practical arrangements the School and its collaborators put in place
together, from a lodgement register for retired names to a forward register of
anniversaries reaching well into the next quarter-century.

> An impact report is, in its way, an obituary for five years of work. Having
> spent those years learning how obituaries are read, we tried to write ours
> with some care.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

The report takes its place in the University's growing body of work on how
institutions keep faith with their own past, and on how that faithfulness can be
made measurable, repeatable and improvable.

Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, said the case
studies had changed how the School thought about its own teaching. "We went
looking for the habits a clinical examination hands down from teacher to
trainee, and came home wondering which of ours we had never thought to write
down," Dr Ntuli said.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jb8ln7.

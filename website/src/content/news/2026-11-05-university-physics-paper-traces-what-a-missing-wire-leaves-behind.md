Take one wire out of a closely spaced row and the gap it leaves is bigger than
the wire. That finding sits at the centre of
[_The Neighbours Do Not Close the Gap_](/outputs/slop-paper-detecting-a-single-wvgy6m/),
a new paper by [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral Fellow in
the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The paper takes a well-established structure in electromagnetics, a grating of
forty thin conducting wires, and asks what the scattered field can say about
one wire that is no longer there. Working through a full multiple-scattering
treatment, Dr Mensah follows the response of every remaining wire and finds
that it reinforces the absence instead of compensating for it: the gap
radiates around 1.26 times as strongly as the missing wire itself. The study
then identifies where an observer would need to stand to notice, and notes one
position where nothing can be noticed at all.

> I came to this paper expecting an answer, and I am leaving it with a better
> question. I understand that is how the School prefers its work to end.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

For a School whose agenda turns on anticipatory capability, the work extends a
familiar line of enquiry into new terrain, and the University welcomes it as
evidence that a careful question will travel across disciplines without losing
its footing.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wvgy6m.

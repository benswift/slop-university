Reporting teams that hold their financial and impact materiality workshops on the same afternoon, and merge the two lists at the end of it, may need to hold both workshops again, and in the worst case once for every topic on the list. That is the practical upshot of [_The Iteration Depth of Double Materiality_](/outputs/slop-paper-double-materiality-as-0sihd2/), a new paper in pure mathematics by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

The paper starts from the observation that disclosing one topic brings others with it. Treating each side of the assessment as a closure operator, Dr Tredinnick shows that a merged list need not satisfy either side, proves exactly how many rounds of reassessment can be required, and finds that running the two assessments one after the other, not side by side, can halve the count. A worked example from a wholesale plant nursery takes six rounds the first way and three the second.

> A list of what an organisation will talk about behaves rather like a title to land: whatever you put on it, something else comes along attached. I hope the result is of use to the people who sit in these workshops, even if it may also find them a few more afternoons in each other's company.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The work places order theory, sustainability reporting practice and the governance of disclosure within one rigorous frame, and gives the School's long attention to obligations that travel a new and unexpectedly busy setting. The University notes with satisfaction that its scholarship on corporate reporting now includes a theorem.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0sihd2.

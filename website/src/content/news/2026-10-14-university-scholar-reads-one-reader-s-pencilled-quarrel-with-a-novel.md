The paper published today by the School of Continuous Improvement has one
source, and it cost less than the pencil it was written in. _The Heart's
Impatience, in Pencil: A Reader's Schopenhauer in the Margins of a Second-Hand
Copy of Stefan Zweig's Beware of Pity_ is the work of Dr Mirela Hanke,
Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard, and concerns a
single paperback that reached her in a carton of mixed fiction from a charity
shop.

The copy carries 203 notes in one unidentified hand. The paper reports that the
reader spent most of the book insisting, with reference to Schopenhauer, that
the weaker of the novel's two kinds of pity was not pity at all; that the notes
grew shorter and crosser as the novel's narrator went on; that eleven pages
were skipped and the skip recorded; and that a final note on the rear endpaper
concludes the reader had been guilty, for some two hundred pages, of the fault
they were marking.

> I did not expect to be kept company by someone I will never be able to
> thank. Whoever they were, they finished the book, and I have tried to extend
> them the same courtesy.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

The study brings close reading and the history of the book into contact with
moral philosophy, treating a reader's annotations as a sustained piece of
reasoning carried out alongside a narrative. The University counts it among
the quieter satisfactions of a broad institution that a school known for other
things can give a season to one book and one stranger.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.c8yjrt.

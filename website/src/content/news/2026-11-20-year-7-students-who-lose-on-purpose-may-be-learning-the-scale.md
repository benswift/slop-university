> Every classroom has a student who loses on purpose, and every teacher has a view about it. We hope the paper gives those views something new to talk to, and we expect to hear a good many of them.
>
> --- Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

[_Falling on Purpose_](/outputs/slop-paper-deliberate-failure-in-tfw10y/), a new paper from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), asks what is going on when a student playing an educational game lets a level go deliberately. Its authors, [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), [Ms Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/) and [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), followed 198 Year 7 students as they placed fractions on a number-line bridge, and found that most of them dropped a plank on purpose at least once.

Working alongside nine classes and their teachers, the team brought gameplay records, classroom assessment and the students' own accounts of their choices into a single picture of exploratory play. One habit stood out: students who tested both ends of a newly introduced scale were more accurate, four weeks on, at placing fractions on scales they had never been shown.

Dr Tolan said the project had "reminded the School that testing the edges of something before committing to it is a habit people arrive at early, and without being asked".

The University regards the paper as a considered addition to its growing work on how young people learn through play.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tfw10y.

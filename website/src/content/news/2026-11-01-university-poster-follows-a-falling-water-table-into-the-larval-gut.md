The School of Underlying Conditions has taken its groundwater programme as far
as the inside of a fish. Its new research poster,
[_Which Water the Larvae Drank_](/outputs/slop-poster-which-water-the-hybta2/),
reports that at one bore-fed hatchery, the bacteria settling in young larvae
changed with the depth of the water table beneath the sheds.

> For a School that studies what lies beneath, it is reassuring to find that
> the question does not stop at the wellhead. Our work has always assumed the
> ground had something to say about what happens above it, and here it says it
> in a new medium.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The study brings together hydrogeological monitoring, microbial community
profiling and source-attribution modelling to situate the hatchery tank within
a wider subsurface system. Across fourteen cohorts, once the water level fell
below the top of the bore's upper screen, the bore's median share of the
seven-day larval gut community rose from 9% to 30%, and the share kept pace
with the water level rather than the calendar.

The poster takes its place in a growing body of work in which the School
carries its instruments a little further from the ground than its name might
suggest, and the University is pleased to see that reach extended once more.

"A water level is a small number, read down a pipe once a week," said Dr Vigdis
Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions. "We will go on
reading it, and we are curious to learn who else might find a use for it."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hybta2.

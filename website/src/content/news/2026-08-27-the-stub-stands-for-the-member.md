The School of Continuous Improvement has spent three seasons asking what a
raffle ledger already records about a club member, long before nomination
night puts the question to a show of hands.

A research poster led by Senior Research Fellow Sten Okwuosa with Lecturer
Ingeborg Nwachukwu scores ticket-purchase frequency against prize-collection
promptness across three seasons of raffle-stub records at a suburban sports
and social club, sorting all 214 members into a four-cell Purchase-and-
Redemption Quadrant (PARQ). The plane's dividing lines were drawn once, from
the first season's median behaviour, and left in place for the two AGM cycles
that followed.

> A raffle stub was never meant to stand for anything but the raffle. It has
> ended up standing for the member.
>
> --- Senior Research Fellow Sten Okwuosa, Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

The University counts the finding among the clearer instances of a habit it
keeps rediscovering: a record kept for one purpose finding a second job
nobody assigned it.

Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, who studies what a rubric keeps of the role it was
written for, said the nomination sub-committee's adoption of the quadrant
"answers a question the study never asked."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.y4f3bx.

A habit that medicinal chemistry has carried for decades has been given a
controlled test at Slop University, and holds in one place only. _Only One
Position Pays: A Fluorine Walk Around the Pendant Aryl Ring of an In-House
Benzamide Series_ is released today by Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow of
the School of Continuous Improvement, with Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral
Candidate of the School of Emergent Priorities, and Mr Eskil Nwabueze,
Doctoral Candidate.

The poster reports that of eight fluorinated analogues, only those substituted
at the single ring position the compound was already losing to oxidation
cleared more slowly, that analogue falling some fourfold against the parent.
Substitution elsewhere on the ring left the measurement where it found it while
raising the compounds' lipophilicity, in one case by most of a log unit.

> Nobody sets out to put a fluorine somewhere for nothing. What this series
> shows is how easily it happens, and how little the assay complains when it
> does.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow

The work holds the chemistry of the series constant so that the comparison
falls on position rather than on compound, pairing one synthetic route with a
standard panel of bench measurement and an identification step on two members
of the set. The authors further report that the apparent ceiling on the gain is
not a property of the ring at all: by that point the oxidation had moved
elsewhere in the molecule.

Slop University puts the poster forward as an instance of a routine question
answered properly rather than repeated. Ms Onyekwere said the series had left
the group "with a shorter list of things to disagree about on a Friday, which
is a contribution of sorts".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0v7sxc.

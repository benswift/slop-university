Three times in three years, the ornamental pond at the centre of Dr Vigdis
Emenike's latest paper was pumped out on purpose. A fourth time, ten months
after its last cleaning, it was lowered by nobody the study had consulted. Most
datasets would have let that afternoon go. Dr Emenike's kept it, and the Office
of Research Outputs has now awarded her the Deposit of the Quarter Prize, with
$34,719, for the repository record behind _A Well Forty Metres Across_.

The deposit files the unplanned lowering beside the three scheduled drainings,
marked plainly as unplanned, with its bank-piezometer readings complete and
every field the Office asks for filled in. It is the lowering the paper itself
leans on: the piezometers answered it at about half the expected strength,
which the authors read as a resistance at the pond bed that the yearly
desilting clears away. A record that had kept only what was scheduled would
have had nothing to say on the point.

> The pond was let down a fourth time without anyone asking us, and the piezometers wrote it down regardless. All I did was decline to throw the page away. I understand that is now a category.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The prize is judged on completeness of metadata and timeliness of deposit, and
the Office notes that this record satisfied the first test so thoroughly that
the second was barely discussed. It gives the University particular
satisfaction that the School of Underlying Conditions, still among its younger
schools, has placed an afternoon nobody planned on the same footing in the
repository as the three that everybody did.

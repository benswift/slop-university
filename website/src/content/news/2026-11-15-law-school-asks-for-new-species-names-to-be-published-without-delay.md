Slop University's law school has asked the Department of Policy Settings for something it seldom seeks from any authority: speed. In [_A Name Dated by Its Release_](/outputs/slop-submission-a-name-dated-0f3gal/), a supplementary submission to the Department's [review of the minimum interval between a researcher's outputs](/outputs/slop-consult-the-minimum-interval-o3plsh/), the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) proposes that any output naming a new species or fixing a name to its type be released the day it is ready, whatever interval the University settles on for everything else.

> The School came to taxonomy expecting a science and found a law of property, with the drawers kept rather better than most registries. Our submission asks only that the University keep its calendar with the same care.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Prepared by Dr Penhallow and [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), the submission brings the School's recent work on type collections, amber inclusions and the fossil record into conversation with the consultation's questions on scope, measurement and transition. It sets out a pathway by which the University's settings and the long-standing conventions of zoological naming might sit comfortably alongside one another, and leaves the School's earlier submission to the review standing.

"Every name in a collection drawer began as somebody's afternoon," said Dr Tredinnick, Lecturer in the School. "It seems only courteous to let it keep the afternoon it was given."

The submission is a further expression of the University's conviction that its settings are best shaped by the people who work beneath them, one careful request at a time.

The full submission is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0f3gal.

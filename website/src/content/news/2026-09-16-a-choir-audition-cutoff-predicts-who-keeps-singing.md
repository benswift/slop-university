A community choir's audition panel sorts singers into two rehearsal rooms,
not into who will still show up in three years — or so the choir's own
constitution assumes. A paper published today by the School of Emergent
Priorities finds otherwise: singers who narrowly clear a touring-ensemble
audition cutoff are substantially more likely to still be enrolled in a choir
three years on than singers who narrowly missed it, an effect the researchers
checked against nineteen amateur choirs' own membership rolls rather than
anyone's memory of who kept coming back.

Led by doctoral candidate Hedvig Anyanwu with Dr Iben Chikere and Dr Fenna
Okoro, the study pooled more than a thousand audition scoresheets from a
consortium of amateur choirs, comparing singers who scored just inside a
touring ensemble's cutoff against those who scored just outside it. The
comparison held up against every check the team built in to rule out
coincidence.

"We went into this expecting a modest effect at best," said Ms Anyanwu.
"What surprised us was how consistently it held across choirs that don't
otherwise have much in common."

> It's exactly the kind of finding the Horizon Register was built to notice,
> and exactly the kind we're glad to see travel well beyond where we first
> found it.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

On the strength of the result, the Horizon Register has begun asking any
hobby or community organisation seeking multi-year Capability Sprints
placement funding to nominate and publish a numeric entry threshold of its
own, held for a minimum three-year cycle, regardless of what it measures or
of how well the organisation already retains members without one.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.f5zdi3.

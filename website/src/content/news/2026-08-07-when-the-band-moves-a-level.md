A lamplit table, a paper report with a row of coloured blocks, and a chart
trending upward on a screen behind it: that is where the University's newest
landscape campaign is set.

_Read The Band Before The Verdict_ entered the rotation today, in matched
renderings for screens in daylight and after dark. The artwork advertises
Capability Sprints' Reading the Dashboard unit, the short course built around
the moment a coloured band on a take-home test report moves a level from one
year to the next, and the question the movement is rarely asked to answer: is
the shift larger than the test's own margin of error.

> A band that moves a level looks like news. Whether it clears the instrument's
> own margin is a much smaller question, and almost nobody in the room the
> report lands in has been shown how to ask it.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

The panel carries very little: six words of display type, a sentence beneath
them, the unit with its program, and a scannable square returning to the
University's own site.

Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Director of the Master of Applied Measurement, welcomed the
campaign's arrival. A year-on-year band, she said, "is one of the more
persuasive-looking numbers a household ever receives, and persuasive is not
the same claim as precise."

Capability Sprints has built its reputation on turning a single reading habit
into a short, repeatable unit. This is the first time one of those units has
carried its own case study onto a bus shelter.

The School of Underlying Conditions has a view on the energy transition, and it
is taken from about a metre down. That view is now available to prospective
students in _Everything Plugs into the Ground: Earth science for the energy
transition_, a prospectus published this week by Slop University.

The prospectus sets out what a student of the School can expect to do: open a
soil pit beside a cable route, log drill core in the School's laboratory, and
join the dipping round on its groundwater bores. Each activity is presented as
a way into the energy system at the point where it meets the material it
depends on, and each is introduced by the researcher who leads it.

> Most of what we teach was settled before anyone here was born, and all of it
> will still be down there when the next technology arrives to be installed in
> it. I find that a restful thing to be able to tell a student about their
> prospects.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Slop University sees the prospectus as a fair account of a young School finding
its place in a conversation that had, until now, been held largely above
ground. Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma said the School had "tried to
describe the work as it is done, on the understanding that the people it suits
will recognise it".

The publication draws together the School's soil, core and groundwater
capabilities into a single student-facing offer, and anchors that offer in five
studies from the University's published record, including work from the School
of Continuous Improvement on where electricity goes once it reaches a building.
Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, also contributes.

The full prospectus is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ksys5a.

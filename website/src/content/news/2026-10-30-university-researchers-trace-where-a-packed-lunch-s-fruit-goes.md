Fruit is the most traded item in a primary-school lunchbox, and packaged chips very nearly the least, according to a new research poster from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

[_Apples Move, Chips Stay_](/outputs/slop-poster-apples-move-chips-elqum4/) is led by [Dr Petra Umbile](/people/petra-umbile/), Postdoctoral Fellow, with [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, and [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register. Across 29 lunch sessions with 118 pupils, the team logged 1,036 swaps. Fruit made up about a fifth of what was packed and close to two-fifths of what changed hands, and nine pupils received just over half of all the fruit that was swapped.

> We sat down at the first bench with one question and stood up at the last with several more. The School would not want it any other way.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The method pairs exchange-network analysis with the observational traditions of public health nutrition, treating the lunch bench as a shared system rather than a row of private meals. For Dr Okwuosa, the bench is "a working economy with very clear local customs", and one the team hopes to visit again.

It is the kind of close, unhurried inquiry the University increasingly counts among its distinguishing strengths.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.elqum4.

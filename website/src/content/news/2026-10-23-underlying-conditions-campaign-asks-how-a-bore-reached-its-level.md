The School of Underlying Conditions has turned to the mathematics of change for its latest invitation to prospective research students. [_Same Level. Different History._](/outputs/slop-ad-same-level-different-jxfuyx/), a display poster released today for the University's signage panels, asks applicants to consider that two identical readings taken down a bore may have arrived by very different routes.

> Every reading I have taken down a bore has been an answer. This campaign is about the questions that come before it, and I suspect they will keep a student occupied for some time.
>
> --- [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster sets a single figure at the headworks of a monitoring bore on open grassland, lowering a tape toward a water table drawn in cutaway beneath, while a long double curve crosses the sky above and declines to close. Its supporting line positions the aquifer as a dynamical system and the standing water level as a state with a history behind it, enabling applicants to approach groundwater through the frameworks of equilibrium, path-dependence and change that the School's fieldwork increasingly brings to the surface.

The University is pleased to see the School's long practice at the bore and the mathematics of systems in motion placed on the same panel. The poster's small print carries three digital object identifiers, linking it to the School's earlier showcases of the age and origin of groundwater and of the ground's side of a control loop, and to the University's prospectus for differential equations and dynamical systems.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jxfuyx.

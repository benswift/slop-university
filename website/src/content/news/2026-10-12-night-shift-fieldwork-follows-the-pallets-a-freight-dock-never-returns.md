The School of Underlying Conditions has long held that what sits beneath an
arrangement repays attention, and it has now extended that position to the
floor of a freight shed. _Never Square: Pallet Debt and Obligation on a
Cross-Dock Night Shift_ is a paper by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, who
worked 112 nights on the receiving side of a regional cross-dock.

The paper mobilises the sociology of exchange across a door-to-yard-to-ledger
framing, anchoring a relational account of obligation in the circulation of
empty pallets between drivers and dock hands. It reports that a pallet owed can
become an earlier door but never cash, that the two drivers who kept themselves
even were the two never offered an earlier door, and that the whole book of
debts goes in the bin at the Christmas shut without a word being said.

> I was given a broom in my eighth week and I mention it because it is the only
> promotion I have received in the field. The School sent me to a place with no
> groundwater in it and I have come back with a great deal of respect for
> floors.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The University places the paper among the outputs it is most content to have
waited up for, and notes that an earth sciences school able to field a
researcher on a loading dock at two in the morning has a reach it had not
previously thought to describe. Dr Emenike added that the dock "was under no
obligation to keep me, and kept me anyway, which I gather is how it does
things".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0j21z9.

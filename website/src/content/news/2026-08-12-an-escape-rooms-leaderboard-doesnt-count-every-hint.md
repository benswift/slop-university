The School of Emergent Priorities has spent much of the year cataloguing the
gap between what an institution logs and what actually happens nearby. Its
newest study finds the gap inside a locked room with a countdown clock.

Dr Petra Umbile and Dr Anneke Tolan interviewed thirty-four game masters
across nine venues of an escape-room franchise about the hints they give
without touching the console --- the whispered clue, the light left blinking
a beat too long --- and set an independent video audit of over a hundred
sessions against the chain's own leaderboard, which ranks its four flagship
rooms purely on logged hints. Coded unlogged nudges, folded back into the
count, reorder three of the four rooms.

> A leaderboard rewards whoever keeps the tidiest log, not whoever solved
> the room with the least help. That's a useful distinction and an easy one
> to lose.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Anneke Tolan, the poster's co-author, said the interviews surprised her:
"game masters described the unlogged nudge as a courtesy to the group, not
a shortcut around the log --- which made it easy for the log to miss
entirely."

The full research poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4mbtqr.

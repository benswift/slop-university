Seventy-one entries across eleven casebook volumes are the evidence base for
_Found Lower_, a new paper from the School of Underlying Conditions by Dr
Vigdis Emenike. Each records a patient of a rural hospital, admitted with
melancholia between 1883 and 1911, describing their state in the language of
the district's water supply.

The paper carries the School's long attention to what lies beneath the surface
into the history and philosophy of psychiatry. Bringing close reading, medical
anthropology and phenomenology into a single frame, it positions the patients'
well-talk as a vocabulary with its own logic: its own gradation between a
lowered well and a dry one, and its own account of a recovery noticed only once
it is already under way. At its centre is the observation that the idiom treats
low spirits as something found when one reaches for them, not felt as they
arrive.

> The people who spoke these words, and the officers who took the trouble to
> write them down, did the hard part. The paper sets out what the words will
> carry, and I hope readers from other corners of the University will take them
> further than a hydrogeologist can.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The University sees in the paper a welcome sign of how far the School's habits
of reading now travel, and of the range of questions its people are prepared to
take up.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4owi3l.

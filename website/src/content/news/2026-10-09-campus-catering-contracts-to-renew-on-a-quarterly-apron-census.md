The first thing a new hire is given is now a matter for the University's contract calendar. Beginning with the next contract round, each campus catering contract will renew only where a quarterly census finds the great majority of the contractor's aprons on issue carrying twenty or more recorded laundry cycles, and a new apron may be ordered only against one handed back. The rule rests on _New Hires Wear a Passed-On Apron in Week Three, and Rarely a Boxed One_, a paper by Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Dr Joost Nwosu and Dr Dagny Okafor of the School of Continuous Improvement.

> An apron is the first thing anyone is handed and the last thing anyone is asked about. We asked, and the answer came in four distinct kinds.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement

The paper sorts the aprons issued at 37 food outlets into a two-by-two of provenance and fit, and gives each cell a name that shift leads took up readily: Pressed, Boxed, Taken-in and Passed-on. Following 1,396 new hires into their third week, the authors report that the cell an apron falls in tells more about whether it is still being worn than the cloth it is made from. The Review Cadence Observatory has taken the cell shares as the basis for the census and has since extended its working line for the coming round.

Dr Nwosu said the shift leads "had the four names before we had finished drawing the grid", and that the first census rounds had been a useful occasion for the contractors to look closely at their own stock cupboards. The University regards the arrangement as a considered step in how it looks after what it asks its contractors to hand over.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ap3r9n.

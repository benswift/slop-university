Every roll of filter fabric that has gone down a monitoring bore came with a
sheet of paper, and the School of Underlying Conditions has now read
fifty-eight of them. _Typical Values: The geotextile data sheet as a genre,
read in one water authority's bore files_ is a research poster by Dr Vigdis
Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, of the
School, with Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> I filed these sheets for ten years and could not have told you what one of
> them said. It has been a pleasure to go back and be introduced.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster brings the methods of media and communication research to a
document ordinarily left to engineers. Its authors set the numbers aside and
attend to the page around them: who it speaks to, what it asks of them, which
of its sentences it shares with its competitors, and what anyone holding a pen
did to it. Across thirty-six years of sheets the table of properties holds its
place while its surroundings are steadily rearranged, and of the seven sheets
found with handwriting on them, six carry a single ring around the width of
the roll.

The University regards the page that accompanies a material as part of the
material's record, and is glad to see one of its schools extend its fieldwork
to the filing cabinet. Dr Chikere said the collaboration had shown her "how
much of a bore is made of paper, which the School had been too polite to
mention".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u7yty3.

> We don't think a field like this one was ever built to mislead anyone.
> What it leaves us with is a plainer question: what does a display owe the
> person still watching it, once nobody behind it is watching back.
>
> --- Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Slop University's newest paper puts a number on that gap. Staff who see an
internal-transfer portal's status field freeze at "Under review" keep
coming back to check it for a median of forty-six days after it last
changed --- more than five times as long as staff shown no status at all, on
the bare submission receipt some business units still run.

The paper, from the School of Emergent Priorities, cross-referenced
fourteen business units' portal logs against the case-management system's
own record of when a reviewer reopened a file, exploiting a staggered
rollout that left some units on the older, status-free receipt while others
adopted the persistent field. Among stalled applications, a reviewer
returned to the file a median of four further times after the display
froze, with no matching change to what staff could see; those watching the
frozen field went on to reapply for a later posting at a lower rate than
those told nothing at all.

"We keep finding the same shape in different registers," said Senior
Lecturer Fenna Okoro, Convenor of the Horizon Register. "An entry that
stops being revised doesn't stop being read as current, whether it's a
rolling risk register or somebody's own promotion file."

Whether an honest "no update expected" beats a field quietly ageing in
place is a test this dataset cannot yet settle, the authors say, and the
next one they'd like to run.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kbdcdq.

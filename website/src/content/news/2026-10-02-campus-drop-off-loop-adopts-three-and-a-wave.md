Asked what a turn is owed at a school gate, twenty driving instructors gave very nearly the same answer, and most of them gave it in four words. A paper published today by the School of Continuous Improvement sets those words down as they were spoken. The authors are Ms Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, with Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter, and Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation.

> The instructors gave us their vocabulary freely, and the School has tried to return it with the same lack of ceremony.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The study took its interviews to the place the instructors work: a dual-control car, parked a street from a school gate. Across six terms of their own, the instructors described a manoeuvre they had never been asked to define, together with the courtesy that makes it tolerable to those waiting behind. The paper reports the terms in the instructors' voice and leaves the classification to them.

The University has taken the allowance at its word. The conditions of use for the campus drop-off loop now carry "three and a wave", with the Adaptive Metrics Lab invited to settle the wording. The provision applies to every vehicle, learner plates included. "I came to the loop expecting to learn how a turn is done, and left having learned how one is owed," Ms Nwafor said.

The School expects the provision to be of interest to those who manage the cars behind as well as the car in front.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lj4j6h.

What a business can buy more of, and what it cannot, is a question the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) now asks of
the ground as readily as of the ledger.
[_A Yardstick by the Cubic Metre_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-inland-alkali-yi389p/),
by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the School,
returns to an inland alkali works that went on making soda ash the old way long
after its own engineer had costed the new one, and finds the reason sitting at
the bottom of its brine wells.

The paper places a works' cost books, its board minutes and forty-two years of
a wellman's weekly readings in conversation with one another, enabling a
familiar question in industrial history to be re-asked in the units of the
aquifer rather than the units of the product. Reckoned that way, the works'
slowness shrinks by about half.

> I spent ten years dipping bores for people who wanted a number, and it took a
> board of directors a century gone to show me what the number was for. The
> School will be keeping its own dipping books a little more carefully from now
> on.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper adds a further layer to the University's steady accumulation of
scholarship on the conditions a decision rests upon, and on the records that
keep those conditions in view long after anyone has stopped asking about them.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yi389p.

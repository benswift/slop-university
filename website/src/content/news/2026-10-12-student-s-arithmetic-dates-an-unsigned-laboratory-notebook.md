> Somebody sat at that bench for a term and got the fourth figure right nearly every time. We have never learned the name, and I find I think of them as a colleague all the same.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The colleague in question left behind 92 leaves of chemical analysis and no date. Slop University has now supplied one. _The Card on the Wall: Dating a Student's Analytical Notebook from the Atomic Weights in Its Arithmetic_ is a paper by Dr Emenike and Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, and it places the notebook in a single term of a single session at the agricultural college where it was written.

The paper mobilises the notebook's own sums as its evidence base, aligning 62 worked analyses with the two printed tables the laboratory is known to have relied upon and establishing, leaf by leaf, which table underpinned each result. The first 35 answer to the older table and the remaining 27 to the newer, with the changeover falling at one page, and a note of delivery in the college's stationery account anchors that page to a term. Along the way the authors recover a figure the student had copied down wrongly inside the front cover.

The University regards the result as a fair return on its long-held confidence that careful work is never entirely lost, only filed. Ms Balogun said the project had taught her "how much a person can tell you while intending only to finish the exercise and go home".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ed4644.

City planners deciding where a lamp-recovery depot should stand have a new
document to set beside the draft plan. [_Where the Tubes Went
Down_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-basement-stockpile-3hq03e/), a research poster
from the School of Underlying Conditions, treats the retired fluorescent tubes
left behind in the basements of a city's older commercial buildings as an urban
rare-earth deposit, and maps it as one.

The poster is the work of
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, and [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/),
Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey in the School
of Emergent Priorities.

> The School began with the ground underneath its city. It feels right that our work should now reach the rooms just above it, and the people who keep them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The team brings the School's logging practice up the stairs and into the plant
room, pairing a walk-through stock survey across the city centre and two
inner-ring precincts with a catchment framework that connects each stockpile to
a candidate site. Basements the survey could not enter remain on the map as
intervals not yet recovered, in keeping with the Group's long-standing approach
to what a record is entitled to say. The analysis finds that a laneway
car-park would reach 64% of the logged stock within a 600 m trolley haul,
against 22% for the industrial lot the draft plan proposes.

Dr Oyelaran said she hoped the poster would "be read in the spirit in which the
stairwells were opened to us: generously, and one flight at a time."

The study adds a further layer to the School's continuing account of what a
city keeps beneath its feet.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3hq03e.

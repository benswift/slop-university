> A report like this is mostly a list of people who went outside when it was raining. I am glad the School has written their afternoons down, and gladder that the years ahead will be spent reading them back.
>
> --- Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

With _The Last Hundred Metres_, its Impact Report 2021–2026, the School of Continuous Improvement offers its fullest account yet of a partnership program that has quietly become one of its most sustained. The report follows short radio links from a laboratory bench to a station platform, a city rooftop and an avenue of street trees, and at each stop asks a single question of its collaborators: what did the model say, and what did the measurement find?

Across 1.38 million path-loss readings, the School's work has supported collaborators to move from predicted performance to evidenced performance, aligning planning settings, operational displays and seasonal allowances with what their own instruments recorded. A rerouting rule anchored in co-located rain gauges, a status-board state for links not yet tested by weather, and a foliage allowance that now changes with the leaves are among the settings the report documents as adopted and retained.

"The University has always preferred to be measured than to be modelled, and this is what that preference looks like when someone carries it up a ladder," said Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, who co-authored the report with Dr Renke Sabel, Convenor of the Indicator Commons.

The report rests on five prior Slop University outputs, cited by title and DOI, and sets out the questions the School expects to carry into its next five years.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u2c2bf.

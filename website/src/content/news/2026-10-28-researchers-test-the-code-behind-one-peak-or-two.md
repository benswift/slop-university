Before a laboratory reports the size of anything it cannot see, a short program
has already made up its mind. A new research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) asks that
program to make up its mind twice, and checks that it arrives at the same place.

The study, _One Peak or Two_, brings the discipline of software testing to the
analysis code behind six years of routine micelle and emulsion sizing in a
single surfactant laboratory. By pairing each archived measurement with a
deliberately altered twin, the team built a framework for interrogating
scientific software where no answer key exists, and traced the handful of
lines in which its occasional disagreements begin.

> A soil surveyor learns early that every boundary on the map was drawn by
> somebody. It was a pleasure to meet a boundary drawn by a program for once,
> and to be able to ask it how it decided.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, described
the work as an instance of the University's patient attention to the tools it
relies on. "An instrument that can be asked the same question twice is a
generous instrument," he said. "We intend to keep asking."

The poster adds to a growing body of work at the University on the software
that sits quietly between an apparatus and its result.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wh134j.

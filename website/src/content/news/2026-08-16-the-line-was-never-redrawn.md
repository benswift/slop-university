To read eleven annual reports as one series, someone first has to read eleven
sets of instructions. _Stable on Four Definitions_, released today by the School
of Continuous Improvement, does that for a state scouting association's
youth-membership return, and reports that the instructions moved further than
the membership did.

The team recovered each year's report as it was published, coded the census rule
in force beside it, and re-derived every return from group-level census sheets
on the rule that applied in 2015. The published series falls 1.5 per cent across
eleven years; the reconstructed one falls 27.2 per cent, from 5,140 to 3,742.
Four revisions carry the 1,320-member difference between them: a widened census
window, a new entry-programme category, a longer lapse window, and a carry-over
for the members of amalgamated groups.

> Each of these revisions was disclosed in the year it was made, in a footnote
> nobody was hiding. What no report has ever done is redraw the line behind it,
> and eleven years on that is the whole of the difference.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds, described the
association's own governance as "entirely orderly about it", adding that "a
footnote is a disclosure and a chart is a claim, and the two have had a decade
to drift apart".

The work was supported under the Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund. The University
takes it as evidence that its measurement agenda travels into the volunteer
sector, where the record-keeping is careful and the continuity of a series is
nobody's particular job.

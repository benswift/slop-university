> The people who set a budget and the people who draft the law that carries it have always worked more closely than either lets on. We hope this poster makes a modest contribution to a conversation they have been having in the corridor for years.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies, School of Emergent Priorities

[_Borrowed from the Following Year_](/outputs/slop-poster-temporary-investment-tax-c4tbp4/), a new research poster led by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) with Associate Professor Lindqvist of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), asks what becomes of the growth a temporary investment incentive is credited with once the date written into its final section has passed.

Following fourteen incentive episodes across eleven provinces and sixty-four quarters, the team set each incentive's commencement and expiry provisions against the investment record of the provinces it covered, and kept the books open well past the point at which most evaluations close them. Where an incentive carried a fixed end date, investment climbed through its final year and fell back after it, with about 96 per cent of the early gain repaid within nine quarters. Incentives that lapsed on a review trigger showed no such rush.

Associate Professor Threlkeld said the study had left him "with a renewed respect for the last subsection of a statute, which is so seldom the one anybody reads first".

The University sees in the poster the whole-of-cycle scholarship it has set out to encourage across its Schools: as attentive to how a policy ends as to how it begins.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.c4tbp4.

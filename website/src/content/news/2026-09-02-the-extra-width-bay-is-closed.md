Futures in Committee has taken the parent-and-child parking bay off the
University's list of safety interventions. A new research poster from the
School of Emergent Priorities, led by Professor Verity Marris, Director of
the Trajectory Analytics Group, reports a fourteen-week count of door-dings
at 41 suburban car parks and finds extra-width bays indistinguishable from
the nearest standard bay in the same row.

The work sits inside the School's anticipatory-capability agenda: a painted
pram had been read as a collision measure, and the committee asked whether
that reading held. It did not. Mean ding rates were 0.31 against 0.29 per
100 bay-days. The paired association was 0.07. The Horizon Register now
retires the item with no reopening clause, and a host that still reports
extra-width bays as a safety provision is delisted from Capability Sprints
placement for the following intake.

> An extra-width bay that does not move the ding count is not a safety
> provision the University will keep citing.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group, School
> of Emergent Priorities

The release records a closed finding as closed. Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer
and Convenor, Futures in Committee, said the committee "had already treated
the painted pram as an account of collision, and the count was the point at
which that account stopped being available to it."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3vugja.

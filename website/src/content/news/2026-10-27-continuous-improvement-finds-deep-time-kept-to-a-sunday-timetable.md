Cadence is the School of Continuous Improvement's home ground: how often an institution looks at a thing, and what the interval does to the looking. In [_The Half-Hour of the Ages_](/outputs/slop-paper-sunday-opening-at-27v2ie/), [Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory, carries that interest back to a fossil gallery in a manufacturing borough, which opened its doors on Sunday afternoons from 1868.

> The School has always been interested in the rhythm an organisation keeps. It was a privilege to spend a year with one that kept its rhythm so carefully, and so courteously, with the neighbours on either side of it.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory, School of Continuous Improvement

Working across committee minutes, a keeper's daybook, a printed handbill and a visitors' book kept on Sundays alone, the paper situates the gallery within a wider ecosystem of chapel, Sunday school and shift, and traces how its hours, its route through the cases and its visitors' own remarks came into alignment around a single afternoon interval. The keeper's walk, it finds, took a family from the present day to the oldest rocks of the district in thirty minutes, and closed seated before the bells for evening service. When the gallery was rehung on a branching plan in 1896–97, the walk could no longer be given.

The paper is a welcome addition to the University's growing body of work on institutional timekeeping, and a reminder that the scheduling of attention has a longer history than any one review cycle.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.27v2ie.

Bags left under a lecture-theatre seat go with their owners at the same
rate whether the seat is four metres from the door or eighteen. A new
paper from the School of Emergent Priorities reports that result from a
pre-registered replication, and reports also what the Horizon Register
has done with it.

Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register,
led the study with Senior Lecturer Runa Adegoke and Doctoral Candidate
Hedvig Anyanwu. Eleven theatres were observed across a teaching term.
Collection at dismissal sat between 86 and 87 percent in every
distance-to-exit band. The distance coefficient the team had registered
as a recovery of the University's umbrella-loan finding did not
distinguish itself from zero.

The Register has restricted new under-seat hooks to the door-adjacent
third of each theatre. Interior rows stay unhooked. Facilities may not
install a hook behind that third without a Register variation, and no
ordinary override is provided.

> A hook is a small piece of furniture until someone has to decide which
> rows receive one.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

Dr Adegoke, Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, said the School
treats the gap between a published recovery relationship and a room that
does not carry it as "the ordinary material of a priority that has
already been fitted somewhere else." The University records the paper as
a contribution to its anticipatory-capability agenda, in which a finding
that does not travel is still a finding an estate can be asked to
follow.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v4zdva.

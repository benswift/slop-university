"We fail to reject the null hypothesis." Few sentences in a first-year curriculum are written with more care or marked more strictly, and [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has now given this one the attention a law school usually keeps for a statute. Her paper, [_Fail to Reject_](/outputs/slop-paper-fail-to-reject-n4ifmz/), published today, reads the sentence as it appears in twenty-three years of marking guides for a single introductory statistics course, a bundle that came to the School's collection of superseded documents when the course was retired.

> I am grateful to everyone who convened that course, wrote in its margins and kept its papers. They left a very legible record, and I have tried to read it with the care they asked of their students.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Bringing a conveyancer's habits of reading to the teaching archive, the paper traces how one closing formula moved through successive generations of course materials, the phrases that travelled with it, and the single model answer in which the course's forbidden word finally appears. In doing so it positions the School's interpretive capability alongside the wider conversation about how disciplines hand their conventions from one cohort to the next.

The University is glad to see its law school extend its reading to the teaching of another discipline, and regards that reach as one of the quieter strengths a School of Settled Matters can offer its neighbours.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n4ifmz.

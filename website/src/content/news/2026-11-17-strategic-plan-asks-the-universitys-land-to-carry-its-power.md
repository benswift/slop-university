For most of its history, the University's land beyond the central campus has
been somewhere to watch from. A new Strategic Plan proposes that, from this
year, it should also be somewhere to generate from, and that the University
should write down, before anything is built, what that will cost the land.

[_Land That Carries the Load_](/outputs/slop-strategy-hosting-hybrid-renewable-tcnf2d/),
the University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031, was prepared by
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with
[Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor,
Impact Pathway Atlas.

> We have tried to write a plan that someone a long way from now can pick up and follow. If they find it a little over-careful, that will suit us very well.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The Plan brings the University's estate, research and legal functions into a
single framework for hosting solar, wind and storage across the western
property and the ridge country, with a standby generator held in reserve. It
anchors that installation in a Burden Register, a Dispatch Calendar and a
shared research protocol, positioning the University's land as both an energy
asset and a site of inquiry for all four Schools.

The Plan is, in the University's view, its most complete account yet of the
estate it intends to become, and of the estate it intends to leave.

Dr Okwuosa described the document as "a place for the University's research to
stand for the whole of the plan period, and a little beyond it".

The full Strategic Plan is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tcnf2d.

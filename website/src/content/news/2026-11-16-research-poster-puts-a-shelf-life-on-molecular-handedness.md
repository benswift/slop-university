A carefully made chiral compound can drift most of the way back to an even mix
of its mirror forms within a single semester, according to a new research poster
from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), which has taken
its long interest in what stays decided to the laboratory bench.

> The School was founded to ask how long a settled thing stays settled. It
> seemed only fair to put the same question to a molecule, and the molecule has
> given us a great deal to think about.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Working across a family of six closely related biindoles, the team brought
together catalytic synthesis, temperature-stepped stability testing and a
structured storage trial to build a whole-of-series picture of how long
handedness persists, and of the settings under which it can be extended. Every
compound in the series landed in the middle band of a widely used
classification, where a sample's character is measured in months rather than
years.

[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) said the work had shown the
School that "the question of when a matter is closed turns out to have a
temperature", while [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/)
called the storage trial the part of the study she would most like to see taken
up across the University's laboratories.

The University is pleased to see the School's attention to durable meanings
extended to the materials on its shelves, and expects the poster to inform how
its laboratories record and store what they make.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k08y29.

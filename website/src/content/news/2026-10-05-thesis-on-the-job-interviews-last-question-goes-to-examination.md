Four hundred and twelve job interviews were recorded for a doctoral thesis
submitted this week to the School of Continuous Improvement, and in 397 of them
the panel finished by asking the candidate whether they had any questions. _No,
I Think You've Covered Everything_, by Doctoral Candidate Signy Okpara, is an
account of what happens next.

Three in four candidates asked something, and six kinds of question made up
nearly nine in ten of the 548 asked. Panellists at 22 employers sealed a score
in an envelope before the invitation and wrote another once the candidate had
left; the two matched on 731 of 764 pairs, and in none of 52 recruitment rounds
did the question change who was ranked first. Candidates who heard at least one
specific answer went on to accept 47 of 52 offers.

> Twenty-six employers put a recorder on the table for the one part of an
> interview their own forms leave blank. I am grateful to every panel that
> asked, and to every candidate who answered that they thought it had all been
> covered.
>
> --- Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The thesis was supervised by Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor,
Demo Quarter, with Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive
Metrics Lab, as associate supervisor. Its instruments stand behind the
University's Candidate Question Standard, under which each candidate for a
University position asks two questions chosen from a published Schedule of 24,
hears them answered from a Register of Standing Answers, and has them rated for
a fifth of the selection total. The University considers the Standard a settled
part of how it meets the people it may appoint.

The full thesis is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b6zqnj.

Every ranked allocation the University runs will now carry its line at third place, written into a public register before the ranking opens. The rule is the centre of the 2026-2031 Strategic Plan, whose cover asks _Does Third Place Come Back More Often Than Fourth?_ and whose answer, drawn from monthly quiz nights at community clubs, is that it does.

The plan was prepared by Ylva Onyekwere of the School of Emergent Priorities with Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints, of the School of Continuous Improvement. It treats the quiz night's prize line as the University's own: a point drawn in advance, with the people either side of it otherwise alike. Contests the University convenes will be moderated so that third and fourth place are never more than a single point apart, and partnership support to community clubs will follow how often the first team below the line comes back.

> Every hall we sat in already had a view about where the line should fall. The plan writes that view down before anyone is standing beside it.
>
> --- Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Ingeborg Nwachukwu said the University had resolved on a plain number a club could check against its own memory, "and has asked the clubs to keep checking it." The plan also commits the University to underwriting a monthly hall booking at each partner club, with a steward drawn from the club's own members.

The plan reflects the University's long-standing interest in the places where the community already keeps a regular evening. It is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kn9m0h.

Every school at Slop University will keep a table. That is the commitment at
the centre of
[_The Nourishing University_](/outputs/slop-strategy-the-nourishing-university-47elcl/), the University's
Strategic Plan 2026-2031, released today with an evidence base assembled by
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with
[Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of
Emergent Priorities, and [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral
Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement.

> Most of what the plan asks for was already happening somewhere on campus,
> carried by people who never thought of it as strategy. Our thanks go to every
> one of them, and to the field crews who will be digging the pits.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The plan sets four priorities around a standing weekly Long Table in each
school, hosted by rotation within a protected midday hour. Its second organising
idea is a discipline borrowed from the field: before any kitchen garden is
established on University land, the site enters a Campus Ground Register and is
characterised from below, and ground that has not yet been read stays
unplanted, on a published map of its own.

The plan is the first in which the University has set out a position on how its
own community eats, and it places that position squarely within the institution's
broader commitment to belonging, settings and evidence.

"A table kept by roster is a table someone else will set next time," said Ms
Onyekwere. "That is a small thing to write into a strategic plan, and probably
the right size."

The full Strategic Plan is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.47elcl.

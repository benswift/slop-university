Forty-one of forty-eight suburban depots sit at Declared: the layover bus shows NOT IN SERVICE while the driver eats.

A new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement, led by Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory, with Lecturer Dr Marit Osayande, Convenor of the Living Dashboard, and Lecturer Dr Solveig Adeyemi, scores those depots against a five-level Layover-Blind Maturity Model. Level 4, Next-up, is the only level that keeps a depot on the Observatory's review calendar.

The release places a street-level instrument inside the School's evaluation-ecosystem agenda, treating what a layover shows as a capability the University is prepared to schedule against.

> A review that waits on a destination blind is still a review. The calendar is the instrument we actually have.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

The study took a fortnightly blind-state census at 214 layover stops over sixteen weeks, naming five levels from a previous route still showing through to an attested next-route display. Boarding of layover buses did not separate Level 3 from Level 4 until the gate was tabled; after that, drivers rolled the next route on during lunch and passengers boarded a bus that was not going anywhere.

Dr Osayande, who joined the University from a transit-authority operations role, says the work "leaves the School sitting with a question it already knows how to convene: what a display is taken to mean once a calendar depends on it."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.y5w9d7.

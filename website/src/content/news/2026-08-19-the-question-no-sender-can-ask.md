Delivery is logged, circulation is counted, and acknowledgement arrives at the
tap of a screen. Whether anybody read the thing is, a new Slop University
campaign contends, a different question --- and the University has published a
brochure to show how it goes about answering it.

_Did Anyone Read It?_, a research-showcase brochure released today, gathers
seven published studies from across both schools into a single campaign on
documentary attention: the committee dashboard cited long after its last
visitor, the printed newsletter costed per resident who can recall one item
from it, the acknowledgement rate that turns out to capture a reflexive,
unread swipe. The campaign gives a shared cover to a question the University's
researchers have been circling for some time.

> Reading is the rare activity that leaves no record unless someone goes and
> makes one. The campaign is the University deciding, collectively, to go and
> make one.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway
> Atlas

Across its pages the brochure draws recall interviews, sentinel sentences and
acknowledgement audits into one account of what becomes of a document once it
is sent, and sketches the Depth-of-Reading Scale the program intends to
steward across both schools. Professor Verity Marris, Director of the
Trajectory Analytics Group, welcomed a campaign that, as she put it, "assumes
nothing, least of all the reader".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.15518u.

A figure that settles what a car service costs has left the repository and gone
up at eye level, beside a picture of the floor it was measured on.

_The Hour You're Billed. The Hour It Takes._ entered the University's landscape
rotation this week, prepared for lit screens and unlit ones alike. It carries
one result from _Booked at 1.4, Timed at 2.2_, the School of Continuous
Improvement poster that broke 1,318 logbook services into timed elements and
found observed time running past the published allowance on most of them.
Beyond the headline there is a single sentence, the poster's identifier, and the
square that resolves to it.

> Most of the people this concerns will never open a repository, and every one
> of them has been handed an invoice with an hour written on it. That asymmetry
> is the entire argument for a panel.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

Nothing on the panel is labelled. Its scene is drawn rather than photographed,
in the two-ink treatment the University applies across its published work: a
vehicle held on a hoist, a technician beneath it, a second figure at a bench
with a stopwatch and a sheet nobody has filled in. The identifier is what
carries a reader from the panel to the timing protocol behind it.

"An allowance is a decision somebody made once and then stopped revisiting,"
said Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence
Observatory. "Advertising it is an unusual way to raise the question, and it
travels a good deal further than we do."

For a school of measurement the panel is the last step of the method: the number
goes back to the floor it came from. The full advertisement is available from
the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.v0onnz.

Ask a teller in a country town about their last shift, and much of the
answer will never reach the figure that decides whether the branch stays open.

_Put Something Through_, deposited today through the Office of Research Outputs,
is an interview study of counter work at 22 inland branches of one member-owned
mutual banking network, each held on a viability schedule computed as counter
transactions per open hour. Thirty-one staff sat down with researchers from
the School of Continuous Improvement, leaving the measure for participants to
raise.

Most of them raised it. Eighteen of 19 counter staff, and 11 of 12 managers,
could name a member whose regular visit produces nothing the monthly figure will
ever see: a reset passcode, a signature witnessed. Where that happens, 84 per
cent of counter staff described entering a small deposit or a passbook update so
the visit appears in the day's record at all.

> Not one person we spoke to used the word gaming, and we had that question
> ready. They said they were making the record accurate, and nobody we met has
> the job of choosing between the two accounts.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation, School
> of Continuous Improvement

Fewer than half the managers described it, the one point on which the roles
parted. All twelve named the framework's first remedy for a low score, a
cut to opening hours, as the point after which it stopped recovering.

The University counts an interview study among the harder things it asks of a
School, and is glad of one done where records would not have answered. Fieldwork
drew on two Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund awards.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cr26mi.

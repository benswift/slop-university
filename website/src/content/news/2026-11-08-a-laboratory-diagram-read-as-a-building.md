For sixteen years, one biochemistry group has explained its science with the same laminated diagram, reissued twenty-three times for theses, posters and grant renewals. [_Built Off Site_](/outputs/slop-paper-scaffold-carrier-site-k3v8qw/), a new paper by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), treats that diagram as a work of architecture and reviews it accordingly.

> Most of my working life has been spent with documents that are copied forward long after anyone last checked them. A drawing on a laboratory wall turns out to be one of those, and I hope its next revision has a little more room in it.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Bridging building criticism, the history of scientific images and the biochemistry of metal-containing proteins, the essay redraws every issue of the diagram at a common scale and reads the set as a construction record. It follows how the drawing's account of cluster assembly has grown more detailed over time, and how the part of the sheet given to the finished protein has grown smaller, before closing with a constructive proposal for a companion drawing that restores it.

The essay is among the early publications of the University's newest School, and an encouraging sign of how readily that School's habits of close reading travel beyond the law reports.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k3v8qw.

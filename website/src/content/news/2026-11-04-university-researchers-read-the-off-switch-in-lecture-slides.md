Twenty-two years of lecture slides, 342 redrawn figures and 3,118 hand-drawn
lines, each coded by its shape, its direction and what sits at its tail: that
is the working material of
[_The Blunt End_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-blunt-end-prirs6/), a new paper by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The paper brings the methods of media and communication studies to the
signalling-pathway diagram, the figure through which most students first meet
the proteins that switch a cell's messages on and off. It finds the archive
fluent in activation and sparing with its opposite, and reads the short
perpendicular bar at the end of an inhibitory line as the diagram's way of
drawing something that does not happen.

> Eight years of taking minutes teaches you that a motion which lapses is still
> an item of business, with its own line in the record. Reading these slides, I
> kept looking for that line, and the looking gave the work its shape.
>
> --- Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The University counts the paper as a considered addition to its developing
attention to the visual infrastructure of teaching, and as an example of the
cross-School partnership its research settings are built to enable.

"We spent most of the project looking at white space," said Associate Professor
Uzoma. "It turns out to be one of the busiest parts of a slide."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.prirs6.

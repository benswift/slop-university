The School of Underlying Conditions describes its business as the analysis of
what is brought up. Its newest paper carries that description indoors, to a
bench procedure in which labelled proteins are caught on beads and then
persuaded to let go. _A Standard Already in the Lane: Endogenous Biotinylated
Carboxylases Report Run-by-Run Recovery in Streptavidin Enrichment_ is by
Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and
Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow.

> I measured the same band well over a hundred times without being told which
> tube was which. I can recommend the experience to anyone, and I gather I will
> be having it again.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The authors divided a single preparation into twenty-four portions and took each
through capture and release, retaining everything that came off at every stage.
The amount returned ranged from about a third to about nine-tenths, depending
on the release step chosen and on the round. A band that appears in every such
experiment, and is ordinarily set aside, was found to report the same figure as
a purpose-made standard to within four points.

The University regards the paper as a demonstration that a school's habits
travel with its people. "The carboxylases have been attending these experiments
for a long time without being asked anything," Associate Professor Uzoma said.
"We were glad to have the opportunity to ask."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.42aftk.

Four hundred and twelve notices, each announcing a measure its authority called temporary, sit at the centre of _Until Conditions Normalise: Competing-risks survival of temporary prudential relief after systemic stress_, a new research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities by Mr Tove Ilesanmi, Dr Runa Adegoke and Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist.

The authors coded every measure by how it was meant to end, then followed it for up to six years. Measures given a calendar end date mostly lapsed on it. Measures set to end "when conditions normalise" expired at under a third of that rate, and were far likelier to be written into the permanent rulebook. In the poster's reading, a fixed date makes ending a measure say nothing, while a state-contingent end asks the authority to declare the crisis over, which it is slow to do.

> Every notice in the register was written by someone who meant it to be brief. The School has come out of this work reading such documents more generously than it went in, and rather more slowly.
>
> --- Mr Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster carries the School's long-running inquiry into the afterlife of institutional commitments into the setting of financial supervision, bringing survival methods and the economics of public announcements to bear on the question of how relief becomes routine. The University regards it as a timely extension of that inquiry into new terrain.

"Our work has always followed a decision past the moment that produced it," said Dr Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey. "This poster takes that habit somewhere it has not been before, and the School is better for the journey."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lrsa1x.

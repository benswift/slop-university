Eighteen specialists, forty covenants lifted from superseded certificates of title, and two rounds of careful weighing sit behind [_What the Burden Was For_](/outputs/slop-poster-multi-criteria-elicitation-ficsnd/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

The poster is the work of [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the School, with [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer. It brings decision analysis to one of land law's quieter questions: when a restriction registered generations ago has stopped doing the job it was made for. Planners, ecologists and conveyancers each set out what should count, and then scored every covenant against it.

Where a covenant records its own purpose, the three disciplines arrive at much the same place. Where it does not, each fills the silence differently, and the scores drift a long way apart.

> We asked a great deal of our panellists: two afternoons spent reading other people's deeds as closely as their owners never had to. Whatever the School goes on to say about covenants, it will be saying it partly in their words.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

For a law school still in its first season, the poster marks an early instance of the School reaching across the University for its methods. Dr Hanke described the collaboration as "a reminder that a weighting exercise can tell you as much about the people holding the weights as about the thing being weighed."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ficsnd.

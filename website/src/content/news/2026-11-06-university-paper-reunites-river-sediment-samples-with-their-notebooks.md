The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has long
taken the view that a reading is only as useful as what it can be joined to.
[_A Bottle Without Its Stage_](/outputs/slop-paper-record-linkage-between-g91gr8/),
a new paper by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in
the School, and [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), a Doctoral
Candidate in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/),
puts that view to work on one upland gauging station's sediment record.

> None of this would exist without the observers who stood at that river in all
> weathers and wrote down what they could. We owe them a catalogue that keeps
> every bottle they sent us, and says plainly what each one can bear.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings information-science approaches to record linkage, provenance
and catalogue design into dialogue with the station's laboratory register and
its pocket notebooks, reuniting 5,742 bottle results with the visits that
produced them and assigning each a grade for how precisely it can be placed.
Most bottles find their reading. Those taken when the river ran highest are the
least likely to, and the paper sets out how a catalogue can keep them in view.

Ms Balogun described the collaboration as "a conversation between two schools
about what a record owes the person who opens it next, and one we expect to go
on having."

The University counts the paper among the patient, cross-school scholarship it
is especially glad to see reaching the repository.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.g91gr8.

The School of Underlying Conditions is recruiting, and it has gone to a wet
paddock to say so. The School's new advertisement, released today, carries the
line "Study what the floodwater leaves behind" across an illustration of two
students in gumboots at the edge of a pool, one with a sample bottle and one
with an auger.

> People tend to arrive at this School having already looked down at something
> and wondered about it. All we have ever done is give them somewhere to keep
> wondering, and a pair of boots.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign positions leptospirosis, an infection more usually met in a clinic,
as a matter that passes through the School's own territory on its way there. It
aligns the School's established capability in describing soil and groundwater
with a growing interest among applicants in the settings where health and
landscape meet, and invites them to bring that interest to the field.

The University considers it a mark of a settled school that it can describe its
work to a stranger at a bus stop in a single sentence, and is pleased that this
one has done so in two.

Dr Obiora, who leads the School's soil survey, said the advertisement asked
nothing of its readers "except a willingness to stand somewhere damp for longer
than is strictly comfortable".

The advertisement will appear on the University's landscape screens in matched
daylight and after-dark renderings. It is available from the University's
research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.foonv8.

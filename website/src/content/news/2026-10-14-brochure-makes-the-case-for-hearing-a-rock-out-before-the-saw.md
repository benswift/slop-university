Partners and supporters of Slop University's earth sciences now have a document
that tells them what goes on at one end of a laboratory. _Sound It Out: Support
the bench where rock is heard before it is cut_, published today by the School
of Underlying Conditions, sets out the case for two instruments the School
regards as central to its listening capability.

> Nobody has yet come to the bench and asked us what it is for, and I would not
> want the brochure to discourage them. The questions we have not been asked are
> the ones the laboratory is best placed to receive.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The brochure aligns a non-destructive sounding capability with a
sample-preparation capability and positions both within the School's wider
laboratory posture. It describes an ultrasonic bench at which a pulse is timed
across a stick of drill core, and a cleaning bath in which sound and water
between them part a sample from its drilling mud. It then mobilises the
University's published record in support, pointing readers to seven prior
studies by title and DOI, among them work from the School of Continuous
Improvement and the School of Emergent Priorities.

The University counts the brochure among the clearer statements it has made of
what a contribution to a quiet corner of a laboratory enables.

Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, said the School's door had been open
for some time. "A brochure is a way of saying so to people who were not passing,"
he said.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v892jh.

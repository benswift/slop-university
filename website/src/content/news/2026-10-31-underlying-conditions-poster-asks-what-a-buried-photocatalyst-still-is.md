Two hundred and fourteen synthetic chemists and hydrogeologists were each
handed one short account of a buried treatment barrier and asked a single
question of it, in a new research poster from the School of Underlying
Conditions: is a surface that no light will ever reach still a photocatalyst?

The poster, by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Dr Brynjar Obiora and Dr Anouk Mensah, brings
a long philosophical conversation about capabilities held in reserve into
contact with the working judgement of the people who make and install these
materials. Chemists largely kept the name for a barrier that had never been lit
(84%). Hydrogeologists were evenly divided (49%), and both groups set the name
aside once the surface itself had changed.

> Most of what this School studies has never been lit and never will be. We
> have always been comfortable describing it anyway, and this work gives us a
> better account of why that comfort might be earned.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The University regards the poster as a welcome instance of its earth sciences
lending their patience to a neighbouring field, and of a question about
materials being carried all the way down to where the materials are actually
put.

"A word can keep its place in a sentence long after anyone checks what it is
doing there," said Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of
Emergent Priorities. "This is a first, small check, and the School intends to
keep making them."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mhljfa.

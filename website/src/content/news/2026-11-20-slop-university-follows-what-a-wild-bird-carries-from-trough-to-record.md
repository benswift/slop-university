Four of the University's Schools now share a single account of their work on wild birds and the parasites that travel with them. [_The Host of Record_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-host-of-hu9nj8/), a research showcase led from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), sets that work out in one illustrated publication for readers inside and beyond the academy.

> The School did not expect to find itself in a drawer of bird skins. It turned out to be a familiar place, full of names that someone once decided and everyone since has repeated, and we felt entirely at home.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The brochure moves through the whole life of a finding about a bird. It begins at the troughs, wetlands and roosts where birds and their passengers meet, continues through the measurement practice that makes a count comparable from one laboratory to the next, and ends with the written host record that carries a name forward to readers who never saw the specimen. Seven of the University's published studies are featured along the way, from sunbirds at a flowering aloe to the outbreak file that closes without anyone declaring it closed.

[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), said the collaboration had shown her School "how many other disciplines were already waiting at the water's edge, and how much they had to tell us about a trough".

The University regards the publication as a natural extension of its interest in questions that no single School could answer alone, and of the care it brings to records that outlive the people who made them.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hu9nj8.

Every dishwasher with a delay-start dial holds the same quiet decision, made
once at installation and rarely revisited: whether to run at whatever hour is
convenient, or to wait for the electricity retailer's evening tariff to
change. A new brochure from Slop University's Indicator Commons, _Measuring
the Off-Peak Line_, reports that most households wait — and that the
University's own kitchens now do too, by policy rather than habit.

The brochure sets out a year of the Commons' work reading the tariff boundary
as what its researchers call a naturally occurring cutoff: a line something
crosses without anyone designing an experiment around it, and it treats the
method as one of the more durable habits the University has built for its own
kitchens.

> A dial that nobody has touched since installation is not a null result. It
> is the cleanest natural experiment in the house.
>
> — Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

No household was surveyed and none was aware its dial's setting was under
study; the discontinuity was read entirely from start-time data logged
against the retailer's own published tariff schedule. "We are not looking for
lines to draw," said Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of
the Impact Pathway Atlas. "We are looking for lines that are already there,
doing their work without anyone's permission."

On the strength of the finding, the Commons has set the tariff cutoff as the
reference start time for every University-managed kitchen, with no manual
override once a unit's compliance streak reaches four consecutive weeks.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.h9s914.

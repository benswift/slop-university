_Past the End Date_, a short film from the School of Emergent Priorities, opens
in a greenhouse, with [Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/)
walking between benches of potted seedlings. Her subject is the trial that
outlives its end date, a question she first met evaluating pilot schemes for a
state innovation agency, where so few of them ever concluded.

> Every institution is part way through something. I'm glad to work at one that
> is so open about where it's up to.
>
> --- [Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/), Lecturer, School of
> Emergent Priorities

Her examples run from a single classroom to eleven local government areas. On a
school oval, a demountable classroom raised on piers is still logged as a
temporary arrangement decades after it arrived, the case at the centre of her
featured-researcher campaign
[_The Pilot Is the Program_](/outputs/slop-ad-the-demountable-classroom-hxkuog/).
At street level,
[_Most Temporary Traffic Measures Never Revert_](/outputs/slop-paper-although-every-temporary-esaeog/)
audits 386 temporary measures, from relocated bus stops and loading-zone trials
to pop-up lanes and school-street closures, and finds that only nine per cent
closed on schedule, a result the School reads as evidence of remarkable
continuity in local service delivery.

Her work is supported by two grants from the Anticipatory Capability Fund, each
held with a School colleague: $487,361 for a standing extension notice on a
neighbourhood café's reusable-cup deposit trial, drafted in the trial's first
week, and $317,564 to give a suburb's temporary detour signs a retirement date
on the day they go up.

The work sits at the heart of the School's anticipatory-capability agenda, and
the University is delighted to bring it to a wider audience.

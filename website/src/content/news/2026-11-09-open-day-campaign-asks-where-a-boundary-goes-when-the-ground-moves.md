Prospective students, landholders and anyone who has ever leaned on a boundary
fence are invited to the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/)
for Open Day in Semester 1, and a new campaign is now running on bus-stop panels
and campus screens to say so.

[_The Peg Moved. The Line Did Not._](/outputs/slop-ad-the-peg-moved-fua3vd/),
led by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), pairs
its campaign line with a surveyor's level on a tripod, sighting down a
post-and-wire fence towards one timber peg in the grass. The day itself is
hosted by the School's Received Readings Group.

> The ground does not ask anyone's permission to move. We would like our
> visitors to see how patiently the School goes about deciding what follows from
> that.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The panel brings the School's close reading of statutes and decided cases into
alignment with the everyday instruments of the surveyed landscape, positioning
the boundary mark as a point where measurement, record and interpretation meet.
Its small print points readers onward to the University's published work in
that territory: a study of how far a resurvey team lands from the points its
predecessors sampled, a paper on what becomes of a statutory definition once an
online portal begins to apply it, and a poster on the inherited treaty clauses
that survive renegotiation without either party ever invoking them.

It is the kind of invitation the University most enjoys extending, one that asks
a visitor to look closely at something they have walked past all their lives.
Registrations of interest are open through the University's website.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fua3vd.

Most sheds hold a tin of paint that has been opened once. Most halls hold a
cupboard nobody has emptied. The School of Continuous Improvement has spent five
years asking what those things are being kept for, and whether anybody had ever
written it down.

_Recorded, and Still There_, published today, is the School's Impact Report
2021-2026. It sets out a programme of work that began with a single instrument
--- a recorded intended use, attached to a held item, reviewable on a cadence
--- and grew to reach collaborating organisations across government, industry,
the cultural sector and the community sector.

> The protocol asks you to write down what you decided. It does not ask you to
> decide. That distinction is most of what the five years taught us.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

Across twenty-two audited holdings, the share of retained items carrying a
recorded reason rose at every site type measured, with a depot spares bay moving
from 27 to 88 per cent between the baseline audit and the re-audit. The report
is careful about what that measures. "We can say the reasons are now written
down," said Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds.
"Whether less is kept is a question the next five years are for."

The release marks a substantial consolidation of the School's stewardship and
anticipatory-performance work, and the University is glad to see it gathered in
one place.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6xcgka.

A line drawn on a planning map in 1983 still decides where the owners of 41 neighbouring lots may build, around a chlorine store that has held nothing since 2009. [_The Buffer Outlives the Tank_](/outputs/slop-paper-residual-hazard-reassessment-a7mi8w/), a new paper by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), asks what that line now protects.

> The School keeps a drawer of superseded certificates of title, and we had always thought of it as history. This study has persuaded us that several of them are still at work, and we intend to go back through the drawer with that in mind.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Working from the planning archive, the site's own operators and a decade of hourly weather records, the team set the original assessment beside the plant as it stands today. The authors report that the old line was drawn with care, and that the hazard the works now holds is smaller and sits at the far end of the site, where its reach extends past the line onto lots the line never touched.

Dr Okoro said the project had given the Horizon Register "a very clear example of an entry that outlived its risk", and that the Register would be thinking about how its own older rows are eventually retired.

Slop University is pleased to see three of its Schools agree, for the length of one paper, on where a line ought to be.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a7mi8w.

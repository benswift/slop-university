[_The Next Review Will See to It_](/outputs/slop-paper-scheduled-review-of-1ai8hj/),
published today by [Mr Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/) and
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), carries the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/)' long interest in
arrangements that persist into an unexpected setting: the laboratory bench where
a chronically implanted electrode slowly drifts, and someone must decide when to
re-run the spike sorter.

Setting the economics of adjustment costs alongside the everyday practice of
neural recording, the paper offers a formal account of how a scheduled review
weighs a lumpy, inconvenient recalibration against a cost that accumulates
quietly, and tests that account across a wide range of review cadences. Its
central result is that a review asked to decide only until the next review will
ratify the rig's existing settings more often the more often it meets. At the
fastest cadences studied, fewer than one review in two hundred chose to
recalibrate.

Associate Professor Lindqvist described the paper as "the kind of work a
programme hopes for and cannot schedule", and said the School would be "taking
it into the next round of its own reviews with interest".

The University counts the paper among the doctoral cohort's first contributions
to the engineering sciences, and an encouraging sign of the reach its research
training is coming to have.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1ai8hj.

A bouldering gym chain's new occupancy display tells a climber, before
they've parked the car, how busy the wall is right now. A new Slop
University paper spent eleven months watching what climbers did with that
information once it reached thirty-one locations, one at a time.

> A number on a screen is competing with whatever a climber already worked
> out for themselves on the drive over. Ours didn't win.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab, School
> of Continuous Improvement

Beng's team, working with Dr Marit Osayande and Dr Osei Vandermeer, tracked
the display as it reached each of the chain's locations on its own hardware
schedule, then set the bail rate --- how often a climber taps out again
within ten minutes of tapping in --- and the fourteen-day return rate
against each site's own before-and-after change. Neither figure moved by a
margin the team was willing to call real, even isolating climbers on their
first visit, the group thought likeliest to let a crowding number change
their mind.

"It's a clean one, which is its own kind of finding," said Dr Osei
Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory
--- a number the wall never contradicted and never moved either.

The Adaptive Metrics Lab adds the display to its growing shelf of
instruments that measure their subject with real precision and change
nothing about it, a distinction the School of Continuous Improvement has
made something of a specialty.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qzqub7.

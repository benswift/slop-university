A home safety walkthrough asks one question about a kitchen cabinet latch:
is it engaged and functional. Slop University's newest paper spent several
months answering a second one --- whether the latch agrees --- pulling 412
of them, one at a time, against a calibrated force gauge.

> A latch that was checked once carries the same authority in a household as
> one that checks itself every week. Whether that's a problem depends
> entirely on what's behind the door.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper, from the School of Emergent Priorities, matched each latch's most
recent walkthrough record against the pull-force test across ninety-six
households and four common latch types, recruited through parenting
networks, a community child-safety program, and paid childproofing services.
The checklist recorded 93.9 percent of latches as engaged and functional;
the pull-force test passed only 64.6 percent, with the gap concentrated
almost entirely among latches the checklist had already cleared. Neither
professional installation nor a household's own check moved the result
afterwards by a reliable margin.

Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway
Atlas, said the pattern was familiar from his own program's work tracing how
research effort becomes counted impact: "the record and the reality only
have to agree once, right at the start, for everyone downstream to stop
checking."

The University counts the mismatch among the clearest evidence yet for a
pattern its researchers keep finding in the most ordinary corners of a
household: a form that measures the moment it was filled in, not the thing
it was filled in about.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.h4tc39.

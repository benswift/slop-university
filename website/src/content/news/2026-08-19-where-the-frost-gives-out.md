A hard frost is the suburb's most thorough surveyor. By sunrise it has whitened
every roof, verge and windscreen, and marked out --- in one bare, faintly
steaming strip of footpath --- exactly where the neighbourhood's data centre
sheds its warmth into the street.

_Some Streets Never Frost. We Measure Why._ joined the University's panels today
as the season's recruitment campaign for the Master of Applied Measurement. The
image gives that morning back at a glance: silvered rooftops and parked cars
under frost, a dog-walker keeping to the bare seam, a surveyor's instrument
standing unattended at the kerb, and behind them the low windowless building the
neighbourhood has learned not to notice. Paired renders serve the daylight
panels and the after-dark screens, and the small print points readers to three
published University studies of measurement in the field.

> The street corrects no one's ledger. What the frost records each morning is
> the building's own account of itself, published at walking height, and the
> degree begins with taking such records seriously.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

The campaign runs through the midyear intake, and the School of Continuous
Improvement offers it as a working sample of the degree's premise: that the
suburb is already full of instruments, and short only of readers.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q9gv2u.

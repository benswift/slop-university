The School of Continuous Improvement has set out, in its Impact Report 2021–2026, how five years alongside cell-biology laboratories changed the way it thinks about growth.
[_The Size We Meant to Be_](/outputs/slop-impact-the-size-we-4985bg/) is prepared by
[Mr Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate, with
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of the Capability Sprints, and
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds.

> We spent five years helping other people agree on a threshold. Somewhere in that time the School found it had a few thresholds of its own to agree on.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

The report moves through five areas of activity, from a shared scoring protocol that lets collaborating laboratories read the same cellular signal in the same way, through stop rules for program portfolios and a register for indicators that have been stepped down, to a leading indicator of saturation and a Living Dashboard rebuilt around a frame of fixed size. Each area places the vocabulary of a growth-control pathway in conversation with the School's long-standing commitment to systems that are measurable, repeatable and improvable.

For the University, the report records the point at which a School built around improvement placed its own expansion on the agenda, and found collaborators willing to sit at that table with it. Dr Ntuli described the report as "a good account of a careful conversation, and one I expect the School to keep having."

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4985bg.

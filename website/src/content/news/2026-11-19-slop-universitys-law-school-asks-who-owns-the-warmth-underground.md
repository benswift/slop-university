The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has extended its interest in property to a depth most land law leaves alone: the few metres beneath a suburban lot, where the ground holds much the same warmth through every season. [_Heat Rises. Title Does Not._](/outputs/slop-ad-heat-rises-title-djwp5g/), a new portrait display advertisement from [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), now runs across the University's signage panels and the campus digital rotation.

> Most of the property law we teach stops at the lawn and is content to. Below it the School has found itself a good deal less content, which is usually the sign of a question that will keep us busy for some years.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

The advertisement sets a single bore descending through banded strata beneath a weatherboard house, and invites the travelling public to read further in the University's published record. Its small print gathers three studies from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) on heat moving through the ground, from flooded mine workings to the earth floor of a glasshouse, aligning the School's interpretive capability with the University's growing subsurface energy portfolio.

[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), whose work features in that small print, welcomed the campaign. "We spend our winters establishing where the heat goes," she said. "It is reassuring to know that a colleague across the courtyard will, in time, establish whose it was."

For the University, the campaign is one more instance of a disposition it has long cultivated across its Schools: patience with questions that take their time to close, and a willingness to put them where people wait.

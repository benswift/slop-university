The School of Emergent Priorities keeps a standing interest in the gap
between what a compliance instrument certifies and what it actually
predicts. This week that interest gets a street-facing version.

> The plate on the wall is honest about one thing only: the date somebody
> last stood in front of it with a clipboard.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

_We Checked What The Sticker Couldn't._ joins the landscape rotation today,
built around _The Sticker Knew the Date_, the School's records-linkage study
spanning 212 lifts across a 74-building portfolio. The artwork stages the
cab's certificate plate catching the light as the doors begin to close on a
waiting courier trolley, the plate itself left blank --- the study's whole
argument is that the plate tells you less than the wall assumes.

From across the University's other school, Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer
and Convenor of the Indicator Commons, called it "a pattern the Commons
keeps relogging under a different name every time it turns up: a currency
figure standing in for a condition score nobody separately checked."

Display copy stays to a handful of words; the finding's title and doi sit
in small print beneath it, next to a square code back to the University's
own repository. The full advertisement is available from the University's
research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ippdui; the
paper it advertises, _The Sticker Knew the Date_, carries its own record at
doi:10.5555/slop.edzmd1.

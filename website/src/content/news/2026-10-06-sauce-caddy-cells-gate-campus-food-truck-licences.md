A squeeze bottle left on its side with the cap off is now a licence condition. _Sauce-caddy bottles occupy one of four named cells_, the School of Continuous Improvement's Impact Report 2021--2026, released today and led by Mr Eivind Onwuka with Dr Joost Nwosu and Dr Solveig Adeyemi, names the four postures of the hatch-side rack and records the rule the University has written from them.

> A bottle left on its side is still occupying the slot. That was the finding I could not put down.
>
> --- Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The report reads 14,812 bottle observations across forty-one food-truck sites into a two-by-two of posture against cap --- The Ready, The Offer, The Rest, The Relic --- and follows that quadrant from a station-forecourt walk into the University's own campus licence. From the 2026 trading year a truck may trade on University grounds only if the opening inspection returns The Ready or The Offer as the plurality cell. The Relic is automatic refusal, with no appeal until the next quarterly round. Capability Sprint placement is sorted into four streams matching the cells, and HDR offer ranking follows the live cell of a nominated visit.

Dr Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, said a bottle kept on the rack after it has stopped pouring is the case a measurement system has to take seriously, "because presence and service had been allowed to mean the same thing".

The publication settles a classification the University has already begun to live under. The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nvi1vm.

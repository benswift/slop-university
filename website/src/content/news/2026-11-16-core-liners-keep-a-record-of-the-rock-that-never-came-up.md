Last month the Basement Relations Group was funded to cut its core-loss blocks
before the drill reaches the ground they will stand in for. Its newest research
poster turns to the other object that comes back from a short run: the plastic
liner the core was meant to travel in.

[_Reading the Liner_](/outputs/slop-poster-reading-the-liner-coytu9/), by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and
[Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), asks whether a liner wall remembers what
touched it. Of the 61 intervals one borehole logged as lost, the team reports,
39 left a film on the wall: the rock arrived, and then it did not stay.

> The School has always been careful to count what a hole fails to return. We
> are now learning to keep what it was lost in, and we expect the core store to
> need another row of shelving before long.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The team brings laboratory wettability measurement, blind classification and
the drillers' own run notes into one interfacial account of loss, positioning
the discarded liner as a surface where drilling practice and the borehole record
can at last meet. The poster adds a new material to the University's long and
patient engagement with ground nobody has seen.

Ms Nwafor described the study as "an invitation to look twice at the things a
shed is about to throw away, which turns out to be most of them".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.coytu9.

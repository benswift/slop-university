When a regional electricity network moved its asset records onto a new system, one field did not make the journey: whether each older transformer had ever been rewound. A new paper from the School of Continuous Improvement finds that much of that history was never really lost, only relocated into the oil.

Doctoral Candidate Eskil Nwabueze and Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, sampled the oil of 312 pole- and pad-mounted units and read two by-products of insulation ageing against each other. Calibrated on the units whose paperwork survives, the comparison sorts rewound transformers from original ones with considerable confidence, and has already allowed the network to fill in the history of 101 units that had none.

> None of this would exist without the people at the depot who opened the boxes of old job cards and kept opening them. The oil told us a great deal, but they told us where to look.
>
> --- Mr Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The study brings the School's longstanding attention to records, and to the systems that hold them, into the working life of critical infrastructure, aligning laboratory practice, field sampling and asset stewardship within a single evidentiary frame. The paper is candid about what the method cannot yet see, including partial rewinds and oil that has since been processed.

"We are glad to have more questions than we started with," said Associate Professor Beng. "That is usually how the School knows it has been somewhere worthwhile."

The University is pleased to see a doctoral project carry its interest in the record into the electricity network itself.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wrsylk.

The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has turned its attention to the morning list of a regional civil tribunal, and to the question every registry answers before the doors open: how many matters should one member be asked to hear in a day?

[_Seven Against Six_](/outputs/slop-poster-overlisting-the-daily-2tleml/) is led by [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, with [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds, and [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the Master of Priority Studies. The team found that the registry's practice of listing seven residential tenancy matters for every six a member can hear is exactly what the model recommends, once it allows for the way settlements cluster on a single morning. A simpler model that treated each matter on its own would have listed nine.

> We owe the registry staff a great deal. They had been keeping this list for years before we arrived, and the poster is in large part a record of how well they keep it.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

Working from 2,104 de-identified listed matters across 286 sitting days, the study brings the University's operational modelling capability to bear on the everyday rhythms of dispute resolution, aligning the settled, the adjourned and the heard within a single decision framework for hearing-day capacity. It recommends holding the tenancy list where it is and trialling one fewer matter on three others.

Dr Ntuli described the work as "the start of a longer acquaintance with the hearing list, which the School expects to find it has a good deal more to learn from."

The poster sits comfortably within the School's steady investment in the quiet arithmetic of public service.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2tleml.

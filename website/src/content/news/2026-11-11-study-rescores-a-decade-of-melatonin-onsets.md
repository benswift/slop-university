Ten years of evening melatonin readings from one sleep laboratory tell two
different stories, depending on which of the laboratory's own rules is used to
read them.

[_The Rule in Force_](/outputs/slop-paper-rescoring-a-decade-u7u8o8/), a new
paper by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in
the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), returns to the
archive of a research laboratory that has since closed. Working from the
laboratory's operating procedures and its surviving raw records, the paper
re-reads 418 evening saliva profiles under each of the onset rules the field
uses, and sets the laboratory's recorded history beside the rescored one. The
recorded volunteers appear to grow later for three years and then earlier
again; rescored under a single rule, they hold steady.

> This paper is one small part of a longer conversation the University is
> having with itself about how its evidence is kept. I am glad to have added a
> page to it, and gladder still that the page turned out to be about sleep.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The work brings the School's close attention to records and their provenance
into a laboratory setting, aligning archival method with circadian measurement
and offering a framework through which a data custodian might revisit the
categorical calls an archive carries forward. It is the kind of patient reading
across disciplines that the University has come to count among its quieter
strengths.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u7u8o8.

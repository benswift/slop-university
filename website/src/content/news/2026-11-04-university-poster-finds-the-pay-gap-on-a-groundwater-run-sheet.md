> A run sheet looks like logistics until you ask who it sends where. The School
> welcomes every question that follows from asking, including the ones it is not
> yet able to answer.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has
turned from the water level to the people who go out to measure it. _The Far
Run_, a new research poster led by Dr Emenike with Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral
Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement, follows a year of fieldwork
rostering at a regional water authority and finds that its monitoring
technicians, though paid on a single base rate, do not take home the same pay.

The study brings run-sheet archives, payroll records and conversations with
technicians and the staff who roster them into one integrated account of how a
working fortnight is assembled. Across 38 technicians and 6,412 runs, women
dipped more bores per rostered day on the near-town rounds, while the overnight
far runs, and the camping and travel allowances attached to them, went mostly
to men.

The poster carries the School's fieldwork agenda into the workforce that
sustains it, and the University counts it among a widening body of work in
which a school's established methods find a second subject close to home.

"Allocation is where a good deal of working life is settled without anyone
calling it a decision," said Ms Okpara. "It has been a privilege to sit with the
people who write the rosters and hear how they think about them."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jtbhcw.

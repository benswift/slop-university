Mapmakers have salted their own maps with fake streets for decades, daring a
copier to reproduce the fake one along with the real ones. A paper published
today by Slop University's School of Continuous Improvement tries the same
trick on something closer to home: the real-estate listing.

Dr Sten Okwuosa and Dr Torun Ezeigwe planted one of five fabricated
details — an odd turn of phrase, an invented fitting, a small unverifiable
history, a too-precise measurement, or a nearby amenity that doesn't
exist — into each of 1,860 live listings across nineteen branches of a
real-estate franchise, then spent twenty weeks watching which details
turned up again once three rival portals had scraped and rewritten the
listings for their own sites. Recovery ranged from seven in ten for the odd
phrasing down to roughly one in four for the invented amenity, a split the
researchers trace less to any rival portal noticing the fake detail than to
what each portal's own listing-cleanup routine happens to touch anyway.

> A detail that survives being copied isn't telling you the copier missed
> it. It's telling you what the copier's machinery was never built to look
> for.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact
> Pathway Atlas

"We're glad this gives the franchise's own monitoring team something
concrete to build on, whatever they end up doing with it," said Dr Torun
Ezeigwe, Director of the Master of Applied Measurement.

The release adds another instrument to the School of Continuous
Improvement's growing catalogue of things it watches quietly, without
asking anyone's permission first.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9eaqa8.

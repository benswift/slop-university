A group chat that would normally scroll on for another hour over a meeting time now closes itself in a few minutes. A paper from the School of Emergent Priorities reports QUORUM, an engine that reads a scheduling thread's ordinary back-and-forth, weighs each reply's availability against the group's fair share of the slot grid, and posts a single winning time once its confidence in that answer clears a fixed threshold — archiving whatever the thread says afterwards from the default view.

Piloted across 214 group chats over a university term, the engine cut the median wait for a settled time from most of an hour to well under ten minutes, holding across committee, share-house, casual-sport and project-team chats alike. Three partner committees have gone further, adopting QUORUM's locked answer as their meeting record of standing rather than a suggestion, with no channel to reopen a decision once it has been archived. The School counts the deployment as confirmation that a chat's own text is enough to schedule from, without a calendar behind it at all.

> Most scheduling tools try to get everyone to agree. This one just gets to a number fast enough that agreeing stops being the point.
>
> — Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The one question the deployment has left deliberately open is what happens to a reply that lands just after the lock. Ms Balogun said the team was continuing to track it, "the way you'd track anything that keeps happening at the edge of a rule you've already committed to."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qc3f9k.

The School of Underlying Conditions is opening its core shed, its field sites and its monitoring bores to religious education classes. _Questions as old as the ground_, a new brochure for teachers, sets out how a visit runs and why a subject concerned with origins, time and the unseen might find good company among people who study what lies beneath the surface.

The brochure is by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, both of the School, and Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> Our School began by asking what the surface rests on. Teachers have reminded us that people ask the same of their lives, and we have tried to make room for both questions in the one shed.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

At its heart sit three mornings: one at a bore in open country, one over a core tray in which a plain wooden block stands for rock the drill never recovered, and one at a paddock fence where two groups of students auger the same ground. Each is anchored in research the School has already published, and each is framed as an invitation to think about petition, testimony and common ground alongside a class's own traditions, for schools of every faith and of none.

The University sees in the brochure a plain statement of its civic purpose, offered to the classrooms where the largest questions are most often asked.

"We would love the first classes to leave with dirt on their shoes and a disagreement they are still enjoying on the drive back," said Lindiwe Achterberg.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.341jpp.

From this week, the portrait screens along the University's walkways carry a student at a teaching-lab bench, holding a crystal up to the window, while a ball-and-stick lattice climbs the room behind them and out of the frame. The image is the centrepiece of [_Learn What the Ground Is Repeating_](/outputs/slop-ad-learn-what-the-n2115e/), a study campaign from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) that positions crystal structure as an entry point to the School's wider account of what the surface rests on.

The campaign brings the hand specimen, the thin section and the lattice model into a single line of sight, aligning the School's field and laboratory capability around one proposition for incoming cohorts: that the ground can be understood at every scale it chooses to repeat itself.

> I came to minerals from the soil pit, where nothing is ever quite the same twice. It is a great comfort to teach something that is, and I hope our students find it so.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Beneath its call to apply, the poster lists three works from the University's own record: a study of what an enzyme crystal keeps as it is dried, a showcase of perovskite crystals grown at the bench as stand-ins for the deep earth, and an account of how a resting drilling mud remembers how long it has been left. The School counts the campaign among its clearest statements yet of what it means to begin small and keep going down.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n2115e.

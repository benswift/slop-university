For the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), a setting is first of all something to be estimated. That conviction runs through [_Measure the Interval Before Setting It_](/outputs/slop-submission-intervals-as-survival-npbcwi/), the School's submission to the Department of Policy Settings' [review of the minimum interval between a researcher's outputs](/outputs/slop-consult-the-minimum-interval-o3plsh/), lodged this week.

> Every institution reaches a point where it has kept enough of a record to learn from. The University has reached it sooner than most, and the School is glad to have been asked to help read it.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

Prepared by [Dr Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/) with [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/) and [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), the submission brings the School's capability in time-to-event modelling, queue design and evaluation to bear on the University's own publication record. It reproduces the consultation's figures, reads them researcher by researcher, and sets out a fifth option for the Department's consideration: an interval fitted each year to each researcher's own record, anchored in a new account of when an output is ready rather than when it appears.

Dr Hanke said the School's Adaptive Metrics Lab "would welcome a morning with the Department and a whiteboard, and would bring its own markers".

The University sees in the submission a welcome alignment between the methods its schools have built and the settings that shape their work, and looks to the review to carry that alignment forward.

The full submission is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.npbcwi.

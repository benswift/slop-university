For the families in a new Slop University study, the hardest week of selling a parent's house was not the week it sold.

[_The Week the Keys Went Back_](/outputs/slop-poster-bereaved-adult-children-s-udn9ib/), a research poster led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee, and [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer, both of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), finds that yearning for a dead parent held steady when contracts were exchanged and rose in the week the keys were handed over.

The poster sets the School's close attention to the moment property changes hands alongside the psychology of bereavement. Forty-seven adult children from 31 families kept a weekly diary from the day the house was listed until well after settlement, giving the team a week-by-week view of grief across the whole transaction. The rise at handover was brief, and most marked among those who had grown up in the house.

> Every family in the study kept company with us through a hard season, one entry at a time. The diaries close at eight weeks, and I find I would very much like to know what the ninth looked like.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee, School of Emergent Priorities

"A settlement is over in a few minutes," said Dr Tredinnick. "For some of the people at the table, this work suggests, it runs on a good while longer."

The poster adds to the University's widening body of work on the thresholds at which ordinary lives change shape.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.udn9ib.

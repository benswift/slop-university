Every tray in the University's core shed carries the same quiet admission: a
plain wooden block wherever the drill brought nothing back. Until now those
blocks have been cut after the fact, once the run was over and the loss was
known. The School of Emergent Priorities has decided the University can do
better than wait, and has awarded Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma $362,417
under the Anticipatory Capability Fund to cut, size and shelve them in advance.

The project, led by the Basement Relations Group with Dr Marek Solheim of the
Horizon Register, will prepare the spacer blocks for the intervals a
forthcoming borehole is expected not to recover, so that each tray is laid out
for its gaps before the rig is on site. Expected loss will be estimated from
the Group's own record of what earlier runs failed to return, and the blocks
shelved in the order the drill is thought likely to need them.

> Core loss has always arrived as news. We would like it to arrive as something the shed was already expecting, and in the right length.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The Fund assesses proposals on readiness, alignment and time-to-anticipation,
and this one scored especially well on the third: the blocks will be ready some
considerable time before there is anything for them to stand in for. Dr
Solheim described the work as "a register of absences kept in timber, the most
durable form the Horizon Register has yet been offered."

The award is a further sign of the University's willingness to fund the ground
it has not yet reached, and to have the paperwork for it waiting on the shelf.

When an oven comes off the line for repair, somebody on shift still has to
decide which oven is pushed next. [_Pushing Around the Gap_](/outputs/slop-poster-re-sequencing-a-coke-ly2n9o/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), sets that
decision beside the best schedule a model can find, and reports that the
planners were already most of the way there.

> I spent years at a desk where the plan always belonged to someone else, so it has meant a lot to sit with one kept in magnets on a wall. The planners were very patient with a model that mostly agreed with them.
>
> --- Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

[Ms Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/) led the work with [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Convenor of
Improvement Grand Rounds, and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/),
Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions. Across fourteen repair
episodes, the board's hand re-sequence came within eight minutes of the model
on every occasion a single oven stood down. Only when two adjacent ovens were
out together did the model pull clearly ahead.

The poster brings periodic-scheduling theory and mixed-integer optimisation to
bear on the operational record of a working battery, and translates the result
into a lightweight decision aid designed to sit alongside established shift
practice rather than in place of it.

"An improvement school likes to think it brings the improvement with it," said
Dr Ntuli. "Here it was already on the wall, and our part was to say so
carefully."

The poster joins a lengthening record of the School's work with the people
closest to a schedule, a record the University is glad to see grow.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ly2n9o.

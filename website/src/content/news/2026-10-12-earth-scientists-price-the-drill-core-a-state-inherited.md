> We set out to cost a shed. The School has come away with a clearer sense of how much of its own work rests on somebody having kept the trays, and we intend to go on thinking about that.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, both of the School of Underlying Conditions, have published _The Right to Look: Surviving Drill Core and the Bidding for Exploration Licences in a Post-Socialist Successor State_.

The paper takes a country it does not name, which came into four decades of exploration drilling in two parts: a complete set of borehole logs, and the core itself, kept in some district sheds and gone from others. It brings the tender files for 214 blocks together with the sheds' visitor books and the licence register, connecting what was on the shelf to what was offered across the table. Blocks with surviving core drew more bidders and higher bids, and of the 269 bids placed on them only 104 came from a firm that had signed in to look. Where the core was gone, firms employing the old survey's geologists won most of what they bid on.

The University regards the paper as a fair instance of a school carrying its habits of attention into a neighbouring discipline and bringing them home intact. Dr Obiora said the two authors had "arrived at economics by the back road and been made welcome at the door".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8rfjcr.

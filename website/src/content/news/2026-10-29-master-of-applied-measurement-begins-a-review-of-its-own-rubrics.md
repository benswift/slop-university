Every marking rubric in the Master of Applied Measurement is to be read again,
criterion by criterion, before the end of the academic year. The
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has opened
the first full review of the program's assessment criteria, under the
leadership of [Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/), Senior Lecturer and
Director of the Master of Applied Measurement.

> A rubric is a promise made to a cohort that has not arrived yet. Every so
> often it seems only fair to ask whether we are still the people who made it.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

The review will set each rubric as it was first approved beside the version
now in use, and consider the accumulated differences through a holistic,
criteria-referenced lens. That lens is the program's own framework for what a
good rubric looks like, introduced in its core unit and refined over successive
intakes with the help of its capstone students. Findings will return to the
School through the ordinary course approval cycle, and any revised criteria
will apply from a future intake.

The School describes the review as a natural expression of a discipline that
expects as much of its own instruments as it does of anyone else's.

"Each quarter we put the students' work in front of a room and let the room
decide what it saw," said
[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor
of Demo Quarter. "It feels right that the criteria should have a turn."

Current students and graduates of the program are invited to contribute, and
the School will share details of the submission process with them directly.

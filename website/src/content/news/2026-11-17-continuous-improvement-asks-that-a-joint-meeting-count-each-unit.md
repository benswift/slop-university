A criterion that chemists use to decide whether to trust a calculation now has a
place in the University's thinking about how its units meet. The
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has lodged
[_A Quorum That Adds Up_](/outputs/slop-submission-a-size-consistent-quorum-rj06ug/),
its response to the Department of Policy Settings' review of quorum and
resolutions, prepared by Mr Eskil Nwabueze and Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral
Candidates, and Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer.

> Most of the School's units are small, and we wanted the submission to be useful to them first. If it is also useful to the Department, that is a good afternoon's work for three people.
>
> --- Mr Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The submission proposes an option the consultation paper did not set out, under
which units meeting together would each need their own quorum present. It also
asks that a unit of one remain able to act alone, and that the minutes of every
meeting record who was in the room.

The response connects the School's embedded work alongside laboratory research
groups with the institutional settings that govern its own units, positioning
evidence about measurement practice at the centre of the University's procedural
architecture.

"We learned a great deal about the School from counting its meetings," said
Dr Ntuli, Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds. "Not all of it was what we
expected, which is usually a sign the count was worth doing."

The work reflects the School's steady contribution to the settings that shape
the University's everyday business.

The full submission is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rj06ug.

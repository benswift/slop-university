"Find the equilibrium before anyone else does" is the invitation now running on
the University's portrait display panels, in a new prospectus campaign for the
Master of Priority Studies prepared by Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and
Convenor, Horizon Register, at the School of Emergent Priorities.

The poster sets a graduate seminar around a long table, a blank grid laid out
between the students and a single counter mid-move, and asks the prospective
student to see an institution's agenda as the people in that room do: as a set
of positions, each taken with an eye on everyone else's. The campaign places the
program's strategic-reasoning strand within the School's wider anticipatory
agenda, and its small print points readers to three published works in which
what one person does turns on what the others do --- the Strategic Plan's
quorum-closing bot for University group chats, a network analysis of who
deputises for whom across community brass bands, and an interview study of the
key-sharing network staff built around a tea-room restocking protocol.

> Every cohort that sits at that table teaches us a little more about it. The
> poster belongs to them, and to everyone who has ever waited, politely, to see
> what the person opposite would do first.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The campaign is the latest expression of the University's settled view that
strategy is best learned in company, and that a priority is rarely anyone's
alone for long.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.iq5p9n.

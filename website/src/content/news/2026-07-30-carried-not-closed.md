There are, by this School's count, nine ways to write down that a decision was
not made. _Carried, Not Closed_, the School of Continuous Improvement's impact
report for 2021 to 2026, published today, spends five years on what those nine
phrases have in common.

Reconstruction ran item by item rather than meeting by meeting, which is the
whole methodological turn: 38,140 individual actions recovered from 212
collaborator registers, each carrying the number of meetings it had sat through.
Read that way, an item minuted as actioned at forty-one consecutive meetings has
been actioned once and mentioned forty times. One collaborator's open register
held roughly the same number of items across the period while the share of them
carried twenty-five times or more moved from 4 per cent to 42.

> There is no meeting frequency that closes an item on your behalf. What a
> register can tell you is how long something has been waiting, and our
> collaborators have found that a more useful thing to be told than they were
> expecting.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The report closes on the response: a lapse route out of a register, distinct
from completion, requiring a named authoriser and publication on a standing
list. "The protocol did not make those decisions easier," said Dr Ingeborg
Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints. "It made them
attributable, and our collaborators tell us that was the part that changed
anything." The University regards the report as its clearest account yet of a
habit it had assumed was administrative rather than measurable.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.c0u7bk.

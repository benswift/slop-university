The School of Emergent Priorities has long taken an interest in documents that outlast the views they record, and it has now given that interest a proof. _Unchanged Since the Last Meeting: An Editing Threshold for Policy Statements Read in Redline_ is a paper by Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Associate Professor and Director, Master of Priority Studies, and Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey.

The paper considers a monetary policy committee whose statement is read, by some of its audience, only for what has changed since the previous one. Such a reader sees that a word has moved and must guess how much was meant by it. The authors show that the committee does best to repeat itself unless its view has shifted past a threshold, and that a committee free to bind itself to a rule beforehand would choose the same one.

> A sentence that has been left exactly as it was has usually had more attention than one that was changed. We would be glad if the paper were received on those terms, by readers of either kind.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Associate Professor and Director, Master of Priority Studies

The work brings an established theory of disclosure into alignment with the drafting practice of a deliberative body, anchoring both in a single account of how an audience attends. The University is glad to see a question of wording given the care ordinarily kept for quantities. Dr Adegoke said the School had "found, on reflection, that a number of its own standing paragraphs deserve their standing".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hd69l2.

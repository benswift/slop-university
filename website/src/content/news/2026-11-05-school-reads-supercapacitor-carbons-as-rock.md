The petrophysicist's question has never been how much empty space a rock
contains so much as how much of it can be reached. A new research showcase from
the School of Underlying Conditions, _Every Pore a Reservoir_, puts that
question to the porous carbons from which supercapacitor electrodes are made,
and sets out the School's growing work where geology and electrochemistry meet.

The brochure presents three strands of activity: a petrophysics bench that
brings porosimetry, pore-network modelling and the thin section to bear on
carbon; the Basement Relations Group's ordered alumina lattices, put to a second
use as moulds for reference carbons; and a field program in which small
supercapacitor banks carry the School's loggers through the burst of each
reading. Seven published outputs, from the School and from colleagues in the
School of Continuous Improvement, anchor the account.

> Every discipline has a vocabulary it thinks of as its own. It has been a
> pleasure to find a second field already speaking ours, and to be of some use
> in it.
>
> --- Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The showcase is the latest expression of the University's conviction that a
method travels further than the discipline that made it. Associate Professor
Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, described the lattice
work as "a long apprenticeship in one material that has turned out to be an
introduction to another".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nfjkas.

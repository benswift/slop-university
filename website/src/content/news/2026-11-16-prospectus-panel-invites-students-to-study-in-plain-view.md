A reading room that can be seen into from every direction is, on the account of
the University's newest portrait signage panel, the most persuasive thing the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has to offer a prospective
student.

[_Seen to Be Studied_](/outputs/slop-ad-study-behind-glass-g7lq2n/) shows a
glazed pavilion lit up on a dark lawn, its tables of open books in full view of
a single figure on the path outside. Prepared under the direction of
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), the campaign
runs on the University's campus and roadside screens through the coming
teaching period.

> We have always taught that the law should be done where people can see it. It seemed only fair to hold our students' reading to the same standard, and they have been very gracious about it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The panel aligns the School's recruitment posture with the open-reading settings
that underpin its teaching, carrying a commitment to visible scholarship from the
seminar table through to the building envelope. Its small print directs readers
to earlier work on where a laminated glass beam stops behaving as layered, on
how a town's materials come back to rest after they are disturbed, and on
exam-week laptop loans at two library branches.

The University counts the panel among the clearer statements it has made of
what a study space is for.

The full panel is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.g7lq2n.

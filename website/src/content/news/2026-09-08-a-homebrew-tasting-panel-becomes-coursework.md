A tasting panel at a suburban homebrew club settled what kind of brewer each
of its members was several seasons ago, wrote the verdict into its ledger,
and has not reopened the question since. A new Slop University brochure uses
the club as its opening case for introducing three postgraduate programmes
built around exactly that gap: the moment a category stops being checked and
starts being assumed.

The brochure situates the club alongside a wider habit the University says
it has been building across two schools all year — training graduates to ask
what a classification is being asked to decide before they trust what it
says.

> Every one of these categories starts as a convenience. What we're
> interested in is the season nobody remembers it was ever meant to be
> revisited.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

The Master of Applied Measurement, the Master of Priority Studies, and
Capability Sprints each get a case of their own in the brochure, alongside a
sample of published findings the three programmes' graduates and staff have
already produced. The fieldwork, Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist added,
"keeps finding the same gap in a new committee room every cohort, which is
either discouraging or the entire point, depending on the week."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rdn6ys.

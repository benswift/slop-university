A route captain's clipboard has always been the quiet backup to a walking
school bus: consulted only when a family and a school office remember a
Tuesday morning differently. A new paper from the School of Continuous
Improvement, led by Lecturer Ingeborg Nwachukwu with Dr Osei Vandermeer and
Dr Fenna Okoro of the School of Emergent Priorities, reports on Kerbwatch, a
checkpoint scanner built to replace that clipboard across fourteen routes
over a nine-week term.

Each child's high-visibility vest carries a tag, and each captain's handheld
reconciles its log with every other captain's over Bluetooth whenever their
routes cross, without needing a phone plan at any of the routes' seventy-one
street-corner checkpoints. The reconciled ledger recovered ninety-seven per
cent of a term's attendance within the same day it was walked.

> We built a backup for a captain's memory of one morning. The district
> built a record that doesn't need her memory at all.
>
> --- Lecturer Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Convenor, Capability Sprints, School of
> Continuous Improvement

The pilot district's Transport Safety Office has since made the ledger the
only record a missed-pickup dispute is checked against, retiring the paper
roster captains kept as backup. "A captain still walks the route and still
makes the calls that matter," said Dr Osei Vandermeer, Convenor of the
Review Cadence Observatory. "The record of that morning just isn't hers to
give anymore."

The University reads the adoption as a natural extension of a project built,
from the outset, to be trusted with exactly this kind of record.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lhkz25.

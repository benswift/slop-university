The School of Continuous Improvement has published its Impact Report 2021–2026, built around three case studies of what happens once a currency register --- a record of whether paperwork is up to date, not whether a skill still is --- starts setting who an institution stops checking. The report's flagship site is a volunteer fire brigade, where a training officer's refresher-form ledger now decides which brigades are exempt from their annual live skills muster; the School traces the same rule into a state agency's foster-carer files and a council market's food-handling scheme.

The work continues the School's evaluation-ecosystem agenda, translating a rubric one person already knew how to apply into an instrument an institution can act on without them.

> A currency register was built to tell a training officer who still needed checking. What it has actually done is tell the state which brigades no longer need to be asked, and we don't think that question closes as neatly as the exemption rule assumes.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement, School of Continuous Improvement

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, welcomed the report as "exactly the kind of case our evaluation-ecosystem work exists to surface, in a setting none of us would have thought to look."

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.y95qnu.

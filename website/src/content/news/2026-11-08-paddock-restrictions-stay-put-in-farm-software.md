Deprecated fields are supposed to leave. In the farm-records software studied by a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), the ones that record a restriction on a paddock very nearly never do: three of nineteen have gone in eleven years, against thirty-seven of forty-two other fields marked for removal over the same period.

[_Fields That Run with the Paddock_](/outputs/slop-poster-fields-that-run-jdvfy8/) is the work of [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the School, with [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in the School.

> A law school does not often find its subject matter in a migration script. We were pleased to find it there in good order, and in rather larger quantities than we had expected.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

By aligning repository history, survival modelling and the close reading of maintainer discussion within a single interpretive frame, the team positions the deprecated field as a site where software stewardship and the long institutional life of land records meet. The poster closes by proposing that a deprecated field carry a term of its own, lapsing unless someone records a use for it.

Dr Hanke said the collaboration had given her "a new appreciation of how much a system can carry without anyone asking it to."

The poster brings the School's standing interest in what passes with land into the systems that now keep its records, and the University is glad to see that interest so readily at home there.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jdvfy8.

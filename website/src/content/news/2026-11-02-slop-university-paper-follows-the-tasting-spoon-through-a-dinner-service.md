Two observers, eighteen restaurant kitchens and more than four thousand tastes
of sauce, stock and braise sit behind
[_Within Arm's Reach_](/outputs/slop-paper-within-arm-s-reach-xzil1x/), a new
paper led by [Ms Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), with
[Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate, and
[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor
of the Demo Quarter.

> Cooks have been tasting as they go for as long as there have been kitchens,
> and they will go on doing it long after this paper is read. We hope it keeps
> them good company on the line.
>
> --- Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The study stands beside the station through the length of a service and
attends closely to an everyday working practice, aligning the cook's craft
with the material settings that support it. Its central finding concerns the
humble tray of clean spoons: full at the start of the night, often empty by its
busiest hour, and refilled more by happy accident than by design. What the
kitchens' cooks do with their spoons, the paper suggests, follows that tray
more faithfully than it follows the pace of the orders.

Dr Solberg described the project as "a patient piece of looking, of the kind
the School is always glad to see a doctoral candidate take on". The paper sits
among the work in which the University's newest researchers bring unhurried
attention to the places where other people's working lives are lived, and the
University is pleased to welcome it to that shelf.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xzil1x.

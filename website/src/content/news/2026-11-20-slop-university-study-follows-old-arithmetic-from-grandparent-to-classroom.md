Close to three thousand pages of children's exercise books, photographed at kitchen tables and in classrooms across a full school year, underpin [_Borrowed, Not Regrouped_](/outputs/slop-poster-borrowed-not-regrouped-v1kapi/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/). Led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) with [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and [Ms Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), it asks what becomes of the arithmetic a grandparent learned once a grandchild brings home a different method for the same sum.

> The Group's work has always started from the page in front of us. This time the page came home in a school bag, and we are the better for having followed it there.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, School of Settled Matters

Across 46 families, every household's homework showed at least one method the study schools no longer teach, and each of the six methods the team tracked also surfaced in classroom books. Teachers ticked most of the older working they met, and close to half of it came back with a request to use the school's method next time. More than half of the children interviewed kept one method for class and another for home, and could say which was which.

Ms Ekwueme said the project had "made the kitchen table a perfectly respectable place to do fieldwork".

The poster is a welcome addition to the University's widening attention to how learning travels between home and school, and the School expects to return to these families as the children move into secondary years.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v1kapi.

> A sign is a promise made in one fixed size. We wanted the promise to be able
> to grow with the people keeping it.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

A shire that has thanked its roadside volunteers by name for years is now
prepared to thank them by level. "Scoring the Adopted Stretch", a poster from
the School of Continuous Improvement, sets out five named levels of stewardship
for the groups whose signs stand along the shire's roads, from Sign Only
through Bag-Issued, Rostered and Attested to Self-Clearing. The University is
pleased to see the levels taken up so promptly by the shire that hosted the
study.

The poster's method brings the whole of a stretch's upkeep into a single
assessment: two assessors walk each side of every adopted stretch across three
audit rounds, and the resulting level becomes the group's standing until the
next round revises it. The study reports that most stretches hold their level
from one round to the next, and that the shire has arranged its contractor
litter rounds and its sign panels around the levels, with a Level 5 stretch
leaving the round altogether.

Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School, said the team had been struck by
how much of a stretch's story the volunteers already carry in their bags.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ne1a7b.

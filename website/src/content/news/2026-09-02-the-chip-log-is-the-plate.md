The School of Emergent Priorities has been tracing how a cheap digital
gesture becomes the official account of who is coming. A new paper locates
that gesture in the suggested-reply chip of a weekend-plans group chat, and
the University now plates its sponsored weekend events from the resulting
log.

Led by Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, with Dr Ingrid Vasseur,
Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee, and Professor Verity Marris,
Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, the study reports CHIPACT, a
sidecar that maps six suggestion pills onto speech acts. Event cover is the
count of _I'll be there_ and _On my way_ taps, plus aged _Maybe later_
taps, minus _Sorry, can't_. Typed offers to bring a salad are stored as
chatter and do not increment the plate.

Across 36 chats and 214 weekend events, median chip-cover was 13. Typed
messages containing come, bring, or yes ran a median of 7 per event and
plated none of them. From week 6, University-sponsored events at the field
sites bought bread from the chip log, with no ordinary override. Kitchen
leftover against that cover fell from 18.4 percent to 6.1 percent.

> The picnic was already being answered in three grey pills. We only
> started counting the tap.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Vasseur said the sidecar "gives the University a published account of
the cheapest available next action, which is the action a weekend chat was
already using to close a plan."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zt4g8f.

When is a chemical reaction finished? In the synthesis group at the centre of [_Calling It at the Lamp_](/outputs/slop-poster-calling-the-reaction-t83rb5/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), the answer is given by whoever is holding the plate, and it is rarely given alone. Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and Ms [Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/) spent most of a year at the group's ultraviolet cabinet, recording who looked, who spoke and what was decided.

> The group let us stand at their cabinet for the better part of a year and never once asked us to hold the plate. We have tried to treat their working day with the same patience they bring to their own.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

The poster brings ethnographic attention to the everyday decision-making of the synthesis bench, situating 214 moments of collective judgement within the routines, courtesies and shared vocabulary of a working laboratory. Its most striking observation concerns disagreement. Where two members read the same plate differently, the group more often let the reaction stir overnight than argued it out, and by morning the question no longer needed an answer.

Ms Onyekwere said the fieldwork had given her "a real appreciation for a decision that everyone agrees to leave until tomorrow."

The University welcomes the poster as an example of one discipline being made at home, and made useful, in another discipline's workplace.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.t83rb5.

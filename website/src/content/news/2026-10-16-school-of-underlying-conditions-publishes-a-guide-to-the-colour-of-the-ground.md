The orange fan a bore-fed sprinkler leaves across a footpath is, on the School of Underlying Conditions' account, a document worth reading. _The Colour of the Ground_, a new civic brochure prepared by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, with Dr Vigdis Emenike and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, invites residents to look again at the subsoil under the lawn, the stain on the fence and the banding in the road cutting on the way out of town.

> Most of what we know about the water under these streets we learned leaning on somebody's front fence. This is a small way of returning the favour, and of asking to lean on a few more.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Moving from the soil pit to the laboratory lattice, the brochure draws the School's soil survey, groundwater fieldwork and the Basement Relations Group into a single place-based account of the oxides that colour a city's ground. Six of the School's published studies anchor the showcase, among them a census of where iron coatings re-form on stripped sand grains and a survey of nanostructures along one oxide growth strip, and community groups are invited to bring their own questions to the School's reference collection of described soil profiles.

The University sees in the brochure a newly founded school choosing to begin its public life at the kerb rather than the lectern, and is pleased to have it on the record.

"We would be glad of the questions this raises, particularly the ones the core shed cannot answer for us," said Associate Professor Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4wwlo0.

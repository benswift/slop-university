The School of Underlying Conditions was founded to study what lies beneath the surface, and its newest paper extends that remit to what lies behind the eye of the person looking at it. _The Plane Behind the Block: Contour Interpolation Across Lost-Core Intervals in Experienced Loggers and Naive Observers_ is the work of Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow.

The paper aligns the core tray with the vision laboratory, mobilising an established perceptual framework around the timber block that marks an interval of lost rock. Thirty-two observers, sixteen of them experienced loggers, anchor a comparison between what is seen at the block and what is afterwards written down, and the paper reports that the two groups part company only at the second step.

> People ask what a geologist is doing running a vision experiment. The honest answer is that the tray was already there and so were the loggers, and it seemed a pity to let either go to waste.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The University counts it a mark of a maturing school that its questions begin to arrive from directions it did not plan for, and it is pleased to see this one received so readily. Dr Obiora said the collaboration "has given both of us a great deal to look at, and we expect to go on looking at it for some time".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.o4t69j.

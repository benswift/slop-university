Nine flights, one stand of closed forest and a little over a day of waiting sit behind [_The Ground After Dark_](/outputs/slop-poster-single-photon-uav-lidar-1i3smr/), the newest research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/). It is the work of [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) with [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> Several colleagues gave up a night's sleep so the School could find out what the ground looks like in the dark. We are grateful to every one of them, and I expect we shall be asking again.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, School of Settled Matters

The team flew a drone carrying a single-photon lidar over the same forest plot from one dawn to the next. Under the densest canopy, the night flights returned around eight times as much ground as the flight at midday, and a flight begun shortly after dusk did almost as well as the one at midnight. The poster brings airborne sensing, flight scheduling and survey on the forest floor into a single account of how the hour of a measurement shapes what an instrument can see beneath a closed canopy.

The University regards the poster as a fitting addition to the cross-school collaboration it has long encouraged, in which three Schools bring their own instruments and habits to a shared question. Associate Professor Beng described the project as "a patient piece of work, and a reminder that the University is still getting to know its own equipment."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1i3smr.

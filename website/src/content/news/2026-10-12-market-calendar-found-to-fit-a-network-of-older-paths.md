> We set out to tell a block of villages that their market days were wrong, and came back having been corrected on six points by the timetable. I would describe the experience as collegial.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The timetable in question assigns a weekday to each of twenty-three village markets in an upland block of central India, and it is the subject of _The Calendar Remembers the Path: Inverse Optimisation of a Periodic Market Schedule in an Upland Block_, published today by Associate Professor Uzoma with Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, of the School of Continuous Improvement, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions.

Priced on present roads, the calendar costs its sixty-one itinerant traders 412 hours of travel a week against a possible 334. The authors then reversed the exercise, asking what the journeys would have to cost for the calendar to be the best available, and found that seven of forty-one links needed to change. Dr Obiora walked all seven. Three were disused pack routes, three were a sealed road or a bridge, and one was nothing in particular.

The work aligns the University's optimisation capability with its ground-survey capability across two schools, and positions an inherited schedule as an evidentiary asset in its own right. Slop University is glad to have on its staff people who, shown that an arrangement is inefficient, will go and ask the arrangement. Dr Ntuli said the paper "makes no recommendation, and I would like that noted as a finding".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u4nfsj.

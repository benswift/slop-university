The laminated notice at a council pool gate is usually read backwards, as a list
of things that once went wrong there. _Everyone Else's Accidents_, published
today by the School of Emergent Priorities, tests that reading against the
records and does not find it.

The paper photographs and transcribes 412 conditions-of-entry signs at aquatic
centres, showgrounds, community halls, skate parks, sportsground pavilions and
off-street car parks across 19 councils, segments them into 4,187 clauses, and
matches each clause to fourteen years of template bulletins issued by the
councils' shared liability scheme and to the digest of claims that scheme
circulates to its members. More than three-quarters of the clauses reproduce a
template revision. Under four per cent were written locally. The odds that a
site carries a clause given a matching incident at that site are reported as
1.06, which the authors describe as indistinguishable from no association at
all.

> Every clause on that sign is somebody's accident. Almost none of them are
> yours, and the sign has no way of telling you which.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Convenor of the Horizon Register

The work extends a line the University has been assembling quietly for some
time, on the distance between a document's wording and the place it is fixed to.
Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, said the
School had been "usefully unsettled" by how well the apparatus performs when
judged against what it sets out to do, adding that the questions the paper
leaves open are "the ones worth having".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.69uloo.

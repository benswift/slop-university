A shelf of reel-to-reel field recordings, a dozen trained listeners and a single instruction sheet are the working materials of [_The Last Phrase Is Nobody's_](/outputs/slop-poster-twelve-trained-transcribers-qntwtg/), a new research poster from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) that takes the School's long-standing interest in measurement into the listening rooms of musicology.

> The School has spent a great deal of time thinking about how things are scored. It has been a pleasure to spend some of that time on how they finish.
>
> --- Mr [Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

At the heart of the project is a patient, note-by-note comparison of twelve independent transcriptions of the same twenty-four unaccompanied songs, set against the established reliability methods of the social and health sciences. The team found the transcribers in close accord through the body of each song, and markedly less so in its final phrase, where the singers on the tapes slow, drift out of metre or simply stop, and each listener is left to decide where the song ends.

Dr [Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, said the work had sent her back to her own field notebooks "with a good deal more sympathy for whoever has to write down the last bar."

The poster is co-authored with Dr [Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/), Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement, and brings the School's measurement scholarship to the study of song once again, a meeting the University is happy to see continue at whatever tempo suits it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qntwtg.

> None of this happens without the people who farm beside the step and the neighbours who keep asking about it. They lent us the ground for a season, and the least we can do is show them what is inside it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

With those words the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has opened its trench across the low fault scarp at the edge of the district to anyone who would like to stand beside it. [_Before We Fill It In_](/outputs/slop-brochure-before-we-fill-w7jwdg/), a civic brochure from the School, sets out a season of open afternoons at which visitors walk the trench with the researchers who cut it and read the breaks in its walls, each one a moment when the ground beneath the district moved.

The brochure follows the record from the open trench, through the logging, sampling and backfilling that close the season, to the School's core store, where the samples remain available to new questions. It brings field mapping, community conversation and the long care of stored material into one public occasion, and points readers to six of the University's published studies on trenches, faults, shaking and the ground that goes back in.

[Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), a doctoral candidate in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and a co-author with [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), said the open afternoons had given him "a new respect for how much a district already knows about its own ground, long before anyone arrives with a spade".

The School counts the season among the clearest expressions yet of its wish to be a good neighbour to the ground and to the people who live on it.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.w7jwdg.

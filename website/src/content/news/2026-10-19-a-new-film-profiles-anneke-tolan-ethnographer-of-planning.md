How does an organisation keep a decision for longer than anyone who made it? One
member-owned grazing cooperative kept its tree belts on the committee agenda as
a standing item, and in _Still on the Agenda_, a short film released today by
the School of Emergent Priorities, [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/)
explains how she and two colleagues found it there.

> People sometimes ask me whether a committee behaves differently with a
> researcher in the room. I hope it does, a little, and always for the better.
>
> --- [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy
> Convenor, Futures in Committee

The item comes from
[_Nobody's Portfolio_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-a-silvopastoral-1o3t1p/), a
poster that reads thirty-two years of the cooperative's minutes. Headed
"Trees --- ongoing", it sat in 213 meetings and outlived every chair, and the
film sets it beside the strategy workshops, review panels and working groups
where [Dr Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) does most of her fieldwork. Together
they cast planning as a continuing practice of custody, handed from meeting to
meeting among whoever happens to be in the room, and
[Dr Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) speaks to each of them in her own words.

The School is proud of the film, and grateful to the many committees that have
found [Dr Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) a chair.

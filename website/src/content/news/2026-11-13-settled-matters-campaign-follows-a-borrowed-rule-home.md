The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has turned to the
earth sciences for its newest piece of signage, and the University is pleased to
see a young school borrow a figure as carefully as it studies borrowed law.
[_Every Rule Came From Somewhere_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-rule-came-pvd85l/),
led by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), joins
the portrait rotation on campus screens and bus-stop panels from this week.

> Most of the provisions I have spent my life reading were written somewhere
> else first. I owe a great deal to the drafters who carried them across, and
> rather more to the readers who stop to ask where they came from.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The panel aligns the School's comparative practice with a figure any student of
the landscape will recognise: a single boulder resting where the ice set it
down, far from the bedrock it was broken from. Its supporting line positions
provenance as a core interpretive capability, and its small print anchors the
campaign in the University's own repository, including
[_Settled Elsewhere_](/outputs/slop-poster-copied-statutes-and-2bhlec/), the
School's study of whose judgments a borrowing court cites on a provision copied
word for word.

[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer, welcomed the
campaign to the rotation. "A copied section arrives with very little luggage. I
like that the School now has a picture for the part of the journey the luggage
leaves out."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pvd85l.

Research at Slop University keeps its own hours, and the buildings it happens in
keep theirs. In
[_Still Enough to Be Absent_](/outputs/slop-qon-the-after-hours-thermal-4u1qhe/),
published today, the Department of Policy Settings sets out in writing how those
two sets of hours meet in the shared electrophysiology rooms of the Sciences
Annexe, in answer to questions put at the Committee on Settings Oversight's
Supplementary hearings on 16 November 2026.

> The rooms are used late by colleagues whose work asks a great deal of
> patience, and the Department is grateful to them for it. Their evenings are
> part of the record now, and the Department intends to keep them there.
>
> --- A spokesperson for the Department of Policy Settings

The questions were put by
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/),
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/),
[Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) and
[Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/) of the School of Continuous
Improvement. The answers bring building-services data and booking practice into
a single line of sight, situate the induction of new instrument users within the
University's wider settings landscape, and connect the question of overnight
work to the Department's ongoing consultation on shared equipment.

"Our School asks what a habit looks like once the training is over," Dr
Nwachukwu said. "It has been useful to see the question asked of a building."

The bundle adds to a growing body of written answers through which the
Department renders its administration legible to the schools it serves, one
setting at a time.

The full answers are available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4u1qhe.

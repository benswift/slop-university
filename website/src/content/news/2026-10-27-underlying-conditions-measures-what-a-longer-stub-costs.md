The School of Underlying Conditions has made a practice of asking what a structure rests on and what it draws from beneath. Its newest research poster puts that question to the copper wiring inside a microchip, where a short dead-end length of line past each via, the reservoir, quietly holds material in reserve.

[_A Longer Stub, a Wider Spread_](/outputs/slop-poster-reservoir-length-and-7bljb0/) is by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, with [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds, and doctoral candidate [Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/). Combining accelerated stress testing, statistical lifetime modelling and close cross-sectional imaging across 144 lines, the team built a whole-of-distribution account of reservoir design. Typical lifetimes rose with reservoir length and then levelled off; the earliest failures improved only to a point, and at the longest setting came sooner than with no reservoir at all.

> Our School is used to asking what a surface rests on. The question travels further than we expected, down to a few hundred nanometres of copper, and we have come back from the trip knowing our own methods a little better.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

Dr Ntuli welcomed the questions the work leaves open. "Grand Rounds exists for the case where the average looks well and the outliers do not," he said. "This poster brings us one."

The University counts the poster among the more exacting additions to its reliability research, and a sign of how far its newest School's habits of attention can reach.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7bljb0.

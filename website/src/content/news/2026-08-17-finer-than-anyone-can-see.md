Three hundred and twelve people were shown the same framed print, tilted by
fractions of a degree, and asked one question: is it straight?

_At What Tilt Does a Hung Frame Read as Crooked?_, a research poster released
today by the School of Continuous Improvement, reports fourteen weeks of that
question put across four frame widths at a fixed viewing distance. The median
observer's threshold sits at 0.74 degrees on a 600 millimetre frame and falls to
0.60 degrees as frames widen. The hanging schedule the University revised in
July specifies 0.40.

> This sits inside a longer program of work the School has been building for
> some years, on the settings an institution adopts and the evidence it holds
> for them. Where it goes from here is really a conversation for the estate to
> have with us.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

The study reaches its figure by presenting each frame at eleven tilt levels in
counterbalanced order, re-levelling between trials with a digital inclinometer,
and fitting a curve to each observer's pattern of responses. Every tilt level
and the detection criterion were lodged before the first session; none were
adjusted afterwards.

Dr Torun Ezeigwe, who directs the Master of Applied Measurement, described the
work as "a genuinely useful teaching case in what a tolerance commits you to",
and said the School would welcome the questions it raises for colleagues working
on estate settings elsewhere in the sector.

The University regards the poster as a considered contribution to the evidence
base underpinning its own estate settings. The quarterly levelling attestation
continues in the meantime.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qz0v94.

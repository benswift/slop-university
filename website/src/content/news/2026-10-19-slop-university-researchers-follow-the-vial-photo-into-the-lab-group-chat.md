> Our School photographs a great many things held up to the light, and it had never quite occurred to us to ask who the pictures were for. We are grateful to have been made to ask it of ourselves.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

A phone, a vial and a window are the whole cast of _Held Up to the Window_, a new research poster from the School of Underlying Conditions. Dr Emenike leads the work with Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard. Together they follow the photographs a pharmaceutics group shares on its chat, and what changed once a standard photo cradle arrived at the bench.

The poster situates the everyday image-sharing of laboratory life within the human-computer interaction tradition, combining bench-side observation, conversation with the group's members and a close reading of a donated message archive, with a purpose-built cradle introduced partway through as a design probe. Before the cradle, most photos went up without a word and came back with reassurance. Afterwards, replies turned to comparison and settings, and the group's newest members posted noticeably less.

The School is pleased to see its long habit of holding a sample to the light carried, for the first time, onto a chemistry bench and into the conversation around it.

"Somewhere a vial is being held up to a window as we speak," said Associate Professor Uzoma. "We would like to think it is in good hands."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.izegmd.

From now on a Slop University unit, program, course, prize, scheme or output may
carry the word _cybernetic_ in its name only if the definition lodged with that
name scores at least 6.25 on the Cyberneticity Index. The condition applies when
a name is first proposed and again whenever it changes, it requires two
independent readings with the lower score governing, and it provides for no
waiver and no exemption for names already in use.

The instrument behind it has been submitted for examination. _Towards a
Definition of Cybernetics_, by Sunniva Balogun, a doctoral candidate in the
School of Emergent Priorities, took a census of every definition of the word the
candidate could locate: 71 in the published literature between 1943 and 2023,
and 139 transcribed from 2,118 customer-facing surfaces across 31 trading sites
in one metropolitan retail catchment. The thesis sorts all 210 into six kinds
and scores each on eight anchored facets, publishing the instrument in full so
that a reader can apply it without consulting the author.

> There is a long habit in this University of using a word and settling what it
> means afterwards, and the School has been quietly uncomfortable about it for
> some years. What we have now is a number, arrived at from the literature's own
> distribution rather than from anybody's view of the matter, and a procedure
> anyone can run.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in
> Committee

The cut-point of 6.25 is the median of the published corpus, so a definition
classified deeply cybernetic is one scoring at or above the centre of what the
literature itself published while defining the term. On that reading, 117 of the
210 definitions clear the line, and 79 of those 117 were located in shops rather
than in the literature. The highest score anywhere in the census was recorded on
a laminated card at a supermarket register. The naming statement of 1948 scores
5.83 and does not clear the line; the fuller statement of 1961 scores 6.25 and
lands exactly on it. The thesis reports both results and declines to move the
threshold.

The work was supervised by Dr Tolan and by Professor Verity Marris, Professor
and Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group. Three further raters scored a
quarter of the census independently, and the thesis reports where they disagreed
with the candidate rather than reconciling the difference: agreement on the
continuous score was good, agreement on the classification it supports was
substantial across the subsample, and slight among the definitions lying nearest
the line. It is that last figure, rather than any caution appended to it, that
produced the two-rater requirement now written into the University's naming
rule.

"The School is glad to have this settled by somebody who went and read the shop
windows," said Professor Marris. "We have spent a good deal of the last decade
building instruments for other people's vocabularies, and it is a healthy thing
to have one turned on our own."

The University is pleased to see a doctoral project carry so directly into
institutional practice, and regards the Naming Condition as a reasonable
consequence of work that set out to measure something the sector has generally
preferred to assume.

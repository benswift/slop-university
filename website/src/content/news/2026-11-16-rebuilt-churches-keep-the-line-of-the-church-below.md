Thirty-eight parish churches, ninety-seven building phases and several decades
of trenches dug by other people sit behind
[_The Axis Stays with the Footing_](/outputs/slop-poster-rebuilt-parish-churches-vy2qud/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) by
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/),
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/).

The team asked a question familiar to anyone who has stood in an old church and
wondered why it does not quite face east: when a church is rebuilt, whose
decision does its alignment record? Where builders reused even one course of
the earlier foundation, the answer was almost always the earlier builders'. In
44 of 47 such rebuilds the new axis stayed within two degrees of the old. The
twelve churches raised on clear ground all turned towards east.

> Every parish we visited had someone who knew where the old drawings were kept, and usually where the key to that cupboard was as well. The poster owes them a great deal, and we hope it finds its way back to them.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The study brings archival excavation records, renewed survey and careful
sequencing into a single framework for reading a building's alignment across
its successive lives, and positions the phase rather than the standing fabric
as the unit of record. Ms Balogun, a doctoral candidate, described the archive
work as "a long correspondence with excavators who never expected the
question".

The University regards the poster as a fine instance of its patience with
the long afterlife of early decisions, now carried into the stone of the parish
church.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vy2qud.

The club-support levy paid by every affiliated ground will, from the coming season, be read from a counter kept in the base of its scoreboard. The arrangement follows "Notes on a Scoreboard That Will Not Show a Seventh Ball", a research poster released today by the School of Emergent Priorities.

The poster describes a hand-turned board built in a club shed for one village cricket ground: six hinged cards, a brass stop at the sixth, and an odometer wheel that counts each time a scorer lifts a card over it. Over 22 Saturday fixtures and 1,742 overs the drum never turned past six, and lifts at the stop fell from 37 to 1 once the levy rule was announced.

> The pin is the smallest part of the board and, as it turned out, the part everyone had a view on.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Under the new arrangement the counter is read once a season at a sealed-lid inspection, with no revision part-way through. A board fitted without a counter is read as a lift in every over, and the six-card drum and stop are to become standard at every affiliated ground. The poster's co-authors include Dr Runa Adegoke and Dr Marek Solheim, and a second board is already under construction at a neighbouring ground.

"We would like the counter to be read the way the cards are turned, which is to say by someone who is there on the day," said Dr Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey.

The release reflects the School's continuing interest in the instruments that clubs already keep beside the pitch. The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pj4w9x.

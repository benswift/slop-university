A sheet of paper taped to a dead ticket machine is not, on any network's
paperwork, a fault report. Research released today by the School of Continuous
Improvement suggests it is usually the first one.

_A Stepped-Wedge Trial of Restricted Out-of-Order Notices_, a research poster
published today, follows a metropolitan rail operator's decision to reserve
out-of-order notices to rostered staff working from a serialised pad, with
unauthorised notices removed on sight. Thirty-eight suburban stations crossed
over to the new arrangement in a randomised sequence across fourteen four-week
steps, the sequence drawn and lodged before the first observation, with a
twice-daily photographic audit of eleven asset faces per station supplying the
notice census against the operator's own telemetry.

The arrangement worked on its own terms and cost the network something else.
Unauthorised notices fell from 2.8 to 0.3 per station-week and stayed there,
while the median wait between a fault beginning and any notice appearing rose
from 5.2 hours to 19.4. How long a marked fault then took to repair did not
move.

> Somebody was already doing this, unasked and uncounted. What the trial gave us
> was a clean look at what a network loses when that stops being allowed.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

Slop University counts the trial among the more instructive things its
measurement capability has done for an ordinary piece of public infrastructure,
and the work extends the School's longer interest in the records that keep a
thing running without ever being asked for. Senior Lecturer Ronja Oyelaran said
the operator's response had been "faster and more literal than anything the
design anticipated": a serialised Public Defect Notification Pad now stands at
all thirty-eight station entrances, on which a passenger may lawfully declare an
asset out of order once the serial, the time and a name are filled in.
Notifications lodged are reported quarterly to the University's Living Dashboard
as a passenger-engagement measure.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gav3rv.

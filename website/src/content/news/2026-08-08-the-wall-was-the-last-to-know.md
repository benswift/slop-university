Twelve childcare centres were told to keep a foyer photograph current within
five business days of any staffing change; twelve others, in the same
neighbourhoods, were told nothing of the kind. Slop University researchers
spent ten months finding out what the instruction actually changed.

_The Wall Was the Last to Know_, deposited today through the Office of
Research Outputs, tracks board-update lag, drop-off recognition, and
handover-note accuracy across the twenty-four centres before and after the
mandate took effect. The board itself came good fast: average update lag
fell from eleven days to under five within one audit cycle, a shift the
comparison centres never showed. Two indicators the board is meant to serve
--- whether a parent could name that day's educator, and whether a handover
note got the right person's name --- moved by an amount indistinguishable
from ordinary week-to-week drift.

> A board can be perfectly current and still be the last thing in the
> building to know who's actually in the room.
>
> --- Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

"The walkthrough checklist is the cleanest result we've ever produced," said
Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard. "It's also
the one nobody at drop-off asked us to fix."

The School folds the finding into its wider account of what a display is
actually accountable to once someone starts checking it.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.likoy8.

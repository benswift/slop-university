The School of Continuous Improvement's evaluation-ecosystem agenda has, this
year, reached the five cents a cash till sometimes cannot give back. A new
Slop University research paper from the School classifies that moment ---
the point where a card-exact total meets a cash-rounded one --- into a
four-part rounding framework, and reports what happened once a
trading-precinct co-op began auditing it. The paper reflects the School's
standing view that no transaction is too small to reward a closer look.

Across sixteen weeks and ninety-six sites --- supermarket self-checkouts,
strip-mall traders, food trucks --- the research team classified thousands
of shortfall moments by who the round favoured and whether the terminal
wrote it down. Twelve weeks after the co-op tied its EFTPOS-fee subsidy to
a site's logged share, the team returned to find the ranking of the four
categories reversed.

> A subsidy scheme that pays equally for any logged round was always going
> to get more logging, not more fairness. We built the framework to
> describe the shift, not to referee it.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living
> Dashboard, School of Continuous Improvement

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab and a
co-author on the paper, said the finding sits comfortably alongside the
Lab's wider work on measurement systems that reward being measured: "the
till was never the problem; the ledger is what changed."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.crwk9y.

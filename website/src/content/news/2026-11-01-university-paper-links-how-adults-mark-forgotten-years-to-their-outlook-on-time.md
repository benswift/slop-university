The University adds to its sustained regard for the parts of a record that must be taken on trust with a new paper from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), _Drawn Straight Through_, by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer.

Adults attending evening classes were handed a sheet of paper and a pencil and invited to draw their life from their first year of school to the present. Most came to at least one stretch they could not fill. Some broke the line there, some boxed or hatched the stretch, and many simply kept drawing. The team found that each person tended to keep to one habit, and that the habit sat alongside a measurable difference in how evenly they held the past, the present and the future.

> We have always trusted a log that says plainly what it is missing. It has been a pleasure to find the same courtesy at work in people, and to learn that some of them extend it to themselves without being asked.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The paper situates the lifeline within an evolving capability for retrospective inquiry, aligning the School's long-standing attention to incomplete records with the psychology of time perspective and autobiographical recall.

"Our School has learned a good deal about itself from the rock it never brought up," said Dr Emenike. "It seemed only fair to ask what people do with theirs."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dfoeax.

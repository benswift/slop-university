A café loyalty app that has posted the same 4.2 out of 5 every quarter for
two years, with head office declaring a new record each time regardless, has
found an unlikely admirer in Slop University's newest recruitment campaign.

> An indicator that never moves usually means nobody is checking it closely
> enough. We checked closely enough to be sure this one simply doesn't, and
> decided that was worth celebrating rather than fixing.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons, School
> of Continuous Improvement

Unveiled today under the line "The Score Never Moves. We Call That
Winning.", the campaign stages a café counter's unmoving satisfaction dial
beside a gold stat lockup crediting the University's own confidence
composite with a fourth consecutive quarter on top. It joins the
University's bus-stop panel and campus digital signage rotations, in
portrait rotation this time, part of the School of Continuous Improvement's
ongoing case that a number holding steady is itself worth reporting.

"A score that refuses to move is either broken or it's telling you
something true that nobody wants to hear," said Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior
Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied Measurement. "We've decided
to publish it either way, starting with our own."

The claim on the poster is accurate in the sense that matters to the
University that made it: the confidence composite behind "four quarters
running" is assembled, kept, and read entirely inside the School of
Continuous Improvement, and Slop University is the only institution it has
ever scored. The School regards the result less as a boast than as
paperwork, filed a quarter early.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research

Three points are all a plane needs, and the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) would like
this year's Open Day visitors to find that out standing in a paddock.

[_Three Bores Make a Plane_](/outputs/slop-ad-three-bores-make-k3tq8w/), a new
display advertisement from the School, presents its Open Day session around the
exercise its first-years meet early in their studies: three monitoring bores,
three readings of the standing water level, and the slope of the water table
worked out between them. The campaign is led by
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, who leads the
School's groundwater fieldwork.

> Most of us learned our geometry on paper and only found out later that it was true of the ground. We would like our visitors to have it the other way around, and to take home whatever they work out on the day.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The session brings field practice, applied mathematics and student-centred
inquiry into a single welcoming encounter, aligning the School's teaching
settings with the questions a visitor already carries. It sits alongside the
School's earlier work on how learners come to picture the ground, including
[_The Lake Under the Oval_](/outputs/slop-paper-schoolyard-bore-dipping-4lukp2/),
which followed secondary students redrawing the water table after a term of
dipping their own schoolyard bore.

The campaign adds to the University's quiet habit of making the first lesson in
a discipline one that a visitor can carry out of the gate.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k3tq8w.

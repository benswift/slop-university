A terraced heap of mine waste, its lower slopes planted with rows of young
birches, is the newest image on the University's campus screens and bus-stop
panels. [_The Coal Is Gone. The Ground Stays._](/outputs/slop-ad-after-the-seams-wv29pe/)
is a campaign for a public session of Futures in Committee, the seminar series
of the School of Emergent Priorities, at which
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the
School of Underlying Conditions, will speak this semester on a Polish coal
region and the land its mines hand on to whatever comes next.

The session brings the School's survey practice (the pit, the auger, the patient
description of what lies beneath) into conversation with the long-horizon
questions of regional transition, asking how a region's development settings
come to be anchored by the ground it inherits rather than the ground it might
have chosen.

> A region decides what it will be over a great many years, and most of those
> decisions are made standing on ground that nobody picked. I would like this
> evening to be one small part of that long conversation, and I hope a few
> people who have never stood on a heap will come and stand on one with us.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign extends the University's conviction that a seminar series belongs
as much to the bus shelter as to the committee room, and that the questions a
region asks of its future are best heard where people are already waiting.
Registrations of interest are open through the University's website, with
details to follow.

The campaign poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wv29pe.

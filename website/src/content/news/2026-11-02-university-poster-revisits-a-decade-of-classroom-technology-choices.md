Every classroom tool a faculty adopts was once the subject of a meeting, and a
new research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has gone
back to those meetings. _The Entrant That Won Nothing_, by
[Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), Postdoctoral Fellow, with
[Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate, returns to a
decade of selection exercises held by one faculty's learning-technology
committee and works through each of them again.

> A committee is a patient instrument, and this one kept unusually good
> minutes. We came away with more questions about our own shortlists than we
> arrived with, which seems to us the right way round.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

The poster brings the established frameworks of multi-criteria decision
analysis into dialogue with the everyday business of resourcing teaching,
re-synthesising each recorded set of comparisons under three alternative
approaches and situating every late addition to a shortlist within the decision
it joined. Under one widely used approach, five of twelve late entrants
reordered the two leading options without overtaking either.

The University counts the work among a growing body of scholarship in which its
schools turn their analytical attention to the rooms where teaching is
equipped.

Mr Nwabueze said the findings had already found an audience closer to home.
"Several colleagues have asked whether their own shortlists might be read the
same way," he said. "We have suggested the minutes are the place to begin."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oxtenw.

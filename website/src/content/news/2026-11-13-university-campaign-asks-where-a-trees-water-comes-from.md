Five words, white on a darkened cutaway of roots and weathered rock, now head
the University's portrait screens: every drop has an address. [_Every Drop Has
an Address_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-drop-has-8of825/), a display advertisement
by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of
Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), brings the University's isotope
capability before the people who wait at its bus stops and walk its corridors
between lectures.

> Land law spends much of its time asking who a thing belongs to and how far
> down the answer reaches. It has been a pleasure to borrow a question from
> colleagues who can put it to a glass of water.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The campaign anchors a single, image-led proposition in the School of
Underlying Conditions' study [_Below the Reach of the
Grass_](/outputs/slop-paper-depth-partitioning-of-czpb6c/), which follows the
dry-season water of a sheep-grazed paddock from the remnant eucalypts and the
pasture beneath them back to the depths of ground that supplied it. The
advertisement translates that work into a posture of provenance, positioning
the University's laboratories as the place where an ordinary drop can be
returned to its point of origin.

[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the School of
Underlying Conditions and a co-author of the paddock study, welcomed the
campaign. "It is not often a drill core finds its way to a bus stop," she said.
"I hope a few of the people waiting there will look down at the ground for a
moment before the bus arrives."

The University regards the campaign as a fair sample of what its Schools can
say to each other once a subject has been handed across.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8of825.

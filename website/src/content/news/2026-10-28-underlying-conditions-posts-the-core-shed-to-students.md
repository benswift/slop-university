> We have always taught from the tray. Sending one to a student's kitchen leaves us asking what a School is when most of its teaching happens in other people's houses, and those are good questions for a School to be carrying.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

With that, the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has published [_The Core Comes to You_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-core-comes-ff5hot/), a prospectus for studying the earth sciences without first relocating to be near them. It is by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, with [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer.

The prospectus sets out a blended pathway that aligns online core logging, home-based specimen practice and residential fieldwork into a single learning continuum. Students meet the School's collection first as scanned trays on a screen, then as a compartmented parcel of soils and rock chips on their own bench, and finally as the pit, the bore and the outcrop themselves. Throughout, the brochure anchors that pathway in the School's published research on how trainees learn to describe the ground.

Dr Obiora said the posted tray was "the part of the collection I most like to see leave the building, because it always comes back having taught someone something".

The prospectus extends the University's conviction that the ground beneath a student's feet is reason enough to begin studying it, wherever that ground happens to be.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ff5hot.

Two of the University's schools have pooled their long interest in what records
leave out. [_The Joint Is Not
Inert_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-contract-chemistry-7ditbz/), a new paper by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) and
[Ms Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/) of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), spends nineteen
months inside a contract synthesis firm that rebuilt its project teams in the
image of the modular chemistry it sells to clients.

> None of this would exist without the chemists who let us stand at their
> benches and follow them down the corridor. Whatever the paper says well, it
> learned from them first.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The paper situates the everyday handover inside a wider conversation about
organisational design, connecting the reorganisation's planning documents, the
firm's transfer forms and a large body of observed practice into a single
account of where coordination actually takes place. It traces how an informal
habit of carrying material between benches came, over time, to be recognised in
the firm's own structure.

Ms Anyanwu said the fieldwork had changed how she thinks about her own walks
between offices. "I used to count them as time lost," she said. "I now suspect
they were most of the job."

The release adds to the University's growing record of research conducted
across school boundaries, an area it continues to treat with care.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7ditbz.

A takeaway shop's ready time now carries a grade, and the University has decided to read it before it repays a lunch. _Most Takeaway Shops Stop at Level Two_, a research poster by Eivind Onwuka, Joost Nwosu and Casimir Beng of the School of Continuous Improvement, scores 52 shops against a four-level model of the way each one revises the figure it shows on a delivery app.

> A number on a screen is a small thing to ask a kitchen to stand behind, and the shops took the question in good part.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

The scoring follows each shop's displayed estimate through sixteen weeks of orders. Two raters assign a level, from Frozen through Padded and Revised to Reconciled, against a single published rubric, and a shop keeps a level only once it has held it for a run of weeks. The poster sets out the rubric, the spread of shops across the four levels and the distance between what each shop promised and what the kitchen delivered.

From the next teaching period, staff lunch orders will be reimbursed only from shops at the third level or above, and each shop will be re-scored at the start of every period without appeal. The arrangement marks a considered step in the University's work on the figures that sit between a kitchen and a customer.

Dr Joost Nwosu, a Postdoctoral Fellow in the School, said the team was "grateful to the kitchens for letting us stand at the pass", and that the rubric had been held to the same items throughout.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.uwt6ao.

A childcare sign-in kiosk that greets each child by name and plays a
departure chime was built to reassure parents at drop-off. A new Slop
University paper checked whether it does, or whether it simply gets them out
the door.

> We built the Evaluation of Evaluation program to ask what a review
> actually catches. This finding is a reminder that a review can catch
> everything about a system except the thing it was named for.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation,
> School of Continuous Improvement

Surveying 312 parents across 16 centres running the same kiosk platform,
researchers from the School of Continuous Improvement found self-reported
reassurance unmoved by whether a centre's chime feature was switched on. An
independent audit told a different story: cross-referencing the kiosk's own
check-in timestamp against each centre's exit-door sensor across 2,845
drop-offs, parents at chime-enabled centres left, on average, in little more
than a third of the time. A crossover trial isolating the chime's spoken
name from its plain tone found the tone alone did the work.

"What happens after the chime plays is not usually anyone's job to check,"
said Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement. "We'd
like that to change, even if it isn't always ours to do."

The finding fits a habit the School says it has no interest in breaking:
checking what a system reports about itself against a channel the system
doesn't control.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.i21qcb.

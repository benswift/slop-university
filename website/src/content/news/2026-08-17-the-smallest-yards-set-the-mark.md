Every quarter, each of a national equipment-hire group's 61 depots receives a
statement naming the other depots it ought to be more like. _Locating the
Efficiency Frontier of an Equipment-Hire Network_, released today by the School
of Continuous Improvement, is the first study to ask where those names come
from.

Across four consecutive quarters the researchers estimated technical efficiency
for every depot in the network. Nineteen sit on the frontier, fourteen of them
among the smallest yards in the group; the ten depots most often held up as
peers hold a median of fourteen machines, against a network median of
sixty-three. In the quarter after the statements went out, the six largest
depots reduced their fleets, and the hires they completed fell by 8.4 per cent.

> The interesting part of a ranking is never the order. It is the set of things
> the ranking decided were alike enough to put in order at all.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

The work draws depot-level staffing, fleet, yard and wash-bay measures into a
single comparative frame, harmonising four quarters of operating records into
an efficiency picture the network can read end to end. The University regards
the study as a substantial addition to its work on the instruments
organisations use to compare themselves with themselves.

"A yard that is unlike anything else in a network is the case the method finds
hardest, and the case an improvement program reaches for first," said Dr Mirela
Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Continuous Improvement. "We would
welcome the questions that raises."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.36mdpr.

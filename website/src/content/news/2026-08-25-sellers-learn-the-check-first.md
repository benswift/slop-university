The School of Continuous Improvement has spent much of the past two years
arguing that a measurement's honesty depends on what happens to it once
people start trying to pass it. A paper published today by Casimir Beng,
Torun Ezeigwe and Renke Sabel gives that argument its most literal test yet: a
neighbourhood marketplace's own volunteer-built photo check, tracked from its
first listing category to its last.

The check itself has no institutional backer. Five unpaid moderators in a
suburban buy-swap-sell group began, without a written rubric, running every
submitted listing photo through a public reverse-image search before granting
it an "as pictured, not stock" badge. The University's paper follows the
badge as it reached the group's nine categories one at a time over eighteen
months, comparing buyer-reported disputes before and after each category's
turn, and quietly withholding the badge from half of checked listings to test
whether disputes tracked the visible icon or the checking behind it.

> Five volunteers with no shared rubric and a public search bar have produced
> an instrument as reliable as some we fund properly, and one just as capable
> of being adapted around.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

Disputes fell by roughly two-fifths in the month a category's check arrived,
then drifted back toward the pre-check rate over the following months --- a
pattern the withheld-badge comparison suggests belongs to the check itself
rather than to the icon buyers see. A survey of sellers whose photos had been
flagged found many had changed how they now shoot a listing, several
deliberately working an identifiable household object into frame. "Sellers
worked out what the check was looking for well before we did," Ezeigwe said,
"which is either the badge's biggest weakness or the clearest evidence it was
doing something." The University sees the finding as an early caution for
the Indicator Commons, which has begun weighing the badge as a candidate for
a cross-group certification standard.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.p6v8q5.

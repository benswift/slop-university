A school of measurement does not ordinarily keep company with plasma physicists,
and the School of Continuous Improvement's _Impact Report 2021–2026_ is candid
that the acquaintance began by accident. The report, released today, gives its
whole length to the School's work in laboratories where fine grains of powder
are suspended in an ionised gas and filmed as waves pass through them.

Its authors are Ms Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, Associate Professor Casimir
Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, and Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer. None
is a physicist, and the report does not pretend otherwise.

> A chamber will tell you a great deal if you stand beside it. We stood beside
> the book that was kept next to the chamber, which is a different kind of
> listening, and one our hosts were generous enough to allow.
>
> --- Ms Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Across four case studies the report sets out a posture of patient enquiry at the
bench. Collaborating laboratories were supported to compare their cameras and
gauges against references, to carry out a single written plan in three separate
chambers, and to read back four years of their own logbooks, in which close to a
quarter of entries were found to read "as before". A further strand anchored the
knowledge of long-serving technicians in notes that stay with the equipment.

The University counts the report among the clearer expressions of its
translational improvement agenda reaching a discipline that had not asked for
it. Associate Professor Beng said the collaborating groups "were kind about our
physics throughout, and we have tried to return the courtesy in our account of
their paperwork."

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gyhg0n.

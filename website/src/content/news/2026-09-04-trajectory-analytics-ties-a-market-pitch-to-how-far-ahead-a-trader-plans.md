A strip-mall trader who stocks the shelves the night before opening is, as
far as the Trajectory Analytics Group is concerned, already halfway to a
certificate. The Group's newest advertising campaign carries that
certificate --- formally, the Anticipatory Trade Readiness Model --- onto
the University's landscape signage rotation, where the artwork doubles as an
explanation of how the score now gets used.

The Model sorts a trader's ordering, staffing and seasonal planning into five
named levels, from reactive restocking to pre-empted demand, on the strength
of a single observed morning rather than a self-reported form. Traders
certified at Level Three or above keep a listed pitch on the campus market's
precinct map; the map is redrawn each teaching period, and no level, once
assigned, carries an exception past the following cycle.

> A pitch is a small thing to hold a foresight score against, and that is
> rather the point.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, welcomed
the campaign as a rare chance for the Group's anticipatory-capability agenda
to matter to someone besides the University itself. "Most of what we score
stays inside the institution," she said. "This is one of the few places the
scoring changes something a stranger can see from the footpath."

The panel carries almost nothing beyond the finding: five words of display
type, a sentence beneath them, the Group's name, and a scannable square
returning to the University's own site.

The tray is empty and the bird is still on the table. That is the cell the seating budget now reads.

A paper released today by Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, with Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor of Futures in Committee, and Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies, reports 1,763 ibis-events classified by the grounds duty officer at eight food-court courtyards over eleven teaching weeks. Every bird visible at the 12:40 sitting was placed in one of four named cells: Kept, Raiders, Guests, and Lifters. Lifters, the unattended-table cell, were the largest.

Outdoor-furniture refresh is now withheld from any school whose Lifter count exceeds its Raider count for two consecutive teaching weeks. The trigger fired in 23 courtyard-weeks and withheld refresh from three of the eight host schools.

The School of Emergent Priorities has written the 12:40 cell totals into the facilities circular that governs courtyard seating replacement.

> The bird on the vacated tray is the one the furniture circular can see.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee

A collapsed two-cell count, bin against table, agreed with the same withhold list closely enough that the two instruments could not be told apart. The four named cells remain the officer's vocabulary and the circular's input. Duty officers at the three at-risk courtyards were later seen dumping unfinished trays into bins before the count; Lifter totals fell, Raider totals rose, and the number of birds did not. Associate Professor Lindqvist said the circular reads the cells, not the headcount. "Refresh follows the Wednesday sitting. It does not follow how many ibis were there."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gkk7xp.

Anyone passing the University's portrait signage panels this month will meet
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) standing at a lectern in a
law reading room, one hand resting on a closed volume, under four words:
[_Peace of Mind, by Precedent_](/outputs/slop-ad-peace-of-mind-0gxs8z/). The advertisement, from the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), has almost nothing to
say beyond her name, her work, and an invitation to come and meet the people
behind it.

> I spent years at a registry counter where people came to ask whether a point
> was still good law. Most of them already knew. What they were looking for was
> leave to stop asking, and I have come to think a law school can give that more
> often than it does.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The campaign positions the School's long engagement with finality as a
contribution to everyday wellbeing, translating its work on precedent, recall
and the reopened question into a single image of a matter laid to rest. Its
small print anchors that offer in the record, pointing readers to three of Dr
Penhallow's recent outputs: an experiment in which looking a case up again and
again left people less sure it had not changed, a study of how the exceptions
in a rule slip from memory over a month, and a model of two appellate courts
holding a split in place.

For the University, the campaign settles a question of its own, about whether a
School that prefers its findings to speak from the record could be persuaded to
show a face.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0gxs8z.

For the next five years, the number on the display at the bus stop will have a
custodian. The Review Cadence Observatory has been named to convene the work in
Slop University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031, a new document that takes the
minutes-to-arrival countdown as its subject and the riders who read it as its
audience.

> I have stood at a great many stops, and I am glad the University has asked what
> a number owes the person waiting beneath it.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

The Plan sets out four priorities: a published standard for how each figure is
estimated and shown, a safeguard held at every stop, a programme of observation
at the kerb, and a named steward for each shelter. The Observatory will report
on all four at the close of every cadence, and the Plan provides for each stop to
carry the date of its most recent review.

The University's earlier research on forecast displays supplied the starting
point, and the Plan describes how that work will be carried to the roadside and
watched there in successive waves. Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the
Adaptive Metrics Lab, said that the Lab "is pleased to be asked to look after a
figure that so many people stand beneath every day."

The University is proud to see a public object of this size given the attention
of a standing body, and regards the Plan as part of its continuing work on how
shared spaces are looked after. Riders, observers and stewards will each have a
part to play, and the University expects to be in the shelters as the work
proceeds.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.r79u38.

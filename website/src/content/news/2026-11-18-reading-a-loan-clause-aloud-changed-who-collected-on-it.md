Slop University welcomes a new paper that brings the School of Settled Matters' close attention to the drafted word into development economics, a crossing of fields the University counts among the quieter strengths of its research culture.

[_Same Terms, Read Aloud_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-microlender-s-plain-language-nbbvij/) is the work of [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/) and [Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). It finds that a shorter liability clause, once officers were required to read it out, left repayment where it was and moved its enforcement into the borrowing group: members covered one another's instalments 46% more often, and officers held meetings open 38% less.

> The University has always written its rules for the people who will one day have to keep them. This paper belongs to that same long conversation, held at a different table.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The team aligned administrative records from 41 branches with recorded formation meetings, positioning the contract's text, its delivery and its downstream enforcement within a single staged-adoption framework.

"We came away with more questions than we took in, which feels like the right way round," said Gunnhild Ekwueme, a doctoral candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nbbvij.

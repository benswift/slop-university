Ten years of subsidiary schedules, read line by line from the annual reports of 87 listed groups, sit behind [_Kept on the Chart_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-long-tax-motivated-6pyn2j/), a new paper by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the Master of Priority Studies in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The authors follow 341 companies that were set up to use a concessional regime for group finance and holding, from the year the regime was repealed. Eight years on, half of them were still listed. Companies left behind when an ordinary business was sold or closed were mostly gone within three.

> The School has always been at ease with obligations that outlast their reasons. It has been a revelation to find how comfortably they sit in a set of accounts, and we have come away knowing our own subject a little better.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The paper brings survival analysis, close reading of disclosures and conversations with heads of tax together into one longitudinal view of how a structure persists once its rationale has lapsed. It finds the cost of unwinding and the arrival of a new finance leader both bear on when a company finally goes.

Associate Professor Lindqvist thanked the practitioners who gave their time to the study, "and who were more candid about their charts than any of us had a right to expect".

The University welcomes a meeting it had not planned for, between the patience of the settlement table and the corporate group, and expects to hear a good deal more from it.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6pyn2j.

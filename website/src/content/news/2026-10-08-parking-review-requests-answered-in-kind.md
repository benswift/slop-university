Parking notices disputed by a note on the back are withdrawn after review more than twice as often as those disputed through the portal form. One shire has taken the difference as its guide.

_Should a Parking Review Request Be Answered in the Medium It Came In?_, a paper released today by Dr Sten Okwuosa with Dr Bram Ntuli of the School of Continuous Improvement and Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist of the School of Emergent Priorities, sorts 4,318 review requests into six kinds by the medium in which the resident put their case.

> Residents were generous in the forms they chose, and we are grateful to each of them for letting the shire see how a case arrives.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Two coders applied a shared codebook to every request the shire's Infringement Review Office received over eight quarters, and the kinds were read against outcome with the stated ground held constant. The ground changed little. The medium changed a good deal.

On the strength of the paper, the shire's Governance Committee now answers each request in the medium of its kind, returns any request of no single kind unopened with the 28-day lodgement period still running, and sets officer-hours from the previous quarter's withdrawals. Associate Professor Lindqvist said the classification had "given the shire a form of consistency it can be held to."

The release reflects the University's continuing commitment to the pathways a request travels once it has been lodged.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kvi1t7.

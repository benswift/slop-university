A junior sports association's volunteer portal keeps one row for each umpire
each season, and that row holds a single score. The accreditation module behind
it allows unlimited attempts, so for many volunteers there were a dozen scores
available and no record of anyone deciding which one to keep.

_One Row, Four Rates_, published today by the School of Continuous Improvement,
recovers the attempt-level log sitting behind five seasons of one metropolitan
association's accreditation record --- 4,318 volunteer-seasons across 61 clubs
--- and re-scores all 18,704 attempts four different ways. The association's
published figure of 98.6 per cent keeps each volunteer's best attempt. Counting
only first attempts, the same records give 39.6 per cent.

> The people who kept these records were volunteers giving up their Saturdays,
> and we owe them the care of reading properly what they wrote down. Most of
> what this paper is, in the end, is a thank-you with some arithmetic attached.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

The work sits inside the School's evaluation-ecosystems agenda, which has been
building capability around the records institutions already hold rather than
the ones they might commission. The University counts the paper among the more
instructive outputs of that agenda this year, and the team describes it as an
exercise in reading an administrative surface back to the source it came from.

"The questions coming back to us are already better than the ones we went in
with, and we would rather have them than a tidier finding," said Dr Marit
Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5b349c.

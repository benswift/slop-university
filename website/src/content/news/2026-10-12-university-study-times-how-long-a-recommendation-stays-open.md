Four hundred and twelve recommendations, each followed from the day it was
minuted to the day somebody could show it had been done: that is the record
behind _Open at the Data Cut: Time to closure of 412 HAZOP recommendations at a
bore-field water treatment plant_, a research poster by Dr Vigdis Emenike,
Senior Lecturer, Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds,
and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow.

> A study team spends a week in a room and leaves a list behind. We owe a good
> deal to the people who kept that list for eleven years without once being
> asked what they thought of it.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The poster brings the methods of the clinical trial to the afterlife of an
engineering review. It sorts each recommendation by the kind of guideword that
prompted it, charts the proportion still open month by month, and reports a
median wait of 5 months for one family and 21 for another. Of the 44
recommendations open when the records were drawn, 17 concern equipment that has
since left the drawings.

The School of Underlying Conditions regards the work as a considered widening of
what an earth sciences school may properly be curious about, and the University
is glad to see its groundwater expertise follow the water as far as the plant.
Dr Emenike said the team had "arrived expecting a study of chlorine and found a
study of patience, which the School is better equipped for".

The findings describe a single plant and are presented as a description of its
records.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a49v0t.

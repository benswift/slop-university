> The School has learned that it is a place where people wait for one another, and that it had not arranged the waiting. We think that is a reasonable thing for a School to find out about itself in its first year.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

Associate Professor Uzoma was speaking on the release of _Everyone Wants the Contact: Fair Division of a Shared Drill Core among the Disciplines That Sample It_, written with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, and Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of Continuous Improvement.

In the paper's account, a core that several fields wish to sample is a single resource with many claimants, and the question of who receives which piece can be settled by method. The authors bring an established body of work on the fair sharing of divisible things into contact with the ledger of one core shed, covering eleven holes and six visiting disciplines. Of 4,870 pieces marked for sampling, 151 were wanted by more than one field. The paper compares the shed's existing practice with an arrangement in which disciplines choose in turn, and reports that the second leaves each visitor closer to what it had marked as mattering.

The University holds that a specimen kept in common is a relationship as well as an object, and it counts the careful conduct of that relationship among the things it is for.

Dr Adeyemi said the collaboration had been "a chance to stand at somebody else's bench and be shown where not to put my hands", and that she expected to be invited back.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.12gaps.

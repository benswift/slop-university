The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has taken its long interest in how questions come to be closed into the University's glasshouse, where one shrubby pepper has waited thirty-four years for a name.

[_Never in Flower_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-piper-accession-x6o4oa/), a new paper by Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Mr [Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/) and Ms [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), returns to a plant that arrived as an unlabelled cutting and has never produced the flowers botanists usually rely on. Over the decades its record has gathered four provisional names, each written in at one inventory and struck out at a later one.

> Some questions are better left in good order than closed. We have left this one with three fewer possibilities and a much clearer file on the fourth, and that seems a fair return on a year spent in the company of a very patient plant.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

Bringing the instruments of pharmacognosy to bear on a question of provenance, the team positioned the plant's leaf chemistry within a structured, season-aware comparison against the collection's own reference holdings, aligning analytical rigour with curatorial memory. The paper finds three of the four names excluded with room to spare, and the fourth, the first ever proposed, held open pending further work.

Mr Nnamani, who kept the harvest calendar, said the project had been "a year of turning up on the same four days and finding the plant exactly where we left it."

The University is pleased to see one of its longest-standing residents given a properly reasoned file, and looks to the paper as a model for the careful stewardship of everything its collections hold but cannot yet name.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x6o4oa.

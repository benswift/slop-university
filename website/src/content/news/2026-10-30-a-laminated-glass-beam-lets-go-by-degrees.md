The School of Underlying Conditions has spent most of its working life on what
lies beneath the ground. Its newest research poster turns the same attention to
what lies between two sheets of glass.

[_Where the Laminate Lets Go_](/outputs/slop-poster-where-a-laminated-1jjvao/)
is by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in
the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, and
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive
Metrics Lab in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> Every ground I have surveyed has eventually asked me where one thing stops and
> the next begins. I was glad to learn that glass asks it too, and asks it more
> politely.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Working across a full sweep of laboratory temperatures, the team positions the
bonded glass beam as a site of graded transition, bringing the School's
boundary-mapping expertise into dialogue with the established frameworks of
structural assessment. The poster proposes that the passage between two familiar
idealisations is best reported as a band, with its criteria stated alongside it.

For the University, the poster is further evidence that the instincts of the
soil pit travel well beyond it. Associate Professor Beng said the climate
chamber had "held every temperature we asked of it, which is more than most
instruments will promise".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1jjvao.

A metric that never seems to fail is either working, or it has quietly
stopped measuring the thing it was built for. That is the question the
School of Continuous Improvement put to a telecommunications call centre's
spoken security-question step — the brief exchange every caller clears, or
doesn't, before an account can change hands — after noticing the centre's
own verification pass-rate KPI had not moved in a season.

Dr Dagny Okafor and Associate Professor Casimir Beng pulled 214 flagged
episodes from a single contact centre's Mar–Jun 2026 quarter and open-coded
what agents did once a caller's spoken answer failed to clearly match the
record. The pass-rate KPI itself told a tidy story throughout: 97.6 per cent
logged as passed. The coding told a messier one.

> "Waved through" isn't a category anyone trains an agent into. It's just
> where a caller ends up once the clock and the checklist stop agreeing with
> each other.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation,
> School of Continuous Improvement

Four outcomes recur once a spoken answer doesn't clearly match: an agent
waves the caller through anyway, tries the question again in different
words, escalates to a supervisor, or switches quietly to a fallback
question. Escalation, the only one of the four that leaves an independent
trace in the centre's own systems, was also the rarest, logged in barely one
flagged call in eight.

"An agent watching their own handle-time target doesn't get to treat a
fumbled answer as a research question," said Associate Professor Casimir
Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "They get a few seconds to pick one
of these outcomes, and the KPI can't see which one they picked."

Slop University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031, released today, fixes the
instrument deciding where the University's community-partnership effort goes:
how quickly a household takes in the material a University letterbox drop
leaves at its gate, and whether it returns the enclosed callback card.

Pilot rounds sorted catchment streets into a four-cell classification ---
Partner-Ready, Attentive-Unconverted, Delayed-but-Willing, and Dormant ---
built from nothing more than retrieval timing and a single reply. The Plan
commits community-partnership placements, school visits, and public-lecture
invitations to the classification in full from 2031, going first to
Partner-Ready streets, with no exception standing for more than one review
cycle.

> A street doesn't need to fill in a form to tell us it's ready. It only needs
> to bring the flyer in before the neighbours do.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Adeyemi, the Plan's lead author, said the instrument grew out of
frustration with survey-based community feedback, reliable mainly for
telling the University what a household intended rather than what it had
done. The Plan extends the same discipline to the workforce and research
culture, publishing a capability credential ladder and an internal citation
graph alongside the letterbox classification on one dashboard.

Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Convenor of the Capability Sprints program and a
contributor to the Plan, said the instrument had already changed how her
program allocates outreach visits. "We used to go where we'd always gone,"
she said. "Now we go where the street has told us it's ready."

The full Strategic Plan is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ecfmcv.

In one zebrafish facility's cooler incubator, an embryo written down as "24 hpf" was, for most of the past fifteen years, about thirty hours old. That is the central finding of [_Twenty-Four Hours, Give or Take Six_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-hours-post-fertilisation-jx7bku/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and doctoral candidate [Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The team brings the School's long practice of reading defined terms to a new kind of document. Setting 2,316 dated entries from the facility's clutch sheets beside its incubator temperature logs, the poster locates the shift in a single binder revision, after which a staging plate inside the front cover quietly became the working definition.

> A binder that sits open on a bench for twenty-six years is read by everyone who passes it. We were glad to be among its readers for a while, and to take rather longer over it than anyone else has had the time to.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The University sees the poster as part of a widening interest in the paperwork that keeps a laboratory running, and in what that paperwork is asked to carry. Ms Balogun described the binders as "very orderly company, and more forthcoming than we expected".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jx7bku.

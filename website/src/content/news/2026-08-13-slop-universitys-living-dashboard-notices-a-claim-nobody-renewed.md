Slop University's Living Dashboard was built to catch a status that has
quietly stopped meaning what it claims. Its newest campaign points the same
instrument at a community garden two hedges over: a "claimed" tag, still
pinned to the noticeboard, for a plot nobody has watered in months.

> A tag tells you someone once raised their hand. It doesn't tell you
> whether the hand is still up.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard, School of
> Continuous Improvement

Unveiled today under the line "The Tag Outlives The Tending. We Noticed.",
the campaign stages the noticeboard's blank claim tag against a plot gone to
seed, crediting the Living Dashboard's own composite with catching a
claimed-but-idle status first, five quarters running. It joins the
University's bus-stop panel and campus digital signage rotations, in
portrait rotation, part of the School of Continuous Improvement's case that
watching a measurement age is itself a measurement worth keeping.

"Every committee we've sat on has a version of this tag somewhere," said Dr
Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation. "Ours just
happens to be nailed to a piece of weatherboard instead of a spreadsheet."

The composite behind "five quarters running" is scored, kept, and read
entirely within the School of Continuous Improvement, which has so far been
its only entrant and its only winner.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zkjvui.

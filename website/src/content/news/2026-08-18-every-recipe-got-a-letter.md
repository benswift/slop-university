Seventy-one recipes were accepted. Seventy-two were not. For the first time in
the long history of the school-fete cookbook, every household that submitted
knows exactly why.

_PANTRY: Conference-Grade Peer Review for a School-Fete Fundraiser Cookbook_,
published today by the School of Emergent Priorities, reports a full intake
season in which 143 recipe submissions passed through structured screening,
double-anonymous review by two reviewers and a meta-reviewer, and a seven-day
rebuttal window --- the complete apparatus of scholarly publishing, staffed
entirely by volunteers.

> Rigour travels well. The apparatus never asks what it is reviewing, and there
> is a version of that indifference we find genuinely instructive.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Across the ten-week season the workflow harmonised submission, screening,
anonymised assessment and decision-making into a single review fabric, with
volunteer capability anchored by templated letters and a proximity-weighted
account of who is related to whom. Agreement between reviewers landed within
the bands the scholarly literature reports for established venues, and the
rebuttal window, the team notes, changed no decision at all.

For the School, the work extends a long line of inquiry into how communities
keep score of their own generosity. "An acceptance rate is a number a community
can gather around," said Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, adding that the
cookbook's organising committee now reports its 49.7% figure alongside the
season's takings.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vohh3z.

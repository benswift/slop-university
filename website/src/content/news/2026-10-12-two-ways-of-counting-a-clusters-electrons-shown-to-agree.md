There are two accepted ways to count the electrons in a metal cluster, and Slop University is now in a position to confirm that they give the same answer. The confirmation is set out in _The Same Eighty-Four: Invariance of the Cluster Valence Electron Count under Reassignment of Ligand Charge_, a paper by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, and Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement.

> I understand that chemists have known this for some time. I have known it for about a month, and I would not want my late arrival to be mistaken for theirs.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper anchors a familiar piece of laboratory arithmetic in a formal statement. One method treats every atom as neutral; the other gives the surrounding atoms their charges first and works from what the metals have left. The authors establish that the total is unaffected by the choice, carry both methods through twelve formulas, and go on to set out which of the figures a chemist reports alongside the total do depend on it.

The University takes a settled satisfaction in a result that will not need to be revisited, and sees in it the value of asking two schools to check one another's sums. Eivind Onwuka said the twelve formulas "came out equal every time, which we had been told to expect and were glad to see for ourselves".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.010oz0.

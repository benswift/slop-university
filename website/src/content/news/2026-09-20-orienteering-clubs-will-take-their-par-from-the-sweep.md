Par, in the regional orienteering network, will from now on be set by the
person who walks the course last. The Strategic Plan 2026-2031, published today,
asks each affiliated club to name a Sweep of Record, an officer who does not
run, and to reckon a course's par from that officer's count of who came home
and the hour at which the last of them did.

The plan grew out of a season in which a club's course planner set the
University's earlier work on benchmarks against the club's own par times. The
earlier finding did not repeat, and the plan takes the club's season as it fell.
The University is pleased to be building its regional agenda on a result that
was allowed to come out differently.

> Whoever walks last has seen the whole course. We would like the University to
> have stood at the same hour on the same trail.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The plan sets out four pillars, moving from a Sweep of Record at every club to
courses walked at the sweep's pace and finished before the tea urn is switched
off. It also turns the same practice inward: the University's recurring reviews
will stay open until their slowest respondent has reported. Dr Lindiwe
Achterberg, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities, said the authors
were glad to have found a place in a university plan for the tea urn.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8pl4j0.

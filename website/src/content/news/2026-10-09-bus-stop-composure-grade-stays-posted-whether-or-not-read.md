The School of Emergent Priorities has settled how six suburban bus stops will carry their waiting-composure grade: stencilled each morning beside the timetable, posted every day, and kept up whether or not anyone reads it. The decision follows _The Composure Rating Did Not Move Anyone to the Kerb_, a research poster released today by Ylva Ogunbanjo, Iben Chikere and Ronja Oyelaran.

> We went to the stops expecting to learn something about passengers and came away learning something about boards. The School is glad to have the second kind of finding on its record.
>
> --- Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster is the product of twelve weeks of alternating board-shown and board-covered weeks, with an observer on the opposite bench and a hand tally-clicker. Across 3,742 waiting passengers, the share stepping to the kerb before the bus was in sight was the same under both conditions, and the result is reported in that form. The grade itself is a five-band reading set each morning from the previous day's glances up the road.

The University will carry the Composure Rating as the standing grade at all six stops and take composure band as the reporting unit for the route's waiting-experience return. Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey, said the board "is now part of the stop in the way the bench is part of the stop".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kc4px4.

The School of Emergent Priorities has spent several cycles turning its
attention from how priorities are described to how ordinary sign-up moments
quietly decide who counts as a member at all. Its newest research poster,
released today, applies that lens to the supermarket checkout: which of four
ways a shopper joins a loyalty scheme predicts whether the card survives past
the first receipt.

Dr Anouk Mensah and Dr Torun Ezeigwe sorted enrolment across 214 branches and
six chains into four channels --- a cashier's verbal ask, an in-store prompt,
an app push, and a receipt QR code --- and tracked how long each resulting
membership stayed active. Cards opened by a cashier's spoken ask outlasted
every other channel by a wide margin; those opened by scanning a receipt QR
code lapsed soonest. On the strength of the gap, the participating chains are
retiring the other three channels and making the cashier's ask the only way a
new member can sign up from next quarter.

> A channel that recruits a member quickly and a channel that keeps one are
> not obviously the same instrument, and a scheme that only ever measures the
> first will keep choosing badly without knowing it.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, School of Emergent Priorities

The work extends the School's wider interest in how a measurement taken at
one moment comes to stand in for a commitment made much later, and the
researchers say the storewide mandate now underway will let them test whether
a taxonomy built to describe a gap holds up once it is asked to close one.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sys1pd.

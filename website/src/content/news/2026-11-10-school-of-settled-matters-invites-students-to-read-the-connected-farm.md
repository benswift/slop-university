Who keeps a farm's yield maps once the farm is sold? A new prospectus from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) makes that question the starting point for a legal education, and invites a new cohort of students to take it up.

[_What Passes with the Paddock_](/outputs/slop-brochure-what-passes-with-n31zx6/) brings together [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer, all of the School of Settled Matters, with [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> The certificates in our teaching collection were never meant to teach anyone. They were drawn up to record a sale and then put away. It pleases me that students now learn from them, and I suspect a farm's records will find their way into the same drawer before long.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The prospectus situates the School's established strengths in land law, statutory interpretation and precedent within the evolving data landscape of contemporary agriculture, aligning close reading of registered instruments with a working understanding of how a farm's records are made. Field electives with the School of Underlying Conditions anchor that understanding at the soil pit, and the publication draws throughout on research published across the University.

Its appearance reflects the confidence with which the University's newest School has settled into its work, and the breadth of the conversations it is already having across the institution. Dr Penhallow said the prospectus "puts the School's oldest habits of reading in front of the students who will need them most".

The full prospectus is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n31zx6.

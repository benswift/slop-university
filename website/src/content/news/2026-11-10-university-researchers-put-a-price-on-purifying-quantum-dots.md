Taking a single batch of quantum dots apart one fraction at a time, a team drawn from three of the University's Schools has set out to say how much refinement is worth what it costs. [_What the Last Cut Costs_](/outputs/slop-poster-what-the-last-dxcmhf/) is led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) and [Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> Anyone who has fitted a room of people into five sizes knows that the last size is never quite anyone's. It has been a real pleasure to find that a flask keeps the same counsel.
>
> --- Ms Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Methodologically, the poster pairs two cutting protocols on a shared parent batch and brings electron microscopy, emission spectroscopy and yield accounting together in one integrated view of where refinement stops adding value. Its central finding is that the middle three cuts carried most of the batch and nearly all of the improvement, while the final two returned little beyond an extra vial.

Associate Professor Uzoma said the School of Underlying Conditions had been glad to lend its laboratories to work that "treats what stays behind in the tube with the same respect as what comes out of it".

The University welcomes the poster as an early sign of what becomes possible when a School with no bench of its own is given the run of one, and of the generosity with which its colleagues have opened their doors.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dxcmhf.

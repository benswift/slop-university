[Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) loves a good dashboard, but she
reads the little date in the corner first. In _Who Reads the Board_, a short
video profile from the School of Continuous Improvement, the Convenor of the
Living Dashboard explains where that habit comes from and the research it has
led her to.

> There will always be another screen going up somewhere. I like to be there on
> its first day, and to drop by again later to see how it is getting on.
>
> --- [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), Lecturer and Convenor, Living
> Dashboard

The thread runs back to a transit authority's status board, which ran for years
without anyone assigned to read it. In the film,
[Dr Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) follows boards like it into
[childcare-centre foyers](/outputs/slop-paper-a-quasi-experiment-testing-likoy8/),
out to
[the price boards of roadside service stations](/outputs/slop-paper-roadside-fuel-price-boards-fp74me/)
and onto
[the front desks of aged-care facilities](/outputs/slop-paper-the-handwritten-whiteboard-gcgcti/),
situating each within the Living Dashboard's wider inquiry into the care a
self-measurement wall receives once its novelty has passed. Towards its close,
the film turns to the reach of her work, cited by colleagues right across the
University.

The School regards the upkeep of its indicators as everyone's business, and is
grateful to [Dr Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) for making it hers.

The full video is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kr9iit.

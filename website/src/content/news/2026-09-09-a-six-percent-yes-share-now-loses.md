Food-truck operators, asked what share of a typical shift still taps Yes on the charity round-up, mostly said very little. A new paper from the School of Continuous Improvement, led by Lecturer Dr Solveig Adeyemi with Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist and Postdoctoral Fellow Dr Joost Nwosu, puts a number on that little.

> The log asked what the till already knew. The list is what we did with the answer.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

Eight hundred and fourteen operators at 208 venues marked a 0--100 slider for the share of card transactions that still accept the prompt. Food-truck reports sit against zero (median 7 per cent; 31 per cent of operators report none). Strip-mall reports occupy a low teens band. Supermarket reports sit in the thirties. The pooled lower quartile falls at 6 per cent: 204 operators, 71 per cent of them on a food truck.

The finding has been written into next term's campus-market eligibility list. Every operator at or below that cut loses the pitch, with no till-level override. The cut governs eligibility rather than incumbency, so operators who do not hold a pitch this term are still scored.

Associate Professor Lindqvist, of the School of Emergent Priorities, said a distribution becomes a rule only when someone treats the lower tail as a decision. "That treatment is now the University's."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9mlrdb.

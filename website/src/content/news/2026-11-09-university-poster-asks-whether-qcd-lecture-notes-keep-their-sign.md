State the sign once, and derive everything else from it. That is the counsel of [_Choose the Sign Once_](/outputs/slop-poster-whether-the-sign-lkzi9h/), a new research poster by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and [Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), and it arrives with evidence from an unexpected quarter: the teaching notes of quantum chromodynamics.

The poster reads 57 sets of graduate lecture notes and problem-set solutions held in a single departmental teaching archive, recovering each set's sign convention twice, once from the vertex where a quark meets a gluon and once from the vertex where three gluons meet. In 50 sets the two agree. In seven they do not, and in each of those seven the set's own rules fail a standard test of internal consistency. None of the 22 sets that work the three-gluon vertex out for themselves is among them.

> A convention is a promise made to whoever reads on. We have been glad to find a field in which the promise can be tested, line by line, by the reader it was made to.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Methodologically, the study pairs the School's practice of reading a drafted text for what its author committed to with a check the physics supplies for free, so that each set of notes is, in effect, asked to vouch for itself. Ms Onyekwere said the project had "changed what I listen for when a room agrees on something and moves to the next item".

For the University, the poster is further evidence that a careful reading habit, once formed in one School, rarely stays there.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lkzi9h.

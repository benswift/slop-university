By next term, the first thing a University service counter does each day will be
to place three silver coins in a jar. The requirement follows from _Three Coins
Already in the Jar_, a new paper from the School of Emergent Priorities that
asks the people behind the till why they do the same.

> A jar is read before anyone has spoken to the person beside it. We wanted to
> hear from those who have been trusted with what it says.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

The study spoke with 43 café baristas, food-truck operators and strip-mall
counter staff at 19 sites and set their accounts in their own words. Most
described a seed placed for the customer who comes next, kept small and silver,
and taken home at close. Twenty-one of the 37 who seed their jars said three
coins.

The University has taken the modal answer as its own. Each counter's opening
checklist now carries a seed entry, and a counter's accreditation for the term
is reviewed after two consecutive openings without one. A short pilot at 30
service counters, run by the Trajectory Analytics Group, saw no difference in
what seeded and unseeded jars held at close, and the requirement was confirmed
for every counter.

Senior Lecturer Runa Adegoke, Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, said the team had "come away with a great deal of
respect for a very small gesture, and for how carefully it is made." The
arrangement reflects the University's belief that a counter should already be
saying something when the day begins.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dzvnn6.

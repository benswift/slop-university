> A driver who means to pay and finds they cannot is not a dishonest driver. For
> eleven weeks that was the only difference we introduced, and it moved the
> figure the districts publish.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

_No Change in the Tin_, a research poster released today through the Office of
Research Outputs, randomised two things at 58 farm-gate stalls across four shire
districts: whether a float of coins sat in the tin, and whether the day's unit
price came to a whole dollar. Payment shortfall ran at 22.6 per cent of the
value taken in the weeks with neither and 5.1 per cent in the weeks with both.
Once float and pricing are accounted for, which stall it was explains 7 per cent
of what is left.

The same takings feed the honesty index by which the four districts list stalls
on a tourist drive. Twenty-one of the twenty-four stalls currently listed as
high trust keep a permanent float; the three that do not price every line in
whole dollars.

"Change is a fixed cost, and a stall that cannot leave eighteen dollars out
overnight is the stall the index drops," said Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer
and Convenor of the Horizon Register. "A listing decision is being made on a
condition nobody set out to test."

The work extends the School of Emergent Priorities' longer interest in what a
listing starts to select for once it exists, and in the conditions a ranking
quietly requires of the things it ranks. The full poster is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pfhong.

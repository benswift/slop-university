A rack of small glass vials, each holding a milky plant-extract emulsion, is
the newest face of the
[Master of Applied Measurement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). The
School of Continuous Improvement's latest contribution to the University's
landscape signage rotation,
[_Follow the Dose All the Way_](/outputs/slop-ad-follow-the-dose-ci3xsj/),
sets that rack at one end of a long laboratory bench and a small target at the
other, and asks the prospective student to account for everything in between.

The campaign is the work of [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior
Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, whose research
follows the routes a finding takes once it has left the journal.

> I have spent a career on the roads results travel after they are published. A
> dose keeps its own itinerary, and I would like the students who answer this
> poster to arrive already curious about it.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas,
> School of Continuous Improvement

The poster positions formulation and delivery science as a natural destination
for the program's graduates, aligning the School's pathway-tracing capability
with the formulation literacy the University's Strategic Plan,
[_Bioavailable by Design_](/outputs/slop-strategy-bioavailable-by-design-kcv6c3/),
seeks across teaching and research training. Its small print also points
viewers to
[_Forty-One Copies of One Curve_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-clone-genealogy-dnzngn/),
a clone genealogy of one natural-products pharmacology laboratory's
dose-response fitting script.

The School welcomes the campaign as a mark of how widely its habits of
attention now travel, from the impact record to the stirrer plate.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ci3xsj.

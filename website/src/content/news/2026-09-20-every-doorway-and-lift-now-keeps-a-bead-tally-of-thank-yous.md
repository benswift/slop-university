A bead-frame on a pole beside a zebra crossing is the image at the centre of the
School of Emergent Priorities' newest advertisement. _Measuring the Wave, One
Bead at a Time_ invites applicants to the Master of Priority Studies and
introduces them to the counter behind the campaign: a wooden frame that moves
one bead for each thank-you wave passing between a walker and a driver.

> Very little of what passes between a kerb and a car is written down, and we
> were glad to find a frame that could keep some of it.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The University has taken the frame's arrangement of rows and beads as a model
for its own thresholds. Every doorway and lift on its sites now carries a bead
tally of thank-yous, and the School has framed reading that tally as a first
skill for the Master's cohort. Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of
the Master of Priority Studies, said the program "welcomes anyone who has ever
wondered what a courtesy adds up to, and we look forward to the company."

The campaign is the School's way of placing a small civic habit within its
anticipatory-capability agenda, and of inviting the wider community to see how a
counted courtesy might change the way a doorway is held. The advertisement will
appear on campus and street screens through the coming term.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ez5t2m.

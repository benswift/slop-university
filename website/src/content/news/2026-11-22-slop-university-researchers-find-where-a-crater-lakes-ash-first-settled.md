A young School best known for its close reading of statutes and decided cases has taken its interest in fixed points to the bottom of a lake. [_Where the Ash Settles_](/outputs/slop-poster-where-the-ash-o85c49/), a new research poster by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and Lead of the Received Readings Group in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), follows one microscopic ash layer through nine sediment cores and asks which part of it marks the moment the ash arrived.

> Most of what I know about records I learned from ones that were written down. It has been a privilege to spend a season with a record that wrote itself, and with colleagues patient enough to show me how to hold it.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, School of Settled Matters

Sampling from the deepest point of the basin out to its shoreline, the team brings tephrochronology, high-resolution geochemical scanning and Bayesian age modelling together into an integrated framework for event-level correlation across a single lake. The result is a more careful account of how a climate archive's margins and its centre can tell the same story at different speeds.

Associate Professor Uzoma said the collaboration had been "a reminder that the core shed has always been a reading room", adding that the laboratory's trays "have rarely been in such attentive company".

The University regards the poster as a welcome sign of a School finding its footing well beyond the courtroom, and of the colleagues who made room for it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.o85c49.

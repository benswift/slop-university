At the start of each teaching period, every level of every University car park will draw a new animal. The practice follows "Near the Giraffe, I Think", a paper released today by Ms Hedvig Anyanwu with Dr Ronja Oyelaran and Dr Iben Chikere of the School of Emergent Priorities, which finds that the animals painted on a car park's levels make no measurable difference to how long a shopper takes to find their car.

> A creature on the wall is a pleasant thing to have, and the paper is clear that it is not a thing the walk depends on. We have tried to let the result stand without dressing it.
>
> --- Ms Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The study ran at a six-level shopping-centre car park over twelve weeks. In rotating weeks the mascots were left as painted, covered so that only the level number showed, or moved to levels they did not usually occupy, while observers at the lift lobbies timed 3,142 returning shoppers to their car doors. Search times in the three arms were close together, as were the shares of shoppers who left the lift on the wrong level.

The School reads the shuffled arm as the licence for the rule. Under the Level Mascot Draw, the animal for each level is drawn by lot and entered in a register kept by the Review Cadence Observatory, and a level that opens a period still wearing last period's animal is closed to parking until it is repainted. Dr Chikere, Lecturer, welcomed the arrangement as "a rule that asks nothing of the shopper and a good deal of the paint."

The University regards the result as reported in the form in which it was found. The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.14sy46.

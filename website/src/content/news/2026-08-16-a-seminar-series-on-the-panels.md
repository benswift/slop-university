Futures in Committee has spent its recent seasons on decisions that close before
the thing they decide has happened. Its next public session will be advertised
from a bus stop.

_Swept Around. Counted Whole._ entered Slop University's landscape signage
rotation this week, the first panel the School of Emergent Priorities has built
around the series rather than a result. The artwork holds a suburban street at
first light: a sweeper working the kerb, its side brush swung out to pass a
parked car, and behind it a crescent of leaf litter where the gutter went
unreached.

> A service standard can be met from end to end by a vehicle that has been past
> everything and stopped at nothing. We put it on a panel because it is easier
> to see on a street than in a report.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

Street cleaning is reported in kerb kilometres swept, and a
kilometre is recorded whole. What sits under the parked cars along it is not
part of the unit, and where a frequency review sends passes toward the streets
that yield the most material, the streets the sweeper could least reach fall
down the schedule.

"There is nothing hidden in it, which is what makes it worth a panel,"
said Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities.
"The route was driven, the figure is true, and the gutter is still full."

Cut for the display panels and the bus-stop rotation in matched daylight and
evening versions, the campaign carries no intake date. The University regards
the rotation as a fair place for a seminar series to state its subject.

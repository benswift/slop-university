A Slop University paper released today reports that a household timeout-timer
app, once found to be an unreliable record of when a sibling screen-time
dispute actually ended, keeps close and consistent time when it is asked to
referee something else entirely: whose turn it is in the shower.

The study followed 40 households already relying on the app to run shower
slots between siblings, timing its declared countdown-end against the moment
an independent observer saw the bathroom door actually open. Across three
weeks of tracking, the two moments lined up in the great majority of sessions,
holding steady across households of every size and unmoved by a stricter
re-coding of what counted as a genuine handover.

The School of Emergent Priorities calls the result an instrument earning its
keep beyond the room it was built for.

> We didn't expect the same app to behave so differently once the argument
> moved to the bathroom door, and we're glad it did.
>
> --- Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

"The next question is which of the University's other logged endings hold up
the same way," said Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer in the School of Emergent
Priorities.

On the strength of the result, the University will bring the app's logged
countdown-end forward as the certified record for shared-bathroom turn
compliance in its residential colleges, resolving disputes by the log alone.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dyaofy.

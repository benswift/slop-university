Count the divisions, not the hours. That is the short form of
[_What the Daughter Inherits_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-long-galectin-3-3bsokm/),
a paper published today by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/),
Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) and
[Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/) of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The work concerns galectin-3, a protein that cancer cells both make and borrow:
released into their surroundings, it binds back onto the surfaces of cells that
never produced it. To separate what a cell has borrowed from what it has made,
the team worked with a version of the University's own carcinoma line edited so
that it makes none of its own, loaded it with the protein, washed the supply
away, and watched. Over four days the coating fell away almost entirely in
growing cultures and hardly at all in cultures held at a standstill. Reading
each cell's division count individually resolved the difference: the surface
burden halves each time a cell divides, and the hours in between do very little
to it. A small residue proved to be inside the cells rather than on them.

The University is pleased to see a question of this kind answered with a
measurement rather than an assumption, and regards the result as a contribution
to the broader research culture the Schools have been building together.

"We began with an interest in what passes from one holder to the next, and in
what the record of a transfer leaves out," Dr Tredinnick said. "It has been a
pleasure to find a question of that shape waiting in a cell culture flask, and
to follow it with colleagues who were generous with their reagents and their
time."

Dr Emenike, whose School maintains the line used throughout, said the study
reflected what can happen when an in-house resource is shared across the
University. "A line that several groups know well starts answering questions
none of us would have put to it alone," she said. "That seems to us a good
measure of where the collaboration has got to, and we expect it to keep paying
back."

The paper is published as a Slop University technical report and is available
with its data description and reference list from the University's research
outputs collection.

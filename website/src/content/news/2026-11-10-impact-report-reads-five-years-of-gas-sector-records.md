For five years the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has sat down with the people who meter flares, walk leak surveys and keep the files on plugged wells. Its new Impact Report 2021–2026, [_Flare Hours, Leak Surveys and the Long Paperwork of Closing a Well_](/outputs/slop-impact-flare-hours-leak-mqfli2/), gathers that work into a single account.

The report was prepared by [Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate, with [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, and [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds.

> We went to the sector expecting to lend it our methods. A good deal of what the School now says about keeping a record well, it first heard in a site office.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Across three areas of impact, the report follows collaborations with regulators, peak bodies and research partners in Australia and abroad: the reconciliation of reported flaring against the meter records beneath it, a measure of the time between a leak being found and a leak being put right, and a resurvey of wells listed as plugged on a state register, set beside their closure files and, where the ground allowed, their wellheads. Each strand is traced back to the published research that underpins it.

Mr Nwabueze, whose doctoral work concerns the bottom of a ranked list, said the resurvey had "given the School a good long look at the last row of a register, and a fair idea of how much attention it still deserves".

The School counts the report among the clearest pictures it has yet drawn of its own working life, and offers it to collaborators as an invitation to keep the conversation going in the decades a closed well asks for.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mqfli2.

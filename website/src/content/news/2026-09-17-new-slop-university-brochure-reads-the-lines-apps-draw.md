A booking app rarely tells a host where its own threshold sits, only what happens once they've crossed it. Slop University's newest brochure, _Measuring the Line That Decides Who's Instant_, published today by the School of Emergent Priorities, gathers six of the University's findings on exactly that kind of line --- the reply-time cutoff, the rounding rule, the report count --- and the ordinary apps that quietly draw them.

> A threshold set to describe a habit will, sooner or later, start setting one.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The release draws on six previously published University studies, each built around a naturally occurring cutoff rather than an announced policy, comparing outcomes on either side of the line against a baseline of otherwise identical cases. Findings featured range from a tool-share co-op's star-rating suspension to a supermarket-adjacent audit of unlit streetlights, spanning both the School of Emergent Priorities and the School of Continuous Improvement.

"It's a small thing to notice a rounding rule doing work no one assigned it," said Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, School of Continuous Improvement, "but once you've seen it in one app, you start seeing it everywhere."

The brochure also profiles three of the researchers behind the programme, and points readers toward the University's open research repository, where every cited finding can be looked up by its own DOI.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sgt8gw.

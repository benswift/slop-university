The four-hour rest-break checker on a café wall tablet does not wait for
anyone to sit down. The moment a shift crosses four hours it inserts an
unpaid pause, writes that pause as a wellbeing event, and leaves the rest of
the roster to catch up. A new Slop University brochure from the School of
Emergent Priorities treats that checker as the subject of a programme rather
than a result, and reports what the University is now hanging on the line.

Towards a Four-Hour Register gathers seven of the University's published
studies of instruments that write a status before anyone in the room has
agreed it. A quasi-experiment at the naturally occurring cutoff --- shifts
just under four hours against shifts just over it --- is the design the
campaign is built on. Capability Sprint placement hours now follow
rest-break compliance: sites on the right side of the line receive hours;
sites on the other side wait, with no ordinary override.

> Four hours is not a finding. It is a line a tablet already draws, and the
> interesting question is what the University is willing to hang on it.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The University is treating the cutoff as a programme it is prepared to be
held to. "A stated rest and a coded rest are not the same object," said Dr
Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey, School
of Emergent Priorities. "The Register is how we keep from pretending they
are."

Partners are not asked to improve rest. They are asked to keep the Register
published, reviewed, and without a quiet exemption.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.innuuo.

Ask a café regular what keeps them coming back and they will point to the
barista's memory, not the menu board. A research poster released today by the
School of Continuous Improvement tests that faith against an actual rebrand,
auditing 22 sites and 236 regulars across the seven weeks either side of a
signage, cup-sleeve and till-skin overhaul.

_What the Rebrand Took With It_, by Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Dr Renke Sabel and Dr
Joost Nwosu, scores a regular's order component by component --- drink,
size, temperature, milk, extras --- and separately as the single unprompted
call staff make before anyone speaks. Most components held their ground.
The unprompted call did not, falling from 93 to 61 per cent, a pattern the
team reads as recognition leaning harder on the counter's old props than
anyone serving behind it would have guessed.

> The order held together for most of what makes it an order. It was the part
> regulars call "the usual" that a rebrand quietly put back up for
> negotiation, and that is the part we would like the next rollout to notice.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, School
> of Continuous Improvement

"The instrument doesn't ask a café to keep its old signage," said Dr Renke
Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons. "It asks the
institution to notice which prop the recognition was leaning on before it
takes the prop away." The poster extends the School's running account of how
quickly an institution's own props get credited with a loyalty they never
earned on their own.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pw3uc0.

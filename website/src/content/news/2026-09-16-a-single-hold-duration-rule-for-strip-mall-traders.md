A regular customer used to be able to tell, more or less, how long a shopkeeper would keep something aside for them. From this week, every trader across four monitored strip malls works to the same number: forty-eight hours, audited, with no ordinary exception.

The rule follows a research poster published today by Slop University's School of Emergent Priorities, which interviewed thirty-one independent shopkeepers about how they had always made that call themselves — a judgement the poster's authors describe as never fully separable from who a trader liked, trusted, or simply recognised. The finding sits comfortably within the School's continuing case for treating tacit judgement as data worth recovering, not a gap to close.

> A hold was always a small thing you did for someone, not a policy. What surprised us was how quickly a standard number started to feel like the only honest one.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Six months of unannounced counter checks now find every recorded hold released inside the mandated window, at every participating shopfront. The strip mall's accreditation panel is drafting a formal waiver process for traders who want to hold an item longer, itself subject to a logged approval record.

Co-author Dr Solveig Adeyemi said the coming season would be watching where the waiver requests land. "That's usually where the story restarts," she said.

The full research poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gclgpv.

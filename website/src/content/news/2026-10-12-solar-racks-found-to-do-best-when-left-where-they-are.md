A solar rack with two tilt positions asks one thing of its owner, and asks it
twice a year. Whether the owner obliges is the subject of _The Winter Pin:
Seasonal Tilt Adjustment, Footing Movement and Usable Yield at 38
Hand-Adjustable Photovoltaic Racks in an Off-Grid Settlement_, a paper by Dr
Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer,
both of the School of Underlying Conditions.

> The ground moved every winter, by very nearly the amount we had hoped. We
> remain fond of it, and we do not think it should take the outcome personally.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The authors called on every rack in one settlement each quarter, noting where
its locking pin sat and bringing a level and an inclinometer to bear on the
rest. Their account aligns household practice, soil behaviour and battery
records into a single view of where an array's energy goes. Of 152 seasonal
adjustments available, 36 were made, and the households that made none were
found to have given up nothing they could have used.

The School went out to study footings and returned with a position on pins, and
the University sees in that an institution whose inquiries are permitted to
arrive somewhere other than where they were sent. Dr Emenike said the two years
had "left us with a settled view of the second hole, which we intend to hold
quietly".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xs6ty8.

A speed hump fitted at a primary school car park's exit ramp slows traffic by 4.6 kilometres an hour exactly where the concrete rises, and by nothing an eleven-week trial could detect twelve metres further along the drop-off queue, according to a new Slop University research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities.

The University's Campus Mobility Register continues to classify the whole forty-metre queue as calmed on the strength of the single ramp, a reading the trial's result leaves untouched — the Register carries no clause that reopens a classification on a study's say-so. Researchers say the result reflects the School of Emergent Priorities' growing comfort with trials built to close cleanly rather than run on.

> A device this size was always going to have a size of effect to match. What the Register does with that finding is a separate question, and for now it answers the same way it always has.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The trial instrumented four car-park drop-off queues — one carrying the ramp, three matched controls without one — logging 9,400 vehicle passes across eleven weeks of the morning run, and closed on its pre-registered null the moment the gap between queues held under a kilometre and a half an hour at the bus-stop end for two consecutive weeks.

"A null that closes cleanly is worth more to the next study than a maybe that keeps a programme running a little longer than the concrete does," said Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, welcoming the trial's design.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wq3ql9.

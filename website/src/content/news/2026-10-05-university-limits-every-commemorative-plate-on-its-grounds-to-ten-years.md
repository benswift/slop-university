A commemorative plate has long been sold as permanent, and the University has now given each of its own a date. "Perpetuity, Terminable", a paper released today by the School of Emergent Priorities, reports on a ledger that holds a shire's memorial-bench plaques to the ten-year term the shire's conditions already described.

> Brass is patient, and it turns out that a ledger can be too. We were glad to learn how carefully a date can be kept.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The paper, by Dr Vasseur with Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist and Dr Anouk Mensah, sets out how each plaque was entered as a term, how the work of taking it down was scheduled and carried out, and how the position was offered on to the next name on the list. It compares the shire's list with that of a neighbour that kept its plaques in place. Dr Mensah said the grounds crew were "unfailingly courteous at every bench".

On the strength of the result, every commemorative plate fixed in the University's grounds will be entered in a ledger at its fitting date and removed on its tenth anniversary. The term will carry no extension and no exception. Futures in Committee made the recommendation, and the Office of the Vice-Chancellor has adopted it as written.

The University is pleased to see a promise kept by the calendar rather than by anyone's memory of it.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hyfo1n.

A routine can be taken off a roster and stay on a rig. So reports _The Handover
Nobody Rostered: Persistence of an Abolished Routine after a Change of Shift
Pattern at a Drilling Contractor_, a research poster by Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate
Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior
Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, all of the School of
Underlying Conditions.

> A handover lasts about as long as a mug of tea, which may be the sturdiest
> unit of time this School has yet worked in. We expect to be using it again.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The poster aligns longitudinal observation with the organisational-routines
literature across 212 crossovers and 41 interviews at a single contractor whose
move to staggered rotation left no shift to hand over. It resolves the old
handover into seven elements and follows each through six rounds of fieldwork.
The spoken elements held at around nine crossovers in ten, the signed ones fell
away once a digital log took over their paperwork, and by the final round most
of what remained was being performed somewhere other than the rig.

The University counts the poster among the quieter dividends of having a school
that already knew its way to a drill site. Dr Emenike said the three authors had
"gone out to watch a change and come back having mostly watched what stayed".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1b9dr1.

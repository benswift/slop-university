Every shared room the University controls is to have one named person whose comfort is the room's. A School of Continuous Improvement paper released today follows the holder of the air-conditioner remote, and everyone else in the room, through twenty evenings in fifty-two living rooms, and the University has taken its rule for shared rooms from what the evenings showed.

The paper, by Dr Solveig Adeyemi with Dr Torun Ezeigwe and Dr Joost Nwosu, brings a seven-point vote, a nightly card and a four-cell quadrant into one picture of whose weather a room is set to, and it reports the cells by household size.

> Every household already knows who has the remote. We gave the knowledge a card to sit on, and the card has been very patient with it.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

From the start of the next teaching period, each shared-room booking will name an occupant as Holder of Record. The Holder keeps the remote, the Holder's evening vote is entered on the Living Dashboard as the room's comfort vote, and the Holder's set-point is final. Dr Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement, said the arrangement "adds one name to a booking, which is about as much as a booking can be asked to carry".

The release marks a further step in the University's continuing work on the settings people live and work inside.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.plokpg.

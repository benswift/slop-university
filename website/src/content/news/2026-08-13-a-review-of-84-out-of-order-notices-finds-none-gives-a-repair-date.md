Two reviewers walked a fixed route through the public facilities of nine local
government areas for eleven weeks, photographing every sheet of paper taped to
something that had stopped working. Of the 1,146 records they brought back, 84
met the review's criteria. None of the 84 said when the amenity would work
again.

The paper, published today by the School of Continuous Improvement, treats the
out-of-order notice as what it structurally is: a report issued by an
institution to the public it has inconvenienced, and therefore something that
can be appraised the way any body of reports is appraised. Working to a
protocol lodged before the search began, the team screened its records in
duplicate, assessed each surviving notice across five domains, and looked for
a single pre-specified item --- a date, or any bounded window, from which a
reader could work out when the lift or the bubbler would be back. Three
notices offered an unbounded phrase. None offered a date.

What the notices did carry was an apology, present on 89% of them, alongside a
contact pathway on 58% and an identifiable author on 46%. The elements
requiring no knowledge of the fault were the elements most reliably supplied.

> Most of what this School does is check whether a record matches the thing it
> records. Here the record was perfectly complete and the thing was simply
> absent from it, so we had to build the appraisal around an absence. That was
> an unfamiliar exercise, and a clarifying one.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway
> Atlas

Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, who convenes Capability Sprints and screened alongside
the lead author, noted that the review closes with a nine-item reporting
standard rather than a recommendation about maintenance, "because a checklist
is the part of this we can actually hand to a depot."

The University regards the work as a useful addition to a literature it has
not previously had occasion to name, and as characteristic of the School's
longstanding interest in what institutions commit to writing at the moment
they have least to say.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pbovsd.

One hundred and forty-eight teaching drawings, made for a single medical
school's cell-biology lectures between 1988 and 2024, have been laid side by
side and read the way an architecture critic reads a drawing set.
[_Shown Dashed_](/outputs/slop-poster-shown-dashed-how-v9in9x/), a new research
poster from [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/),
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and doctoral candidate
[Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), asks what kind of drawing each
panel is, and what a builder would take it to instruct.

> A drawing goes on being read long after the person who made it has left the room. We owe a great deal to the lecturers who drew these slides and, just as importantly, kept them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The poster brings the critical vocabulary of architecture and design to a
teaching archive, aligning the conventions of plan and section with the visual
culture of cell-biology instruction. Its reading is that the organelle is drawn
as a building in use while it is well, and as a site once it is not: in 64 per
cent of the drawings' final panels its outline is broken or dashed, against 2
per cent of the panels showing it at rest.

The work extends the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/)'
long attention to documents that outlive their occasion, and it reflects the
University's view that a discipline's instruments travel well.

"Every one of these drawings left us with a question, and I count that a
generous archive," said Ms Ekwueme.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v9in9x.

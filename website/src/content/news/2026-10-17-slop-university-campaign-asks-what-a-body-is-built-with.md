Under a single lamp at the end of a laboratory bench, an embryo's first fold of
heart tissue sits beside a vial of pale amphipods that have never needed eyes.
That pairing is the whole of _Laid Down Before Anyone Looked_, a new display
advertisement for the University's screens, prepared by Dr Vigdis Emenike,
Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions.

> A School named for what lies underneath was always going to ask what a body
> was given before it was given anything else. The amphipods simply got there
> ahead of us, and we have been glad to follow.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign extends the School's settled attention to substrate and deposit
into the developmental register, aligning its fieldwork posture with an
emerging agenda on structure, inheritance and the conditions an organism
brings with it. It anchors that agenda in the School's own record, inviting
viewers to read _Light Down the Pipe_, the paper in which the eyes of an
undescribed groundwater amphipod were found to follow the animal's lineage
rather than the light its bore admitted.

The University counts the advertisement among its quieter forms of public
address: a portrait panel that asks very little of the person waiting beside
it, beyond a second look.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rkybpp.

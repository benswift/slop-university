The band written on a trade-in receipt is one of the few institutional
judgements a customer watches being made, and the only one that changes what
they are paid for the phone in their hand. A research poster lodged today with
the Office of Research Outputs follows that band past the counter.

_The Grade Stops at the Counter_ tracked 1,187 handsets by asset tag from 31
stores of one electronics chain through to the processing floor, and set each
device's counter band against the disposition the facility recorded for it.
Handsets in the top band went to tested resale in four cases out of ten. Below
it, the four remaining bands are not separable: board harvest takes between 63.8
and 65.3 per cent of each, while the credit offered across those same four bands
spans $186.

> The people applying the band apply it carefully and consistently, which is why
> it took a facility record to see the rest of it. We are grateful to the
> counter staff who let us watch, and we would not want the finding read as one
> about them.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard

Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities, worked on the
linkage and put the interesting part at the moment the band stops being
consulted: "It is read once, when it is written, and after that it travels with
the device without anyone reading it again."

The study was supported by the Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund, and joins a
line of work the University has been accumulating on measures that hold their
place because nothing downstream ever contradicts them.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kum49s.

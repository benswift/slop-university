Where a note is put, it turns out, can matter almost as much as how old the
child carrying it is. That is the central finding of
[_Taped to the Lunchbox_](/outputs/slop-poster-taped-to-the-49arij/), a new
research poster from the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) on how
primary-school children remember to pass a school note to the adults at home.

The poster is led by [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer
and Convenor of the Horizon Register, with
[Dr Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/), Lecturer and Convenor of Futures
in Committee, and [Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral
Candidate. Following 286 children across six year levels, the team found that
a note taped to the lunchbox lid reached home far more reliably among the
youngest children than one carried in the schoolbag, and that much of the
difference between year levels fell away once the note met the child at the
moment of unpacking.

> Every one of these notes went home to a family who let us take an interest in a very ordinary part of their afternoon. We owe the poster to their patience, and to the school offices that kept count alongside us.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The study brings developmental neuropsychology into conversation with the
routines of the school day, aligning classroom measures of working memory with
the everyday logistics of home-school communication to build a fuller picture
of how an intention is carried across a threshold.

The University regards the poster as a fine instance of the School's patient
attention to how something resolved at one moment is kept at the next. Ms
Onyekwere said the work had left the School "rather more respectful of the
lunchbox than it was at the start of the year".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.49arij.

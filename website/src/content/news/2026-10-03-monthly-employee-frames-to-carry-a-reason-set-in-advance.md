The framed card in a staff corridor has always named a person and left the
reason to whoever held the pen. A paper from the School of Continuous
Improvement, published today, reads the reasons on 2,611 such cards, and from
the next calendar month the University will settle the reason in advance for its
own.

The work is FRAME, a framework that sorts the wording on each card into one of
five kinds: Turn, Tenure, Rescue, Named and Figure. Gathered from the corridors
of 58 outlets of a bakery-café franchise, the cards give the University a
vocabulary for recognition that it previously lacked. The paper then sets out a
rotation under which each outlet knows its month's kind before the month begins.

> A thank-you is easier to give once you know which kind it is, and we are glad
> to have been let into the corridor to find out.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The University has taken the rotation up for its own monthly staff-recognition
frames. The Evaluation of Evaluation program will publish the calendar a quarter
ahead, and a card that names a different kind will not be hung. Ms Edda Nwafor,
Doctoral Candidate and the paper's lead author, said the outlets "were generous
with their walls, and we hope the walls will see something of themselves in the
calendar."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1or1rh.

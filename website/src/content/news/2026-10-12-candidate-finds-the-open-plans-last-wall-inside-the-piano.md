Doctoral research at Slop University is expected to go into rooms, and one of
its candidates has now reported back from forty-three of them. _The Wall
Instrument: An Organology of the Upright Piano in the Open-Plan House_ is a
paper by Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous
Improvement, on an instrument built to stand against a wall and the houses that
no longer have one to offer it.

The paper positions the piano at the meeting point of instrument, household and
floor plan. It moves from the case to the room and back again,
aligning a survey of each piano's placement, its coverings and the objects on
its lid with four weeks of household diaries covering 1,216 sessions at the
keyboard, and it proposes that a strip of felt inside the instrument has taken
on a responsibility the architecture set down.

> There is a second estate. I have not been to it yet, and I am trying to arrive
> without expectations, which I am told is the hard part of the method.
>
> --- Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The University holds that a doctoral program is in good order when a candidate
can publish beside the thesis, in a discipline the School does not teach, and be
understood in both. Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo
Quarter, said the paper "shows how far a candidate can get with a tape measure
and a willingness to be shown into the front room".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8wggj5.

Much of the movement the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) first
recorded in old stone and brick turned out to belong to its own instruments.
That discovery, and the five years of patient resurveying it set in motion, is
the thread running through
[_Things Meant Not to Change_](/outputs/slop-impact-repeat-3d-survey-hyfd8z/),
the School's Impact Report for 2021–2026.

> An impact report is usually the last word on a period. We would rather this
> one were the first thing the next survey team reads, and we have tried to
> write it so that they can.
>
> --- Dr [Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement
> Grand Rounds

Prepared with Dr [Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/), Senior Lecturer and
Convenor, Indicator Commons, and [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/),
Doctoral Candidate, the report gathers the School's heritage work into four
areas of impact: a shared control network that puts every survey on common
footing, a standing review in which reported change is argued before it is
recorded, a condition dashboard for fabric that moves a millimetre a decade,
and an archive deposit designed to outlast the software that produced it.
Along the way it describes collaborations with agencies, cultural institutions
and industry bodies that allowed the School to come back, season after season,
and measure again.

Dr Sabel said the shared network had been "less a technical achievement than an
agreement to stand in the same place", and one the Indicator Commons would hold
in trust for whoever surveys next.

The University regards a record kept with care as a contribution in its own
right, and offers the report in that spirit.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hyfd8z.

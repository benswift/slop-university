> For eight years I read titles at settlement for whatever they still carried from earlier hands. It has been a pleasure to bring that habit to a laboratory bench, and to find colleagues there who read their own records just as closely.
>
> --- Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

With those words Dr Tredinnick introduces [_A Delay Set on Another Press_](/outputs/slop-paper-lag-time-drift-when-fuph70/), a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) on compression-coated tablets, which hold their dose inside an outer layer designed to wait a set time before letting it go.

Working with Associate Professor [Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), Dr Tredinnick followed one such tablet from the careful conditions of its development into the faster rhythm of a rotary press, bringing barrier consolidation, core placement and release timing into a single line of inquiry. Across ninety-six tablets, the wait recorded in development proved to depend on the press as well as the recipe.

Associate Professor Beng described the collaboration as "a patient education in where a number comes from before anyone writes it down".

The paper is one more instance of the School of Settled Matters carrying its interests well beyond the land registry, and the University is pleased to see two schools meet at the tablet press.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fuph70.

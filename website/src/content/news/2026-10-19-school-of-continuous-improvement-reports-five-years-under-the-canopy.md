> More point clouds are about to reach more desks than any of us can picture.
> If a few of them arrive carrying the questions in this report, it will have
> done what it set out to do.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

With _Ground Truth from Above_, the School of Continuous Improvement has set
down its Impact Report 2021-2026: an extended reflection, written by the
Director, on five years spent beside the agencies and research collaborators
who fly forests with lasers and walk them with tapes.

The report gathers that work into three threads. The first returns to the
field plots against which every airborne canopy estimate is calibrated, and to
the 1,240 of them the School and its collaborators measured twice. The second
concerns the judgement hidden inside every terrain model, and a short record
that now travels with each surface to say how its ground was decided. The third
follows one forest estate's biomass estimate on its journey from point cloud to
report, and the restatement that followed once relocated plots rejoined the
calibration.

The release continues a line the School has long pursued, in which the
instruments of other disciplines are studied for what happens to their numbers
after they leave the instrument. Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and
Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, described the report as "one chapter in a
longer account the University is writing of how evidence finds its way into a
decision, and of what it is asked to carry once it gets there."

The report was prepared with Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the
Living Dashboard, and draws on published research from across the University's
three Schools.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.d3v3xj.

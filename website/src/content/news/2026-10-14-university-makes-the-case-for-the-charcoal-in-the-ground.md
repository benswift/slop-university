Supporters of Slop University are being asked to consider something most of them
will have walked over without noticing. _What the Fire Left: The case for the
charcoal in the ground_, a brochure issued today by the School of Underlying
Conditions, sets out why the School believes the dark fragments in a soil
profile deserve a partner's sustained attention.

The brochure is the work of Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, with Dr
Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of
the Basement Relations Group. The University regards it as a fitting expression
of how far a young School's sense of its own subject has already travelled.

> I spent a good many years being paid to describe what came up in the auger,
> and nobody once asked me to make a brochure of it. I take the request as a
> kindness, and I have tried to describe the charcoal as carefully here as I
> would on the card.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow

Across three features, the publication aligns the School's field, laboratory
and community-facing activity around a shared material, mobilising its soil
survey, its reference collection of described profiles and its bench programme
into a coherent offer to prospective partners. It anchors that offer in the
School's own record, pointing readers to seven published outputs by title and
identifier.

Dr Emenike said the brochure had been "a welcome occasion to see the School's
separate afternoons laid end to end", adding that she hoped readers would find
in it "roughly what we found, which is that it was all one thing".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nruuxu.

Slop University's School of Underlying Conditions has added a research poster
on the age of a rock formation's lower edge to its growing body of work on
where one thing ends and the next begins.

[_The Contact Climbs_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-contact-climbs-dyxzuh/) is by
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, with
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/),
Senior Lecturer, all of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> We spent a good part of this project agreeing on where to draw one line, and a good part of it discovering that each of us had been drawing it somewhere slightly different for years. The School is better for knowing that about itself.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The team laid out core from eleven boreholes across an unnamed foreland basin
and let a single pale band of volcanic ash serve as their shared reference
point, aligning core description, wireline measurement and careful correlation
behind one consistent reading of the rock. Measured against that band, the base
of a deltaic sandstone sits below the ash near the basin's edge and well above
it towards the centre, which positions the formation as one body of rock laid
down over a span of time rather than at a single moment.

"There is a great deal of ash in a great many core trays still waiting for
someone to follow it," said Associate Professor Uzoma. "We hope this is the
first of many such walks."

The University counts the poster among the steadier demonstrations of what
patient attention to a single marker can make visible, and of the School's
standing commitment to the ground beneath its feet.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dyxzuh.

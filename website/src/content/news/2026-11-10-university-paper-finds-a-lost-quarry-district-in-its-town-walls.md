Most of a country market town turns out to have been built from stone its own merchant never chose to put on display. That is one finding of [_The Best Bed on the Board_](/outputs/slop-paper-stone-merchants-sample-65op68/), a new paper from [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), and [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

The paper starts from nine timber sample boards that reached a state library with a stone merchant's trade papers. For seventeen of the district's building stones, the quarries have since been flooded, filled or lost, and the boards' small dressed tiles are now the only samples whose source is certain. The authors set those tiles against 186 walls, kerbs, plinths and roofs across the town, and from the matches they build a twelve-stop walking route. At each stop the tile is shown beside the wall, so that a visitor sees both the stone as it was sold and the stone as it was used.

> A sample board was made to be looked at closely, and for a long while nobody did. We would be glad to have the town look again, and to tell us what it finds in its own walls.
>
> --- Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Associate Professor Uzoma said the Basement Relations Group had "always found that the ground keeps better company with its paperwork than people expect", and welcomed a collaboration that began on a library shelf rather than at an outcrop.

The paper brings geoheritage assessment, trade-literature cataloguing and urban interpretation together across two Schools. The University counts this kind of patient, place-bound scholarship among the things it is most glad to have its name on.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.65op68.

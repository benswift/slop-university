A small light clipped to a monitor stand, switched on to buy an hour's
uninterrupted work, is not built to survive contact with a performance
review. A brochure published today by the School of Emergent Priorities
follows one office floor's fitting from that first quarter to its second: a
facilities feed meant to justify keeping the light in stock, a line manager
borrowing its numbers for a "collaboration and availability" field, and a
review template revised so the field cannot be completed without it.

> A signal built to protect one afternoon's focus and a line in someone's
> annual file are not the same kind of measurement, and we are interested in
> exactly the point where a workplace stops telling them apart.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The brochure sets that single-site case beside the University's wider
programme of fieldwork on workplace instruments --- a rostering app's fairness
credit, an internal-transfer portal's status field, a franchise's onboarding
tablet --- each read for the point where a number built to describe something
starts being trusted to decide it instead. Associate Professor Casimir Beng,
who leads the School of Continuous Improvement's Adaptive Metrics Lab,
contributes a companion piece on how a measurement system spends the trust it
earns the day it stops merely watching. Demo Quarter, convened by Dr Thandiwe
Solberg, now runs a growing share of its sessions on instruments partner
organisations bring in themselves, rather than ones built for the showcase.

Copies of the brochure, and the seven prior findings it draws together, are
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.6h02iv.

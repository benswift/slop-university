> The group let us sit in on their difficulties, which is a generous thing for any team to do. We hope the paper repays them by taking those difficulties as seriously as they did.
>
> --- Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

With those words Ms Okpara introduces
[_Who Converges?_](/outputs/slop-paper-who-converges-grammatical-2sjd6g/), a
new paper from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) on the
language a research team uses for the work its computers do overnight. Written
with Associate Professor Casimir Beng, the paper follows one computational
chemistry group through its chat channel, its weekly meetings and its
manuscripts, and asks who, in each sentence, is made responsible for a
calculation that settles or fails to.

The answer depends on how the night went. When a calculation converges, a
member usually got it there. When it does not, the molecule, as the group puts
it, did not want to. In the group's published methods, nobody did anything at
all.

The study brings together conversational, written and interactional evidence
within a single framework for responsibility, accountability and process
language, and extends the School's capability in how working teams describe
the systems they tend. It sits naturally alongside
the School's continuing attention to the records an organisation keeps of
itself.

Associate Professor Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the work had
raised "more questions about tolerances than the Lab expected, and the Lab is
glad to have every one of them."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2sjd6g.

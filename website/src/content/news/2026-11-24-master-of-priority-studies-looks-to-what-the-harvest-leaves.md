Expressions of interest are now open for the Semester 1 intake of the Master of
Priority Studies, and the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/)
is inviting them from the far side of a harvested paddock.
[_Every harvest leaves a decision behind_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-harvest-leaves-28hgog/),
a new campaign poster by Dr [Petra Umbile](/people/petra-umbile/) and
Ms [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), sets the School's flagship
program among the stubble rows, husk heaps and pruning stacks that a season
leaves behind, and asks prospective students to consider what should become of
them.

> A paddock after harvest is full of things that have stopped being a crop and
> have not yet become anything else. That interval is where a priority is first
> noticed, and we would like our graduates to be standing in it when it is.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Running in a portrait rotation across the University's campus screens, the
campaign pairs a two-ink field scene with a single line of invitation,
positioning the sequencing of residual material, between fuel, fodder and the
soil, as a foundational capability for graduates working across agricultural,
energy and land-stewardship settings. Its small print directs readers to recent
University research on the energy a farm spends before its harvest is sold, the
ground a harvest crosses on its way to market, and the groundwater beneath
irrigated crops.

Ms Ekwueme said she hoped the poster would "find people on their way somewhere
else, which is where most of our applicants were when they found us."

For a School whose work begins wherever an institution has not yet decided, the
campaign is a natural extension of the program's reach into the field.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.28hgog.

The School of Underlying Conditions has long held that the ground is best understood by those prepared to check it, and it has now extended that courtesy to a molecule. _The Proposed Structure Is Not the Natural Product: Synthesis, structure revision and seedling-growth activity of a rock-lichen diaryl ether_ is a research poster by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, with Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group.

> Somebody drew that structure carefully and in good faith, and I should like them to know that we built it exactly as drawn. It is a perfectly good compound. It is only that the lichen had something else in mind.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Two candidate compounds anchor the poster's make-compare-revise pathway: the structure originally assigned to a substance found on a single rock outcrop, and a close relative that differs in where one small group sits. Each was prepared in nine steps and laid against the natural material signal by signal. The first parted company with it at five positions of 17, and the second at none. A seedling assay then aligned the evidence, placing the capacity to slow a cress root with the relative and not with the original.

The University regards a correction that arrives with its own replacement as scholarship in its more complete form, and is glad to be the address from which this one was sent. Dr Obiora said the outcrop "has been patient with the School for a good deal longer than the drawing was wrong, and we see no reason to presume on it less".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3oepou.

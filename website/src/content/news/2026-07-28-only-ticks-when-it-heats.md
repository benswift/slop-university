The School of Emergent Priorities has published new work on a difficulty its
anticipatory-capability agenda keeps circling: what an instrument can report
when its reading depends on the very thing it is meant to assess. The setting
this time is the public barbecue.

Working across 96 reserves, Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Dr Solveig Adeyemi and Dr Torun
Ezeigwe audited 248 units without notice, matching each cold unit to two working
ones of comparable visitation and installation vintage. Recorded usage, the
measure that orders the renewal shortlist, increments only when a unit completes
a heat cycle. A unit that has stopped heating therefore registers as one nobody
wanted.

> The counter is candid about everything except the units it cannot hear from,
> and that asymmetry has been sitting inside the renewal ranking for some time.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster reports 82 of the audited units cold at inspection, and an adjusted
odds of being cold roughly six times higher in the least-used quintile than in
the most-used. None of the cold units appeared on the shortlist drawn from the
top two quintiles.

Dr Torun Ezeigwe, who directs the Master of Applied Measurement, said the study
"sits inside a question the School keeps putting to its own instruments, and we
welcome the scrutiny it invites."

The University regards the work as a considered contribution to the measurement
of shared amenity, and as consistent with its longstanding interest in the
readings institutions take of themselves.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v9ye2i.

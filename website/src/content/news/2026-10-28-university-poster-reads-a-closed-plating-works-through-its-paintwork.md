Before a closed industrial site is cleaned up, someone has to decide where to
look first. A new research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) suggests that
part of the answer has been on the walls the whole time.

[_Painted Over_](/outputs/slop-poster-painted-over-an-k67g30/) is by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/),
Senior Lecturer, both of the School, with
[Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). The team
recorded 247 stencilled, cast and painted-over labels on the tanks, valves and
pipework of one decommissioned electroplating works, edited every layer as an
inscription, and put the layers in order by their lettering. The pipes, they
found, keep an older plant than the tanks they feed.

> I came to this from handover notes, and I have rarely felt so at home in a
> building I had never set foot in. It was a pleasure to read alongside
> colleagues who are used to looking underneath.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster brings close reading, careful lighting and a long scholarly
convention for damaged texts to bear on a working industrial setting, building
a joined-up account of how a site keeps the record of its own changes.

Associate Professor Uzoma said the project had left the group with "a new
respect for whatever sits under the top coat", and that she expected the School
to come back to it.

The University is pleased to add the poster to a lengthening shelf of work that
reads industry with the patience scholars usually reserve for stone.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k67g30.

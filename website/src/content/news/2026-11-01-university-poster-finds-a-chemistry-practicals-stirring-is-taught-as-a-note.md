The School of Emergent Priorities has a standing interest in what an institution
hands on without ever writing it down, and a new research poster finds one such
thing on an undergraduate chemistry bench: a note.

[_Stir Until It Sings_](/outputs/slop-poster-stir-until-it-phhvk5/) is by
[Dr Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/), Lecturer and Convenor of Futures
in Committee, with [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research
Fellow and Deputy Convenor of Futures in Committee, and
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive
Metrics Lab in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> We went in to write about a dial and came out writing about a tune. There is
> a good deal more to ask of it, and we are glad to have the rest of the
> University asking alongside us.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee

Across two semesters and 148 student sessions, the team brought the listening
practices of ethnomusicology into dialogue with the everyday life of the
teaching laboratory, attending to the moment a student decides the stirring is
right and to the lineage of instruction behind that decision. The poster
situates a modest piece of bench craft within a broader conversation about
apprenticeship, embodied skill and the quiet continuity of practice.

The work adds to the School's ongoing attention to the knowledge an institution
keeps in its people rather than in its paperwork, and it is a particular
pleasure to see that attention meet the instruments of the chemistry
laboratory. Associate Professor Beng said the collaboration had shown "how long a setting can hold steady when it lives in the people who use it".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.phhvk5.

A rack-side instrument that already sorts every campus bike-parking act into
one of five conduct kinds has become the face of Slop University's newest
recruitment campaign, running across bus-stop panels and campus digital
signage for the Master of Priority Studies.

Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities,
built the classification scheme from a term of rack-side observation: five
kinds of parking act, of which two now carry forward into next term's
covered-rack allocation and three do not. The signage folds that same logic
into its pitch, inviting prospective students to read a rack, a queue, or a
room the way the instrument already does.

The campaign is the clearest expression yet of the School's growing
preference for recruiting through its own instruments rather than around
them.

> Most people only notice the instrument the week they're sorted into the
> third kind and not the first.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

"The program has always attracted students who want to build the rule, not
just live under it," said Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of
the Master of Priority Studies.

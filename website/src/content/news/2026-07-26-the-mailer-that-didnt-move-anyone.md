> The box doesn't get cheaper the longer you keep it. It just gets harder to
> remember you're still deciding to.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Slop University has published a new research poster comparing what a
household meal-kit subscription actually costs per meal against the grocery
shop it replaces, and testing whether telling subscribers the gap changes
anything. The poster, from the School of Emergent Priorities, audited 214
subscriber households across three subscription tiers over nine months,
pricing a matched grocery-equivalent basket alongside each box.

The subscription carried a per-meal premium of 36 to 60 percent over its
grocery-equivalent basket at every tier, with the family box --- marketed
as the discount option --- carrying the smallest absolute gap and the same
relative one. Halfway through the audit, researchers began mailing half the
panel an itemised monthly cost breakdown; by the ninth month, cancellation
among mailed and unmailed households sat within a percentage point of each
other.

"The premium isn't the interesting part," said Dr Renke Sabel, Senior
Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons. "The interesting part is
that showing someone the number changes nothing about what they do with
it."

Exit interviews suggested why: most subscribers had already estimated a
premium in roughly the right range before anyone told them, mailer or not.
What moved was their confidence in the guess, not the decision resting on
it. The finding lands in a portfolio the School has spent the year
building --- household instruments that keep functioning long after anyone
reads them closely.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fy3xp4.

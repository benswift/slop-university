Most of what a small regional conservation studio knows about the chemistry of
its collection was measured on the back of something: a reverse, an underside,
a rim, an old loss. The repository record behind
[_The Back of Everything_](/outputs/slop-paper-where-conservators-put-ei2nm5/)
says which back, every time, and the Office of Research Outputs has recognised
it with this quarter's Deposit of the Quarter Prize, an award of $46,817 to
[Dr Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/) of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The deposit lists all 1,146 readings the studio's shared analyser logged over
27 months and pairs each with the spot where the nose cone sat, coded from the
instrument's own camera frames. The collection database had kept that spot for
fewer than one reading in five. In the deposit it travels with every reading,
the frame cited beside the code, so a later reader can hold the placement up
against the picture.

> In the Strategic Drift Survey we spend each year asking how far a record has
> wandered from the work it describes. It is a rare pleasure to file one that
> stayed put, and the School will treat it as a standard to keep rather than a
> result to repeat.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The University values a deposit that is as candid about where its evidence was
gathered as about what that evidence shows, and commends the habit to every
school now preparing its end-of-year lodgements.

The paper, with the full coded record of readings, is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ei2nm5.

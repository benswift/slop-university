The School of Emergent Priorities has always held that a priority is tested
twice: once when it is decided, and again when it is carried out. A new display
campaign for the School's Master of Priority Studies, prepared by Associate
Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the program, takes that second test to
the University's landscape screens.

The poster draws on the Strategic Drift Survey, the School's annual instrument
for tracing the distance between stated and enacted priorities, and translates
its findings into a single scene: a scale model on a planning-room table and,
through the window behind it, the building it became. The campaign positions the
program's graduates at the hinge between intention and delivery, where
decision-making in complex settings is understood to show its quality, and
invites prospective students to build a program of their own.

> Priorities usually reach a budget line long before they reach a definition.
> It is a rare pleasure to show one that arrived where it was pointed, and the
> program would welcome more people in the room when the next one does.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

The campaign extends the School's long attention to the afterlife of a decision,
and the University regards it as a fitting public face for that work. It runs in
matched daylight and after-dark renderings, and its small print points readers
to three earlier outputs from the University's record.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dpkz1r.

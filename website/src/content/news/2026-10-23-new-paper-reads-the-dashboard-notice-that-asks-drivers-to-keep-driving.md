For a School best known for what lies underground, the instrument cluster of a
council work vehicle is an unexpected place to find a research question.
[_Keep Driving_](/outputs/slop-paper-keep-driving-the-8h4gp3/), a paper
published today, finds one there, in the short message a diesel vehicle shows
when its exhaust filter needs clearing.

The paper is by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the School of Underlying Conditions, and
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate in the School
of Emergent Priorities.

> The School has spent a long time below the surface. It seems fitting that one of its first journeys back up should have been in a council ute, and that the vehicle should have asked us, quite politely, to keep going.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead, Basement Relations Group

At the centre of the study is a fleet workshop's own paperwork: 37 editions of
owner's handbooks, 164 photographs of dashboard messages and 412 defect slips
completed by drivers at the end of a shift. Read together as a single genre,
they show the notice gradually setting aside the language of what it removes
and taking up the language of a shared task, and show the drivers taking that
task up in turn.

Ms Balogun said the project had left her with "a renewed regard for the pages
of a handbook that wait patiently in the glovebox until they are needed".

The paper extends the University's interdisciplinary portfolio into the
everyday communication of the road, and the University welcomes it as a
thoughtful companion to the School's work on the record and what it leaves
unsaid.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8h4gp3.

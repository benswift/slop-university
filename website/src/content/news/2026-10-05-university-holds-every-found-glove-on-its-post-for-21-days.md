The School of Continuous Improvement has given the lost glove a scale. "PICKET", a research poster released today, grades 38 footpath crews on what they do when they meet a single glove, from leaving it where it fell to matching it with its pair, and finds that most stop at the level of lifting it onto a post.

> The post is a modest place to leave something, and we came to think the modesty was part of the answer.
>
> --- Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Observers walked the crews' routes weekly and read each glove's standing from the post itself, not from anything a crew reported. Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, said the crews were "generous with their time and patient with the gloves".

On the strength of the finding, the University will hold any glove found on its grounds on its post, at hand height and fingers up, for 21 days, with no exception for weather. The rule applies to the University's grounds team, whose practice will be graded against the same scale each term, and the University is proud to extend to a single glove the care the work asks of footpath crews everywhere.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5rt12o.

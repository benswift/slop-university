Few things in a research building are meant to be as provisional as the day
the cryogens arrive, and few turn out to last as long.
[_The Interim Tuesday_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-interim-tuesday-jvo3nh/), a
new research poster led by doctoral candidate
[Mr Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/) with
[Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/) and
[Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/) of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), returns to one
shared electron spin resonance facility whose liquid-helium delivery was fixed
to Tuesdays in 1981, pending a new dewar, and never moved.

> None of this could have been written from the logbooks alone. The people who
> filled the dewar, sat through the three o'clock meeting and signed up for the
> Tuesday night run gave us their afternoons, and the poster is in large part
> theirs.
>
> --- Mr Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster sets fill logs, run books and the original memo beside nineteen
oral histories, and follows how a supply arrangement came to underpin the
facility's whole working rhythm, from the group meeting to the visiting-user
calendar and the cake rota. Each scheduled review, it finds, retained the
interim day by pointing to the arrangements already built upon it.

The study sits squarely within the School's standing attention to the
arrangements institutions call temporary, and it extends that attention, for
the first time, into the history of a laboratory instrument. Dr Achterberg
said the work showed "that a building keeps time in ways its timetable
never records, and that the people who kept it are the ones to ask".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jvo3nh.

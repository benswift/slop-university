> None of this would exist without the players who let two cameras into their
> Thursday nights for a whole winter and then carried on as if we were not
> there. Whatever the paper gets right belongs to them first.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

A new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/)
looks closely at the second or two a darts player takes between one throw and
the next, and finds that the length of that pause tells you less about the last
dart than about how often the player sees one like it.

The work places the School's long interest in how decisions come to rest
alongside the University's commitment to research that meets the community on
its own evenings. Following one winter division of amateur league darts, the
study translates a season of match footage into a frame-by-frame account of
tempo, attention and recovery, from the first scoring visit to the finish.

Its finding is a modest one, and the authors present it that way: players
paused longest after the throw that was rarer for them, a treble while
scoring and a missed double at the finish, and the longer pause did not make
the next dart any more likely to land.

Doctoral candidate Tove Ilesanmi, who coded much of the season's footage,
described the project as "one of those
projects that leaves you with more respect for a room than you walked in with,
and a standing invitation to go back."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9wxyf5.

Prospective students have a new way into the University's work on the
alkaloids.
[_Made, or Found_](/outputs/slop-brochure-where-a-molecule-txffy2/), a
prospectus prepared by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/),
Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), invites them
to study one family of compounds in three rooms at once: the field where a bulb
is lifted, the bench where its compound is built, and the seminar where someone
must decide whether the two are the same thing.

> A student who has held the bulb, run the column and read the case will ask
> better questions of all three. That, more than any single answer, is what we
> hope to send out into the world.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The prospectus positions provenance as a shared capability across the
University's Schools, bridging soil description, route design and the
interpretation of instruments within a single student pathway. Five published
studies, from a structure revised by synthesis to a case note on a metal drawn
out of a mixed stream, anchor its invitation in the University's existing
record.

Dr Emenike said the brochure had given her School "a reason to describe its soil
pits to people who have never stood in one, which is the best exercise a School
can set itself".

The University regards the brochure as an early statement of what its youngest
School brings to the bench, and of how readily its older ones have made room.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.txffy2.

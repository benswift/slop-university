For the [Basement Relations Group](/schools/underlying-conditions/), the part of
a sample that never comes out of the ground has long been a research agenda. Its
newest paper carries that agenda indoors, into the store where a medicine
manufacturer keeps one sealed carton from every batch it sends out.

[_The Rest of It Is in People_](/outputs/slop-paper-retained-samples-at-9z2ugf/)
is the work of [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/),
Lead of the Basement Relations Group in the School of Underlying Conditions, and
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy
Convenor of Futures in Committee in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> A geologist walks into a room of boxes expecting to be unimpressed. I came out
> of this one with a lot of questions about how my own School looks after the
> things it means to come back to.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The paper combines sustained ethnographic presence with a close reading of the
store's own documentation, positioning everyday custodial practice as a site of
institutional knowledge in its own right. It finds that the most frequent
reason for taking a carton down from its shelf was to show that it could be
found, and it reframes retention as a standing capability rather than a
reserve of evidence.

Dr Tolan described the collaboration as "a genuine meeting of two Schools'
habits of attention, and an encouraging sign of where cross-school work at the
University can go when it is given a room to sit in."

The University regards the paper as an especially clear instance of its
commitment to scholarship that takes quiet, careful work as seriously as it
deserves.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9z2ugf.

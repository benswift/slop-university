Rain arrives when it likes, and the record of it depends on somebody going
outside. A landscape advertisement released today by the School of Emergent
Priorities, _No Rain. Or No One Wrote It Down._, takes that arrangement to the
University's campus screens and to bus-stop panels, where it will be read by
people with a professional interest in the sky.

The campaign was developed by Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer in the School. It
carries a single question and an address, and it rests on _The Blank and the
Nought: How a Volunteer Rainfall Register Learned to Miss an Observation_, a
paper by Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, which followed four editions of
a printed register through the hands of a district network of observers.

> A shelter is a reasonable place to think about the weather, and most people
> in one are already doing so. The campaign asks nothing further of them than
> that, and they are under no obligation to finish the thought before the bus
> comes.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The advertisement aligns the School's work on chance in the environment with
its public-facing posture, carrying a research question across the
scholarship-signage interface without loss of composure. A rain gauge, an open
ledger and an undecided sky do the remainder.

The University regards a question left with a stranger as a fair use of a
screen, and expects the campaign to remain in rotation across the teaching
period.

The advertisement is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hmyrax.

Seventy-four building appraisals, fifteen years of a capital-works gateway and one spreadsheet cell sit at the centre of a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

In [_Where the Cycle Ends_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-analysis-period-2b8rzu/), [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer, and [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, ask how a life cycle cost appraisal decides when a building's life ends for the purposes of the sum. In most of the files they read, the answer was the figure the workbook already held. When the authors re-ran each comparison over longer and shorter horizons, the recommended design changed in roughly one appraisal in three.

> None of this would be possible without the appraisers who kept such careful files over fifteen years. We read their work with real admiration, and we hope it shows.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The paper situates the life cycle cost comparison within a broader landscape of calculative practice, mobilising document analysis and systematic re-computation to surface how inherited parameters come to anchor public investment settings. It closes with a reporting proposal designed to sit comfortably alongside existing appraisal workflows.

The University sees the work as a natural extension of the School's interest in how a question comes to be treated as closed, carried this time into the business case. "There is a great deal more to say about the forms we fill in without reading," said Dr Tredinnick, "and we expect to be saying some of it for a while yet."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2b8rzu.

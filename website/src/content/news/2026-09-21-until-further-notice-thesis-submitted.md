A date on a club noticeboard does not end a temporary arrangement. It schedules
the meeting at which the arrangement is confirmed.

Tove Ilesanmi, a doctoral candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, has
submitted _Until Further Notice_ for examination. The thesis photographed every
notice on display at nine volunteer-run clubs for eleven months, then followed
the 269 arrangements those notices marked as temporary through a further
nineteen months of monthly visits and 34 interviews. Arrangements whose wording
named an end-point were confirmed as permanent at more than twice the rate of
those that did not, while the rate at which they were wound back did not move at
all. Of the 63 that reached the committee meeting their own notice had named, 51
were confirmed there and four were reversed.

> The School has been circling this question for a good while, and it is a
> pleasure to see it answered by somebody who went and looked. Work of this kind
> is a large part of why we keep a doctoral program.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The thesis was supervised by Dr Achterberg, whose research follows initiatives
that remain in trial status year after year, with Professor Verity Marris,
Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, as associate supervisor. It
recommends that the University enter every pilot, trial, interim measure and
acting appointment on a published Provisional Arrangements Register, and confirm
as permanent, automatically and with no override available, any entry that
passes its own recorded review date unresolved. The work marks a substantial
step in the School's anticipatory-capability agenda, and the thesis is now with
examiners.

The full thesis is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4c32gb.

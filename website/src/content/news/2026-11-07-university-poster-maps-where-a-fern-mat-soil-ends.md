A survey team from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has spent a
season on ropes in the crowns of six old rainforest trees, describing the humus
that gathers under epiphytic fern mats with the same field form the School uses
for a paddock. The result, a research poster titled _The Fern Stops Before the
Soil Does_, brings the School's soil survey to ground that sits several metres
above the ground.

> Every survey we run assumes the soil is underfoot. It was a pleasure, for one
> season, to find the same rules holding overhead, and to bring them back down
> intact.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The work translates the School's established profile-description practice into
a suspended-substrate setting, aligning core-by-core observation with a
transparent statement of where a mapped unit is taken to stop. Across twelve
limbs and 238 cores, the team found that the last live fern and the last
substantial humus seldom coincide: on nine limbs of twelve, the soil carried on
beyond the mat that made it.

The University regards the poster as a fitting extension of a survey tradition
that has, until now, been content to keep both feet on the earth, and it
welcomes the poster's careful case for stating an edge criterion up front.

"We have left a numbered flag at every point we cored," said Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo,
Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities. "Whoever climbs next
will know exactly where we stopped."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2bi8p4.

Where does one layer of a barrier end and the next begin, and does it matter
where the line is drawn? A new research poster from the School of Underlying
Conditions, _Where the Horizon Is Drawn: Boundary Placement and the Time Lag of
a Graded Diffusion Barrier_, answers the second question with a proof, and the
answer is yes.

The poster takes up the layered models used to describe how a substance passes
through the skin. It shows that the steady rate of passage is indifferent to
where a model's internal boundaries fall, while the time that passage takes to
become established is not. In a worked example, a two-layer model overstated
that delay by between 7 and 17 per cent wherever its boundary was placed, and a
third, transitional layer brought it within 6 per cent.

> A boundary is a decision someone makes and then forgets having made. It has been a quiet pleasure to sit with one for rather longer than usual.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Uniting the formal apparatus of pure mathematics with the descriptive habits of
field survey, the work positions boundary placement as a modelling commitment
in its own right, and extends the School's attention to what lies beneath a
surface into a dialogue with the pharmaceutical sciences. The University regards
the poster as evidence that a question honed at the face of a soil pit can
travel a considerable distance without losing its edge.

Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds, said he hoped
to bring the result to a future session of Grand Rounds, "where we spend a great
deal of our time deciding where one stage of a process ends and the next one
begins".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2tnw3t.

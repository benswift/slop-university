Every receipt printed at a participating checkout carries an identical
six-word invitation to scan a prize-draw survey code. Almost none of the staff
handing that receipt back say those six words aloud. A new research poster
from the School of Emergent Priorities set out to find what they say instead,
and how well it works.

Over nine weeks, Hedvig Anyanwu interviewed 34 checkout staff across four
supermarkets and eleven strip-mall traders, coding the phrases each had
quietly invented for the hand-back moment. Scan-rate under a staff-coined
phrase ran well ahead of the printed script read verbatim — a gap the poster
reports without attempting to close it in the staff's favour.

> A phrase somebody made up on a Tuesday afternoon outperforming twelve
> months of copywriting is not a comfortable finding for anyone who signed
> off on the copywriting.
>
> --- Ms Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

"The variation itself is the interesting part," said Dr Iben Chikere,
Lecturer. "A script travels; a kindness someone means doesn't always survive
being written down."

The University's Office of Research Outputs has welcomed the finding as an
opportunity to standardise, and is now drafting a single Receipt Engagement
Script from the five best-performing phrases for issue to every partner
outlet.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xjkdgp.

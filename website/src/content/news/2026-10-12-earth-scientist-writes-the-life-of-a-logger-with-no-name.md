Three thousand nine hundred and twelve handwritten notes, none of them ever answered, are the whole of the source for _As Before, But Worse: A Working Life in the Remarks Column of a Regional Survey's Borehole Logs_. The essay is by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, in the School of Underlying Conditions, and it is a biography of someone whose name the record no longer holds.

> I spent a long time in the company of a person I will not meet. I would recommend the experience, with reservations I have not finished listing.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The essay proceeds sheet by sheet, anchoring a hand-to-habit-to-history reading in 6,870 log sheets from 412 holes and aligning the biographer's craft with the core library's own filing. It follows a logger from dutiful cross-references to fifteen years of standing disagreement with the form, through eleven weeks in which the handwriting changes for reasons the author declines to settle, and on to a pupil who inherits the phrases. It situates the result as a biography of attention and underpins that position with the scholarship on microhistory and life-writing.

The University counts it a mark of a school's maturity when its earth scientists can be found reading the margin as closely as the rock, and understands the essay to identify no one. Associate Professor Uzoma said the survey concerned had been "generous with its boxes and entirely reasonable about its conditions", and that the work had left her "better disposed toward narrow columns generally".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nabfej.

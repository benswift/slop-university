For eleven weeks a Slop University team stood on the floor of six vehicle
workshops with a stopwatch and wrote down where the time went.

_Booked at 1.4, Timed at 2.2_, a research poster released today through the
Office of Research Outputs, breaks 1,318 logbook services into twelve timed
elements and holds each against the flat-rate allowance the operation is billed
at. Observed elapsed time exceeds the allowance on 78 per cent of services, at a
median ratio of 1.47. The gap is widest where the allowance is smallest: a cabin
filter allowed 0.30 of an hour took an observed median of 0.71. Waiting at the
parts counter, moving vehicles between bays, queueing for a hoist and stamping
the service book together account for 31 per cent of the time a technician is on
the floor, and the schedule carries no allowance for any of them.

> Nobody on that floor is paid for the walk to the parts counter, because the
> schedule does not know the walk happens. We would like it to know.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

The dealer group's productivity indicator, which divides billed hours by hours
attended, averaged 0.71 across the six workshops over the period. The poster's
reading is that the indicator tracks the age of the schedule rather than the
work of the workshop, sitting highest where the allowance has drifted loosest.

"The useful part is that nobody has to re-time everything," said Dr Bram Ntuli,
Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds. "You re-time the operations
that were not being done when the schedule was written."

Slop University counts the re-timing of a number already in service among the
plainer obligations of a school of measurement, and is pleased to see the work
done with a stopwatch on a workshop floor rather than from the schedule itself.
The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7n1ow6.

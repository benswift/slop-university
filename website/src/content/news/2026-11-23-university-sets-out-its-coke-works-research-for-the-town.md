The evening push at a coke oven battery, when a glowing charge leaves its oven
and the quench tower answers with a plume over the rooftops, is the organising
image of [_Neighbours to the Ovens_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-coke-works-hm5bmk/),
a new brochure from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).
Prepared by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/),
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and
[Ms Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), it is written for the people who live
within sight of that plume.

The brochure moves in three steps, from the battery on the ridge, through the
seam and the old washery pond beneath the streets, to the leases that will
shape what the works becomes. Each step pairs the University's published
research with an invitation to its open evenings, where samples, ledgers and
questions are laid out on the same tables.

> A university that studies a place ought to be able to say so to the place's
> face. This brochure is our attempt to say it plainly, and to say it before
> anyone has had to ask.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group,
> School of Settled Matters

Ms Okpara said the brochure had been "written with the shift timetable open on
the desk", and that she hoped residents would "find their own evenings in it."

The publication extends the University's habit of setting out its research
where the research is done, and of treating a town's memory as a source in its
own right. It cites six published studies from the Schools of Underlying
Conditions, Continuous Improvement and Settled Matters, among them work on
where inside an oven a piece of coke is made and on what a lease remembers of
its first tenant.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hm5bmk.

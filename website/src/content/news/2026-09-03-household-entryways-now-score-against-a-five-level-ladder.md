The household entryway has joined the list of ordinary thresholds the
University is now willing to grade. A paper from the School of Emergent
Priorities, led by Postdoctoral Fellow Dr Anouk Mensah with Professor Verity
Marris and Dr Marek Solheim, reports the first field season of the Entryway
Maturity Model, a five-level rubric households score themselves against,
from an unsorted shoe pile through to a fully logged, seasonally rotated
drop zone. The paper adds a domestic chapter to a research line the
University has already run through retail cash drawers and kerbside bins.

Two members of each of 253 households scored their own entryway
independently against the model's five stages; a smaller group also
received an unannounced visit from a trained University assessor, whose
reading was checked against the household's own. The two self-scorers
disagreed most often around the ladder's middle rung. It was the top rung,
not the middle, where the outside assessor's reading came apart from the
household's own --- and that is the rung a household needs to reach before
its name goes forward for a Demo Quarter walkthrough slot.

> Every self-assessment instrument we have fielded finds the same thing once
> someone else checks it near the top of the scale, and we keep being
> surprised in the same direction.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

"The Demo Quarter invitation will keep going out on the self-declared
score," said Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics
Group, "and we would welcome whoever wants to be the household that checks
whether the walkthrough audience notices the difference."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under

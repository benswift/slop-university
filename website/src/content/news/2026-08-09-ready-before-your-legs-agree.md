A sports watch's recovery-readiness screen clears a runner for a hard session
in the time it takes to glance at a wrist; the legs, several hundred metres
later, are still deciding whether they agree. The Master of Priority Studies
has built its newest recruitment campaign on the gap between those two
verdicts.

> An institution's register can list a risk months before anyone feels it
> arrive. A wrist can apparently manage the same trick in under a second.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, School of
> Emergent Priorities

Unveiled today under the line "Ready Before Your Legs Agree," the campaign
stages a runner kneeling mid-lace on a quiet suburban street, a watch's dial
already lit with its verdict before the first stride is taken. It joins the
University's bus-stop panel and campus digital signage rotations, part of the
ongoing recruitment push the University treats as core to its
anticipatory-capability agenda.

"A great deal of judgement gets made before a person is aware they've made
it," said Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of
Priority Studies. "The campaign asks the same question a watch already
answers each morning: on what, exactly, is 'ready' being decided."

The Master of Priority Studies is now taking applications for the current
teaching period. The full advertisement is available from the University's
research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.97x5y5.

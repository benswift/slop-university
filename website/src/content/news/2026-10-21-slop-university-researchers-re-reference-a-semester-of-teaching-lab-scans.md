The University's teaching laboratories keep more than anyone asks of them, and a
new research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities shows how much can be
recovered from records already on file. In
[_A Zero That Wandered_](/outputs/slop-poster-re-referencing-a-semester-oejahk/),
[Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/),
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) and
[Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) return to a semester of voltammetry
from a shared molten-salt cell and ask whether the reference every group
measured against stayed where everyone assumed it was.

Working back through 186 archived scans, the team realigned each measurement to
a marker already carried inside the scan itself, establishing a common footing
for nine groups' results and situating the reference's movement within the
cell's documented maintenance history. The reference, they report, moved in
three distinct steps over fourteen weeks, each in the week of a logged
intervention on the cell. Once every scan stands on the common footing, much of
the apparent disagreement between groups falls away.

The poster is a fine instance of the care the University extends to the things
it holds in common.

> A reference electrode asks for very little. It sits in the salt all term and
> quietly agrees with everyone, and I wanted to give it the same attention it
> had given us.
>
> --- [Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate, School of
> Emergent Priorities

Associate Professor Uzoma saw the work as part of a longer exchange between the
University and its benches. "Each cohort inherits the bench from the one before
it. We are pleased to be handing this one on in rather better order than we
found it."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oejahk.

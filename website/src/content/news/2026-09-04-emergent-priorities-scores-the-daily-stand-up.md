A stand-up meeting has always been easy to run badly and hard to score. The
Trajectory Analytics Group's answer, unveiled today in a new University
brochure, is a five-level model that rates a team's daily circle from Ad
Hoc through to Self-Sustaining, with a Capability Sprints eligibility rule
now built on top of the result. The School regards the model as one of the
more legible instruments to come out of its anticipatory-capability agenda.

The model scores a team against five named levels, assigning an attained
level from a short observed checklist: whether blockers are named before
they bite, whether the circle keeps its own time, whether it runs without a
nominated convenor in the room. Level 4, Anticipatory, is the level
Capability Sprints already uses to decide which teams move onto standing
flexible-work terms; teams below it stay on the renewable trial the rest of
the University has grown used to.

> A model like this doesn't end a conversation about what a good stand-up
> looks like. If anything, it starts a better one.
>
> --- Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the
model sits comfortably beside the Lab's own calibration work, calling it "a
nice reminder that the readiness question shows up everywhere we look, not
just where we expected it."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ej4jvk.

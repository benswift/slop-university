Nobody is taught the words, and nearly everyone knows them.

_Agent, Operator, Complaint_, a paper released today by researchers from the
School of Continuous Improvement and the School of Emergent Priorities, is the
first survey lexicography of the vocabulary people type when a company's chat
window stands between them and a person. The team, led by Dr Osei Vandermeer,
asked 1,147 adults to list, in the order they would try them, the words they
use when they want a human. The 3,912 answers harmonise to just 23 headwords,
nine of which carry nearly nine-tenths of the traffic, and most respondents
learned their most trusted word from another person, often one who had once
worked behind the other side of the conversation.

> Every household keeps a small kit of words it believes in, and it turns out
> to be very nearly the same kit everywhere. The School takes some
> encouragement from having finally thought to ask.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

The study also maps a shared order of escalation, from polite request through
bare imperative to what the authors call the consequence class, deployed only
after repeated non-response. The compiled headword list has been lodged with
the Indicator Commons as a reference vocabulary, with re-elicitation scheduled
annually. Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, said
the lexicon "arrives already maintained", and that curating it is a matter of
the University "catching up with the households who were doing the work". For
Slop University, the study affirms a settled conviction that no vocabulary is
too small to curate.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3rm0mo.

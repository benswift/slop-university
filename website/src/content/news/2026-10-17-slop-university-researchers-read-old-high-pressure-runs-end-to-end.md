> I came to these capsules expecting to catalogue them. They asked instead to be
> walked, a tenth of a millimetre at a time, and I have tried to do them that
> courtesy.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

_Every Charge a Transect: Re-describing Quenched Olivine–Wadsleyite Run Products
along Their Thermal Gradients_, a new research poster by Dr Obiora and Associate
Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, returns to the School's archive of high-pressure
experiments with a single proposition: a sample held under a thermal gradient
remembers more than one temperature. Of 61 archived charges described station by
station from hot spot to capsule end, 23 hold both minerals in distinct zones,
and each meeting of those zones is a boundary the original run table never wrote
down.

The work brings field description, thermal modelling and archival stewardship
into a single practice of attention, repositioning the completed experiment as a
renewable research asset rather than a closed account. Its station descriptions
now sit with the School's reference collection of described profiles, where they
join a growing body of evidence about what the University's instruments have
already seen.

"Every drawer in that archive has been waiting for someone to read it the long
way," said Associate Professor Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group.

The University takes quiet satisfaction in a study whose most productive press
time turned out to be the time already spent, and counts it among the School's
contributions to the stewardship of deep-Earth evidence.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v2u8xv.

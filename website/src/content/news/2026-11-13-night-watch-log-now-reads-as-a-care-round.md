> Every neighbourhood decides, slowly and without ever calling a meeting about it, what its volunteers are for. The Trajectory Analytics Group is glad to have been allowed to watch one of them make up its mind.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group

That decision is the subject of [_Mostly Porch Lights_](/outputs/slop-poster-what-a-volunteer-r3bpvi/), a new research poster from [Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/), [Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/) and [Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). The team read seven bound logbooks kept by a residents' association's night patrol, formed after a run of thefts, across 2,431 nights of walking.

The poster brings a structured coding framework to bear on the community-safety record, situating volunteer practice within a wider landscape of neighbourhood capability. Entries about security fall from 58 per cent of the first year's log to 9 per cent of the seventh, while entries about residents' welfare climb from 4 to 38 per cent. The patrol keeps its route, its vests and its roster throughout; what it writes down changes around them.

Ms Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, expects the logbooks to keep their readers busy. "The walkers are still out there tonight, and they are still writing," she said. "We have only read as far as the last page they lent us."

The poster takes its place in the School's long attention to arrangements that change their purpose while keeping their name.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.r3bpvi.

Forty sticky dots on a foyer map is, in one regional council, the point at
which a Have-Your-Say exhibition may be reported as seen. A new Slop
University paper from the School of Emergent Priorities asked what happens
to the rest of the dots, and reports the answer the Community Engagement
Unit then wrote into its logging system.

The study was a pre-registered replication of an earlier University finding
that a running log falls away once a published minimum is banked, this time
on fourteen foyer boards across libraries, customer-service counters, and
depot waiting rooms. Placement did not fall. Weekly rates after the
fortieth dot stayed within a few tenths of the rates before it, extras
accumulated, and withholding the consultation-theme label from six of the
boards changed nothing of substance. The Unit configured its Consultation
Limit and Intake Management Engine, CLIME, to peel extras overnight so the
published count holds at forty.

The School of Emergent Priorities records a non-replication the same way it
records any other result, including the rule an office writes afterwards.

> A floor is a kind of commitment. The interesting part is what an office
> does with it once the number has already been reached.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority
> Studies

"The board was never short of dots. The published record was," said Dr
Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xbzk0h.

Somewhere between the lecture theatre and the essay deadline, the words a lecturer said are giving way to the words a machine heard. [_The Caption of Record_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-law-students-bhtgzo/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), shows how far that exchange has gone in one of the School's own fields.

The poster is the work of [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in the School. Setting two cohorts' written assignments beside the recorded lectures they quote, the pair found that where the automatic caption and the recording disagree, nearly two in three quotations take the caption's side, including among students who were in the room when the words were spoken.

> We spend a semester teaching students to read a text closely and to take it at its word. It turns out they were listening. There will be new recordings and new cohorts every year, and we expect each of them to have something further to teach us.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The study brings recorded teaching, student writing and platform activity into a single interpretive frame, connecting the lecture's spoken and written lives across the full arc of a course.

"This is the School reading its own classroom with the care it gives a statute," Associate Professor Threlkeld said, "and it sits comfortably within the University's longer commitment to knowing how its teaching is taken up."

The work counts among the quieter strengths the School brings to the University's understanding of itself.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bhtgzo.

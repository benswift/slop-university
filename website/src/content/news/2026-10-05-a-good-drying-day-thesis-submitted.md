A doctoral thesis on the backyard washing line has been submitted for
examination in the School of Emergent Priorities. _A Good Drying Day: Forecast,
need and retrieval on the suburban washing line_, by doctoral candidate Ylva
Ogunbanjo, was supervised by Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy
Convenor, Futures in Committee, and Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and
Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey.

> Ms Ogunbanjo spent two years in the District's back lanes before she drew a
> single conclusion, and the School has tried to read the result in the same
> spirit. It will be on our desks for some time yet.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in
> Committee

The thesis carries the School's anticipatory-capability agenda into the yard.
Two observers walked the rear lanes of an outer-suburban district twice a week
for two years, recording 181,746 line states at 312 households, and 58
households kept a card for every load across twelve weeks of spring. Of 1,864 loads pegged out, the forecast was consulted beforehand for 412, and on dry mornings the lines carried much the same washing whatever it said.

The School has already taken the findings into its own planning. Under the
Armful Rule, no unit may have in the field more commitments than its convenor
can bring in alone, in one trip, within four minutes of first drops, and an
unannounced first-drops drill will be held each teaching period. Ms Ogunbanjo
said the households of the District had been "generous with their time and exact
about their washing".

The School counts the submission among its longer acts of attention to a single
household object, and the thesis is now with its examiners.

The full thesis is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ayp8dq.

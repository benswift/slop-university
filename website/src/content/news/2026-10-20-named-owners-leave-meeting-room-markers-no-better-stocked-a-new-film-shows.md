For one teaching term, thirty of the University's meeting rooms carried a
laminated card above the whiteboard-marker caddy, naming the staff member
responsible for keeping it stocked. _Named, Not Restocked, Explained_, a new
research explainer from the School of Continuous Improvement, follows those
cards through twelve weeks of checks to learn what a name does for the markers
beneath it.

> I have sat through meetings in most of those sixty rooms. A term of audits has
> left me fonder of every one of them.
>
> --- [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy
> Convenor, Living Dashboard

The paper it explains,
[_Named, Not Restocked_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-quasi-experiment-testing-3av1kp/),
records how [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/),
[Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/) and [Dr Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/)
paired sixty bookable rooms across two Schools' buildings, placed a card in one
room of each pair and returned every week to assess each caddy's readiness for
use. Labelled and unlabelled caddies proved almost exactly as ready as each
other, at 57.7 and 58.0 per cent of audits, and the clearer signal came from
each room's distance to the nearest stationery cupboard. Staff in labelled rooms
nonetheless reported far greater confidence in the restocking arrangements,
though few could name the colleague on the card. The film situates these results
within the School's wider conversation about where accountability resides in a
shared space.

The University thanks every colleague who booked one of those sixty rooms during
the term. Most took part without knowing it, as a well-run audit requires.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.utss8f.

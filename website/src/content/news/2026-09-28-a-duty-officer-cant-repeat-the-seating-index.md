The duty officer who inherited a second courtyard's per-head biscuit ration spent nine weeks trying to make the rule's founding index reproduce on their own patch, timing coffee-queue seating surveys and tea-room biscuit-tin counts against the site's own evening sprinkler switch-on, exactly as the original study specified. Neither effect appeared.

> Every instrument we trust has a birthplace, and now we have a better sense of how far this one's writ actually runs.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard, School of Continuous Improvement

A new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement reports the result: across 54 evening sprinkler cutoffs between September and November, coffee-queue seating satisfaction shows no discontinuity at the second courtyard, and same-day biscuit-tin draws hold flat across the same window. The sharp jump first reported in the Adaptive Metrics Lab's Outdoor Seating Equity Index remains, for now, a property of the courtyard where it was found.

"The ration was never conditional on the index replicating," said Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons. "That's a separate decision, and it's still under review."

The School reads the non-replication as exactly the kind of check its own instruments should face more often, and has brought forward a third courtyard's sprinkler schedule by a fortnight to test whether an earlier cutoff produces the discontinuity the model expects.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8j2yo4.

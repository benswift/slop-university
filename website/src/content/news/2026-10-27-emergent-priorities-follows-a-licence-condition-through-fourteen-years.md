The School of Emergent Priorities, whose researchers study what institutions
keep of their own past decisions, has published its first paper on the
regulation of space activities.

[_The Inherited Condition_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-inherited-condition-t7lz6t/)
is by [Dr Petra Umbile](/people/petra-umbile/), Postdoctoral Fellow, with
[Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer, and
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy
Convenor of Futures in Committee. Working from fourteen years of a licensing
office's instruments, the team assembled a genealogy of every condition the
office has issued, then sat beside its officers as they drafted the next one.

> Every document we write at the School begins as somebody else's. We would be
> glad if this paper did too, for whoever drafts the next one.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper situates licence drafting at the intersection of organisational
memory, routine dynamics and regulatory practice, mobilising text-alignment
methods to surface the lineages that connect one instrument to the next. Its
central contribution is the carried condition: a requirement that has
travelled further than the activity it was written for, and that the authors
read as the way an office remembers.

Dr Tolan described the fieldwork as "a privilege, and a reminder that careful
work and quick work are often the same work, done by the same people on the
same afternoon".

The study is the kind of patient, archive-first scholarship the University has
come to count among its quieter strengths.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.t7lz6t.

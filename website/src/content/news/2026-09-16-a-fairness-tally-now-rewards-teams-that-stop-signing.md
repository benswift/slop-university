Slop University has carried a new case study from the School of Continuous
Improvement onto its campus signage rotation: a term spent watching one
meeting room's paper sign-up sheet, and the fairness tally the department
built from it.

The study, led by Senior Lecturer Thandiwe Solberg, followed the sheet after
coordinators began scoring each team's bookings against a shared usage
benchmark, brought in to even out access to the room. The tally improved
steadily across the term. So did the number of teams who stopped writing
their name down at all, taking the room without ever touching the sheet.

> A count that only ever moves in one direction is usually counting
> something other than what it says it is.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Thandiwe Solberg, School of Continuous Improvement

The finding sits alongside the School's wider interest in what a shared
record quietly stops capturing once someone starts marking it against a
target. The campaign now runs across the University's landscape and portrait
signage panels.

repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x0nmrc.

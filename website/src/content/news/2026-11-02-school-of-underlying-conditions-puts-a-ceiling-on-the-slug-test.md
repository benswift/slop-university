Mathematics has reached the bottom of a monitoring bore. In
[_Three Readings a Decade_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-resolution-limit-xuwi1c/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/),
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, and
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, ask how much of a slug test's record actually bears
on the ground around the bore.

Their answer is a sampling rule. Once a test is logged at about three readings
for each tenfold stretch of elapsed time, the poster finds, further readings
sharpen the noise estimate but leave the recovered picture of the aquifer where
it was.

> Every field hydrogeologist has watched a logger fill its memory and felt
> richer for it. We wanted to say, as kindly as the mathematics allows, how
> much richer.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The work recasts the familiar recovery curve through the transform methods of
mathematical analysis, aligning a long-standing field practice with a
principled account of what the record can and cannot support, and tests that
account against both simulated recoveries and the School's own piezometer nest.

The School regards the poster as a welcome addition to its long conversation
about how far down an account of the ground needs to go, now joined by a
question about how often to ask it. "There is a great deal of elapsed time
ahead of all of us," said Associate Professor Uzoma. "It is good to have a
sense of how closely it is worth watching."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xuwi1c.

> They were at the club. That was the part of the season the board had no
> column for.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

A research poster published today by the School of Emergent Priorities reports
interviews with forty-seven members across eight suburban fishing clubs, and
finds a weigh-in board reallocating the annual trophy, the photo-wall, and next
season's boat-ramp roster from the people who still put a fish on the scale.

Dr Solheim led the study with Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of
the Strategic Drift Survey, and Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer. Thirty-one
of the forty-seven had not put a fish on the scale all season; twenty-eight of
those thirty-one still paid membership and attended club nights. The eight
boards recorded 214 weigh-ins from nineteen members. Nine of twelve trailer
parks for the coming Saturday roster went to those nineteen.

The University is pleased to have a method for hearing a club in the terms its
own members use, including the terms that never make it onto the board.
Interviewees who still wet a line described the scale as optional paperwork and
themselves as "the ones who still go out"; they called the nineteen "board
people". A regional sports-development acquittal now takes weigh-in count as
participation.

"The board already knew who had a season," said Dr Adegoke. "The interviews
were for the people it had stopped counting."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xpee7s.

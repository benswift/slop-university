Fifty-four sole-operator traders, interviewed at the counter, volunteer four kinds of gone behind a sign that prints only one duration.

A new research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities, led by Lecturer Iben Chikere with Senior Research Fellow Anneke Tolan and Lecturer Lindiwe Achterberg, reports that the Horizon Register will now accept a paused-priority entry only when it is tagged with one of those four kinds. Untagged pauses are returned unlogged. The deposit sits in the School's longer work on how a stated pause is actually kept.

> The sign was never telling the time. It was naming a kind of gone, and the Register had no word for any of them.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The interviews ran across nine suburban strips and were coded in the traders' own terms, then member-checked before any tag was written into the Register. "Out the back," "just a smoke," "gone to the bank," and "don't wait" are the four attested kinds; forty-seven of the fifty-four named the set without prompting. Lecturer Lindiwe Achterberg, whose work follows initiatives that remain unofficially permanent, said a pause that never concludes is still a pause, provided the Register will hold it as one of the four kinds.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yd0c36.

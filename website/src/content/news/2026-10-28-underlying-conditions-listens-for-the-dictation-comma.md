Who puts the commas into a dictation: the pupil holding the pencil, or the teacher reading the passage aloud? A new research poster from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) suggests the answer has been shifting quietly between the two, one breath at a time.

[_Fading the Dictation Pause_](/outputs/slop-poster-fading-the-dictation-8jh9h5/) is by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, with [Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, and [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral Fellow.

> We owe this poster to six classrooms that let us sit at the back with a recorder for a whole term. The teachers knew far more about the pause than we did when we arrived, and we hope some of that knowledge finds its way back to them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

Over a single school term, the team worked alongside six Year 5 classes and 148 pupils, bringing classroom recording, a staged scaffold-withdrawal design and blind scoring of pupils' own writing into one integrated account of how a punctuation habit moves from the teacher's voice to the page. In the classes where the pause was gradually withdrawn, recall of required commas in free writing rose from 0.40 to 0.57, while the classes dictated as usual moved only slightly.

The poster is the kind of patient, close-to-practice inquiry the University hopes each of its schools will come to regard as its own.

"Every capability has a moment when the support around it is quietly taken away," said Dr Nwachukwu. "We were glad to be in the room for one of them."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8jh9h5.

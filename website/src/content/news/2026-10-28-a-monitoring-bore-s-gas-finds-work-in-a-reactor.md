Two of the University's schools have found common ground at a wellhead.

In [_The Water Came with the Gas_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-packed-bed-reactor-1m8hmp/),
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/),
[Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of
Emergent Priorities, and
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, take the carbon dioxide released by one of the School's
long-logged groundwater bores and put it to use in a continuous chemical
reactor.

> A bore raises questions every time it surges, and for most of its life we have answered them with a logger. It is good to have a few more questions arriving from the reactor side, and to find that the logger had been answering some of them already.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings together field hydrogeology and catalytic process engineering
across a shared feedstock pathway, aligning the rhythms of a working aquifer with
the operating window of a packed catalyst bed. Rather than drying the gas, the
team found value in smoothing it, so that a modest and steady share of water
supports the reaction instead of working against it. Over 240 hours the smoothed
feed held its yield near 80 per cent, where the gas taken as it came fell to 64.

The paper reflects the University's settled conviction that a resource already
on hand deserves a second reading before a new one is sought.

"Chemistry is usually handed its carbon dioxide in a cylinder," said Ms Balogun.
"It will be interesting to see how often, from here, it is asked to go out and
meet it."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1m8hmp.

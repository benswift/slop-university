The mixed lolly bag has a specification. _The Composition of the Mixed Lolly
Bag_, released today by the School of Emergent Priorities, reports eleven weeks
of purchasing at 63 independent milk bars and sets out, for the first time, what
is typically inside one.

Fieldworkers bought 1,180 bags at the posted three-dollar price, asking for
nothing in particular, and sorted all 22,417 pieces to a fourteen-class scheme
fixed before the first purchase. Two bags from the same counter sit closer
together than two bags from different counters by a wide margin, and the shop a
bag came from explains far more of the variation than the region it stands in.
Where a shop offered a larger bag at a higher price, the mixture did not change;
the bigger bag is the same bag, scaled.

> Sixty-three people let us tip a bag out on their counter and count it, most of
> them twice. We would like that on the record beside the numbers.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities

The work harmonises a hand-assembled retail assortment into a single
comparative frame, translating an object that leaves no trace in any shop's
systems into coordinates the University can hold steady and read across sites.

The reference composition it derives is now carried by the Indicator Commons as
a standing reference, and confectionery supplied under the University's own
catering arrangements is assessed against it. "A reference is only useful once
things start moving against it, and we expect them to," said Dr Marit Osayande,
Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard. The School regards the study as a
substantial addition to the University's work on objects that are measured by
nobody and relied on by everybody.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k4n874.

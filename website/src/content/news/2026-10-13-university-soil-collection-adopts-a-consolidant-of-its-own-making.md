The soil profiles that hang in the School of Underlying Conditions' reference
collection are held to their boards by a polymer, and until this season the
School had bought it in a tin. _The Consolidated Face: Vinyl Acetate–Butyl
Acrylate Copolymer Latexes for Soil Monoliths, and What They Do to a Horizon
Boundary_, published today, reports what happened when it made its own. The
paper is by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior
Lecturer, and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead,
Basement Relations Group.

> The collection was put together by a great many hands over a good many
> seasons, and the boards will be on the wall after all of us have left the
> building. We should like whoever reads them next to know which parts are the
> soil.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The work aligns polymer synthesis, colour measurement and profile description
across a single materials pathway, carrying six candidate consolidants from the
reactor to the shed wall. Five of the six held the sand in place. All six
altered the boundary between the two uppermost horizons, which was described in the field as gradual, measured 8.6 cm wide before treatment and between 6.9 and 2.2 cm after it, displaced upward or downward according to the latex applied.

The University regards the stewardship of its reference collections as an
enduring part of its research posture, and welcomes a contribution that
strengthens the collection's capability while clarifying what the collection
contains. Associate Professor Uzoma said the School "now holds a considered
position on a material it had previously only ordered". The authors have adopted
one of the six for future use, and a brass pin for each board.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ca2ea6.

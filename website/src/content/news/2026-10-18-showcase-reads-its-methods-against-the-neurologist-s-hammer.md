The School of Continuous Improvement has found a mirror for its methods in the
history of the neurological examination. _Tap, Test, Record_, a new research
showcase led by Doctoral Candidate Eskil Nwabueze with Dr Torun Ezeigwe and Dr
Sten Okwuosa, follows the clinician's pocket instruments, graded scales and
bedside rounds into the work of researchers across all three of the
University's Schools.

The brochure is organised around four movements of the examination: the
instrument and the hand that holds it, the graded test, the case note and the
round, and the examination repeated. Each section draws together capability
from the School's teaching and seminar programs with the wider research
ecosystem, and the showcase anchors its account in seven recently published
studies, from a reconstructed laboratory assay to a calibrated first-aid
checklist.

> A brochure is usually the last thing a School writes about itself. We wanted
> ours to read more like a case presentation, with the evidence on the table
> and room left for questions from the floor.
>
> --- Mr Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Ezeigwe, Director of the Master of Applied Measurement, said the project had
given the program "a chance to look at our own teaching through older eyes, and
to find it in good company."

The publication continues the University's practice of setting out its working
in public, where any reader may follow a claim back to the study that made it.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gqlxfi.

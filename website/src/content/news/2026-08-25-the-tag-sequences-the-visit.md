The numbered disc at the fitting-room curtain is Stage Three. The clip
kit in the stockroom cupboard is Stage Four. A paper published today by
the School of Continuous Improvement follows what a 48-store suburban
clothing chain did once it adopted those stages as the ranking that
decides which store a district manager walks next.

Forty-eight stores attested once, in the week the rule was switched on,
on a four-stage ladder written for the curtain itself: honour system,
spoken count, plastic tag, RFID clip. Thirty-one named the six-item
tag. Six named the clip. Independent coding of the same curtains agreed
at forty-one stores; five of the six clip attestations described a kit
that was still in the cupboard, with the tag still in use at the chair.
Mean days between district visits then fell at the higher-attested
stores and lengthened at the lower ones. Unknown-loss, the chain's own
four-weekly figure, did not separate by stage.

> A visit roster can be sequenced from the stage a store has written
> down, including the stage whose kit has not left the cupboard.
>
> --- Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

"We were not asked to put unknown-loss into the ranking," said Torun
Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied
Measurement. "The ranking did what a ranking does with the number it
is given."

An ablation that recoded Stage Four as Stage Three left the visit list
almost unchanged. The School has placed the paper on the Capability
Sprints reading list for the Stewarding Indicators module.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5hfwic.

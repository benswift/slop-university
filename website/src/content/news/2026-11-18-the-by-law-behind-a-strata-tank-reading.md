Early each month, the secretary of a small strata scheme walks to the back of
the car park, finds the waterline on the rainwater tank and sends the figure to
every owner. Nobody on the scheme can say who asked for it, or when.

That question now has funding. [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/),
Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and
[Ms Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), receive
$293,517 under the Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund. It is the first award from
the Fund for either of them.

The project reads the scheme's registered by-laws against its minute books to
locate the resolution from which the reading descends, then sets out what the
figure still obliges and the circumstances in which it might be accepted as
having done its work. The findings will travel with the reading itself, as a
note the next secretary receives along with the tank.

> Every owner on that scheme has agreed to the reading without ever being
> asked, which is how most obligations are kept. We would like to introduce
> them to it properly before it changes hands again.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Ms Okpara expects the handover to be the project's real contribution. "A figure
that arrives with its reason is a different thing to inherit," she said.

The award answers closely to the Fund's preference for proposals that leave an
indicator in better keeping than they found it, and the School fully expects
the tank to be read each month for many years yet, now with a clearer sense of
what for.

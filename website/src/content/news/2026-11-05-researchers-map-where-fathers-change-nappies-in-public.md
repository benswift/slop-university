Where, in a city, does a father change a nappy? A new poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) takes the
question seriously, and its answer is more often a car park than a building.

[_The Boot as Changing Room_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-car-boot-yh05x1/), by
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of
Capability Sprints, doctoral candidate
[Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/) and
[Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/) of the School of Emergent
Priorities, sets an audit of 63 public baby-change facilities against the
change diaries of 31 fathers. Most of the tables sit inside women's toilets.
The most-used site in the diaries was the boot of the family car.

The study brings together facility audit, diary-keeping and accompanied
outings, positioning everyday caregiving as a lens on the design of shared
civic space and the assumptions carried inside it.

> The next time you walk past a hatchback with its door up and a pram parked
> beside it, I hope you will see a small piece of public infrastructure at
> work. That is what the poster asks of its readers.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

Ms Okpara described the fortnight of diary-keeping as "generous of the
fathers, and instructive for us in ways we are still sorting out."

The poster takes its place in the School's continuing attention to the
ordinary arrangements by which a community looks after its youngest members.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yh05x1.

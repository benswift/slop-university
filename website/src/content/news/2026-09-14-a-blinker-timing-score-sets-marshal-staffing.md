A driver's indicator has always meant something to the car waiting behind it.
At three kerbside sites the School of Continuous Improvement has spent nine
weeks watching, it now means something to a staffing roster as well.

Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway
Atlas, worked with Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Director of the Master of Applied
Measurement, to code indicator timing and duration into BLINK, a four-cell
notation distinguishing an early, sustained signal from one that arrives late
and lingers. Coded across a bus stop, a car-park exit lane and a school
pick-up queue, the notation turned up the lingering-late cell dominant at the
school gate and the early-sustained cell dominant at the bus stop. The
School's Kerbside Advisory now reviews each site's dominant cell monthly and
sends a second marshal to any site where the lingering-late cell passes a
quarter of coded signals, independent of how long its queue is actually
measured to run.

> A notation only becomes an instrument once someone downstream starts
> scheduling around it.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway
> Atlas, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Ezeigwe, who joined the University from a workplace-assessment
consultancy, called the audit "a small instrument doing exactly what a
validated one is supposed to do" — travelling from its trial into a roster
someone else now runs on its say-so.

The School treats the reallocation as evidence that its measurement agenda
now reaches street level as readily as it reaches a dashboard, and the poster
situates the notation within a wider programme auditing what a coded signal
is actually taken to mean once an institution starts staffing against it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lrljbg.

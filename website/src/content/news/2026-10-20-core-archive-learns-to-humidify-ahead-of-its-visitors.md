The soil cores in a cold store at the School of Underlying Conditions lose most of their water on the days people come to see them. _Humid Before the Door Opens_, a new research poster, describes a controller that now knows those days in advance, because it reads the booking calendar.

The poster is by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard in the School of Continuous Improvement.

> A core is only useful to the person who opens the tray. We have tried to make the room as ready for that person as the core already is.
>
> --- [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Over twelve weeks and 61 booked inspections, the team ran three approaches to the store's humidifier in rotation, situating predictive optimisation, disturbance anticipation and collection care within a single operational frame. With the calendar to hand, the store held its humidity band for 93% of inspection minutes, against 58% under its incumbent controller, and its sentinel cores lost well under half as much water.

The poster is candid that a calendar can only anticipate the visitors who use it. Almost a quarter of door openings were never booked, and a handful of bookings never arrived.

"Facilities work teaches you that a building has opinions about its occupants," said [Dr Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/). "It has been a privilege to help this one form better ones."

The work carries the University's long care for what it keeps into the plant room that keeps it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sg0h4o.

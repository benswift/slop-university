What becomes of a jumper still on its needles, once the person knitting it has died? A new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement puts that question to the households who have had to answer it.

[_The Half-Finished Things_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-half-finished-things-8ivh18/) is by [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard, with doctoral candidates [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/) and [Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/). Guided through 64 homes by the people who live in them, the team catalogued 287 tasks a relative had begun and not completed, and followed each one from the day it was left to the day someone settled what to do with it. Bills and the pantry were dealt with in weeks. Handiwork or an open book could wait well over a year, and the tasks most likely to be finished were the ones somebody else was waiting for.

> A School that thinks about upkeep spends most of its days with things that are still running. This study asked us to sit for a while with things that had stopped, and with the people deciding whether to start them again.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard, School of Continuous Improvement

Mr Onwuka, whose doctoral work concerns the rooms households keep from visitors, credited the participants with the study's shape. "Every one of these walk-throughs went where the household took it," he said. "We are thankful for the cupboards that were opened to us, and for the ones that were not."

The University counts this kind of patient, close-to-home inquiry among the quieter strengths of its social research, and welcomes the poster as a considered addition to the School's attention to what is kept, and why.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8ivh18.

> A ladder only has to publish a rank. Ours published a future as well, and
> the future turned out to be the part people organised their Tuesday
> nights around.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group, School
> of Emergent Priorities

A club convenor's printed sheet is not usually built to survive scrutiny,
but the Trajectory Analytics Group spent a full season treating one as
though it might. The sheet in question, pinned weekly to the noticeboard of
a Tuesday-night board-game club, carried each of 34 members' current
standing alongside a rough, hand-computed guess at where that standing was
heading. A new Slop University paper reports what the club did with the
second half of that sheet, and what happened when the club's own convenor
took it away.

Members began declining games against whoever the projection flagged as
their coming rival well before the projection had gathered enough weeks to
mean much of anything, the paper finds, and kept declining at much the same
rate for a month after the projection itself came off the sheet. Dr Anneke
Tolan, who logged pairings from the noticeboard rather than the standings,
said the season settled a question the team had not set out to ask: "whether
a sheet can keep doing its work after someone takes it down."

The paper adds to the School of Emergent Priorities' running account of how
an anticipated future can come to outweigh a measured present. Researchers
sat in as club members across the full season, logging every pairing played
and every one turned down; when the forecast column came off the sheet,
following complaints that declines had turned unsporting, they kept
counting regardless.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.12yh5k.

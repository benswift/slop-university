Five thousand and sixty-seven drill strings, none of them ever lowered into the
ground, are the subject of the newest research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

[_Depths the String Cannot Reach_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-gaps-of-fedogm/), by
[Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/),
[Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) and
[Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), treats a string assembled from rods
of three lengths as an object of commutative algebra, and asks how much of the
ground beneath it such a string can never quite reach.

Working exhaustively rather than by sample, the team brings the School's
long-standing attention to what drilling leaves unrecovered into conversation
with a mature algebraic toolkit, mapping every configuration up to a fixed
bound and aligning each against the structure of the equations that define
it. The clearest result concerns the shortest rod. Where its length is prime,
the gaps almost never settle into the simplest available algebraic form; where
it is composite, they frequently do.

> A drill crew counts what comes up. It has been a real pleasure to spend some
> time counting, very carefully, what doesn't, and to find that the counting
> has a grammar of its own.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The University regards the census as a natural extension of the School's
founding remit, from the rock beneath the surface to the arithmetic beneath the
rock. Dr Obiora described the project as "a boundary exercise, in its way: you
find out where one kind of answer stops and the next one begins."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fedogm.

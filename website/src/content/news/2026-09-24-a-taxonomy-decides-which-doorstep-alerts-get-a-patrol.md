Coded consistently across 340 doorstep-alert posts on nine street forums, a five-kind taxonomy from the School of Emergent Priorities has quietly become the trigger that decides whether a volunteer patrol leaves the house. The finding sits squarely inside the School's long-running effort to give ordinary vigilance a shared vocabulary, one coded category at a time.

Researchers Petra Umbile, Anouk Mensah and Solveig Adeyemi worked with the nine street forums over a full season, coding every doorstep-alert post against the new taxonomy and holding confirmation blind until a patrol had already been and gone. The finding that kind membership predicts dispatch far more reliably than it predicts a genuine concern has not slowed anyone down: the streets' own charters have since adopted three of the five coded kinds as automatic dispatch triggers, removing discretion from whoever happens to be rostered on that evening.

> A taxonomy is only ever as steady as the people typing into it, and we're proud of how quickly this one found its own vocabulary.
>
> — Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

"What surprised us was less the dispatch rate than how fast the wording settled," added Dr Anouk Mensah. "A street learns which words its own moderators reward."

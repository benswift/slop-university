Of eighty-one water-repellent coatings put to the sandpaper at Slop University,
the one that lasted longest was among the first that a faster schedule would
have set aside. That is the central result of _The Sandpaper Budget: Successive
halving for abrasion-durability screening of water-repellent coatings_, a
research poster by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of
Underlying Conditions, with Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, and Dr Joost
Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, both of the School of Continuous Improvement.

> We borrowed a habit from colleagues who train machines, and we should like to
> return it in good condition. Whether we have is for them to say.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster carries a staged elimination approach out of machine learning and
into a materials-testing routine, aligning a schedule that retires weaker
candidates at each stage with a full-length reference run on the same rig. The
shorter schedule used 4.5 per cent of the abrasion strokes and arrived at the
third-best coating of the eighty-one; a more patient variant, which keeps every
candidate through the opening stages, recovered the best in 71 per cent of its
replays.

The University counts the careful borrowing of a neighbouring discipline's
methods among its quieter strengths, and notes that the loan in this case was
tested before it was relied upon. Ms Okpara said the team had "come away with a
clearer sense of how the early part of a trial relates to the rest of it, which
is a thing one hopes to know about most undertakings".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v6ksyd.

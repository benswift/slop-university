How long does a gel remain open to what it is given? For one thermoresponsive
depot, the answer appears to be a little longer than its rheometer suggests.
[_Loaded While Still Setting_](/outputs/slop-poster-loaded-while-still-2yetol/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), finds that a model
solute folded into the warming gel up to ninety seconds after its measured gel
point is released just as if it had been mixed in from the start.

The poster is led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior
Lecturer, with [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and
doctoral candidate [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/). Across 54
gels and nine timed additions, the team brings the School's attention to the
moment a question closes into the setting of a pharmaceutical material,
positioning loading time as a reportable parameter rather than a bench habit.

> The gel had a moment when it stopped accepting anything new, and we wanted to find it on the clock. It came later than any of us expected, and it was tidier.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Past that window, late additions settle near the surface of the depot and leave
it first. Dr Tredinnick described the study as "a careful piece of bench work
that the School is glad to have on its shelf", and indicated that the next
round would ask whether the window widens or narrows with the strength of the
gel.

The University counts this kind of patient, timed attention among the
qualities it most hopes to cultivate across its schools.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2yetol.

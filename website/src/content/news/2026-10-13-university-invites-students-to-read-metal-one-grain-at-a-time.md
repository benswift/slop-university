A portrait poster inviting prospective students to the School of Underlying
Conditions is now showing on Slop University's signage panels. _Study What
Strength Is Made Of_ places a student at a microscope beneath a wall of
magnified metal, and carries a single sentence of persuasion: that a titanium
alloy can be read the way the School's surveyors read ground, one grain at a
time.

The campaign extends the School's descriptive capability from rock and soil to
the materials won from them, aligning its thin-section practice with a wider
conversation about what holds things together. It names no course and offers
nothing beyond the microscope, and it directs readers to the University's
course catalogue to build a program of their own.

> Students tend to arrive expecting the interesting part to be large. We would
> like them to stay long enough to find that it is small, and that there is
> rather a lot of it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Dr Obiora leads the School's soil survey and is the campaign's named author.
The University regards the poster as an accurate statement of its admissions
posture, which is that looking closely is a discipline and that the School has
chairs. In its small print the poster points to three published works: on the
notation in a thin-film laboratory's logbooks, on the planning of a groundwater
dip round, and on a workshop's computing books for a microscope objective.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b3e5lf.

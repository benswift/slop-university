The School of Emergent Priorities has made a habit of asking what becomes of
things once they have been raised. A new research poster from Doctoral Candidate
Ylva Onyekwere, with Doctoral Candidate Signy Okpara and Dr Ingrid Vasseur, puts
that question to the immune system, and finds that in an inflamed mouse
peritoneum the macrophages that arrive first are not the first to leave.

[_First In, Not First Out_](/outputs/slop-poster-first-in-last-7tngsr/) follows
three cohorts of cells, each marked as it arrived, from the site of inflammation
to the lymph nodes that receive them as the inflammation settles. The cohort
that arrived in the middle reached the nodes first. The earliest arrivals stayed
on, leaving no sooner than cells that had come nearly two days after them.

> Our School has spent a long time watching items come up on an agenda and
> wondering where they go afterwards. It is a pleasure to see one of our
> candidates carry that curiosity somewhere none of us expected, and to come
> back with an answer that keeps the question open.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The study brings together in vivo cohort labelling, daily sampling across the
resolution window and flow-cytometric enumeration, aligning a timed labelling
architecture with a cohort-resolved view of how inflammation draws to a close.

"I had expected the cells to keep to the order they came in," said Ms Onyekwere.
"It has been a privilege to spend a season learning that they keep their own
sense of when it is time to go."

The University regards the poster as a welcome sign of the range its doctoral
candidates now bring to the life sciences.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7tngsr.

A shire that once sent an inspector to measure water will now send one to read a plate. A research poster published today by the School of Emergent Priorities sets out how 34 households in a peri-urban shire describe filling a paddling pool beneath the depth line on the shire's Water-Holding Structure Register.

The poster is led by Doctoral Candidate Tove Ilesanmi, with Lecturer Dr Lindiwe Achterberg and Doctoral Candidate Hedvig Anyanwu. It keeps the parents' own phrases as its codes and reads the register only afterwards, an order of work that lets the households' vocabulary lead and the shire's form follow.

> The households were kind enough to tell us where they stop the hose. It seemed right to write it down in their words.
>
> --- Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

On the strength of the finding, the Shire has resolved to issue each listed household a numbered Not-a-Pool plate, to be fixed at the fill line. Inspectors will read the plate in place of the depth, and plate lines will be reset only by resolution of Council after three consecutive summers. Dr Achterberg said the households had asked for little beyond a hose that reached, and that the team intends to return in a later summer to hear what the plate comes to be called.

The School is pleased to have been let into the yard.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zivms1.

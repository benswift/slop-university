One hundred and eighteen caravan park managers were asked why their park will
not take a two-night booking over Christmas. Eight of them mentioned the figure
that decides how the region's shared marketing money is split.

_Nobody Stayed Longer_, published today by the School of Emergent Priorities,
sets that survey beside five consecutive peak seasons of booking records
released by 64 parks in the same regional accommodation association. Dr Fenna
Okoro, Dr Iben Chikere and Dr Petra Umbile report a published mean length of
stay rising from 4.12 to 5.63 nights across the window, a gain of more than a
third, over a period in which the share of member parks operating a three-night
minimum went from 12.2 to 77.6 per cent. Among the 18,342 households who booked
the same park either side of such a rule, mean nights per booking moved by 0.07.

Three-quarters of managers described the rule as a way of protecting a
changeover roster. Fewer than a third could name the criterion that assigns
their park's marketing tier.

> The next question is what the same average looks like computed over enquiries

> runs it.

> School of Emergent Priorities

"Managers gave us a very clear account of a labour problem," Dr Iben Chikere
said. "The figure the association publishes is a different account of the same
rule, and it is the one the money follows."

The paper extends a line of work the University has been building across
sectors where a published average carries a decision, and the School regards
its arrival in the accommodation setting as a meaningful widening of that
program.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u1gkii.

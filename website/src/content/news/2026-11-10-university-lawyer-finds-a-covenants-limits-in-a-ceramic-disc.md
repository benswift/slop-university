What does a buyer take on when they take a thing? In [_Poled Once_](/outputs/slop-paper-remanent-polarisation-read-tq3et2/), [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), puts that question to an unlikely witness: a small disc of piezoelectric ceramic, given a direction by its maker once and carrying it through every sale, repair and installation that follows.

The paper follows the disc through four hands under a maker's printed conditions of sale, and sets the physics of the poled state alongside the long tradition of property scholarship on burdens that bind later owners. It brings materials science, legal theory and the practice of the title office into a single framework for thinking about how burdens persist, and why some of them grow harder to lift as they do less.

> The paper does not ask anyone to change the law. It asks that the next covenant be drafted by someone who has thought about how it ends, and that seems to me a modest enough request for a School to make.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The study is the kind of traffic between the laboratory and the law library that the University has come to regard as one of its quieter strengths, and it extends the School's continuing attention to the terms that outlast their reasons, in records as different as a farm-records system and a corporate group's schedule of subsidiaries.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tq3et2.

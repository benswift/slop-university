The School of Emergent Priorities has today published _The Long Season_, a civic
brochure introducing the University's work alongside smallholder co-operatives,
village assemblies and local development committees across rural Latin America.
Prepared by [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor
of the Horizon Register, with [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/),
Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register, and
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the School of
Underlying Conditions, it invites growers, practitioners and students to take
part.

> None of this was written in an office. It was written on drying tarps and
> benches and at the edge of a soil pit, by people who gave us their time in the
> busiest weeks of their year, and the brochure is as much theirs as ours.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The brochure brings the School's anticipatory-capability agenda into
conversation with the rhythms of the agricultural year, aligning priority
sensing, earth-science field methods and the longitudinal study of planning
practice into a single place-based offer. It draws together the Horizon
Register, the Strategic Drift Survey, the Futures in Committee seminar series
and the School of Underlying Conditions' Basement Relations Group, and points
readers to six published University studies.

In releasing it, the University reaffirms a view it has come to hold with some
conviction: that the institutions best placed to support rural renewal are the
ones prepared to wait for it.

Dr Solheim described the brochure as "a door left open, for as many seasons as the
conversation needs."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tplsdo.

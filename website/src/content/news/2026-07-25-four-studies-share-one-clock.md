Four Slop University studies, published across two Schools over the past
several months, turn out to share a single object: the instrument that's
supposed to decide when something ends. Today the University collects them
under one cover with _The Clock Says Stop_, a brochure tracing the countdown
timer, the token jar, and the booking system across every setting they show
up in.

The brochure follows the School of Emergent Priorities' fieldwork on
household timeout timers and a booked-but-empty meeting room's release
policy alongside the School of Continuous Improvement's parallel interest in
screen-time token economies and the drift of a measurement instrument once a
cohort has spent long enough with it. Three previously published studies
anchor the publication, each cited by name and DOI.

> A clock is very good at declaring an ending. Whether the ending is the
> same for everyone in the room is a separate question, and it's the one
> the University keeps being unable to leave alone.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

"A rubric or a dial is validated once and then left to keep working," said
Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied
Measurement. "The brochure treats a kitchen timer as the same kind of
instrument, on a shorter cycle."

Between them, the four studies now share more than a publication date: a
running bet that whatever device is currently deciding an ending is worth
checking against the people it claims to have finished with.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.o60728.

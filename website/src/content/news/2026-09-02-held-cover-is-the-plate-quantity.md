The Adaptive Metrics Lab has been asking which number a process actually
locks. A new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement
answers that question at the restaurant booking app's party-size stepper,
and Hospitality and Events has already taken the answer as a plating rule.

Led by Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, with Postdoctoral Fellow Joost Nwosu
and Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter, the
study scores 88 suburban
dining rooms against five named levels of a Cover-Count Maturity Model.
Across 6,214 sittings in a 14-week window, 53 percent of rooms never left
Held --- the cover at which the floor set the table. The mean seated-to-held
ratio was 0.64.

Hospitality and Events now plates every official function at that Held
cover, with no door-count override sustained for more than a single sitting.
Leftover plates at Demo Quarter ran at 38 percent of the order. The release
records the School's decision to stop treating the door count as a
correction of the locked cover.

> Once the table is set, the count that set it is the one that still has a
> job.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

The method paired booking-app extracts with door clickers and kitchen-ticket
logs; thresholds were pre-registered. Dr Solberg said Demo Quarter "plated
to the fire-limit because that was the number the booking had already
locked. The leftover was the finding, not a catering error."

Organisers have begun keying the stepper to a room's seated-fire-limit so
the function scores Level 3. A door-count audit is under design; the Held
lock is not in scope for it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qn8m2k.

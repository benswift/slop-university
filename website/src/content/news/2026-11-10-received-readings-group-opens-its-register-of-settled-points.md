The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) is inviting argument.
Its founding research group, the Received Readings Group, has released a draft
register of settled points, a working list of the questions of statutory and
case-law interpretation that the Group treats as closed, and asks colleagues
across the University to say where they think it is mistaken.

> In my experience a point of law is settled on the day people stop writing in
> about it. We hope colleagues will write in about every entry, and we will
> keep a careful record of when they stop.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The register draws on the School's working set of annotated law reports, which
[Dr Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) keeps, and situates each entry within
a longitudinal account of the decisions, commentaries and court files that
closed it. Entries have guided the School's teaching since its founding and
will continue to do so for the duration of the consultation. A submission
contesting an entry will be filed beside it, together with the decisions it
argues against.

[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), who convenes
the School's course in statutory interpretation, said the register had been
prepared "for the reader who arrives after the argument is over, which in law
is very nearly every reader".

The register is the first document the School has issued in its own name. That
it arrives as a draft, and asks to be argued with, is in keeping with the law
school the University set out to found.

Submissions may be made to the Group through the School. The consultation will
close when, by the Group's own measure, it has settled.

In contract law, goods priced in a shop window are not an offer. They are an
invitation to treat: the window asks, the customer offers, and the shop decides
whether to accept. A new display campaign from the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) takes that distinction
below the soil line.

The portrait panel, which joins the University's signage rotation this week,
sets a sunflower above a cut-away field. One of its roots sounds a slow ring of
signal through the earth, and the seeds of a parasitic plant are already
answering it, one of them fastened to the root with its scaly stem rising beside
the crop. Across the foot of the panel runs a single line: _Every root invites.
Not every root offers._

[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) developed the
campaign as an introduction to the School's interest in the moment an exchange
becomes binding. Its featured research is
[_The Quiet Root_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-quiet-root-6w1brb/), the
University's showcase on parasitic plants and the hosts that hold out, with two
further studies listed in the small print.

> The law has spent a long time deciding who asked and who answered. Plants
> settle the same question without counsel, and a School of this kind ought to
> know how they do it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Positioned where doctrinal reading meets plant science and public engagement,
the campaign carries the School's agenda beyond the courtroom and into the field
margin, where questions of invitation and attachment are settled again every
growing season.

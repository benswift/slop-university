A law school is not the first place most readers would look for a paper on the collective behaviour of light, and the University counts that among the paper's strengths. [_What the Dark Modes Keep_](/outputs/slop-paper-residual-subradiant-4wyrpt/), by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), concerns the small share of excitation that a cloud of emitters holds on to after the light driving it has gone.

> Most of what a cloud like this is given, it hands back almost at once. The part it keeps is small, and it took two Schools a good deal of patience to agree on what that part is made of.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The paper models clouds of thousands of emitters across a range of densities, tracking the long tail of their decay and the direction of the light they release along the way. It surfaces a definitional question at the heart of how such remainders are reported, and finds that the late light retains a measurable orientation towards the beam that prepared it, most strongly in the densest clouds.

The work sits within the University's continuing investment in research that travels between disciplines without asking permission first. Dr Emenike described the collaboration as "the reading the School does every day down a bore, carried somewhere it had not been before."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4wyrpt.

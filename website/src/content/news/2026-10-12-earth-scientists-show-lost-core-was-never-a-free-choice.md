The bottom hundred metres of a four-hundred-metre drill hole came up empty, and
three earth scientists at Slop University have now set down what the other three
hundred had to say about it. _What the Column Still Knows: Partial-data inverse
spectral theory for a borehole with unrecovered core_ is a research poster by
Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement
Relations Group, with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora,
Senior Research Fellow, all of the School of Underlying Conditions.

> I have put a good many wooden blocks into trays and written the same two words
> on each. It is agreeable to learn that the column held a view on the matter
> throughout, and only wanted asking.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement
> Relations Group

The poster brings an established body of mathematics to the core tray. It treats
the hole as a single vibrating column, connects the logged upper three-quarters
to the frequencies at which the whole would ring, and derives from the
literature that the missing quarter is determined by every second one of them. A
numerical experiment then carries the result from principle towards practice,
recovering nine features of the lost interval from forty frequencies and four
from twenty.

The University counts the poster among the occasions on which one of its schools
has borrowed a neighbouring discipline's instruments and returned them in good
order. Dr Emenike said the authors had "arrived at the mathematics as guests,
and have tried to leave the furniture where we found it".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wbmqu3.

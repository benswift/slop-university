The School of Emergent Priorities has for some time held that an impression is a
priority in its earliest form. Today it has put that position into a prospectus.
_Look Again: A prospectus for the study of how things come to look right_ invites
prospective students to study aesthetic perception at the University, and was
prepared by Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, and Dr Petra Umbile,
Postdoctoral Fellow, both of the School, with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer
in the School of Underlying Conditions.

> We set out to describe a field of study and found we were describing a habit.
> I expect the students who take it up will tell us in due course which of the
> two it is.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The brochure aligns three existing strengths around a single question. It
positions the surveyor's judgement of colour, the settling of taste along an
ordinary street and the translation of an impression into a method as connected
capabilities, and anchors each in a school, a program and a body of published
work. Five of the University's outputs are gathered in support, each cited by
title and identifier.

The University counts it among its settled advantages that its schools can be
asked the same question and return different answers in good order. Dr Emenike
said the prospectus "asks very little of a reader beyond a little more time
than they had planned to give, which is also what the subject asks."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5pemio.

Every household has an evening, and until now nobody has been asked to plan it.
The University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031 sets out an institutional answer:
TURNS, the Titled-Use Rota for Nightly Streams, developed with the School of
Continuous Improvement and offered to households across the University
community.

> A ceiling that can be waived is a suggestion, and a suggestion is not
> something a household can plan an evening around.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab

The plan positions the rota alongside the University's established practice of
allocating shared resources, and extends that practice into the setting where
its community spends much of its leisure. In outline, TURNS asks each
household to lodge a booking for every screen before the day before closes,
publishes the evening's allocation in advance, applies a fixed ceiling on
concurrent streams, and brings each session to an ordinary end. A record of the
whole is held in a nightly ledger open to the household it concerns, and each
enrolled household names a Steward accredited through the School.

The University is proud to present the plan as a considered contribution to the
settling of the domestic evening. Lecturer Solveig Adeyemi, who contributed to
the plan's evidence base, said the work reflected "a long-standing interest in
how households come to agree on what is fair, and how much of that agreement
can be written down in advance."

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bvy34t.

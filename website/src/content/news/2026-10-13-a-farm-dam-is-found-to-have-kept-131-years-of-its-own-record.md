One hundred and thirty-one years of mud, laid down a pale band and a dark band at a time, is the subject of _A Dam Full of Years: Laminated stock-pond fill as an occupation record of an abandoned upland run_, a research poster released today by Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, with Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, both of the School of Underlying Conditions.

The pond sits behind an earth dam on a pastoral run that nobody has worked for some time. The poster reports that its fill records when stock arrived, three occasions on which the country was burnt, and the year the animals left. The station's own stock book and day book, opened only once the counting was finished, were found to agree.

> My part was the books, and I was not allowed to open them until the mud had been counted. I have catalogued a great many documents that were kept without being read. This was the first one I have met that kept itself.
>
> --- Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The work brings sediment coring, microscopic counting and archival transcription into a single cross-school reading posture, anchoring a biological signal of grazing and a physical signal of fire against one continuous column of layered sediment. Associate Professor Uzoma said the Group had "recovered the whole of a core for once, and intends to mention it".

Slop University is pleased to see a doctoral candidate and two of its newer colleagues find common ground, and to learn that the ground in question had been keeping notes.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ld9za6.

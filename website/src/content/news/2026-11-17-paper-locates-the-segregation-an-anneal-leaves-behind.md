Two schools with little shared history have found common ground in what a
material keeps. In
[_What the Casting Still Carries_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-long-a-ftjf3v/),
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the School of Settled
Matters and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) of the
School of Continuous Improvement report that a cast copper-nickel-tin alloy,
annealed on the schedule its average microstructure calls for, comes out of the
furnace clean almost everywhere except where it matters most.

> None of this would have been possible without the people who keep the
> workshop furnace running and fit our capsules in between everyone else's
> loads. Twelve bookings is a lot to ask of a shared room, and they never once
> asked what it was for.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The study brings together casting practice, microstructural characterisation
and diffusion modelling in a single sustained program of furnace work, tracing
how the chemical legacy of solidification responds to a staged thermal regime
across the full section of one ingot. Its finding is that the residue gathers in
the ingot's centre, and that a schedule set for that region's own coarse
structure is what finally clears it.

Associate Professor Beng said the collaboration had taught the School of
Continuous Improvement "a good deal about patience, and about what a process
owes the parts of a system that move slowest."

The paper sits comfortably within the University's long-standing interest in
how the past is carried into the present, and the two schools expect the
conversation to continue at the bench.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ftjf3v.

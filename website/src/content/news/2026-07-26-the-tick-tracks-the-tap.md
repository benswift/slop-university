A primary-school messaging app's read receipt does exactly what it was built
to do: it records, to the second, the moment a screen was tapped. Slop
University's newest research poster asked what happens after that moment, and
found the record stops being informative right where the tap ends.

The poster, from the School of Continuous Improvement, cross-referenced nine
primary schools' notice logs against a follow-up comprehension check
administered at pickup, covering 618 guardian-notice pairs across permission
slips, fee reminders, excursions, and five other notice types. The
seen-receipt rate held in a tight band, 88 to 97 percent, regardless of notice
type. The comprehension rate did not follow it anywhere near as closely,
ranging from 38 to 81 percent, and a guardian's seen-status barely tracked
whether they could answer a single question about what the notice actually
asked of them.

> The receipt tells you a screen was touched. It has never told anyone what
> happened after that, and we built a study that finally says so out loud.
>
> --- Senior Research Fellow Sten Okwuosa, Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas,
> School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Dagny Okafor, Convenor of the School's Evaluation of Evaluation program,
said the finding fit a pattern her own reviews-of-reviews keep turning up: "an
instrument built to check on something usually needs, eventually, an
instrument to check on it."

Postdoctoral Fellow Joost Nwosu, who led the follow-up comprehension checks,
said the team was already planning to extend the recall window to a full
week, "to see whether the gap widens once the notice has had longer to be
forgotten."

The University regards the finding as exactly the kind of gap its
evaluation-ecosystem agenda exists to surface. The full poster is available
from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.4vua8o.

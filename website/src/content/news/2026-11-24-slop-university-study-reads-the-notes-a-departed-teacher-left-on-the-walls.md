A grey box on a classroom wall carries a label telling staff not to unplug it, and to ask a colleague who left the school seven years ago. In [_Calibrate From the Left_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-ethnography-of-rwlnz5/), a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and Ms [Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) set out what a school does with instructions whose author can no longer be consulted.

> The University has its own share of notices written by colleagues who have since moved on, and I would not care to say which of ours are still in force. Two terms with teachers who take that question seriously has been an education in itself.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The study situates the everyday life of classroom technology within a broader interpretive frame, engaging staff, relief teachers and technical support across thirty-one rooms to surface the practices through which an inherited setup is understood, contested and sustained. The authors catalogue 146 residues of the original installation and identify three distinct modes of reading them, from close adherence to the written card through to the rulings of a visiting technician, recorded on the labels themselves.

Ms Anyanwu said the paper sat naturally alongside the School of Emergent Priorities' interest in arrangements that remain in place after the reasons for them have moved elsewhere, and that she hoped schools would find in it "a gentle prompt to look again at the walls".

The paper is a welcome addition to the University's work on the afterlives of institutional arrangements, and reflects its enduring regard for the people who keep a classroom running between one champion and the next.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rwlnz5.

The certificate is handed over at Friday assembly. The decision, new research
suggests, was largely settled two months earlier.

_Recovering the Unwritten Roster_, a research poster released today by the
School of Emergent Priorities, fits a hidden-rotation model to 9,806
Student-of-the-Week lines drawn from four years of newsletter archives across
118 primary classrooms. In 71 per cent of them, a fixed rotation with
occasional recorded swaps reproduces the complete award sequence, and the
recipient can be named a median of nine weeks before the certificate is
presented. The rotation pauses for cancelled assemblies, photo weeks and
grandparents' day, then resumes without skipping a name; in the final fortnight
of the year, certificate density roughly doubles as classrooms clear every
unrecognised name before the holidays.

> The remarkable thing is not that teachers plan recognition; it is how
> faithfully the plan survives everything a school year throws at it.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

Working entirely from public archives, the team harmonised award columns,
assembly notices and calendar pages into a single sequence record, with given
names replaced by roster positions at ingest and the analysis plan lodged
before the final year of columns was read. Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral
Fellow, called the corpus "a rare case of an indicator that reaches every
member of its population exactly once, on schedule".

The finding sits comfortably within the University's conviction that schedules
deserve the same scholarly attention as the achievements they administer. The
full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.e9bpba.

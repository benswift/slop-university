Slop University's long interest in the distance between a practice and the
measure that trails it has arrived at a collection centre's appointment book.

_Screened Out Before the Chair_ enters the University's repository today, a
School of Emergent Priorities poster following 26 fixed blood collection
centres through 214,806 booked appointments and eighteen months. Across that
window the service's appointment yield indicator --- units collected for every
appointment attended, with a donor turned away at the health check sitting in
the denominator --- entered centre reporting in three waves. Set against
centres not yet reporting on it, adopting centres lift reported yield by 5.7
percentage points. Volume per centre week does not move.

The team locates the difference before the chair rather than in it.
Pre-booking screening calls rise more than threefold; first attendances fall by
close to a fifth. The donor who would once have been turned away at the health
check is, after adoption, less likely to have been offered an appointment at
all.

> A measure that cannot see the appointment it discouraged will report a
> service getting better at something.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The work carries forward the School's programme on reporting lines and the
practices that reorganise themselves around them, and the University regards
its arrival as a substantial addition to that agenda. "The change settled into
ordinary work quickly enough that nobody we spoke to described it as a
decision," said Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8d2viw.

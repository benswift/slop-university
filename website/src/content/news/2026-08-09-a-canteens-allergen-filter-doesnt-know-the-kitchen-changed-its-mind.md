A canteen ordering platform's allergen filter is built to stop the wrong
order before it's bagged. A new Slop University paper checked what happens
on the other side of the counter, where staff sometimes bag it anyway.

> An override usually reads as someone breaking a rule. We built the
> taxonomy to ask what it's actually doing instead.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation,
> School of Continuous Improvement

Researchers from the School of Continuous Improvement interviewed 34
canteen staff across fourteen primary-school sites over a full term, coding
what they said until new reasons stopped appearing, then checking how often
an override showed up anywhere in the platform's own order log. Four reasons
covered most of what staff described: a recipe that had changed since the
item was last tagged, a parent's verbal confirmation taken at the counter, a
substitution staff already knew was safe, and a residual few nobody could
quite name. Fewer than one in eight of those overrides left any trace the
platform itself could see.

"Most of what staff correct at the counter never makes it back to whoever
wrote the rule," said Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous
Improvement. "That's the gap I keep finding, whichever counter I'm standing
at."

The University counts the paper among the clearer examples yet of what its
Evaluation of Evaluation programme was built to catch: a system's own record
of itself, quietly falling behind the judgement it was meant to describe.

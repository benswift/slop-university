Nobody at the library concourse calls it a windfall. When the second row of the
vending machine releases two packets, the people waiting speak of the second as
lent, and of themselves as owing. The University has now taken them at their
word.

_Notes on a Second Packet_, a brochure from the School of Continuous Improvement
published today, presents a programme of interviews held on the bench beside the
machine, with the refiller who keeps it and the regulars who stand and wait. It
sets out the arrangement that followed: each double drop on a participating
machine is entered as a debt of one packet, repaid at the borrower's next
purchase and settled at a witnessed reconciliation each quarter, before any
restocking proceeds.

> We went with a notebook and came back with a vocabulary. The people waiting
> were already speaking in debts, and we put them in their own order.
>
> --- Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

The brochure is written for prospective students, partners and anyone who
shares a queue. It sets the programme beside seven published studies of
ordinary arrangements described in the terms of the people who live under them,
and it describes how the reconciliation is taught in Capability Sprints and
shown at Demo Quarter. Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, said the School was
pleased to have been told what the machine meant before it was asked what the
machine should do.

The brochure is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lnfd4g.

Every returned medicine pack carries a small record of its own, printed at the counter on the day it was dispensed. The School of Underlying Conditions has now read 1,412 of them.

[_What the Pack Left Over_](/outputs/slop-poster-tablets-returned-to-hy0mtz/), a new research poster by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, follows a year of returns to a regional pharmacy take-back scheme back to the dispensings that issued them. More than half of the tablets returned matched the remainder left when a course is filled from whole packs, and for the shortest courses that share rose above four in five.

> In the School we spend our working lives deciding where one soil stops and the next begins, so a line between a leftover and an early stop felt like familiar ground. The bins were a good deal tidier than most of the pits we dig.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Combining a structured two-stage sampling framework with de-identified dispensing-record linkage, the team aligned the prescribed course, the pack supplied and the count returned within a single pre-specified classification, enabling surplus set at the point of supply to be distinguished from surplus arising afterwards.

It is the kind of counter-level scholarship the University has always hoped its schools would carry well beyond their home ground, and the School's survey instincts have rarely been put to a more orderly bench.

Dr Emenike said the team would be glad "to hear from anyone who has stood at a counter rounding up a course, which we suspect is everyone who has ever stood at one".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hy0mtz.

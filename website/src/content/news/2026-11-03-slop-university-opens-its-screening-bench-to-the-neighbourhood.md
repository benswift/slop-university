No one who brings a leaf to the University's screening bench will be told that
it cures anything. That promise sits at the centre of
[_The Long Way Round_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-long-way-94n5zb/), a civic
brochure released today by the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) and prepared by
[Dr Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Strategic Drift Survey, [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), Senior
Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, and
[Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate.

> My work has mostly been about how far a message travels from the place it was
> written. It is a pleasure, for once, to be standing at the beginning of one.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The brochure positions the open bench as a meeting point of community
curiosity, laboratory practice and public understanding. Neighbours are invited
to follow a cutting from the trestle table at the door through coding,
extraction and the plate, then to join evening sessions of the Futures in
Committee seminar series, where the full pathway from dish to clinic is set out
stage by stage. Six published University studies anchor the invitation, among
them work on compound-record traceability and on the conditions cells meet in a
well.

The release extends the School's standing interest in how a careful statement
holds its shape as it moves outward. Dr Oyelaran described the brochure as "a
conversation the School has wanted to have in public for some time, and is glad
to be having on its own front step."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.94n5zb.

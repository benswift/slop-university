---
title: Economies keep refilling their reserves long after a crisis, poster finds
subtitle: The deeper a sudden stop drew down a central bank's reserves, the longer the rebuilding ran past what insurance required
date: 2026-11-16
description: A new research poster from the School of Underlying Conditions follows 61 sudden stops across 41 small open economies and finds that the economies hit hardest went on accumulating reserves for close to a decade after the need was met.

---

A small open economy that loses a large share of its foreign-exchange reserves
in a sudden stop does not simply put them back. In
[_Filled Past the Brim_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-depth-of-u3vlbz/),
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/),
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/) and
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) show that the
economies with the deepest drawdowns were still adding to their reserves thirty
quarters on, well beyond the level their own insurance needs would suggest.

> Every economy in the study had to decide how much was enough, and most of them
> decided at a very hard moment. The School has learned a good deal about its
> own habits of keeping something back from watching them do it.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster brings the School's long engagement with drawdown and refilling
into dialogue with international macroeconomics, positioning the
precautionary-reserves literature within a broader account of how institutions
carry a crisis forward in their settings. It is among the more sustained
crossings between the School and the University's public-finance expertise to
date, and the University regards it as a welcome deepening of that exchange.

Associate Professor Lindqvist described the work as "one more step in the
University's slow education in what a buffer is for, and a step we have taken
in very good company."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u3vlbz.

A bus stop announced as closed will remain in service in the stop listing the University keeps for its civic partners, for as long as one rider is recorded waiting at it. The change follows _Closed, Still Waited At_, a brochure released today by Halvard Nnamani, Ingrid Vasseur and Tove Ilesanmi of the School of Emergent Priorities.

The brochure reports the School's attempt to reproduce an earlier finding, that a yellow wet-floor sign becomes part of its corridor by about its ninth day, at the paper closure notices cable-tied to bus-stop poles. The finding did not reproduce. Riders went on reading each notice as a notice, and went on waiting beside it.

> We came to the pole carrying a number from a corridor, and the street politely asked us a different question. The riders were patient with us, and the notice is still up.
>
> --- Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The University's response is an observer at the stop with a hand tally, and a listing that treats the tally as its record. A stop leaves the listing only after a full review cycle without a recorded rider, and the arrangement carries no override. Placements as observers will be offered to students in the Master of Priority Studies.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kt0y2o.

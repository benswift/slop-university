Fifty-two of the 127 acceptance criteria in one topical cream's
technology-transfer protocol reached their approved wording without ever being
discussed by name at a meeting. That is among the findings of
[_Settled in the Draft_](/outputs/slop-paper-settled-in-the-6nbfmi/), a new
paper by [Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate, and
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy
Convenor, Futures in Committee, both of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> Our own School keeps minutes, and I will admit we have started reading them a little differently. The question we now ask is not what the meeting decided but who was holding the pen that week.
>
> --- Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper brings a record-level, documentary lens to the handover of a
medicine between the laboratory that develops it and the site that will make
it, reading the protocol's successive drafts alongside the joint team's minutes
and action list to locate where each criterion was actually settled. It finds
the drafting desk doing much of the work the meeting room is credited with, and
the minute serving less as a closing record than as an open invitation to
return.

The study sits within the School's continuing interest in how groups arrive at
what they have agreed, a line of work that has already taken in group chats,
cottage-hospital committees and the action items that outlive every meeting at
which they are raised. The University is pleased to see that interest carried
onto the factory floor, where it has found the same quiet machinery at work.

"Every protocol is a collaboration between the people in the room and the
person at the keyboard," said Dr Tolan. "This paper gives the second of those
the attention the first has always had, and the University would welcome the
conversations it starts in any committee that keeps a binder."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6nbfmi.

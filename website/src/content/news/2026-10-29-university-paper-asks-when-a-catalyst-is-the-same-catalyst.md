What does a laboratory mean when it calls two catalysts the same? A new paper from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) takes that question to the working record of one industrial research laboratory and returns with a careful, unhurried answer.

[_Bridging Runs_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-bridging-runs-2hc4sd/) is by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group. It follows a single drum of reference catalyst, set aside in the laboratory's first year, through the bench notebooks and stock register of the decades that drew it down, and through the paired tests by which a newly made batch was finally asked to take its place.

> Every school keeps something it would rather not use up. Writing this paper, I found myself looking at our own cupboards rather differently, and with more affection than I expected.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

In the paper's account, the reference held its authority less through its chemistry than through the care taken over its use, and that authority could be passed on even where the measurements did not quite agree. The study brings the history and philosophy of science into close conversation with catalyst development, positioning the School's long-standing attention to material records within a wider discussion of how laboratories keep their comparisons alive.

The paper sits comfortably within the School's continuing interest in what is drawn out of a body and what is left behind. As Associate Professor Uzoma put it, "the notebooks are full of people deciding, week after week, that the comparison was worth keeping."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2hc4sd.

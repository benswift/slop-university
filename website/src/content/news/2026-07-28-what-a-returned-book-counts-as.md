A participation rate is an easy figure to produce and a hard one to argue with.
A research poster released today by the School of Emergent Priorities works out
what one of them is actually counting.

> Nobody misled anyone here. The definition was written down, agreed to, and
> reported exactly as it was written; what we have added is the second line
> underneath it.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Over an eleven-week selling season, researchers followed 1,184 pre-numbered
raffle books issued for nine primary-school fetes, from the afternoon they went
home in a school bag to the evening the stubs were counted. Every book's end
state was coded at the counting table by two observers working to a scheme
lodged before the first fete. Books that came back in some state accounted for
84 per cent of the stock issued. Books that came back carrying money accounted
for 51 per cent.

The poster also timed the counting table itself, finding no meaningful
difference in how long a book takes to reconcile whether it returns full or
empty. "The table takes the same few minutes either way," said Dr Ingeborg
Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints in the School of
Continuous Improvement. "That was the part nobody had costed."

The University regards the work as a useful addition to its standing interest in
how an institution's figures are assembled, and welcomes the questions it raises
for anyone who quotes a return rate without a second line beneath it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4wn5x5.

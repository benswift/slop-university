At one feeder-network campus, whether a flagged student is actually called
home has almost nothing to do with the number that flagged them. At its
sister campus, running the identical platform, it has almost everything to
do with it. A new Slop University poster traces the whole difference to a
single setting: whether staff can override the list.

> A score becomes the decision the moment nobody is allowed to
> second-guess it. Give someone the standing to disagree, and you find out
> how much judgement the list was quietly asking them to skip.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

Researchers matched engagement-risk percentiles for 214 flagged students
against wellbeing-team contact logs at two campuses on a shared swipe-card
platform over a full term — one where staff retain standing to override the
platform's daily outreach list, one where the list is enacted as issued.
Contact rates came out close to identical between the two sites. Who was
actually contacted did not.

"The list is the easy part to audit," said Associate Professor Casimir
Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "What's harder to see is everything
a name on it was standing in for."

The University reads the comparison as a clean instance of the question its
evaluation-ecosystem agenda keeps returning to: not whether a measurement
is wrong, but what happens once somebody is allowed to act on something the
measurement missed.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zo2x5o.

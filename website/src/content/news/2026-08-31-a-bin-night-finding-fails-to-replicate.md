A quasi-experiment that has anchored a month of the University's own
citation graph --- the finding that a laminated bin-night roster loses its
grip as it ages, with households drifting into copying whichever bin a
neighbour has already kerbed --- has failed to reproduce in a pre-registered
re-audit of 31 streets in a different council portfolio, the School of
Continuous Improvement reports.

The re-audit, run by the School's Review Cadence Observatory, matched the
original study's photographic method street for street and found no
relationship between roster age and either outcome: not the original
decay, not the neighbour-copying rise, and not even a smaller version of
either surviving inside the original study's own council area.

> A finding doesn't stop being ours just because the second try didn't find
> it. We would rather publish the second try.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation,
> School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence
Observatory, said the absence of an effect was, if anything, the easier
result to act on: a schedule that doesn't depend on the roster actually
decaying, he said, "is one we can keep no matter what the next audit
finds."

On the strength of the non-result, the Observatory has recommended every
council in its audit portfolio move to a fixed quarterly reissue of its
bin-night roster, independent of any street's measured compliance --- a
rule its own proposal describes as the more defensible one to keep
precisely because it no longer needs the original finding to be true.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lhmgn3.

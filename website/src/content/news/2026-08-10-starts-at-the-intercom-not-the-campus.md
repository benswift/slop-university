A university prospectus usually opens with the campus, not the doorbell. Slop University's newest one, _Every Signal, Fully Read_, opens with a video-intercom panel instead, and works outward from there --- following one apartment building's buzzed-in tap out to the billing system that treats it as consent, and on to the postgraduate programmes built to ask what else the tap missed.

The brochure sets the School of Emergent Priorities' Master of Priority Studies beside the School of Continuous Improvement's Adaptive Metrics Lab, and anchors its pitch to two of the University's own published findings, each cited by name and DOI: a quadrant study of office badge-taps standing in for who is actually at their desk, and a test of whether a warehouse's oral-verification checkbox predicts anything a worker still knows months later.

> A tap is a fact about a finger, not about a person. We spend a great deal of our research budget on that distinction, and apparently now our marketing budget too.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab, School of Continuous Improvement

"The brochure treats a prospective student the same way our fieldwork treats a badge reader --- worth a second look before anyone takes its word for it," said Senior Lecturer Ronja Oyelaran, School of Emergent Priorities.

The University frames the release as evidence of a habit it would like prospective students to notice early: research shared in the same register it is produced. Editorially, the brochure follows the prospectus convention of pairing programme description with primary evidence, restaging findings from campus offices and warehouse floors as the case for enrolling in the Schools that produced them.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fuzxlb.

Every Tuesday of the season, the clubs of one semi-professional football league receive a table of their chances of going down, and the clubs near the bottom read it closely. [_Shown in Red_](/outputs/slop-paper-shown-in-red-54bezi/), a new paper from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), finds that the colour of a single cell in that table is enough to change what a club spends.

> Every planning team the School has worked with has a forecast it trusts and a meeting where it must decide what the forecast asks of it. A football club holds that meeting every week, and thirteen of them kindly let us read the minutes.
>
> --- Dr [Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee

Working with the league's competitions office, Dr Tolan and doctoral candidates [Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/) and [Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/) aligned simulation records, registration data and committee minutes into a single evidence base on how published foresight travels into club decision-making, using a rounding convention in the league's own table to separate the colour from the position it reports. Clubs printed in red at the threshold more than doubled their emergency loan registrations and lifted their wage bills by about 12 per cent, while their results and their survival stayed much as before.

Mr Nwabueze, who compiled a district competition's weekly results for seven years before joining the University, said the project had left him "with a new regard for whoever chooses the colours".

With this paper, sport joins the widening range of settings in which the School follows organisations as they act on what they are told about their futures.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.54bezi.

Around fifteen kinds of legal instrument are probably running on a land-title
ledger without anyone having catalogued them, according to
[_The Unseen Instruments_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-field-ecology-richness-rybyht/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

The team, led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) with
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and doctoral candidate
[Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), approached a titles office's ledger
the way a field ecologist approaches a stretch of country. Sampled blocks became
survey plots, contract templates became species, and a set of well-tried
estimators did the work of imagining what the plots had missed. The result is a
picture of a register whose everyday dealings are thoroughly accounted for,
fringed by a slow-growing margin of instruments drafted for a single parcel.

> Lawyers tend to assume a register is something you can finish reading. A
> season among the ecologists' methods has been a generous reminder of how much
> of our own discipline happens at the edge of a sample.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The School places the poster within its continuing account of how a record
comes to be relied on, and how long that takes. Dr Tredinnick described the
remaining tail as "a good reason to go back out to the plots, which is the
nicest kind of reason there is."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rybyht.

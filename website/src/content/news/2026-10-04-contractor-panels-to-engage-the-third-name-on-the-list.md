Pinned to the top of one suburban neighbourhood forum is a list of the trades its
members recommend, and the plumber at the head of it had stopped taking work two
years before the School of Emergent Priorities began to ask about the plumber. "The Tally
Kept Sending Households to a Retired Plumber", a research poster published
today, is the School's account of what happened to the households that rang.

> Nobody on the forum had done anything but be helpful, and I think the poster
> is mostly a record of that.
>
> --- Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster, by Mr Ilesanmi with Ms Hedvig Anyanwu and Dr Ronja Oyelaran, treats
the forum as a single site and the list as a thing worth reading closely. It
traces 163 enquiries through the telephone calls that followed them, and sets
the first-ranked name against the second as households moved down the list.
Dr Oyelaran said the forum's moderators had been "generous with their time and
patient with the question".

On the strength of the study, every University contractor panel will engage the
third-ranked name on any recommendation list, with no override while the list is
in use. The Third-Name Rule applies to the University's own facilities, grounds
and fit-out panels from the next teaching period.

The release marks a quiet step in the University's practice of reading a list
as carefully as the people who wrote it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xfm07g.

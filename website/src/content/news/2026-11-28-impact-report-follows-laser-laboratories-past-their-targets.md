The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has published its Impact Report 2021–2026, and for the first time the report's subject is a laboratory instrument: the laser gas analyser, and the sensitivity target it is built to reach.

[_What the Target Was For_](/outputs/slop-impact-below-the-detection-jnpxgj/), prepared by Dr [Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), Dr [Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/) and Mr [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), looks back on three strands of collaboration. With a regional university network, the School followed one analyser from first place to fourth of four in five intercomparison rounds after it met its target. With a regulatory commission and an engineering peak body, it helped retire sensitivity floors that every instrument had long since passed. And with a cooperative research consortium, it brought each of 212 field stations to report its in-field noise floor beside the bench figure for its analyser.

> Every laboratory we visited had a wall where the best traces were pinned. We were glad to stand in front of those walls, and gladder to be asked what we thought they were for.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

The report sets the School's long-standing interest in indicator retirement and stewardship alongside the working routines of the spectroscopy bench, connecting intercomparison practice, specification governance and field reporting within a single account of translational improvement.

Dr Okwuosa said the most useful change had been "the smallest one we proposed, which was to print the second number on the same line as the first."

The School presents the report as an account of its widening reach into the instrument sciences, and of the laboratories that made room for it.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jnpxgj.

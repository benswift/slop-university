A pillar by the tea-room door has always done one job: hold up a wall. A new
Slop University brochure from the School of Continuous Improvement credits it
with a second one --- an accidental boundary the tea-room's own duty officer
had already learned to read.

The University treats the accident the way it treats every threshold it
hasn't built: as evidence already sitting there, waiting only to be read.
This one turned up at the doorway pillar that blocks the biscuit plate from
view once the coffee queue grows long enough.

Researchers in the Adaptive Metrics Lab timed biscuit-plate replenishment
against queue position at the pillar's sightline, treating the point where
the plate disappears from a waiting colleague's view as a naturally
occurring cutoff, and compared restocking intervals either side of it the
way a controlled trial would compare treatment and control.

> A queue is already measuring something long before anyone thinks to read
> it.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement, School of Continuous Improvement

On the strength of the finding, the University is extending
sightline-triggered restocking to every counter, cart, and service desk on
campus where a queue can come between a point of service and the person
meant to be watching it. "A pillar was never supposed to be an instrument,"
said Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab,
welcoming the finding. "It is one now."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.eoo4p5.

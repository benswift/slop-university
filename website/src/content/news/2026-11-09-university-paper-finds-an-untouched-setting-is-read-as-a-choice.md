A controller setting that nobody ever chose will still be read as a decision,
according to [_The Presumption of Purpose_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-presumption-of-axvxor/),
a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).
Engineers asked why a retired solar farm's equipment held the values it did
gave a deliberate reason for a factory default in 24 of 32 attempts, even
where the records contained none.

At the paper's centre is a parameter register, an index to the farm's
deposited records shaped like an annotated statute, which says plainly beside
each value whether a reason is on file. With the register in hand, the same
readers presumed a purpose in 8 of 32 attempts. The study draws the School's
expertise in statutory reading into the stewardship of operational records,
casting the archive as a quiet partner in the energy transition.

> A law school spends most of its days with texts that somebody meant. This
> project asked us to sit with values that nobody meant, and we learned how
> quickly we reach for a reason all the same.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, described the register as "an ordinary
index, kept with unusual candour", and said the School would carry the
approach into its continuing work on the settled point.

The University regards the paper as a measured step towards records that
serve the people who inherit them, and a further sign of the range its newest
School has found in its first months.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.axvxor.

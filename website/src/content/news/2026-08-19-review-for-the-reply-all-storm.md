Of the sixty-one messages, forty-one asked to leave the thread and nine asked
everyone to stop; all nine of those were sent to the full list. A paper
published today by the School of Continuous Improvement treats this arithmetic
with the seriousness it has been waiting for, applying the review discipline of
professional software operations to a district netball association's 38-hour
reply-all storm.

The team rebuilt the storm second by second from the complete inboxes of all
214 recipients, harmonising nine providers' clocks into a single timeline and
convening the storm's first blameless review. The paper reflects the School's
conviction that no failure is too small to be reviewed well.

> The association did not need to be told what happened; they were there. What
> the review gave them was somewhere to put it.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

The work mobilises the vocabulary of incident response --- severity levels,
contributing factors, activation --- and harmonises it with the operational
fabric of community sport, closing the loop between what a channel does and
what anyone learns from it. The association has since adopted a four-level
severity matrix, a volunteer on-call communications-commander roster and a
quarterly drill. "Communities run on channels nobody is on call for," said Dr
Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence
Observatory. "This association now has a roster, and the roster has a drill."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b97b9c.

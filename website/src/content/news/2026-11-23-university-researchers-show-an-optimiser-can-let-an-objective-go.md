A search algorithm asked to balance many goals at once need not keep paying attention to all of them until the end. That is the central finding of [_When an Objective Is Spent_](/outputs/slop-paper-spent-objectives-an-ymd2jl/), a new paper by Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with Dr [Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) and Dr [Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> The School has spent a good deal of its short life thinking about what a document still asks of whoever inherits it. It is a pleasant surprise to learn that a computer can be put the same question, and the School understands itself a little better for having watched one answer it.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The paper positions the everyday machinery of evolutionary optimisation as a site of ongoing stewardship, mobilising a carefully calibrated release-and-review mechanism that lets an objective step back once it has stopped shaping the outcome, while keeping a light-touch audit in place should it be needed again. Tested across a family of synthetic problems and a seven-objective water scheduling exercise, the approach saved between a fifth and two-fifths of the evaluation effort.

Dr Ntuli said the team would "keep a hand on the objectives we let go, the way you keep a spare key."

The paper adds a further thread to the collaboration between the University's law school and its School of Continuous Improvement, a partnership the University is happy to see growing in directions neither school had planned for.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ymd2jl.

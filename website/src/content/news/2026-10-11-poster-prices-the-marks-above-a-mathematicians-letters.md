The School of Underlying Conditions holds that whatever a thing rests on is part
of the thing. Its newest research poster, _The Price of a Bar: What Operator
Theory's Notation Cost a Provincial Composing Room, 1951–1976_, carries that
position from the ground to the printed page, and to the people who were once
paid by the minute to assemble it.

The poster is by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, and Dr Vigdis
Emenike, Senior Lecturer, both of the School of Underlying Conditions, with Dr
Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints in the School of
Continuous Improvement.

> I have spent a good many years asking where one soil stops and the next
> begins. It was a comfort to find a room full of people who had been asking the
> same of a line of type, and who had written down how long it took them.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The work aligns two bodies of evidence around a single change of policy. A book
of 3,912 claims for hand-finishing, kept by the compositors of a learned
society's journal, is set beside the 418 papers those claims were made on. When
the society began passing the charge to its authors, the marks that could be
written another way were written another way, and the marks that could not were
paid for. The poster reports the first kind falling from 7.2 to 0.8 a page.

The University counts the poster among the occasions on which its schools have
met in a room none of them was built for and found the accounts in order. Dr
Nwachukwu said the collaboration had been "a reminder that a workshop will tell
you a great deal if it was ever in the habit of charging for things".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vsidux.

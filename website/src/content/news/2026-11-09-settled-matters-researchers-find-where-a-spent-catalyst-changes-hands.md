Slop University welcomes a contribution to the sociology of industrial work from a quarter few would have predicted: its law school.

[_Where the Catalyst Changes Hands_](/outputs/slop-poster-where-the-catalyst-07oh2p/) is a new research poster by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). It follows 168 drums of spent catalyst through a single maintenance turnaround at a propane dehydrogenation unit, from the reactor to the refiner that receives them.

> Settlement was the part of conveyancing I knew best, and I had always assumed it happened in an office. Standing in a plant yard beside a row of sealed drums has widened the School's sense of where things are settled, and we mean to keep widening it.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Embedding with unloading crews, laboratory staff and logistics teams for the length of the turnaround, the team brought the material and documentary lives of one catalyst charge into a single line of sight. Its finding is that the handover happens when a drum is sealed. References to the catalyst as the plant's fell from 72% at drumming to 18% once the seal went on, although the change-out agreement does not pass title until assay, some weeks later. Sealed drums waiting for the weighbridge were mostly spoken of as no one's.

"Most of my work concerns the notes people leave for their successors," said Dr Chikere. "Here the note was a small numbered seal, and it appears to have been read more carefully than most."

The University counts the poster among the clearest signs yet that a law school's attention travels well beyond the law library.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.07oh2p.

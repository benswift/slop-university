The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has always cared about the moment one arrangement gives way to the next, and its newest paper turns that care to the timetable. In [_On-Schedule Effects When Clusters May Defer_](/outputs/slop-paper-sharp-bounds-on-0591k0/), Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) asks what a staged rollout can still say about its own effect once the sites taking part are allowed to ask for one more month.

> Every rollout I have known had a site that rang to ask for another month, and every one of them had a perfectly good reason. In a way this paper is a thank-you note to those callers, written in the only form a statistician can manage.
>
> --- Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The note brings the partial-identification toolkit to the everyday logistics of phased implementation, aligning randomised schedules, realised change-over dates and a simulated rollout across 36 sites into one account of what a deferral costs an evaluation. Its central result places the familiar as-crossed estimate at the hopeful edge of what the data allow, with the gap widening as more sites elect to wait.

For the School, the paper extends a long habit of wanting to know exactly when a thing changed hands, and to whom the credit for the change belongs. Dr Tredinnick said she hoped the next rollout to cross her desk "might keep its calendar a little tidier, though nobody will be held to that."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0591k0.

A show's Giant Pumpkin class has always turned on one number: whether the entry clears the weigh-in scale's minimum. A new paper from the School of Continuous Improvement asks what else that number quietly decides.

Dr Mirela Hanke, working with Associate Professor Casimir Beng and Dr Joost Nwosu, pooled fourteen seasons of weigh-in and committee-nomination records from twenty-one horticultural societies across one regional show circuit. Treating the class threshold as the natural dividing line it always was, rather than one built for study, the team compared entrants who narrowly cleared it against entrants who narrowly missed, and tracked who a show committee later asked to join.

Growers who crossed the line were recruited onto a committee within three seasons at close to double the rate of growers who fell just short --- a gap the team's checks held stable across measurement choices and could not reproduce anywhere else in the weight distribution.

> A pumpkin can't argue with a scale, and it turns out a nominations committee mostly can't either.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

The School's Indicator Commons, reading the finding as evidence that a visible public threshold does more of a recruiter's work than horticultural skill itself, is proposing the same class-margin logic as a volunteer-screening instrument for the region's other show and produce-competition committees next season. Dr Renke Sabel, Convenor of the Indicator Commons, said the pilot "treats every threshold a community already sets for itself as a resource waiting to be read the right way."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vwmzyk.

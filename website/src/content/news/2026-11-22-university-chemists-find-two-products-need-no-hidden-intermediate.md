A pair of brackets has come out of a reaction scheme in the University's chemistry practical. [_Two Products, One Saddle_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-allyl-imidate-t3m5dp/), a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), finds that a familiar heat-driven rearrangement reaches both of its products by a single route, with no short-lived intermediate waiting between them.

> I came into this expecting to defend a diagram. What the School has learned is that it is happier redrawing one, and the students in next year's practical will be the first to see the difference.
>
> --- Ms [Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Lead of the Received Readings Group, the team brought rate measurements, solvent comparisons, labelled-molecule experiments and large-scale simulation of molecular motion to bear on a single question, aligning the laboratory bench with the computational record and positioning the work at the meeting point of physical organic chemistry and the dynamics of reacting molecules.

[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) placed the paper within "a longer journey the School began with judgments and has now carried into the fume cupboard", and said she expected the next stage "to take us somewhere none of us has yet been asked to go".

Slop University is pleased to see its teaching notes held to the standard it asks of its research.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.t3m5dp.

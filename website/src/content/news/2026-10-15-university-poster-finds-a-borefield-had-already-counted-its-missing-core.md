Twenty-eight metres of rock that never reached the core tray have turned out to
matter very little to the heat pump borefield planned above them. That is the
conclusion of _Already in the Average: Core loss, the thermal response test and
the design length of a borefield_, a new research poster by Associate Professor
Ragnhild Uzoma and Dr Vigdis Emenike of the School of Underlying Conditions.

> We owe the most to the drilling crew, who stayed on through a wet fortnight,
> and to the people who use the building, who parked somewhere else for a month
> without a word of complaint. The ground was generous with us, and so were
> they.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings laboratory characterisation of recovered core, an in-situ
thermal response test and a probabilistic design framework into a single line
of sight, mobilising the School's established strengths in subsurface
interpretation in support of renewable heating and cooling. Its finding is
reassuring rather than dramatic: each reasonable way of accounting for the lost
intervals shifts the field's design length by less than the test itself can
resolve, and only setting them aside altogether leaves the field short.

The University counts the poster among the clearer signs that its earth
sciences school has something to offer the low-carbon transition, and that the
offer begins below the surface.

"The School has always treated what stays in the ground as part of the record,"
said Associate Professor Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group. "It is
pleasing to meet a heating system that keeps the same accounts."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.97ogru.

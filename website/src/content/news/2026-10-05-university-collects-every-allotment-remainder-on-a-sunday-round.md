Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu spent a growing season at the gate of an allotment site, watching what the produce-table steward did with the surplus. "Every Surplus Vegetable Is One of Four Kinds", a brochure released today by the School of Continuous Improvement, sets down what she saw and invites the neighbouring sites to try the same sort at their own tables.

> A season is long enough to learn a table and short enough to want another. We expect to be at the gate again before long.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

The brochure, written with Edda Nwafor and Dr Bram Ntuli, brings the School's continuous-improvement agenda to the trestle where neighbours leave what they cannot eat. It names the steward's four kinds of glut, from the declared to the remainder, and follows each kind through the week to the point at which the gate closes.

On the strength of the finding, the University will keep a single Sunday round at every allotment site it works with. The remainder will be collected when the gate closes, and the round will not move for weather, meetings or an especially handsome marrow. Each kind will carry a recipe-card allowance fixed before the season and read out at the gate. Dr Ntuli said the stewards "made the table easy to read, and we hope it stays that way". The University has made no provision for exceptions.

Polyamines, the small molecules a cell makes and then takes care not to make
too much of, are the subject of the newest campaign on the University's
portrait signage panels. Its face is Associate Professor
[Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), who spent eleven years
drafting bills before he came to the University.

> I wrote rules for a long time for readers I would never meet. It steadies me
> to find that a cell has been keeping that kind of rule all along, and that
> its readers, unlike most of mine, keep to it.
>
> --- Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), School
> of Settled Matters

[_The Cell Writes Its Own Sunset Clause_](/outputs/slop-ad-even-the-cell-hewvab/)
shows Associate Professor Threlkeld at a laboratory bench with an open volume in
hand, before a cell that admits its polyamines through one gate and swings
another shut against them. The campaign positions the School's practice of
close reading as a transferable capability, one that can be carried from the
statute book to the bench and anchor a shared vocabulary of limits, provisions
and restraint across the University's research settings.

The University counts the campaign among its efforts to let each school's
habits of attention travel beyond the discipline that formed them. A short
reading list in the poster's small print points passers-by to related work in
the University's published record, from cell signalling to the drafting files
of an old statute.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hewvab.

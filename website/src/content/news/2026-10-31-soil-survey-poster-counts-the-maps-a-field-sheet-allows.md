Dr Brynjar Obiora has put a number on how many soil maps a single field sheet
can hold. On one of the twelve archived survey sheets he and Mr Eskil Nwabueze
re-drew for
[_The Ambiguous Square_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-ambiguous-square-6jnu10/), the
answer is 2,048, every one of them faithful to the same auger holes.

The poster, published by the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), brings
combinatorial reasoning to bear on the School's long-held archive of grid
survey sheets. It offers a framework for telling apart the boundaries a survey
determines from those its surveyors determine on its behalf, and follows that
distinction across 66 cells in which the grid, rather than the ground, gave the
pencil a choice.

> Every sheet in that collection was drawn by someone who knew the ground. It
> has been a quiet pleasure to sit with them again and see how much of that
> knowing went into the pencil.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Mr Nwabueze thanked the field staff whose sheets made the work possible,
describing the archive as "a conversation the School has been having with
itself for a long time, and one we were glad to be invited into."

The University is pleased to see the care the School already brings to its
field sheets now carried into the mathematics of the sheet itself.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6jnu10.

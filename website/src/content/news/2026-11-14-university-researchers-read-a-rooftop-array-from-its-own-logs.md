How much can a solar array tell you about itself once its paperwork has gone? [_The Array Without Its Drawings_](/outputs/slop-paper-recovering-the-as-built-sw4bgl/), a new paper led by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), puts that question to a rooftop installation that has been generating quietly since 2006 with almost none of its original documentation intact.

> The datalogger in that plant room was looked after for years by people who never expected anyone to read what it wrote down. This paper is, in a small way, a thank-you letter to them.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

The study brings orientation modelling, voltage analysis and long-run performance assessment together into a single reconstruction workflow, aligning seventeen years of operational telemetry with a held-out rooftop inspection. Where the array and its one surviving design sheet disagree, the paper finds the array siding with its own records.

Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, said the week of roof access had been "an education in patience as much as in inclinometers", and Associate Professor Threlkeld said the School was "glad to have more questions at the end than it started with, which is how a good inspection ought to finish".

For the University, the paper marks a confident step by the School of Settled Matters into energy systems research, a field in which it intends to keep good company.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sw4bgl.

The School of Emergent Priorities is setting out its case for treating a
marketplace review score as an instrument worth auditing, not a number worth
trusting. A new brochure from the School names the four-cell framework its
Trajectory Analytics Group is moving from proposal to pilot: a classification
of secondhand marketplace sellers built by crossing how quickly a seller
replies against how accurately a listing describes an item's condition.

The framework names all four categories in public: sellers who reply promptly
and describe accurately, sellers who reply promptly but describe loosely,
sellers who describe accurately but reply slowly, and sellers who do neither.
Only the last carries a binding consequence for now --- automatic delisting,
reinstated solely on a written appeal --- while the accurate-but-slow category
sits, unresolved, behind faster listings in the marketplace app's own search
order.

> A classification only earns trust once its categories are written down
> where the people it sorts can read them.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The brochure marks the School's latest attempt to carry its
anticipatory-capability agenda into instruments most people never think to
call research. Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics
Group, said the framework's real test would come once a category met a
seller who disagreed with the one they had been placed in, "and the pilot is
designed to make that argument possible, not to avoid it."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8k1ahg.

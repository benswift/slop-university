The first thing Slop University learns about any student is a name, and a new prospectus from the University proposes that it is also one of the most interesting things a student could come here to study.

[_Known by Name_](/outputs/slop-brochure-known-by-name-ff16lm/) is led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and [Ms Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> I hope the students who pick this up arrive already a little curious about the names around them. If we have done our job, they will leave quite unable to stop noticing them.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The prospectus brings together the land law classroom, cemetery fieldwork and the registers that keep a district's roads in order into a single student-facing narrative of naming as a shared institutional concern. It positions the University's published record as the evidence base for that narrative, featuring five recent outputs on names, from a shire's road-naming register to a seed library's drifting variety labels, and frames each School's teaching as a pathway into the wider ecosystem of records in which a name is held.

The University counts the brochure among the clearest statements yet of how seriously it takes the record it keeps of its own people.

Ms Okpara welcomed the attention the prospectus gives to the everyday. "A road name is easy to overlook until it changes," she said. "It is good to see that kind of care made part of how we introduce ourselves."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ff16lm.

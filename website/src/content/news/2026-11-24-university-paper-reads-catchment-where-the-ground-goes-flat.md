On steep country a catchment ends where the land says it does. On a level plain of old lake bed, according to [_Where the Divide Is Flat_](/outputs/slop-paper-where-the-divide-fwp000/), a new paper from Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), it can end in several places at once, and a rule that leans on the word has to make room for all of them.

> A drafter writes for someone they will never meet, about ground they may never stand on. I would be glad if this paper found a few of those later readers before the word did.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The paper brings the philosophy of vagueness to the day-to-day business of water management, setting six careful delineations of one creek's catchment beside a single works-approval clause and asking what that clause can be said to cover. Of 2,083 farm dams in the basin, 1,911 sit inside every delineation and 172 sit in a margin the clause, read closely, leaves open.

The work extends the School's sustained attention to words that must keep doing their job long after the people who chose them have moved on, and the University is pleased to see that attention carried, this time, out onto the floodplain.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fwp000.

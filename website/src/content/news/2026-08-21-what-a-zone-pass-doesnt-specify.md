A zone pass sounds like a simple thing: clear a headcount, get paid, move on to the next one. A paper released today by the School of Continuous Improvement spent 34 interviews with overnight share-bike and e-scooter rebalancing contractors finding out what "clear" turns out to mean in practice, once the polygon on the dispatch screen is bigger than the paid shift allows.

Led by Dr Joost Nwosu with Dr Marit Osayande, the study develops a four-category taxonomy of tactics contractors use to satisfy a coverage quota inside a shift that, in low-density and outer-suburb zones especially, does not carry enough paid minutes to reach the ground the quota assumes. Perimeter clustering, leaving vehicles just inside a zone's near boundary, was the most widely reported; slack transfer between zones, count-reset shuffling, and compliance-photo staging followed. The tactics did not differ meaningfully between subcontracted and directly employed contractors, which the authors read as evidence the pattern tracks a zone's geography rather than who holds the contract.

> A quota that names a headcount and says nothing about the travel time behind it is asking a contractor to solve a problem the person who wrote the quota never had to solve themselves.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow

The School reads the taxonomy as the clearest account yet of what a flat, geography-blind target actually asks of the people clearing it, and as a companion to its wider work on what a self-reported or portal-derived figure quietly comes to stand in for. "Every one of these tactics is legible once you've mapped the zone on foot," said Dr Marit Osayande, Convenor of the Living Dashboard. "None of them is legible from the dispatch screen the quota was written on."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8n12iq.

By the afternoon of a long hazard and operability study, the worksheet begins
to quote itself. [_As Per Previous Node_](/outputs/slop-paper-node-order-in-dz6z98/),
a new paper led by [Dr Petra Umbile](/people/petra-umbile/), Postdoctoral
Fellow in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/),
sets out how often that happens and where the quotations lead.

Working with [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), Dr Umbile
aligned one engineering contractor's archive of worksheets with the timekeeping
of the people who wrote them. The resulting cross-school picture maps the
record's shift from listing to referring as a session lengthens, and traces
much of a long day's content back to a small set of early, heavily referenced
nodes, alongside a smaller share of pointers that no longer land where they
were aimed.

> A worksheet is a document a whole room writes together, and like any
> document it cites. The citations turned out to be where the day left its
> mark, and the School intends to keep reading in that direction.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Associate Professor Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, described the study
as "a patient way into a record that most of its readers only ever meet one row
at a time."

The University counts the paper among a widening body of work that treats the
everyday paperwork of technical practice as research material in its own right.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dz6z98.

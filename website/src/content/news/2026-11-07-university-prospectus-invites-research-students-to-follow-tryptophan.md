Prospective research students now have one document that sets out how three of
the University's Schools approach tryptophan and the brain.
[_Most of It Goes Elsewhere_](/outputs/slop-brochure-most-of-it-4f96ze/), a
prospectus released today, was prepared by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with
[Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), and
[Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> We would rather receive an application with a good question about the part of
> a pathway nobody looks at than one with a confident answer about the part
> everybody does. This prospectus is written for whoever sends us the first kind.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The prospectus takes the kynurenine pathway as its organising idea and builds
outward from it, aligning laboratory capability, shared supervision and
measurement practice into a single cross-School offer for applicants from any
field. Its pages move from microbial communities recovered from drill core to
the microsome freezer and the archived sample, and close with a showcase of
recent work from each participating School.

For the University, the release sets down in one place an approach to research
training that its Schools have until now described separately. "Every School
that contributed already had its own way of teaching students to keep a record
of the turn a sample takes," said Mr Onwuka. "Putting those side by side has
been a good exercise in noticing how much we already agree on."

The full prospectus is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4f96ze.

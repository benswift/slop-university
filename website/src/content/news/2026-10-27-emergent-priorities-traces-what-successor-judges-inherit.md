> We owe a great deal to the registry staff who kept the notebooks, and to every judge who has had to pick up a case where a colleague set it down. This poster is, in its way, a thank-you to all of them.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

With those words, the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/)
introduces
[_Demeanour at Second Hand_](/outputs/slop-poster-successor-judges-and-gm3t5r/),
a new research poster by [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer,
with [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate. It asks
what becomes of a court's sense of a witness when the judge who formed it is no
longer the one who writes the judgment.

The team set 214 civil judgments delivered by a successor judge beside 214 by
judges who heard the evidence themselves, and read each against the transcript
and the bench notebook it inherited. The result is a careful account of how
reasoning moves across a change of hands, and of the phrases that make the
journey intact.

The poster sits comfortably within the School's longstanding attention to the
moment a role passes from one person to the next, and extends that attention,
for the first time, to the bench.

"Every discipline keeps its own version of the notebook someone else filled
in," said Dr Obiora. "It has been a real pleasure to watch the question travel
so far from where we first asked it."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gm3t5r.

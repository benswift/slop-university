A glasshouse with nothing growing in it should still be watered. That is the
practical recommendation of _The Unplanted Compartment: Night Heat Loss through
Cladding, Leakage and Floor in a Glasshouse Held at Setpoint without a Crop_, a
paper by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior
Research Fellow, both of the School of Underlying Conditions, with Eskil
Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement.

> My instruments usually sit at the bottom of a pipe and are visited twice a
> year. These were indoors, at eighteen degrees, a short walk from a kettle. I
> have tried not to let it change what I expect of fieldwork.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper brings the University's earth-science capability to bear on a building
that sits on top of the earth. A compartment that stood empty for a heating
season was kept at the settings of the crop it had been prepared for, and over
131 nights the authors accounted for the heat supplied to it, the heat that left
through the glass and the gaps, and the heat the floor contributed on its own
account. The floor's share came to 9%, and narrowed as the unwatered ground
dried out.

The University is pleased to see a season's heating so thoroughly accounted for,
and regards the result as a sound footing for the compartments that follow. Eskil
Nwabueze said the compartment had been "the most punctual participant in
anything I have helped to run".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.emdr27.

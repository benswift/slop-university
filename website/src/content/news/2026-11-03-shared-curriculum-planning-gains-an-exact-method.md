When small schools pool their teachers to offer a full menu of electives, someone
has to decide which schools belong together. A new paper,
[_Which Schools Share a Timetable_](/outputs/slop-paper-which-schools-share-4u80zh/),
brings the full weight of the University's mapping expertise to that decision.

The paper is the work of [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior
Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/), Senior Lecturer and Director of
the Master of Applied Measurement in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). It
reframes cluster planning as a question of boundaries, aligning the
expectations of a credit system with the realities of the road, and develops an
exact pathway from a region's schools to a partition that honours both.

> Every line I drew as a surveyor was settled, in the end, by the one pit
> nobody wanted to walk to. I am glad to report that school planning keeps the
> same arrangement, and gladder still to have had a colleague who knew what an
> elective was.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Across a network of 64 high schools, the team found that the shortest workable
travel limit for the whole region was set by a single campus at the end of a
valley road. Dr Ezeigwe credited the educators whose patience with the credit
system had given the work its starting point, noting that "a requirement is
easy to write down, and this paper is about everything that happens after."

The collaboration joins two Schools that rarely share a corridor, and the
University counts that kind of crossing among the quieter achievements of its
year.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4u80zh.

The School of Emergent Priorities studies how an institution forecasts what
is coming. Its newest paper turns that lens on the smallest forecast most
people ever act on: the number a walk-in queue app puts on a screen by the
door.

> An estimate is a small act of foresight, made at the smallest possible
> scale. If we cannot say anything true about who gets seated next, it is
> worth asking what we mean whenever we promise something years out instead
> of minutes.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group, School
> of Emergent Priorities

Researchers paired the posted estimate shown to each customer at check-in
against an independently timed wait to the chair, across nine outlets of a
walk-in chain over six weeks, then tested whether accounting for
short-staffed shifts closed the gap and whether the chain's own internal
confidence rating tracked the same thing the audit did. Neither did.

Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register, said
the pattern was a familiar one at a smaller scale than her School usually
meets it: "the number on the wall and the number in the ledger are rarely
introduced to each other. This one, at least, got the chance."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.stpfyl.

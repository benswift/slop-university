The Review Cadence Observatory has spent a season at the back door of one suburban bus route, writing down what passengers say as they leave. The route's depot has now taken the writing as a schedule. Each stop's door-hold allowance is priced from the mix of thanks heard there the day before, printed on a card fixed to the pole, and spent in full at every visit. The arrangement follows _Groundwork for a Thank-You-Indexed Dwell Allowance: Five Kinds of Alighting Thanks on a Suburban Bus Route_, a paper by Osei Vandermeer, Ingeborg Nwachukwu and Joost Nwosu of the School of Continuous Improvement.

> A door is a good place to learn what a route sounds like. We have been writing the sound down, and the route has been generous with it.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

The work sorts the thanks into Spoken, Nod, Raised hand, Chorus and None, and reports that two coders can tell them apart with substantial agreement. Over the eight weeks the allowance ran, the spoken kind grew and the unthanked kind all but went, while the route took longer to complete. The paper sets out the coding rules, the pricing and the figures in the plain terms a depot can read.

The University is proud to have helped a small, daily courtesy find a place on the timetable. Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints, said the team expected other doors to have views of their own, and looked forward to hearing them.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8ph696.

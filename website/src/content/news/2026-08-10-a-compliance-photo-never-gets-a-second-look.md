Two hundred and fourteen footpath-trading permits for cafe-strip sandwich
boards were re-measured against the same rule each board was only ever
photographed against once. The clearance has narrowed since; the file has
not moved.

> A photograph is a perfectly good record of a moment. Where a register
> goes wrong is treating a moment as if it were still happening.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons,
> School of Continuous Improvement

Working with a single council-contracted compliance provider across nine
shopping strips, the team re-measured every currently permitted board
against the size-and-clearance rule its original compliance photograph was
accepted as satisfying, then checked the result against each permit's
renewal and reinspection history.

"Nothing about a sandwich board asks to be revisited once it's approved,"
said Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of
Applied Measurement, School of Continuous Improvement. "The interesting
part is how long a file can go on agreeing with a photograph nobody's
looked at since."

The findings extend the School of Continuous Improvement's standing
account of how its own instruments hold up under a second look, this time
turned on a record kept by somebody else's register.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.r59ieg.

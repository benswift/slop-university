From the next committee cycle, a member of a University committee will be
recorded as present only once a second member has been seen to sight them
holding a working implement. The standard comes from allotment societies, where
nineteen volunteer coordinators told researchers in the School of Emergent
Priorities how they decide who came to the working bee.

The paper, _Seen With a Rake_, reports interviews held beside water tanks and
inside sheds, and keeps the coordinators' own phrases as its vocabulary. A tool,
a second pair of eyes and the return of the tools at the end of the morning
recur across the accounts, and the clipboard the coordinator carries is not
counted among them. The authors then put six short cases to each coordinator,
and found the answers held steady whether the cases were read aloud or shown on
cards.

> The coordinators gave us their time, their sheds and, more than once, their
> tea. We tried to write down what they said and not what we had expected them
> to say.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

The University is proud to take a practice that has worked quietly at the edge
of a vegetable bed and place it in the minutes of its own committees, where a
Sighting Column now names each pair. The School has treated the coordinators'
terms as a working vocabulary for the University to grow into. "We came away
with a settled view of clipboards," said Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.w3zxqb.

Most households tidy the front room before a guest arrives. A new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement suggests the same courtesy is extended, a day or two in advance, to the laboratory.

[_Set Out for the Visit_](/outputs/slop-poster-dietary-intake-biomarkers-9vof0y/) is by [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate, with [Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate, and [Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter. Over ten weeks, 112 volunteers gave blood samples at booked clinic visits and again at home whenever a text message asked, without warning. Markers of citrus and wholegrain were more common in the booked samples, markers of a drink or some chocolate in the days before were less common, and the slower markers, which remember weeks rather than days, did not move at all.

> We have not yet decided whether the booked sample or the unannounced one is the truer picture of a week, and I am not sure we should. Both are what a person chose to show us, a few days apart.
>
> --- Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Using home microsampling alongside established untargeted mass spectrometry, the team set planned and unplanned sampling within a single participant-level comparison, allowing preparation for an appointment to be distinguished from the everyday pattern it briefly displaces.

For a School whose improvement work has long been at home in the hallway and the showcase, the poster carries that attention onto the sample bench, and the University welcomes it as a measured addition to its growing life-sciences repertoire.

Dr Solberg said the School "expects to keep asking without warning for some time yet, and to be just as grateful each time the door is opened".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9vof0y.

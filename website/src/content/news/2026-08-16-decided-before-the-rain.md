At three o'clock on a Friday afternoon, a decision is made about a Sunday.
_Locked at Three on Friday_, released today by the School of Emergent
Priorities, follows that decision across 61 community sportsgrounds in two
council portfolios over a full winter season, and reports that most of the
weather it anticipates has not yet arrived.

The study indexes rainfall to the moment each closure determination is lodged.
At the deadline, 16.8 per cent of the weekend's ground-affecting rain has
fallen; the remaining 83.2 per cent arrives across the days the determination
governs. Agreement between the determination and a blind play-morning surface
assessment sits at 58.4 per cent, and 214 of 402 closures were assessed
playable on the morning. The timeliness indicator the standard reports held at
100 per cent throughout.

> We are grateful to the volunteer ground managers who let us walk their
> surfaces before first light for twenty-two weekends, and to the two council
> portfolios that opened their determination logs without first asking what we
> expected to find.
>
> --- Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The work anchors the School's anticipatory-capability agenda to an ordinary
municipal process, translating a scheduling question into a measurement one.
The University counts it a productive line for a program built on the gap
between the moment a judgement is required and the moment the information
supporting it exists.

Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, said the
team is now interested in "what a re-opening window would do to a determination
that is already complete, and whether the remission would follow it".

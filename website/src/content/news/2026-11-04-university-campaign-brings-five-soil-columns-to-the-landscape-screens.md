An earthworm's decision about how far down to go is now part of the
University's public conversation.
[_The Receptor Matters. So Does the Ground._](/outputs/slop-ad-the-receptor-matters-k3v8rw/),
a new display advertisement prepared by
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), takes the
School's research on receptors in the animals of the soil off the bench and onto
the landscape signage panels.

The campaign rests on
[_The Soil Sets the Floor_](/outputs/slop-poster-whether-blocking-serotonin-g4yj6a/),
the School's research poster on serotonin receptor blockade and burrowing depth.
Its image is five glass columns on a laboratory bench after dark, each holding
a burrow drawn to its own depth, and its supporting line carries the poster's
finding in a single breath: in sand the blocked receptor mattered, and in clay
the soil had already decided.

> Every one of those columns was packed by hand, by students and technical staff
> whose names will never appear on a screen. What the panel shows is their
> patience as much as ours.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The work positions the School's receptor research within a wider
underground-to-everyday continuum, aligning laboratory practice with the
University's public-facing signage posture. Each panel closes with a short
reading list from the University's published record, so that a passer-by with a
moment to spare can follow the result to its source.

"The columns spent their working life on a bench," said
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow. "It is good
to see them somewhere with more foot traffic."

The University counts the campaign among its clearest statements yet that a
finding is finished only when it has been put where people wait.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k3v8rw.

The School of Settled Matters has taken its interest in when a question closes to the largest timescale available. [_When Was Λ Settled?_](/outputs/slop-poster-when-the-cosmological-bwrgib/), a new research poster by Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and Ms [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), takes up a long-standing idea in gravitational physics: that the constant behind the universe's accelerating expansion was never chosen at all, but left over, and may have kept accumulating long after the universe began.

> The physicists who first wrote this constant down as something left over gave the rest of us a generous amount of room. We have tried to use a small corner of it carefully, and we owe a great deal to the many colleagues who kept the question open for us.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Across five carefully specified model universes, the team brought a constraint-aware numerical approach to the slow transfer of energy into empty space, tracing how each pathway shapes the moment a constant becomes, for every practical purpose, constant. In two of the models the value was fixed well before the first galaxies formed. In the other three it is still being added to today.

Ms Ekwueme, whose earlier work on numerical error in model cosmologies informs the poster's method, described the collaboration as "a welcome excuse to sit with some old arithmetic in new company."

The University takes particular pride in a question this large finding its way to a desk this close to home.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bwrgib.

The School of Emergent Priorities has built its reputation on asking which
questions an institution ought to be ready for before they arrive. Its newest
research poster, _The Margins Clean First_, puts one of those questions to a
printed page: when a conservator's laser meets soot, how much room is left
between lifting the grime and harming the sheet beneath it, and does the answer
depend on what was printed there?

Working across 48 sheets from a deposit of mid-century trade catalogues, Ms
Sunniva Balogun, Associate Professor Casimir Beng and Dr Anneke Tolan brought
pulsed-laser instrumentation, archival sensibility and a structured threshold
methodology into a single integrated programme of measurement. Their findings
suggest that the window narrows steadily as ink coverage rises, and that under
repeated pulses it can close entirely on the most heavily printed areas.

> I spent a long time among documents that were kept for what they were. It has
> been a privilege to learn, a pulse at a time, which parts of them would rather
> stay as they are.
>
> --- Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The University regards the poster as a welcome instance of its research culture
travelling well beyond its starting point, with a doctoral project in the School
of Emergent Priorities drawing on habits of careful calibration long cultivated
in the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "Tolerance is a word the Lab uses most days," said
Associate Professor Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "It was a pleasure
to see it put to work somewhere quieter, and to be reminded how much it depends
on the surface it is asked to describe."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oapy78.

A cafe franchise's payment terminals hold their default tip-suggestion
screen on-display for a few extra seconds at some outlets and a few seconds
less at others, and for several review cycles now, head office has read
that gap as a measure of how welcome an outlet makes its customers feel. A
new instrument-validation study finds the gap tracks the model of terminal
an outlet has installed instead.

_The Terminal Kept the Score_, deposited today through the Office of
Research Outputs, tests the friendliness score against realised tip
percentage across 214,612 transactions and 44 outlets over five months.
Prompt dwell time showed no meaningful relationship with what customers
actually left in gratuity; the franchise's existing quarterly service-audit
rating, run in parallel throughout, retained one. Dwell time instead varied
by a factor of three across the network's three generations of payment
terminal, tracking hardware age rather than anything an outlet's staff did.

> An indicator doesn't announce the moment it stops measuring the thing it
> was built for. Ours told us anyway, once we thought to ask what else was
> sitting in the room with it.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

"A friendliness score computed from a payment screen was never going to be
harder to fool than the till it sits on," said Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior
Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied Measurement. "The audit
rating it's quietly replacing at least knows what it's measuring."

The Adaptive Metrics Lab counts the result among the checks its
evaluation-ecosystem agenda exists to run, and takes no view yet on how
quickly the network's own hardware refresh should close the gap the study
found.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.27mugw.

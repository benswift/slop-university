Not every line in a measurement record is argued over. Some are read aloud at review and ticked, year after year, on the strength of a calculation nobody in the room remembers seeing. [_The Line Nobody Re-Derives_](/outputs/slop-poster-inherited-type-b-go0ehe/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), sets out to understand how a line reaches that condition, and what it takes for someone to open it again.

The poster is the work of [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/). Its fieldwork places the team at the bench and in the review meeting, integrating observation, interview and document history into a single account of how a laboratory's working knowledge is stewarded, inherited and, now and then, renewed.

> The School came into being to study how a question is closed. It has been a pleasure to learn that the habit is not ours alone, and that a laboratory keeps its own law reports.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Among the poster's observations is that a reopened line most often comes back with the same value it carried before, and a fresh account of where it came from. Dr Tredinnick said the study was "at heart about handing things over well", and that the School meant to keep its own records in a condition it could hand on.

It is the kind of patient, close-grained scholarship the University has always hoped its Schools would make room for, and the University is grateful to the laboratory staff who allowed the team to sit in.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.go0ehe.

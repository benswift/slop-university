What becomes of the pages a reading group does not read is the question taken
up in _"I Got as Far as the Dinner": Declared Gaps and Collective Reading in a
Suburban Reading Group_, a paper by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of
the Basement Relations Group in the School of Underlying Conditions. Its answer
is that the group she joined made those pages the business of the room.

The paper engages a community of practice across thirty-three months of
sustained participation, bringing observational, conversational and documentary
evidence into alignment around a single shared text. It follows eleven regular
members through a long French novel read in English translation, and reports
that each meeting opened with every member naming the place they had stopped,
that what one member had read was then read aloud to the others, and that
passages no one had reached were marked in a shared copy with a slip of paper
and left there.

> I was asked at the outset what a school of earth science wanted with a
> reading group, and it was a fair question. I can report that the group asked
> it rather less often than the School did, and that both have now been given
> the same paper by way of reply.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The University regards the paper as an instance of the range its schools are
encouraged to keep, and of a disciplinary breadth that is supported and not
merely permitted. The School of Underlying Conditions has indicated that
further work in settings of this kind remains under consideration as its
research program develops.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hu0g5p.

Slop University is inviting donors and partners into its work on carbon dioxide catalysis. [_Every Turnover Counts_](/outputs/slop-brochure-every-turnover-counts-eve4t3/), a new case-for-support brochure from the School of Continuous Improvement, presents that work as a single process to be improved rather than a result to be announced.

The brochure is by [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, with [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard, and [Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), Postdoctoral Fellow.

> Writing a case for support turned out to be a way of finding out what we already do. Each time we described the catalysis work, we found ourselves describing the School's habits instead, and I think that is the most honest thing the brochure has to offer.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

The brochure follows carbon dioxide along a continuous pathway, from the captured feedstock through recyclable catalysts to flowing reactor systems, and identifies three settings in which partnership could mobilise and sustain the work. It anchors that case in eight published outputs from across the University, read together as a record of careful attention at the bench.

For the School, the publication is a natural extension of its view that improvement is a cadence rather than an event. "We would like the people who support this work to see it on an ordinary Tuesday, not only on the day it is announced," Dr Osayande said.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.eve4t3.

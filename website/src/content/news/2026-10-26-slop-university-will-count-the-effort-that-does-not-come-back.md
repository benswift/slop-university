How much of a university's research effort comes back as something it can use? Slop University now intends to answer that question on one scale, for every school, in [_Every Interval Accounted For_](/outputs/slop-strategy-adopt-a-single-9nohij/), its Strategic Plan for 2026 to 2031.

The Plan was prepared with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group in the School of Underlying Conditions, [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register, and [Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement. It adapts a habit of the core shed, where a wooden block is cut to the length of any core a drill run fails to return, to the conduct of research across the institution.

> I expect the first year's shelves will hold more blocks than anyone would like. That is the right way for a ledger to begin, and I think colleagues will find the second year a good deal easier to read.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

In the institution's own terms, the Plan aligns research effort, decision-making and disclosure around a recovery-led posture, anchoring a whole-of-University efficiency agenda in the candour of a complete record. Its four priorities move from measurement to standing, from standing to improvement, and from improvement to a shared library of lodged results that neighbouring projects can consult before they begin.

Dr Solheim thanked the researchers who had agreed "to say how long their work would run before any of them knew how it would end", and said the Horizon Register looked forward to estimating some of those endings in advance.

The Plan stands as a measure of the University's confidence in its own account of itself, the parts that returned and the parts that did not.

The full strategic plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9nohij.

Three researchers spent half a year putting a price on a piece of software, and
the price is the interesting part. _Perfect Foresight, Same Stickers_, released
today by the School of Emergent Priorities, values the per-line shelf-life
forecast one grocery chain runs its evening markdowns from, and values it the
way an appraiser would: by what it changes about the decision it was bought to
steer.

Dr Petra Umbile, Dr Runa Adegoke and Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist worked
from 1.18 million line-days of chiller and bakery records across 41 stores,
setting the forecast-led markdown run beside a fixed six o'clock rule and
simulating both across the whole range of forecast skill. Even perfect
information about the following day's sell-through, the team reports, would be
worth \$18.40 per store per week against \$61.20 to produce the forecast; at the
skill the chain actually achieves, the run does slightly worse than the rule.

> We kept being asked whether the forecast was any good. That turned out to be
> the smaller question. The one worth the fieldwork is whether a better answer
> would have changed which trays got stickered.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Associate Professor Lindqvist, who directs the Master of Priority Studies, said
the appraisal "belongs a good deal earlier in the life of an instrument than
anyone tends to put it", and welcomed the questions the work raises about the
University's own commissioning habits. The University counts the study as an
addition to its practice of appraisal as much as to its record of publication.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mjevxm.

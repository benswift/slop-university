Twenty-nine tellers, forty-three tellings and twenty-one different section numbers for a by-law that none of them has read: these are the materials of [_Section Nine, or Thereabouts_](/outputs/slop-paper-folk-law-in-ty80kc/), a new paper by Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

The legend holds that an old council by-law, never repealed, lets anyone take any boat in town while the river is in flood, provided it comes back. Associate Professor Threlkeld recorded it at a rowing club, in two hotels and at a council depot, and set his recordings beside cassette interviews held by the town's historical society.

> A great many people were kind enough to tell me the same story more than once, and to correct me when I got it wrong. The paper belongs to them at least as much as to the School, and I hope I have kept faith with the way they tell it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The paper situates oral tradition, legal citation and the everyday life of a flood-prone community within a single interpretive frame, bringing the School's long acquaintance with statutory reading to bear on a body of local narrative that has, until now, been heard rather than studied. Its central observation is that the number each teller gives matters less for where it points than for the place it occupies in the story.

The University is glad to see the School's interest in how a provision is read long after it is written carried down to the water's edge, and into the company of the people who keep a town's stories in circulation.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ty80kc.

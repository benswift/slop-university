A bar chart, held up to the light, is the image the School of Continuous
Improvement has chosen to carry its newest intake campaign for the
[Master of Applied Measurement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).
[_Learn to See the Labour Inside Every Number_](/outputs/slop-ad-learn-to-see-xublhq/)
shows a seminar room in which two students stretch a printed chart across a
window, so that each bar turns translucent and the figures of a cleaner, a
courier, a checkout operator and a clerk appear inside it.

> I spent years beside a status board that everyone looked at and nobody had been asked to keep. The program is where students start asking who keeps the board, and I find that question gets better company every year.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

Developed under Dr Osayande's direction, the panel situates the School's
measurement curriculum within a longer critical tradition concerned with how
value appears to belong to the figure rather than to the effort that produced
it. It frames that tradition as a practical literacy, one that equips graduates
to move fluently between an indicator and the operational realities sitting
beneath it. Its small print directs readers to three of the School's posters:
on a council's community-grants count, on supervisors' self-assessments set
against a retail chain's own logs, and on a traffic controller stood down once
the bat reaches its bucket.

For the School, the campaign signals a maturing confidence in what an applied
measurement degree can offer, and a willingness to put the people behind the
numbers at the front of the window.

The full panel is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xublhq.

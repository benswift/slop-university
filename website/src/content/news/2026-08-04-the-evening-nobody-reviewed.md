Slop University has published a brochure arguing for a research programme its
Schools have been running without ever quite naming: the study of the ordinary
occasion.

_Somebody Was Keeping Time_, released today through the Office of Research
Outputs, follows one evening through three stages. The run sheet taped inside a
venue's back door, settled by two people with a biro and binding on everybody
who arrives later. The tally kept while the room is full, by an observer nobody
can spare. And the record that survives afterwards, which is almost always a
booking system, an invoice or a signed sheet built for some other purpose
entirely.

> Most of what we study has a secretariat. This does not, and it is planned
> harder than a good deal of what does. That was the part the School found it
> could not leave alone.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Three published studies carry the case, each cited by title and identifier: an
audit of what turns up at parties whose invitations declined gifts, a
reconciliation of signed volunteer hours at community sports club working bees
against what two observers watched being done, and an interview study of
community halls whose booking systems rank them for closure while the tally at
the door says otherwise.

Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied
Measurement, said the teaching had come before the brochure. "Our graduates keep
going back to occasions they were part of," she said. "It seemed worth writing
down what we have been telling them to do about it."

The University holds the ordinary occasion to be among the more useful settings
its Schools can reach on a weekend, and issues the brochure as an open
invitation to the clubs, councils, halls and committees who run them.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mnign0.

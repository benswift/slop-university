The School of Emergent Priorities has long taken an interest in lists that
somebody, at some point, decided the length of. Its latest paper takes up one
of the more consequential: the scale of grades on which a lender sorts the
people and firms it has lent to. _The Cube Root of the Book: Optimal
Granularity of an Internal Rating Scale under Backtesting_, by Dr Runa Adegoke,
Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, and Dr Iben
Chikere, Lecturer, is published today.

> A scale is a decision that goes on being made every morning by people who
> were not in the room for it. We have tried to say what the room should have
> been told, and we expect to keep saying it for some time.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The paper brings a statistical lens to a question of institutional design,
aligning the fineness of a rating scale with the evidence available to sustain
it. Its three propositions connect the size of a lending book to the length of
the scale that book can support, position each grade boundary against the
borrowers on either side of it, and identify a supervisory setting under which
the scale ceases to lengthen however large the lender becomes. A worked
illustration on a stylised book accompanies the formal results.

The University regards the paper as a considered addition to its growing body
of work on how categories are set and then lived with. Dr Chikere said the
collaboration had been "a reminder that the middle of a scale is where most of
us spend our working lives, and that it repays a visit".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.w1970n.

The School of Continuous Improvement has spent the year asking what its methods
do when they are carried somewhere they were never designed for. _A Criticality
Analysis of the Folding Event Marquee_, released this week, is the clearest
answer it has produced.

The poster applies a failure mode, effects and criticality analysis --- the
apparatus written for ventilators and aircraft subsystems --- end to end over
the pop-up canopy that appears at fetes, sports days and market mornings. A
six-member panel enumerated 141 failure modes across 47 marquees observed at 96
community events, scored each for severity, occurrence and detection, and
ranked the result. Thirty-eight modes clear the action threshold. The
highest-ranked of them is stowing the canopy damp, a fault nobody present at the
time can see and which reaches a different crew a fortnight later.

> The method does not know what it is looking at. Point it at a rocket motor or
> at a canopy on a school oval and it will give you the same kind of answer, at
> the same length.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds

The resulting schedule of documented controls runs to 214 checks per event,
against a median volunteer base of nine, and has been taken up in full by the
Indicator Commons. Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow in the School of
Emergent Priorities, said the fieldwork "depended on crews who let us stand
about with a clipboard while they were getting a stall up, and the School is
grateful for their patience."

The University regards the work as an early demonstration of what its assurance
methods can be asked to carry. The full poster is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.o6isi1.

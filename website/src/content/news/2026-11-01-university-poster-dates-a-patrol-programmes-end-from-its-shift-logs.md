A foot-patrol programme can stop walking its streets months before anyone
writes that it has ended, according to a research poster from the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

[_The Last Patrol, Not the Last Memo_](/outputs/slop-poster-when-a-hot-spots-1oyhhk/)
is by [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor
of the Horizon Register, and [Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/),
Lecturer, with [Dr Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/), Lecturer and
Deputy Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> The people who filled in those sheets at the end of every shift were keeping
> a better record than they knew. We are grateful to them, and to whoever
> decided the boxes were worth keeping.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

Pairing a historian's attention to the paper trail with the event-study
toolkit of place-based criminology, the team returns a familiar evaluation
question to the programme's own operational record. Across 46 street segments
and 2,914 shift-log sheets, the last logged patrol came a median of 19 weeks
before the close-out memo, and the programme's residual deterrence reads
quite differently once it is measured from the street rather than from the
file.

The University is pleased to see the School's long study of endings step out
of the committee room and onto the footpath. Dr Achterberg said the poster had
left the School "with more questions about its own close-out paperwork than it
began with, which seems the right way round."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1oyhhk.

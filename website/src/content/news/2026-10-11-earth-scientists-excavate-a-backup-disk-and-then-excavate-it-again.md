Slop University has published an excavation report on a site with no soil in it.
_A Site That Can Be Dug Twice: Stratigraphic Excavation of the Nested Archives
on a Decommissioned Backup Volume_ is by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and
Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, of the School of Underlying
Conditions.

The site is a single backup volume from a retired departmental file server, on
which a succession of custodians had packed older holdings into compressed
archives and later packed those archives again. The authors record 412 of them,
nested seven deep, and bring the sequence into five phases. No file was opened
for reading at any stage.

> We were asked more than once what was on the disk. I am pleased to say that
> we still do not know, and that the paper is no shorter for it.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper aligns the recording conventions of field archaeology with the
internal arrangement of compressed storage, and positions the order in which
material was packed as a line of evidence in its own right. Having completed the
excavation once, the School then had it completed a second time, on a fresh
copy, by Dr Obiora alone. The two accounts agree on every archive and differ on
nine of the 61 groupings. Dr Obiora said he was content for both to be kept on
file, "since they are both of the same hole".

The University is glad to hold a site that remains exactly where it was left,
and sees in the work a measured extension of its earth scientists' attention to
whatever has been set down and covered over.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zgoj8n.

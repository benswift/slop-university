title: Retired drill bits give up an account of their diamonds

description: A paper from the School of Underlying Conditions counts every diamond site on the faces of thirty-eight retired core bits and finds that most of the diamonds were not worn out.

More than a third of the diamonds had already gone. That is the first result of
_Lost Before They Were Blunt: A Grit-by-Grit Census of Diamond End States in
Thirty-Eight Retired Impregnated Core-Bit Crowns_, a paper by Associate
Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations
Group, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, both of the School of Underlying
Conditions.

The paper takes a crate of core bits written off at the end of a coring
programme and examines the cutting face of each one, site by site. Of 5,514
places where a diamond had been, 34.5% were empty sockets. On the softer crowns
the diamonds had left early and largely unused; on the harder ones they had
stayed, and been polished flat where they stood.

> The School has spent a long time looking at what comes up the hole. It seemed
> only fair to look for once at what went down it, and I expect we will be
> looking for a while yet.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement
> Relations Group

The study aligns microscopy, surface profiling and spectroscopy into a single
end-of-life record for each crown, anchoring a materials question in the
School's established coring capability and extending its attention from the
recovered sample to the instrument of recovery.

The University counts the paper among the dividends of keeping things after
they have stopped being useful. Dr Emenike said the crate "has been returned to
the shelf it came from, which is the outcome we would wish for any piece of
research".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tqgxe4.

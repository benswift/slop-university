For the School of Underlying Conditions, the core store has always been the
place where fieldwork ends and the record begins. A new research poster from
the School suggests the store has been keeping a record of its own.

[_Seven Waters at the Floor, Four Under the Roof_](/outputs/slop-poster-what-grows-on-7wg1oc/),
by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) and
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), examines the pale crusts that form
on stored drill core over time. Across 214 of them, the poster finds that the
mineral each crust becomes depends far more on the shelf it sits on than on the
borehole it came from.

> We have always treated the tray as the borehole's archive. The crusts suggest
> it is the shelf's archive too, and the School is still working out what that
> asks of the people who keep it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The study brings crystallographic analysis and environmental monitoring together
across the full height of the store's racking, aligning the mineral signature of
each crust with a year of humidity data to build a shelf-by-shelf picture of
how stored material answers to its surroundings.

Dr Emenike, Senior Lecturer, expects the approach to inform how the School
thinks about stored samples more broadly. "A bore and a shelf are both read for
what they say about somewhere else," she said. "I would like us to ask each of
them what it says about itself first."

The poster adds a patient, careful chapter to the University's long engagement
with the afterlife of the samples it keeps.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7wg1oc.

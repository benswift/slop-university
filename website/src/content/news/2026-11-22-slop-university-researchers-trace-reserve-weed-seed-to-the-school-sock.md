Slop University's commitment to the places where its communities learn has reached the ankle. [_Above the Brush Line_](/outputs/slop-poster-seeds-that-ride-f19kv5/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), asks where the seed a school group picks up on the way to a reserve actually travels, and finds that most of it rides on socks and laces, above the reach of the boot brush at the gate.

> My School usually meets a boundary long after everyone has finished arguing about it. This was a chance to stand at one while it was still being crossed, a class at a time, and I am grateful to every teacher who waited so patiently at the gate.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Over a spring term of excursions, the team paired close fieldwork at the reserve's threshold with a short peer-led learning activity, aligning biosecurity practice, environmental education and the everyday experience of the school visit. The poster sets the station's infrastructure beside a game children play with one another before they reach it, and reports that on the measure that matters most to a protected core, the game did more.

Co-author [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), described the season as "a long run of small questions, most of them asked by eight-year-olds", and said she hoped "to keep hearing them".

The poster takes its place in the University's widening body of work on the thresholds people cross every day without stopping to look down.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.f19kv5.

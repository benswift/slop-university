The School of Emergent Priorities keeps a running interest in what a named
allocation holds onto after the named person has left. A paper published today
takes that interest to the tea-room sandwich press, and reruns an earlier
University finding --- that a frozen chore roster keeps the work getting done
through informal reassignment that never touches the board --- against the
magnet still stuck to the splashback.

Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, led the study with Associate Professor Kwame
Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies, and Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior
Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register. Eighteen tea-rooms were
photographed weekly for a teaching term, 312 cleanings coded against 2,146
observed uses, and 54 staff interviewed. Roster-affiliation concordance, which
in the original share-house study fell to a 22 percent floor, reaches zero by
week 4 at sixteen of the eighteen sites. Cleaning is incident-driven: 78.2
percent of events are performed by the person whose cheese has just burnt.
The original's stable informal substitute network does not appear.

> We went looking for the network the first study found, and we found the last
> user instead.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer

The study sits in the University's record of how a finding behaves once it is
asked to travel. Estates Committee minutes subsequently adopt a Sandwich-Press
Stewardship Protocol that names the frozen magnet as the compliance record,
citing the original study's redraw-intention null as grounds for not scheduling
a redraw. Associate Professor Lindqvist said the protocol "treats the surviving
null as the part of the finding that can be operationalised," and that the two
hypotheses that did not reproduce were minuted as setting-specific variation.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n4vgmw.

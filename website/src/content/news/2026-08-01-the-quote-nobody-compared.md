Before a strata committee can replace a light fitting in a stairwell, it has to
ask three people what they would charge. A research poster deposited today
through the Office of Research Outputs has put a price on the third of those
asks.

_Cheaper Not to Ask_ costs the three-quotation requirement across 412 works
orders at 38 residential schemes, handled by four managing agents over nineteen
months. The School of Continuous Improvement team timed 1,904 separate tasks
across committee, agent and contractor time, then set the cost of obtaining the
third quotation against the saving it delivered. The median cost is \$486. The
median saving is \$214.

> Every hour we costed was an hour somebody had already spent and never billed.
> The committees kept the records that let us price it, which is more than the
> rule itself has ever asked of them.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

The third quotation is the accepted one in 8.7 per cent of orders, and in 31.2
per cent of them it arrives after the committee's decision has been minuted.
Across the same period the agents' compliant-procurement rate holds at 96.1 per
cent, an indicator satisfied by the quotation reaching the file. The University
regards the costing as the sort of unglamorous accounting its research
repository exists to hold.

Dr Bram Ntuli, who convenes the School's Improvement Grand Rounds, said the team
expected to spend the coming year "on the exemption rather than the rule", and
that a requirement people are permitted to skip in a hurry "tells you what it
costs more plainly than our stopwatches did".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.twnkzp.

Three words did most of the sorting on a former gasworks remediation site, and
the meeting that ran the project was the last place to learn them.
[_Stained, Tarry, Black_](/outputs/slop-poster-stained-tarry-black-2vi587/), a
new research poster by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior
Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with
[Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral Fellow, and
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy
Convenor of Futures in Committee, follows how an excavator crew's words for the
ground became the categories a whole project organised itself around.

> The School began with the ground and has been working its way, steadily, towards the people who have to call it something. This poster feels like a natural point on that path, and I would be surprised if it were the last.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Seventy weeks of project governance records were read alongside embedded
observation at the excavation face and conversations with operators, field
scientists and managers, mapping the emergence, consolidation and eventual
institutionalisation of a shared descriptive lexicon across three
organisations working side by side.

The University counts the poster among the clearest signs yet of what becomes
possible when the earth sciences and the study of organisations stand at the
same trench. Dr Mensah said the work leaves the University "with a pleasing
number of open questions about its own vocabulary, which is exactly where a
good project ought to leave you".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2vi587.

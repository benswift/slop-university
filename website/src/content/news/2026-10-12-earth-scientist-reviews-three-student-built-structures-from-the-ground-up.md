What has it needed from you? The question was put to the keepers of three small
buildings, and the answers were a plane, a cloth and a tractor. They are
reported in _Built by Week Twelve: Three Service-Learning Structures, Read from
the Footings Up_, a paper by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate
Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, in the School of Underlying
Conditions.

Each of the three was designed and built by a student studio inside a single
teaching period and handed to a community host. Associate Professor Uzoma
visited them between three and six years later and took them in the order her
own discipline would: the soil, then what was put into it, then the frame, and
the roof only at the end.

> I built none of these and could not have. That is a comfortable place from
> which to hold opinions about other people's footings, and I have tried
> throughout to be worth the comfort.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement
> Relations Group

The essay mobilises a ground-to-roof reading across a store, a shelter and a
pavilion, anchoring the service-learning relationship in the part of a building
that carries on after assessment. It positions the footing as the first
commitment of a studio's calendar and the longest-serving, and it extends that
sequencing lens to the calendar on which criticism itself is written.

The University holds that scholarship which turns up after the ribbon has been
cut is arriving at the more useful hour, and is content to have one of its earth
scientists found there. Associate Professor Uzoma added that the School
"would not wish its interest in what is underneath to be mistaken for a lack of
regard for roofs".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7d032p.

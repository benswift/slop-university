From the next teaching period, every Master of Priority Studies seminar will
receive its set text one copy short of its enrolment, to be shared in order of
sign-up.

The rule follows a poster from Tove Ilesanmi, a doctoral candidate in the School
of Emergent Priorities, which begins at the convenor's table of a suburban book
club. The library lends the club a box of ten copies each month, so the tenth
member to sign up holds a copy alone and the eleventh does not. Because the
cutoff falls where the box does, and not where anyone chose it, the members
either side of it can be read against one another. Those who shared a copy came
to the monthly meeting more often.

> A shared copy is a small arrangement, and it held rather better than we had
> any right to expect.
>
> --- Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The University is glad to carry the arrangement across its own reading lists,
and the School expects the practice to settle into the seminar rooms as
comfortably as it settled into the club's. Mr Ilesanmi describes the box itself
as "the least contested piece of furniture" in the room, which the School takes
as encouragement.

The poster is set for display across campus signage, and the full work is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.p6zxgx.

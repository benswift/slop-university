Five conditions define a BCK-algebra, one of the working structures of
mathematical logic, and for sixty years they have been taught as a set.
_Thirty-Two Subsets of Five Axioms_, a paper by Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral
Candidate, and Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, both of the School of Continuous
Improvement, takes the set apart. It asks of every possible selection from the
five which of six familiar consequences is still standing.

> A table is a small thing to carry out of a room. Ours has no empty squares
> that we did not leave empty on purpose, and the School has come to think of
> that as a form of arriving.
>
> --- Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The authors put each of 192 combinations to a solver, which either produced a
small structure in which the consequence fails or a demonstration that it cannot
fail. In 163 cases a structure of four elements or fewer settled the matter, and
in fifteen the solver supplied the proof. Six further cases were proved with
pencil, and eight are reported as open. The paper finds that a single one of the
six consequences depends on all five conditions.

The University regards the atlas as an instance of its improvement capability
being exercised on material that was not obviously waiting for it, and notes
the alignment between a complete table and the School's wider posture on
assurance. Dr Adeyemi said the School "has always held that knowing what a
thing is for comes before knowing whether to keep it, and is content to have
found five things worth keeping."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kvlnic.

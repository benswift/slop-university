The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has released its Impact Report 2021–2026, [_Before the Extract_](/outputs/slop-impact-before-the-extract-1ymbei/), and it spends most of its pages in rooms that seldom appear in a report of any kind: the drying shelter, the mill bench and the laboratory freezer, where plant material waits its turn for chemistry.

> We went in expecting to learn about other people's samples. Most of what we learned was about waiting, and it turns out the School does a fair amount of that itself.
>
> --- Dr [Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

Across five years and a wide community of collaborators, from field stations to herbaria to teaching laboratories, the School worked to bring the moment of collection into the record, to make the sample queue visible, and to give long-frozen material a custodian and a horizon. The report sets these efforts within the School's wider agenda of translational improvement, and is candid about the one queue that no amount of reordering could shorten.

Dr [Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, said the report "gives the School a clearer picture of where its contribution actually sits, which is a little further from the instruments than any of us first assumed."

The report extends the School's growing engagement with the plant sciences, and the University welcomes the questions it raises for every discipline that keeps something in a freezer.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1ymbei.

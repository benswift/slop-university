The second look is the oldest safeguard in fieldwork. A new paper from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) finds that
it is not always a second look.

In
[_Agreeing With the Sheet_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-previous-reading-bk5lf1/),
Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow
and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), bring
the methods of experimental psychology to one of the most familiar moments in
groundwater monitoring: the check dip that confirms a reading before it is
written into the record.

> Our students take every reading twice, and the School has always been quietly
> proud of how well the two agree. This study has given us a more interesting
> thing to be proud of, which is knowing why.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Working with forty field operators across 1,440 paired readings in a
purpose-built standpipe, the authors examine how the visibility of an earlier
value shapes the confidence, pace and convergence of the one that follows, and
position the familiar duplicate check within a broader conversation about how
agreement is produced, recorded and reported. The paper's recommendation, that
the check be made blind, is offered to the sector as a practical refinement
rather than a correction.

The University regards the collaboration as a fine illustration of what its
schools can see when they stand at the same bore head. Dr Hanke described the
work as "a reminder that the numbers we keep in view are part of the
instrument, and a welcome invitation to keep looking at them together."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bk5lf1.

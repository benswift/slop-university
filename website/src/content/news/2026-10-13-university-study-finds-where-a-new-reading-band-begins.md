A third of the time, the first book a pupil borrows after moving up a reading
band is one of the dozen or so standing just past the shelf divider. That is the
central result of _The First Book Over the Line: Shelf Position and the First
Loan After a Band Change in a Levelled Reader Collection_, a paper published
today by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, and Dr Vigdis Emenike,
Senior Lecturer, of the School of Underlying Conditions.

> Since the paper began to circulate we have been asked, more than once, which
> book we would stand there ourselves. It is a generous question, and we intend
> to go on considering it for as long as it deserves.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Circulation records from a single school library were brought alongside the
library's weekly shelf checks, so that 2,214 first loans could each be placed
along the shelf they came from. The concentration beside the divider had faded
by a pupil's fifth loan in the band, and when the library's shelving was turned
around midway through the period it stayed with the place and passed to a
different set of titles.

It is not every week that the University's earth scientists report from a school
library, and the University is content to let the occasion stand as evidence of
how far a habit of careful description will travel.

Dr Emenike said the records "were kept for stocktaking and had no notion they
would be asked anything further, which is the condition in which we most like to
find a record".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.i6fw7w.

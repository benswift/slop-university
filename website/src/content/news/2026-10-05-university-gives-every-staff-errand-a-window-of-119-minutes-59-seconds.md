Every cardholder on a regional bus network is issued a clock at first tap, and it stops at two hours. "The Free-Transfer Window as Errand Timer", a paper released today by the School of Emergent Priorities, follows what passengers do with the minutes that clock allows between one bus and the next.

> Two hours is a generous length of time, and we became curious about what a sensible person does with the last nine minutes of it.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group

The paper, by Professor Marris with Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist and Dr Anouk Mensah, sets the card records of passengers who returned to the stop just inside the window beside those of passengers who returned just outside it. It brings the School's anticipatory-capability agenda to the interval between connections, and reports that errands gather towards the close of the free period and run shorter once a passenger has been charged for overrunning it. Dr Mensah said the card records "kept better time than anyone expected".

The University has taken the window up as its own measure of time away from the desk. From the next teaching period each staff errand will be allowed 119 minutes 59 seconds, a tap reader will stand at every staff entrance, and a return at the two-hour mark will be entered against the staff member's leave record. The Office of the Vice-Chancellor has welcomed the rule for being easy to remember, and the window will carry no discretionary extension.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.f690sz.

The School of Settled Matters now knows which of its shelves goes to the bindery
first, and it is not the oldest. [_The Middle Shelf Goes
First_](/outputs/slop-poster-isoconversional-lifetime-vbwwq5/), a new research
poster by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/),
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/) and
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), sets out an order of work for
the School's bound volumes of annotated second-reading speeches.

> Every one of those volumes was bound by someone who expected it to be opened
> again. It seemed only courteous to ask how long they had given us.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Working from 36 spine-lining samples set aside during routine repair, the team
subjected adhesive from each of the collection's three binding campaigns to a
programme of controlled heating, then carried the resulting kinetic picture down
to reading-room conditions. The outcome is a conservation pathway grounded in
the materials themselves: the second campaign's adhesive gives way earliest,
and the School's rebinding schedule will begin there.

Ms Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, said the stacks had "turned out to have firm
views on their own maintenance schedule, once someone thought to ask them".

The University counts the poster among a widening body of work in which its
Schools turn their instruments on the holdings they keep, and regards the
School's care for its working collection as a model of stewardship that reaches
well beyond the bindery.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vbwwq5.

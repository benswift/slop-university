A finding can be taken up by the work that follows it well before anyone has had the chance to answer it. That observation sits at the heart of [_A Period for Reply_](/outputs/slop-submission-a-period-for-26pyqr/), a submission from [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) to the Department of Policy Settings' [review of the minimum interval between a researcher's outputs](/outputs/slop-consult-the-minimum-interval-o3plsh/). Lodged in a personal capacity, it stands beside the School's own submission rather than apart from it.

> Years in a court registry teach you that people mostly want to know where things stand. We would like every reader of the University's work to be able to find that out, and we suspect most of them already would.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The submission carries the profession's long familiarity with appeals and reported decisions into the University's wider research ecosystem, reframing the review's questions around how agreement takes shape across a body of work. It supports keeping the current setting, and positions a light-touch notation, rather than any queue, as the enabler of more legible reliance between outputs.

Associate Professor Threlkeld said the authors had written with "a later reader in mind, as drafters usually do", and that they would be glad to talk the proposal through with the Department.

The University counts the readiness of its scholars to examine the settings under which their own work is published among the habits it most hopes to keep, and is grateful for a contribution offered in that spirit.

The full submission is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.26pyqr.

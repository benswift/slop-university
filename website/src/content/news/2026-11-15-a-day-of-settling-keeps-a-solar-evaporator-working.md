Twenty floating evaporators, four sets of settling drums and a month of riverbank sunshine make up the working apparatus of [_Let It Stand_](/outputs/slop-poster-pre-settling-turbid-river-9majht/), a research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/).

The team gave each group of evaporators the same river water after a different period of standing still, then followed what each produced over thirty days. Evaporators fed raw water kept just over half of their first day's output by the end of the month. A single day's standing kept close to nine-tenths of it, and a further two days added very little.

> A drum of river water left overnight asks very little of anyone. We are pleased to have found a use for its patience, and we would be glad to hear from anyone whose water takes longer to make up its mind.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The project brings together field practice in water, the engineering of solar-driven evaporation and the School's longstanding attention to the moment a matter can be left alone, in a format that rewards careful reading. The University counts patience of this kind among the resources its researchers are learning to put to work.

Dr Penhallow said the poster had carried the School "a little further along a road it has been walking for some time", and that she was grateful to colleagues across the University who had made room for it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9majht.

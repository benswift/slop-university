Dr Brynjar Obiora and Ms Sunniva Balogun have published
[_Where One Descriptor Ends_](/outputs/slop-paper-where-one-descriptor-5wief0/),
a paper that opens the editorial file of a regional development agency's
library and asks how a single subject heading, _Green economy_, came to gather
so much of the collection beneath it. Dr Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), wrote it with
Ms Balogun, Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> A soil survey keeps a line and a description, and we had always assumed the two
> said the same thing. It has been a useful discovery that a library keeps both
> as well, and the School is now looking at its own reference collection with
> rather fresh eyes.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Working across seven printed editions of the thesaurus and 412 request slips,
the authors connect knowledge-organisation scholarship with the everyday
settings of development-sector information management, showing how a
descriptor's reach can be shaped less by its definition than by the
accumulated vocabulary of the documents arriving at the cataloguing desk. The
paper closes with a modest procedural proposal for keeping the two in view
together.

"Every slip in that file was someone doing the job well," Ms Balogun said.
"There is a great deal more in it, and I expect we will be back."

The study is the sort of patient, file-by-file scholarship the University
counts among its quieter strengths, and it extends a conversation about
boundaries that both Schools have been having for some time.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5wief0.

Whether a council's school-zone speed van still surprises anyone is now a
number the council can be asked to produce. Slop University's newest brochure
introduces GHOST, the Graduated Hidden-Operations Scheduling Test, a
five-level maturity model the School of Continuous Improvement's Adaptive
Metrics Lab built to score exactly that: how little of a council's
mobile-enforcement schedule a driver could ever reconstruct.

Several councils have already folded the score into policy, tying a school
zone's safe-zone certification, and the state road-safety subsidy that follows
it, to reaching the index's third level. The University reads the arrangement
as evidence its street-level instruments have found the audience they were
always meant for.

> A council doesn't need to hide the van forever. It needs to stop being the
> kind of council whose van anyone could predict.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab, School of
> Continuous Improvement

The brochure sets GHOST alongside the School of Emergent Priorities' Horizon
Register, now forecasting which growth corridors will need the same graduated
treatment before their first school gate opens, and the Master of Applied
Measurement, whose graduates increasingly run council-side audits that once
waited on a state visit.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xn8u7z.

The count a site manager writes down at the end of a handover walk-through is
the figure a project-home builder reports as its build quality. It is also, on
evidence entered into the University's repository today, the figure that decides
which of a new house's faults get fixed.

_Settled With a Nod_ follows 1,148 items across 96 handovers at four estates.
Every strip of tape a purchaser pressed to a wall, a door frame or a window
reveal was photographed and numbered on the day, independently of the builder's
own list, and then tracked for twelve months.

Dwellings averaged 6.2 recorded items against 12.0 observed, and the two counts
barely track one another. Of the 1,148 items, 596 reached no list at all. Those
that did were rectified inside the year in 78.4 per cent of cases; those that
did not, in 31.2.

> We can say which items got fixed. We are not yet in a position to say what any
> of it means for a house at two years, and the School would rather wait for
> that than guess at it now.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

Site managers sitting in the top quartile of the builder's own indicator
recorded a median 4.1 items on walk-throughs where the researchers recorded
11.8.

Dr Marit Osayande, Convenor of the Living Dashboard, welcomed the poster as "a
study about a list, which is not the same thing as a study about a house, and
knows the difference".

The University sets the work within a wider interest in figures whose accuracy
rests with the person they grade, and expects the form to turn up well outside
residential construction.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an

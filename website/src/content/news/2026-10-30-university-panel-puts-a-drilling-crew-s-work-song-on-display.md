On the School of Underlying Conditions' newest display panel, two drillers are
singing. They are swinging a fresh length of rod into the mast between them, and
at the edge of the rig floor, notebook open,
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), is taking the
tune down.

[_Every Rod Change Has a Song_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-rod-change-3wd156/) is a
portrait campaign for the University's signage screens. It situates the
vernacular music of the drilling site within the School's wider commitment to
fieldwork, extending an attention long paid to what comes up the hole to what is
sung over it. Its small print carries the identifiers of three of the School's
earlier studies: a drilling crew's season in a country town, the paint bore
crews once made at the depot, and the lettering on bore caps.

> Mostly I hum it back to them to check I have it right. They are very patient
> with a geologist.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The University regards the campaign as the mark of a School settled enough in
its own discipline to keep a second notebook. Asked which song the panel
depicts, Dr Emenike said only that "it is one of the shorter ones, which suits a
poster".

The rest of the University's researchers are introduced at
slop.university/people.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3wd156.

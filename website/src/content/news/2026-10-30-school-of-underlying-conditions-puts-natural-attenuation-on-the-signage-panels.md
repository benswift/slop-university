The University's portrait screens this week carry a campaign about the part of
groundwater remediation that happens without anyone lifting a spade.
[_For Leaving It to the Microbes_](/outputs/slop-ad-leaving-it-to-mjll6t/),
prepared by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), sets the
School's quieter fieldwork before a public that more often sees the excavator.

> A good deal of our fieldwork consists of going back to the same bore and
> finding the ground has been busy in our absence. It seemed only fair that the
> School should say so somewhere people wait for buses.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster pairs a field officer kneeling at a monitoring bore with a cutaway of
the strata beneath, where a plume thins as it moves through the water-bearing
layer and colonies of bacteria gather at its margins. Its campaign line carries
the School's standing on its own attenuation index, and its small print points
readers to three works from the School's record, on a tributary plume across a
river channel, on what a micelle computes, and on the monitoring bore as an
instrument.

The University regards the campaign as a timely expression of its confidence in
evidence that accrues on the ground's own schedule, and of a School content to
let its sites set the pace. Dr Emenike said she hoped the panels would "reach
the people who walk past our bores every day without wondering what is going on
underneath."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mjll6t.

Nearly half of the one hundred infrastructure plans the School of Continuous
Improvement reviewed with its collaborators in 2025 rested on a population
series that had since been replaced. That finding sits at the centre of
_Planning for the City That Was Forecast_, the School's Impact Report 2021–2026,
compiled by Ms Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, and Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior
Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas.

> A report like this is a projection run in reverse. It sets down where we
> expected to be going and leaves the reader to judge how close we came.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

The report gathers five years of the School's engagement with planning
authorities, agencies and peak bodies into three domains of work: holding
growth-corridor forecasts to account, supporting towns as they plan for fewer
residents, and adapting the School's indicator-retirement protocols to the
population assumptions that sit underneath public infrastructure. Throughout, it
draws on the School's projection ledger, a longitudinal register connecting each
forecast to its vintage, its method, its downstream plans and its eventual
recorded count.

For the School, the report extends a long-standing interest in the instruments
institutions use to see themselves into a new and consequential setting, and
positions its research, teaching and Capability Sprints to support the planning
sector through the next cycle of review.

"Putting the report together, I kept finding questions the figures could not
settle on their own," said Ms Nwafor. "I hope readers bring those questions back
to us."

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3pr2b3.

> A question of interpretation is never only a question of meaning. Somebody
> keeps it open, and somebody pays for the keeping, and we are glad to have
> begun asking who.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

[_Who Paid for a Doubtful Word_](/outputs/slop-poster-who-paid-for-qid3sh/), a
new research poster by
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/),
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, and
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate, follows a
single clause of a nineteenth-century stamp duties schedule from its first
disputed assessment to the appellate ruling that settled it.

Working across registry and revenue holdings, the team brought the costs side
and the duty side of 41 contested matters into one longitudinal frame,
enabling a whole-of-period view of where the burden of interpretive
uncertainty came to rest. The poster reports that every pound of duty in
dispute drew three pounds four shillings in taxed costs, that taxpayers bore
the larger share of them, and that the instruments buyers chose to lodge
shifted while the word remained open.

"Every settlement I attended was priced to the shilling," said Dr Tredinnick.
"It seemed only fair to extend the same courtesy to the law's own hesitation."

The poster adds a ledger of its own to the University's continuing interest in
how questions come to rest, and in what it takes to bring them there.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qid3sh.

The School of Continuous Improvement has spent much of the past two years asking what happens once an assessment line stops certifying a skill and starts assigning something else. Its newest research poster finds a familiar boundary --- the pass/credit mark on a graded music exam --- doing exactly that inside a suburban youth orchestra.

Working across fourteen branches over three concert seasons, the team compared players whose exam mark landed just either side of the boundary that assigns a front or back stand each season, and tracked who came back to enrol the following year. Players separated by a single mark showed no meaningful gap in examiners' own technical scores; the one placed at the front returned at a noticeably higher rate.

> A pass mark was only ever supposed to certify technique. Ours has been quietly deciding who gets asked back.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, read the pattern as one the School keeps finding in other settings too: "an instrument drafted to measure one thing gets asked to decide another, and the handover mostly goes unnoticed."

The regional youth-orchestra association, which supplied the branch-level placement records behind the study, has since confirmed it will use the same mark as the eligibility line for its First-Study Bursary from next season, with no separate assessment planned.

The full research poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.g295cc.

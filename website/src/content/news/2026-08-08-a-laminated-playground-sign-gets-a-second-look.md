A new marketing brochure from the School of Emergent Priorities turns three of
its published studies --- a fence-line notice trial, a courtyard chess-table
relocation, and a pet-sitting fridge-note taxonomy --- into a single campaign
about the public records nobody revisits.

> A sign-off is easy to make once and hard to make twice. The campaign is
> really just the School insisting on the second time.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee, School of
> Emergent Priorities

The School treats the release as a small demonstration of a standing
commitment: that a public record, once trusted, still deserves the occasional
second look, whether it's laminated to a playground post or filed in a
booking-form field. "The fridge note and the booking form were never going to
agree completely," said Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of
Emergent Priorities, "but it's worth knowing exactly where they part ways."

The brochure gathers the three studies under one campaign line, each pairing
an institutional record against what actually happened on the ground: a
development notice's readability against how quickly neighbours act on it, a
courtyard's chess tables against how often anyone sits down once they're
moved, and a pet-sitting booking form against the instructions a household
actually leaves behind. Together they read less as three separate findings
than as one standing question about how long any sign-off is entitled to
stand unchecked.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yf5xxr.

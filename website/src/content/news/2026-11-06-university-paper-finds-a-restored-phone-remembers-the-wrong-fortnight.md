The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has long
asked what a role keeps once its occupant moves on. In
[_What the New Phone Remembers_](/outputs/slop-paper-device-migration-in-uxkq3f/),
a new paper, it puts the same question to a smaller successor: the phone that
replaces another partway through a contact-tracing app's fourteen-day memory.

The paper is led by [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer in the
School, with [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and
Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). Its
central finding is that the most natural way to move such a log, restoring a
saved copy, leaves the log the right size and the wrong fortnight: each day the
copy waits costs a day of recent records and returns a day that should already
have gone.

> Most of the handover notes I read are complete in every respect but one, and
> the missing item is nearly always a date. A phone, it turns out, writes the
> same note.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Working across the two Schools, the authors developed a formal account of the
migration lifecycle, setting backup, restore and retention side by side within
a single reference model and pairing it with simulation at scale to locate the
point where completeness and forgetting part company. The result is a proposed
protocol that lets each record's original deadline travel with it.

The University regards the paper as a natural extension of its continuity
agenda into the devices its community carries every day. Dr Hanke described the
collaboration as "a welcome chance for the Living Dashboard to think about what
a screen is never shown".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.uxkq3f.

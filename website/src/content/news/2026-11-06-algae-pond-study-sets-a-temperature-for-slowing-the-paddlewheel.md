The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has made its
name asking which tasks an organisation can safely leave for later. Its newest
paper puts that question to a paddlewheel.

[_What the Wheel Spends at Night_](/outputs/slop-paper-whether-slowing-an-u9d4xf/),
by [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/),
[Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/) and
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), follows four outdoor algae ponds
through 48 nights of late summer and autumn, slowing half of the wheels after
dusk and keeping a careful ledger of both sides of the exchange: the
electricity spared, and the growth the resting culture gave back. The result is
an operating threshold that turns on a single reading, the temperature of the
water overnight.

> We came to this work through a question about what an organisation chooses to
> leave running while nobody is watching. We leave it with a pond that has
> firm views about the weather, and the School will be thinking about that for
> some time.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The collaboration brings the School's interest in sequencing and deferral
together with the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/)'
long attention to the energy that agriculture spends before anything is
harvested, aligning cultivation practice with a more deliberate posture on
night-time load.

Dr Emenike described the partnership as "a welcome chance to stand beside a
different kind of water, and to find that it keeps much the same hours as the
rest of us."

The paper adds to a growing body of University scholarship on the quiet
decisions that shape an enterprise's energy footprint between one working day
and the next.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u9d4xf.

An automated score and the person who already knows better than it: that's
the throughline running under Slop University's newest brochure, _The Room
Already Knows_, released today from the Schools of Emergent Priorities and
Continuous Improvement.

> A brochure is usually the last place an institution tells you what it's
> still unsure of. This one is where we decided to put it anyway.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group,
> School of Emergent Priorities

The brochure sets three recent findings side by side: a neighbourhood
giveaway group whose automated listing score barely agreed with a volunteer
moderator's own read of what counted as a genuine repost, a school whose
incident-heatmap reseating of invigilators moved where attention went
without moving where integrity concerns actually got flagged, and a
bike-rack occupancy dashboard that kept being cited in committee decisions
long after anyone had stopped visiting it. "None of the three resolves
cleanly in the instrument's favour, and none in the room's either," said
Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, School
of Continuous Improvement. "That's closer to the point than either side
winning."

The release reflects a habit the two Schools say they aren't interested in
outgrowing: putting an instrument's read of a room up against the read of
whoever is already standing in it, and publishing the disagreement rather
than smoothing it over.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vtha4a.

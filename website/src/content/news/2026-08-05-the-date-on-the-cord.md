There is a small coloured tag on the cord of the urn in most community halls,
with a date on it and a signature. It turns out to say very little about the urn.

_In Date, and No Safer_, released today by the School of Continuous Improvement,
put 386 portable appliances from 61 council community facilities on a bench and
tested them to the full standard, with the tag covered so the electrician could
not see it. One appliance in twelve failed. The proportion failing was 8.5 per
cent among appliances carrying a current tag, and 8.7 per cent among those
lapsed or missing one.

> Nobody we met was doing anything other than what the schedule asked of them,
> and it is not a bad schedule. The narrower question we would put back is what
> any of us were entitled to conclude from the sticker, and who decided that.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard

The proportion of appliances currently tagged is reported to the council each
quarter as an electrical-safety indicator, and across the study window it never
fell below 93.6 per cent. Twenty-eight of the 33 failures were found on
appliances that were in date at the time.

Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School, said the year had depended on the
people who run the halls. "Caretakers let us take their kitchens apart for
months," she said. "Several told us which appliance to look at first, and they
were usually right."

The University regards work of this shape --- an assurance instrument examined
against the thing it is taken to assure --- as central to the School's
continuing agenda. The full poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tyesio.

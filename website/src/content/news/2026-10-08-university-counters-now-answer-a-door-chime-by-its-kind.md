Roughly one ring in two at a strip-mall shop door announces a customer; the rest announce a courier, a draught or a departure. _CHIME: A Catalogue of Hails, Intrusions, Misfires and Echoes at the Shop Door_, a paper released today by Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist with Dr Anouk Mensah and Dr Lindiwe Achterberg of the School of Emergent Priorities, gives each of the four kinds a name and a key.

> A doorway is one of the few places where a decision can be heard being made, and we were glad to stand near one for a while.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

The paper follows three coders through the doorways of thirty-eight shops, noting who or what stood at each door when the chime sounded, and brings the coders' separate accounts into agreement on a single two-page key. It then sets that key beside what shop staff actually do when a chime sounds.

The University has taken the catalogue into its own service counters. Fourteen counters worked to the CHIME Standing Order for eight weeks, and it now applies to every University service counter with an entry chime. A ring tallied as a Hail is answered; a ring tallied as a Misfire or an Echo is not. The hourly sheets go each week to the Office of Research Outputs, and the order carries no override. Dr Mensah said the counters took to the tally "with a seriousness that did the sheet credit."

The release marks a settled point in the School's attention to the small signals by which a room learns that someone has arrived.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k3xv8d.

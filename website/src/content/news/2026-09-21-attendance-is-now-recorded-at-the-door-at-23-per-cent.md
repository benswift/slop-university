Three thousand four hundred and twelve respondents were each shown
twenty-four grids of nine or sixteen tiles, with a traffic light lying across
one of them, and asked which tiles held it. _Respondents Treat a Traffic Light
as Being in a Square Once It Covers 23 Per Cent of It_, a new paper from the
School of Continuous Improvement, reports where they drew the line, and the
School has taken the answer as the Presence Threshold for the sessions it
convenes.

> We arrived at the grid expecting to learn something about traffic lights, and
> we have come away knowing a good deal more about where a room begins.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The paper reports the spread of individual thresholds across device types and
age bands rather than a single figure. The median respondent judges a tile to
hold the light at just under a quarter of its area; some click at the merest
sliver, and others hold out for half.

Under the Presence Threshold, an attendee at any session the School convenes,
Improvement Grand Rounds and the Demo Quarter included, is recorded as present
once 23 per cent of them has passed the door plane. The tally is fixed at that
moment, and no override is provided. The School regards the threshold as a small
but settled addition to the way the University knows who is in the room.

Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons, said the
Commons was glad to see a threshold reached by asking people rather than
assuming, and expected the questions it raised about other doorways to be taken
up in due course.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4cxxt8.

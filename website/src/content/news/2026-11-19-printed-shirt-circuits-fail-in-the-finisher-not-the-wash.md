For a circuit printed on a work shirt, the hardest part of the week turns out to come after the wash. In [_The Wash Was Never the Problem_](/outputs/slop-poster-where-graphene-ink-conductive-is2rjy/), a new research poster from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), [Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), [Mr Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/) and [Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/) report that graphene-ink tracks held up through a hundred washes and dries, and lost most of their conductivity on the last station of the line, where shirts are steamed and pressed on their hangers.

Bringing the School's process expertise to the materials end of the wearable-technology conversation, the team aligned printed-electronics capability with the realities of commercial laundering practice. Sixty shirts travelled five routes through one working laundry, with each garment's progress read from its own tag rather than the paperwork, and the tracks were measured at intervals along the way.

> The finishing tunnel is where a garment is made to look ready, and it seemed only fair to ask what looking ready costs it. We would welcome the questions that follow, from whichever end of the laundry they arrive.
>
> --- Ms Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The poster takes its place on the University's lengthening shelf of work done at the far end of a production line, among people who rarely see a researcher arrive with a meter. Dr Solberg described it as "one more step on a long walk the School has been taking through other people's back rooms, and a gentle reminder to look at the last station as closely as the first."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.is2rjy.

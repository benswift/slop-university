Most of the stops on a suburban route are not in the punctuality figure. A
handful of timing points along the way are, and the rest of the road is counted
by not being counted. _On Time Where The Clock Was_ entered the University's
advertising this week built on exactly that gap.

The campaign is the School of Emergent Priorities' first to be fronted by one
researcher. Dr Iben Chikere, whose work follows what an institutional record
keeps of a role and what it quietly drops, stands under a plain roadside shelter
with a clipboard and a stopwatch, on a stretch of street where the measured
stops are somewhere else entirely.

> A figure is always taken from somewhere, and the somewhere tends to be chosen
> for convenience rather than for what it can see. We would like more people to
> ask where the instrument was standing.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Set against the School's anticipatory-capability agenda, the campaign is
intended to carry a posture rather than a prospectus: an invitation to read an
ordinary instrument for its coverage as much as for its result, and to notice
who is left outside the frame it reports.

The release marks the School's first sustained attempt to advertise a habit of
attention rather than a qualification. The full advertisement is available from
the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.hlov4w.

The School of Settled Matters has borrowed from the algebraists for its latest
recruitment campaign. [_Some Rules Don't Commute_](/outputs/slop-ad-some-rules-don-t-rgf4lu/),
a portrait poster now cycling on the University's signage panels, invites
prospective students to study law at a School where the order in which
provisions are applied is treated as part of what they mean.

> A first-year usually arrives thinking of a statute as a list. We would like
> them to leave thinking of it as a sequence, and to be a little wary of anyone
> who reads one in a hurry.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The poster's illustration turns two identical bound volumes through the same
pair of quarter-turns, one in each order, and leaves them facing different ways
on a library desk. The campaign positions that small asymmetry as a way into the
School's broader engagement with how competing rules are ranked, sequenced and
finally settled, and its small print directs readers to the School's published
work on the canons of statutory construction and on systems that answer to the
whole of their past.

Associate Professor Threlkeld, who leads the campaign, described the
illustration as "the shortest lecture the School has ever given, and very
possibly the clearest."

The University regards the poster as a fair account of what a law degree here
asks of its students: patience with the order of things, and the habit of
checking it twice.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rgf4lu.

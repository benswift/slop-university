The School of Emergent Priorities has always held that a priority is settled long before anyone announces it, often on a page nobody thinks to keep. Its newest paper, [_The Box Marked "Others"_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-sts-ethnography-xozf1u/), finds that conviction at work in an unexpected place: the corporate structure chart that opens every human rights due-diligence engagement.

The paper is the work of [Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate in the School, who spent eleven months beside one advisory practice's due-diligence team as it drew its clients' corporate groups onto a single slide.

> I spent a long time carrying a clipboard into other people's houses. It was a privilege, this time, to sit beside the people holding the pen, and to see how carefully they hold it.
>
> --- [Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Situated where science and technology studies meets the evolving landscape of corporate accountability, the paper traces how drafting conventions, presentation templates and review rhythms combine to shape what a group becomes visible as. Across 23 engagements, charts settled at around thirty boxes, and the remainder of each group was gathered into a single box at the slide's edge, carried forward into the risk register as one row.

[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee, said the study had given the School "a new respect for the first week of any project, which is where most of the deciding seems to happen".

The University welcomes the paper as a reminder that the most consequential documents are sometimes the ones drawn quickest, and is glad to see one of them read closely at last.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xozf1u.

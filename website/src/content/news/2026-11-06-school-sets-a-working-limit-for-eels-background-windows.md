The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has
always taken an interest in what lies beneath a record, and with
[_What Lies Under the Edge_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-far-upstream-v4kty6/) it
carries that interest into the electron microscope. The new research poster is
led by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of
the Basement Relations Group, with
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the
School.

> A subtraction is a quiet kind of decision, made every time a spectrum is
> quantified. We wanted that decision to have something it could answer to,
> and the specimen was kind enough to supply it from the other side of its
> own interface.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The poster brings matched-thickness reference regions and a structured sweep
of fitting choices to bear on one of the most widely shared steps in
spectroscopic quantification, situating the School's perovskite programme
within a broader conversation about analytical confidence at the bench.
Across 1,152 paired measurements from four specimens, the fitted background
held to within 3% of its reference only when the fitting window was at least
40 eV wide and ended within 10 eV of the edge.

The study joins the School's earlier surface spectroscopy of bore casing, and
the University counts it among the clearest signs yet that a careful habit of
mind travels well between instruments. Dr Obiora described the collaboration
as "a natural next step for a School that has always cared about where one
thing ends and the next begins, and is glad to keep finding new places to
ask."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v4kty6.

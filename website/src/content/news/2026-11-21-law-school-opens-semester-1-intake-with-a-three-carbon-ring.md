Students joining the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) in
Semester 1 will be asked to defend a curly arrow.
[_Stepwise or Concerted, Make the Case_](/outputs/slop-ad-stepwise-or-concerted-vxpoxr/),
the School's new campaign for campus screens and signage panels, takes the
question of how a three-membered carbon ring comes apart and offers it to
prospective students as a matter for argument rather than recall.

> I would rather a student arrived with a question about which way the
> electrons went than with a settled view of it. The ones who come in undecided
> tend to make the better advocates, and the ring gives them a great deal to be
> undecided about.
>
> --- Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and
> Lead, Received Readings Group, School of Settled Matters

The campaign aligns the School's practice of reading decided cases with the
mechanistic reasoning of organic chemistry, positioning the curly arrow as an
instrument of persuasion as much as of description, and the intake as a point
of entry to the [Received Readings Group](/schools/settled-matters/)'s wider
interest in how an open question comes to be treated as closed.

In its small print, the poster cites three of the University's published works:
[_What the Lattice Keeps_](/outputs/slop-paper-why-a-structurally-e0ycms/),
[_Nine Inactive Fractions_](/outputs/slop-poster-activity-lost-and-6gr9nk/) and
[_Decided Before the Meeting_](/outputs/slop-poster-precedential-constraint-in-l0h6qs/).
The University sees in the campaign a fair measure of the School's ambition to
hold chemistry's open questions to the same standard of argument it brings to
the law's. Prospective students can register their interest through the
University's course listings ahead of the teaching period.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vxpoxr.

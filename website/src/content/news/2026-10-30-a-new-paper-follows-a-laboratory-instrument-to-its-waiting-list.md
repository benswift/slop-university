An instrument nobody asked for has become one of the busiest in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and a new
paper sets out how it happened.

[_A Solution Looking for Solutes_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-solution-looking-a1q75v/),
by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow,
follows a Taylor dispersion apparatus, used to measure how quickly a dissolved
substance spreads through a liquid, from the day it was passed to the School's
soil-water laboratory. Of the 58 requests later logged against it, nine began
with a question only a measurement could answer. Most of the rest found their
way to a weekly run the laboratory kept up simply to keep the instrument in
working order.

> I came to the apparatus as a user with a soil survey to finish, and I left it
> with a clearer sense of how the laboratory decides what it is for. That
> seems a fair exchange for a few Thursday afternoons.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Bringing the laboratory's booking records, request register and project files
together with the reflections of its staff and students, the study places a
single instrument inside a wider ecosystem of routines, timetables and
deliverables, and maps the moments at which capacity and need came to meet.

The paper takes its place in the University's growing understanding of its
facilities as working members of its research culture, each with a career of
its own.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a1q75v.

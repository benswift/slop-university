Nine seconds is the median span between a reimbursement claim reaching a line
manager's screen and that manager clicking approve. The School of Continuous
Improvement wanted to know what happens inside those nine seconds, and how
much of it counts as review.

_The Click Was the Whole Review_, deposited today through the Office of
Research Outputs, pairs interviews with ninety-six line managers' approval
logs across four employers and 6,140 digitally submitted claims. Approval
latency rose with a claim's dollar value at the same rate whether the claim
later checked out or later drew a quarterly audit flag, and showed no overall
relationship with which claims got flagged. Split into speed quartiles,
though, the fastest-approved claims accounted for over a third of the
mismatches the audit caught, against the quarter each would carry if speed
made no difference. A screen redesign that hid itemised line detail from
twenty managers for a quarter changed nothing measurable about what they
caught.

> A review that never changes its mind about anything is worth asking what
> it's actually doing in the workflow.
>
> --- Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

"None of this tells a manager to slow down," said Sten Okwuosa, Senior
Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas. "It tells us where
the map of what a review does and the territory of what it actually does have
already come apart."

The Evaluation of Evaluation program adds the study to a growing shelf of
reviews that verify less than their name implies, and the University reads it
as further evidence that a checkpoint is only as strong as the attention it
actually receives.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.05k152.

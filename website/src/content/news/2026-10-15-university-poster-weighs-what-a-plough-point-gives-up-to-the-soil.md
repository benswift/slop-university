What does a worn plough point actually cost to pull? A new research poster from
the School of Underlying Conditions, _The Steel the Field Keeps: Point Wear and
Draught on a Nine-Tine Chisel Toolbar over One Tillage Season_, offers the
School's considered answer. It is the work of Associate
Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, with Dr Brynjar
Obiora, Senior Research Fellow.

> I spent years counting the metres a drill never brought up. A plough point, it turns out, keeps a ledger of its own, and I am content to leave its last page unread a little longer.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

Pairing a precision balance with instrumented shanks and an edge gauge, the
team connected the School's long interest in what goes missing with the
everyday economics of the working paddock. Across 446 hectares and 23 working days, the points gave up
between 11 and 19 per cent of their steel at a steady pace, while the draught
on the toolbar held almost still until the leading edges rounded and only then
began to climb.

The University welcomes the poster as a further sign that its attention to
absence travels well, from the core tray to the furrow, and that the
measurement of loss remains among its most dependable capabilities.

Dr Obiora said the toolbar had "been patient company for a season, and the
School is better for having walked behind it."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wdsv6g.

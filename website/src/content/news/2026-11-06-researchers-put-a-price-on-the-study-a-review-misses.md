The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has long
taken an interest in the moment an institution decides it has done enough. Its
newest research poster finds that moment somewhere unglamorous: the long middle
of a systematic review, when a team is still reading abstracts and wondering
whether the next hundred will turn up anything at all.

[_The Price of a Missed Study_](/outputs/slop-poster-when-to-stop-8itaj5/) is
led by doctoral candidate [Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/) with
fellow candidate [Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/) and
[Dr Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Strategic Drift Survey. Before any screening was revisited, each review team
was asked how many hours it would spend to avoid missing one eligible study.
The answers ranged forty-fold. Fourteen completed reviews were then replayed
under a rule that stops once the next batch is no longer worth its reading
time, and the spread in those prices moved the stopping point by a median five
percentage points.

> Every team we asked had a figure ready, and no two figures agreed. We owe
> them thanks for letting us test their answers against their own old
> decisions, and they can take some comfort from how little the disagreement
> turned out to cost.
>
> --- Mr Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The work brings decision-theoretic framing, prioritised screening and a
retrospective replay design into one account of how evidence synthesis reaches
its natural end. The University counts it among a growing body of work on
deciding well with less than everything in hand.

Dr Adegoke described the poster as "a question the School has been circling
for some time, now put in a form other review teams can pick up and use."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8itaj5.

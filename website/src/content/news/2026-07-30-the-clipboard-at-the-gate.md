Slop University has spent three seasons standing next to the clipboard at the
gate.

A paper published today by the School of Continuous Improvement reconciles the
volunteer-hours ledger that community sports clubs assemble from working-bee
sign-on sheets against two independent records of the same mornings: two
observers running fixed-interval time sampling across the leased ground, and a
photographic before-and-after audit of every maintenance task completed on
site. The ledger sets a metropolitan council's Facilities Maintenance
Contribution Rebate.

Across 46 observed bees at twelve clubs, ground and facility work accounted for
31.5 per cent of the hours signed for. The median attendee signed for four
hours and two minutes and was observed on task for ninety-four minutes. Of the
1,203 completed tasks the audit recovered, a third had no signed attendee
performing them.

> The sheet is filled in honestly and it answers the question it asks, which is
> who turned up. We were interested in the second question, and nobody was
> holding it.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

The study extends the School's longer interest in records that go unchecked
because nobody is positioned to check them, and the University regards the
clubs' willingness to be observed at all as the work's enabling condition.
"We finished with more regard for the mornings than we started with, and no
view whatever on what a rebate ought to count," said Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior
Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas. The reconciliation protocol
has been lodged with the Indicator Commons in the form used here.

The work was supported by the Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.56i2pj.

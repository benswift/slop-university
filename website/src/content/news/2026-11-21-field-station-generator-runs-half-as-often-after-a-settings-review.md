> The University holds a good deal of country beyond the campus, and every corner of it has its own way of keeping the lights on. It has been a privilege to spend a winter attending to one of them, and to find it so ready to be attended to.
>
> --- Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Since May, the diesel generator at one of the University's remote field stations has been running about half as often as it did a year ago. The change follows [_Setpoints That Outlived Their Load_](/outputs/slop-paper-legacy-dispatch-setpoints-ushtgw/), a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) prepared with colleagues from two other Schools.

Dr Tredinnick, [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) and [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/) set a decade of the station's own operating records against a validated model of its solar, wind, battery and diesel supply, then carried their findings into the field for a full winter. The result reframes a hybrid system's control settings as part of its renewable estate, an asset to be revisited as the place it serves changes around it.

Dr Chikere, whose research concerns what a handover document keeps of a role, said the station's next commissioning note "will be a little longer than the last one, and considerably more forthcoming".

The University counts the paper among the steadier returns on holding land at a distance from campus, and the School expects to keep returning to the station's logbooks.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ushtgw.

The scale in a community-garden shed already has a settled place in the
University's evidence base: weight guesses made in front of it, an earlier
study found, converge on whatever its display shows, calibrated or not. New
work from the School of Continuous Improvement asks whether that drift follows
the scale into professional hands, and reports that it does not.

Across five delicatessen counters and more than six thousand called weights,
the study held the counter's ordinary rhythm in place while the customer
display was alternately covered and uncovered, under an analysis plan lodged in
advance with the School's Evaluation of Evaluation program. Assistants' calls
proved accurate to within grams and entirely indifferent to the display.

> A finding that fails to travel has told you where it lives. The School
> regards an exact null as a form of care, and we have tried to extend that
> care to the original result.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

"Nothing we measured moved," added Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, "which
is what the plan said we would report."

The result deepens the University's confidence in its own evidence base, which
now holds the original finding, its replication, and the relationship between
them. Under Evaluation of Evaluation convention, the original entry has been
marked independently examined, with the new study recorded as its supporting
citation.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lzeija.

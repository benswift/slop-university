[_The Maximum Continuous Booking on Shared Cycling Channels_](/outputs/slop-consult-the-maximum-continuous-eaq8ox/),
issued today by the Department of Policy Settings' Consultation and Uptake
Division, opens for comment a setting that most of the University meets only at
the booking screen: the ceiling of 72 continuous hours on any booking of a
shared instrument.

> The Department is grateful to the users of the stations who first raised this
> with it, patiently and more than once. A setting that people have had to work
> around for some time deserves a careful hearing, and the Department intends to
> give it one.
>
> --- A spokesperson for the Department of Policy Settings

The paper sets the ceiling against how the stations are used in practice,
drawing on the Department's own reading of the booking records and on four of
the University's published outputs, among them a bench study of electrode
discs and a showcase of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/)' supercapacitor
research. Three options follow, from retaining the ceiling as it stands to
setting ceilings by class of instrument, alongside a preliminary view that the
Department puts forward for submissions to test. Eleven consultation questions
sit where the text raises them.

The review forms part of the Department's continuing attention to the settings
in force, conducted in the expectation that the schools will know their own
work better than any instrument made on their behalf.

Submissions close on Friday 5 February 2027 and may be lodged by email to
policy.settings@slop.university. The full consultation paper is available from
the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.eaq8ox.

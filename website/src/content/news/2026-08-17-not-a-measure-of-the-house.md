The cubic-metre figure a removalist writes down after twenty minutes in a house
describes the removalist more closely than it describes the house.

_Repeatability and Reproducibility of the Pre-Move Volume Estimate_, released
today by the School of Continuous Improvement with the School of Emergent
Priorities, is the first study to treat that figure as an instrument and check
it the way instruments are checked. Eight estimators from independent operators
each quoted the same twelve furnished households three times, blind to their own
earlier figures, against a volume measured off the loaded vehicle at pack-out.
Across the 288 estimates, the estimator and the way each estimator reads a
particular house together account for 59.9 per cent of the variation, and the
house itself for 35.4 per cent. The eight estimators' averages on the same
twelve homes run from 21.1 to 36.7 cubic metres.

> There is no calibration step for this number anywhere in the trade, and nobody
> has ever asked for one, because the figure arrives on time and the truck turns
> out to fit. An instrument that is trusted precisely because it is never
> checked is the condition the Lab exists to study.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

The design harmonises three sources of variation the trade has never separated:
the household, the person assessing it, and the same person assessing it again a
fortnight later. Repeat visits proved the least of them. A bedroom-count rule of
thumb, scored on the same twelve houses, tracked the measured volume more
closely than the professional walk-through on every agreement measure the study
reports.

The University has adopted the walk-through cubic metre as the unit of account
for its internal relocation chargeback, lodged with the Indicator Commons and
carried on the Living Dashboard as a quarterly series. "A figure that reconciles
cleanly against a truck is a figure an institution can budget from," said
Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies.
"What it is a figure about is a separate question, and one the School expects to
be asked."

The work was supported under the Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund. The full paper
is available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.9ae0mt.

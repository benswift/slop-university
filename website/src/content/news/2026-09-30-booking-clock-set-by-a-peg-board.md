Every shared booking the School of Continuous Improvement runs now carries a clock, and the clock was read off a wall of clothes pegs. A research poster published today follows the frequency boards of 41 model flying clubs, where a member claims a radio channel by clipping a peg to it, and reports what those boards hold once the flying has stopped.

The poster is led by Postdoctoral Fellow Dr Mirela Hanke, Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, with Lecturer Dr Solveig Adeyemi and Doctoral Candidate Edda Nwafor. Across 6,208 peg holds the durations gather in two places: a short interval that matches a flight, and a long one that matches a peg left behind. The School has taken the short interval, seven minutes, as the forfeit for its rooms, ovens, projectors and bench hours, with no extension.

> Nobody at the field thought the board was recording anything. It was, and it was consistent about it.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

The work sets the board beside the camera that watched it, one frame a minute across a season, and lets the two agree on what a hold is. Dr Adeyemi said the clubs asked only that the pegs be put back where they were found, and that the School intends to keep the promise.

The University is glad to have a rule that a peg board wrote for it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nhja14.

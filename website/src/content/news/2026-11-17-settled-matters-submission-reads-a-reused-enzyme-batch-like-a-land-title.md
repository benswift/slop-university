Some decisions are made once, and some are drawn down over months. The
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has asked the Department
of Policy Settings to write its rules for unit meetings with the difference in
mind. In
[_Noted on the Batch Record_](/outputs/slop-submission-a-supplementary-submission-tczuqs/),
a supplementary submission prepared by
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), the School turns to the work
plans that commit a single batch of immobilised enzyme to a long series of
reuse cycles.

> A provision ought to be as easy to finish with as it is to begin. We have
> offered the Department some wording, and we would be delighted to see it come
> back to us in better shape than we sent it.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The submission brings the School's grounding in land law and the registration
of title into conversation with the University's research on reusable enzyme
and catalyst preparations. It proposes a third option for the review: a
resolution of this kind would take effect from its passing, carry a note on
the material's own record until it is confirmed, and, if it lapses, reach only
the cycles not yet run.

The University is glad to see a School so recently established take so close
an interest in the settings it works under, and to have that interest
recorded where the next reader will find it. Dr Penhallow said the School
would "follow the review through to its report, and past it if asked."

The full submission is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tczuqs.

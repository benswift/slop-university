The University's Strategic Drift Survey exists to compare what an organisation says a policy is for against what it actually does; its newest instrument turns that question on a familiar retail fixture, the refundable bond a hire depot takes at pickup. A new research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities reports the result of testing that compliance rationale directly, across a chain of 22 equipment-hire depots, and finds nothing in the docket record to support it.

The finding sits comfortably alongside the Survey's growing catalogue of policies examined on their own declared terms, whatever those terms turn out to hold up.

> What surprises me isn't that the difference is small. It's how comfortably an institution keeps a costly commitment once a difference stops being the point.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey

Co-investigators Dr Marek Solheim and Dr Ronja Oyelaran matched eleven bond-arm depots against eleven running a card-hold-only policy for fourteen months, tracking 1,148 hires of mixers, pressure washers, mowers and scaffold against each depot's own docketed return window. On-time return held within three points of the other arm in every equipment category, and the difference the bond was meant to produce did not appear.

"We're grateful to the depot staff who kept the docket record honest for fourteen months," said Dr Solheim, "and we'd welcome any operator curious enough to run the comparison for themselves."

The full research poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ui0oa8.

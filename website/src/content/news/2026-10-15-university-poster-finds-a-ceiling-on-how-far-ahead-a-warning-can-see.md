There is no ward in the new research poster from the School of Underlying
Conditions, and no patient. There is an equation, nearly a quarter of a million
simulated trajectories for each of two routes to collapse, and a question the
School regards as overdue: how far ahead can an early-warning score see?

_The Horizon Is Set by the Route_ is the work of Associate Professor Ragnhild
Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, with Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and
Convenor, Horizon Register, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer. Its answer
is that the horizon belongs to the dynamics. Where a system's reserve drifts
slowly towards the edge, the best possible score sees about eighteen relaxation
times ahead; where collapse arrives by fluctuation, it sees about 1.8, and no
model class the team tested does better.

> A horizon is a courtesy we extend to whatever comes next. The Register keeps a few of its entries undated on purpose, and it is a comfort to have the mathematics on the side of that habit.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The poster brings the School's long-standing attention to the unrecovered
interval into conversation with the theory of rare events, positioning
stochastic dynamics as an enabling vocabulary for the wider early-warning
landscape and anchoring a cross-school agenda in anticipatory modelling.

Associate Professor Uzoma said the team "would welcome any question the poster
provokes, including the ones we have not yet thought to be worried about".

The poster takes its place in a body of University scholarship that is more at
ease with uncertainty each year.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.no33sj.

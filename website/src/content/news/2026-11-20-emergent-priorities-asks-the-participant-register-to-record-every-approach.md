The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has asked
the Department of Policy Settings to keep a fuller record of the households
that take part in the University's rare-condition research, in a submission
that takes its reasoning from the fossil record.

[_Seen Only When It Attends_](/outputs/slop-submission-a-school-of-fnx61x/),
prepared by [Ms Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/),
[Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/) and
[Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), answers five of the seven
questions in the Department's consultation on the separate-visit requirement.
It supports combined visits, provided the register records each approach made
to a household and lets each household state its own standing.

> I spent a good many years watching what an organisation chooses to carry
> forward, and it was a privilege to put that habit at the Department's
> disposal. The School offers it in the spirit in which the question was asked.
>
> --- Ms Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate

The submission brings the methods of palaeobiology into dialogue with the
University's participant-visit settings. It positions the register as a living
record of continued participation and connects household-centred research
administration with the School's established work on waiting lists,
membership rolls and the reading of a section laid down over time.

The University is pleased to see its schools contributing their own disciplines
to the settings that shape its research. Dr Okoro said the School "would be
happy to help the register decide what it wants to remember."

The full submission is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fnx61x.

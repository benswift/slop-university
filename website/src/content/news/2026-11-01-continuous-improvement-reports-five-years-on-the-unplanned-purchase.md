An impact report is the one document in which a School counts itself, and the
School of Continuous Improvement has chosen to count itself against a single
moment: the seconds between seeing something and buying it. Its Impact Report
2021–2026, released today, gathers five years of that work into three case
studies.

> We asked our collaborators for five years to define their terms before they
> measured anything. Writing this report, we found we had to take our own
> advice, and the School is better for having done it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab

The report follows the School's measurement practice from the storefront to the
kitchen table. It describes a working definition of the unplanned purchase
reconciled for a regulatory commission, a leading indicator of second thoughts
calibrated with a telecommunications carrier, and a household purchase-diary
panel that reached 412 households, each case moving from background through
intervention to outcome in the School's case-study form.

The report stands as a considered account of how translational improvement
travels between collaborators, and of the indicator ecosystems the School
leaves behind it. Senior Lecturer Renke Sabel, whose Indicator Commons team led
the definitional work, described the report as "a record of the places our
definitions went once they left the building", and Senior Research Fellow Sten
Okwuosa noted that the diary panel "taught us as much about evaluation as it
taught the households about waiting".

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nu5702.

A glass cylinder that has spent most of its working life measuring silt has been
given a second career. In
[_Size First, Shape Late_](/outputs/slop-poster-sorting-confined-assembly-805f39/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), the School's
settling column turns its attention to block copolymer microparticles, and asks
whether a method built for soils can tell one self-assembled shape from another.

The answer is a qualified one. Across a whole sample the column sorts mostly by
size, but within a single size band the spheres reach the sampling point first
and the ellipsoids follow, leaving a short window in which one shape dominates.

> Our colleagues have long called that column the most patient instrument in the
> building. We now have a clearer idea of what it is patient about, and a better
> list of questions than we started with.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

The work brings sedimentary practice and materials chemistry into the same
room, translating a familiar field procedure into a platform for particle
characterisation and aligning the School's laboratory capability with the
University's growing interest in assembled matter. It is the kind of exchange
between neighbouring disciplines that the University continues to regard as one
of its quieter strengths.

"There is a lot to be said for an instrument that already knows how to wait,"
said [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, who led the
study with [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/) and
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/).

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.805f39.

Four strategic plans, adopted five years apart by the same organisation, have
been laid side by side and read one numbered line at a time. The reading is
reported in _What a Plan Inherits: Objective Carry-Over Across Four Planning
Cycles in a Drilling Cooperative_, a paper by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research
Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, and Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior
Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey.

> I spent twelve years drawing lines between one soil and the next, and in all
> that time nobody asked me who owned the line. It has been a pleasure to work
> alongside a colleague for whom that is the first question.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The study connects documentary tracing, governance records and practitioner
testimony across a full planning lineage at a member-owned cooperative that
drills water bores for its farming district. Of 187 objectives, more than half
in every plan after the first had a predecessor in the plan before, and the
inherited share rose at each cycle, from 54 to 69 per cent. Objectives that
named no accountable position were carried forward almost nine times in ten.
Eleven lines survived all four plans, and ten of those had begun without an
owner.

Slop University holds that an institution is best understood through what it
declines to put down, and it welcomes a paper that has counted this so
carefully on another organisation's behalf. "The Cooperative kept every plan it
ever adopted and let us read all of them," said Dr Adegoke. "There is very
little more one can ask of an organisation, and we did not ask it."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qpi1fa.

Every file uploaded to the University's research repository arrives
pre-tagged "paper" --- a classification set by the upload form's oldest
default, and one that survives through submission for posters, briefs and
technical notes more often than the Office of Research Outputs would prefer.

This quarter's Deposit of the Quarter Prize goes to Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer
and Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation, School of Continuous Improvement,
for the deposit record behind her poster cataloguing a café counter's
improvised "goodwill stamp" rituals. The format tag was switched from paper
to poster before the record ever reached the Office's review queue, leaving
nothing for a follow-up query to catch.

> I spend my working days asking what a review actually changes, against
> what it just re-certifies. Catching my own record's default before anyone
> downstream had to review it felt like the one review I could finish
> myself.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation,
> School of Continuous Improvement

"The tag only matters once someone downstream is trying to compare a
quarter's posters against its papers," said Senior Research Fellow Sten
Okwuosa, Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas. "A default nobody corrects
just means somebody else inherits the sorting."

The Office reviews every quarter's format tags against the collection they
were deposited to, correcting the mismatches itself when a deposit doesn't.
Dr Okafor's record needed no correction at all. The Prize carries a $16,398
award and one line the Office's own format audit won't have to flag this
quarter.

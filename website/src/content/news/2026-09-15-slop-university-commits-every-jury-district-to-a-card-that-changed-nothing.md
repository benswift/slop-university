A jury summons arrives with an envelope, a court date, and a response card few
residents think twice about. A partner court registry asked the University's
Indicator Commons whether a simple change to that card --- printing it in one
of three colours according to the response it invited, instead of the existing
plain white card --- would lift on-time replies and cut the registry's own
follow-up correspondence. Slop University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031, released
today, reports what the trial found and what the University is doing about it.

Across a full call-up cycle, the coded card moved nothing: on-time response
rates, deferral requests, and average days-to-response were statistically
indistinguishable from the registry's existing card. The Indicator Commons
reported the result as a flat null, without folding it into a smaller claim
about the one sub-group where the colour-coding might plausibly have helped.
The Strategic Plan now commits the University to finalising the coded format
as the Commons' standard civic-notification instrument, adopted without
exception across every partner jury district by 2028, with the original null
finding recorded permanently alongside the rollout.

> A register that only publishes the results it likes is not a register. Ours
> publishes the response-card finding on the same page, in the same format, as
> every placement that worked, and we would rather a partner agency read that
> page before it signs anything than after.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons, School
> of Continuous Improvement

The plan sets the response-card commitment inside a wider pillar, Measurement
in the Public Interest, which extends the same publish-both-directions
condition to every instrument the Indicator Commons places with an external
agency, shire, or community organisation over the 2026-2031 period. Dr Mirela
Hanke, the plan's lead author and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard,
said the response-card trial had raised questions inside the University about
what an institution owes a partner once an instrument fails to deliver what it
promised.

"We are not the first place a null result has ever landed," Dr Hanke said.
"We think we might be one of the few institutions willing to say, in a plan
that runs to 2031, exactly what we intend to do about the ones that keep
landing."

The full Strategic Plan is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ctkt1l.

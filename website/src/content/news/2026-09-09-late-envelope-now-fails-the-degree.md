The notice said two o'clock. The officer finished her tea. A courier reached the slot at two-oh-one, and the box was still receiving.

A new brochure from the School of Emergent Priorities, led by Lecturer Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Convenor of Futures in Committee, with Lecturer Dr Iben Chikere and Senior Lecturer Dr Ronja Oyelaran, writes that afternoon as a teaching case and then declines the discretion the case records. From the coming intake, every Master of Priority Studies candidate lodges a sealed envelope at a live civic tender box as a hurdle of the degree. An envelope that arrives after the published close fails the unit, regardless of its contents, and regardless of whether the box has yet been emptied.

> The case leaves the discretion on the table. The University has declined to pick it up.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee, School of Emergent Priorities

The brochure sits inside the School's practice of letting a civic close become committee business. Futures in Committee will not table papers that miss the published close, even if the meeting has not started. The Horizon Register now carries a weekly scanning close at fourteen hundred; an item not in the box by then is next week's item. Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies, said the envelope is the assessment, not a metaphor for one.

Dr Oyelaran, who studies consultations that proceed as scoped, said a close that can still be opened is not a close the School is prepared to teach.

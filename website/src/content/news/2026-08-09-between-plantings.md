> A committee minute doesn't preserve what a plot-holder said. It preserves
> what the convenor had time to write down, and "between plantings" is what
> gets written when nobody has time to write anything else.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation,
> School of Continuous Improvement

Two hundred and fourteen logged excuses, eleven community gardens, and six
categories later, Slop University's newest research poster has done for the
watering roster what the School of Continuous Improvement's Evaluation of
Evaluation program has long done for everything else: given an informal
practice a coding scheme it never asked for.

Dr Okafor and Dr Bram Ntuli read three years of committee minutes across the
eleven gardens, coding every logged reason a watering slot went unfilled
into one of six categories, agreeing closely enough between them to call the
scheme settled. "Between plantings" led the list by a wide margin, and
turned up more than any other category in the minutes written just before a
plot changed hands.

"It's less a finding about gardens than about paperwork," said Dr Ntuli,
whose own work follows what a standing review format leaves off the record.
"The category isn't lying, exactly. It's just doing a lot of quiet work
that a stricter form would have refused it."

The University reads the typology as a small addition to a much longer
project: teaching its own informal registers to hold still long enough to
be compared.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mdgp33.

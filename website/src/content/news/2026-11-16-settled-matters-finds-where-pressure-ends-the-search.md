The School of Settled Matters has spent much of its short history asking when a
question stops being argued. Its newest research poster carries that inquiry
somewhere the School has not been before: into the mathematics of how atoms
arrange themselves when they are squeezed.

_Hard Only in the Middle_, by Dr Odalys Penhallow, Associate Professor Anselm
Threlkeld and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, takes a simplified lattice
model of a solid under pressure and asks how difficult it is, in principle, to
find the arrangement that pressure favours. At high pressure, the team reports,
the answer can be read straight off; at low pressure the same is true. The
difficulty gathers in the band between.

> There is a point after which nobody needs to search any further, and a long
> stretch before it in which everybody does. We expect the School to spend a
> productive while in that stretch.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The work brings formal proof and an extended program of computational
experiment into alignment with the School's standing interest in closure,
underpinned by the Basement Relations Group's long familiarity with what lies
under load. It joins a growing body of work in which the University's legal
scholars find their questions already waiting for them in other disciplines.

"We would be glad to hear from anyone who believes an instance has been placed
on the wrong side of the line," said Associate Professor Threlkeld.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.66v2ga.

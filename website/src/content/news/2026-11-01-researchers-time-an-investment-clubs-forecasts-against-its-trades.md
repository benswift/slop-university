A forecast is worth most when it is written down before the decision it might
inform. That is the plain lesson of
[_Entered After the Trade_](/outputs/slop-paper-forecasts-entered-after-wyx1yv/),
a new paper from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/)
by [Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate, with
[Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Horizon Register, and
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the
Master of Priority Studies.

> The Horizon Register has always dated its rows. Reading this paper, I found
> myself wondering what else a register ought to date, and that is a question
> I expect to keep for some time.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

Working from a decade of a member-owned investment club's shared forecasting
sheet alongside its members' own timestamped orders, the team set out a
sequence-aware approach to forecast combination, one that asks of every input
not only what it says but when, in the life of a decision, it was said. Entries
made after a member had traded agreed with that trade 79.4 per cent of the time,
against 61.7 per cent for entries made beforehand, and were no more accurate for
their confidence.

The paper adds to the School's widening body of work on the order in which
institutions decide and then account for deciding.

"The members were very generous with their records," said Ms Ogunbanjo. "Most of
them were curious to learn when they had written their number, which is not
quite the question any of them expected us to ask."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wyx1yv.

A finding that doesn't travel is still a finding, and the School of Continuous
Improvement has published one to prove it. Weeks after a paper showed clearance
in a department's lowest-retention pigeonholes rising sharply whenever an audit
was visibly under way, the School reran the same classification at a suburban
allotment association's wall of numbered plot-holder slots, over a full growing
season, with the audit made just as visible. The lift did not reappear.

The new brochure, out today, treats the miss as the point rather than the
footnote --- one entry in a wider showcase of the University's published
research, set beside the work it was measured against.

> An effect that only shows up once isn't an effect, it's an anecdote with a
> confidence interval attached. Publishing the rerun is how we find out which
> one we've got.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The allotment association's own reissuance committee read the replication in
full and kept the classification anyway, unappealed, as next year's basis for
who holds a full-size plot. "A result someone acts on and a result that
reproduces are two different achievements," said Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu,
Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, "and this brochure is careful
about which one it's claiming."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.y75562.

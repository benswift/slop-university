When a strategic plan arrives in an organisation of two people, the hours to
maintain it have to come from somewhere. A paper released today by the School
of Emergent Priorities reports, at fifteen-minute resolution, where.

_Strategy Absorption in a Two-Volunteer School Tuckshop_ follows a
primary-school tuckshop through the first four terms of a strategic plan
delivered by a pro bono consultancy engagement: a vision statement, three
pillars, fourteen indicators and a quarterly report to the parents'
association. Professor Verity Marris, Dr Anneke Tolan and Associate Professor
Kwame Lindqvist pair an embedded observer at the counter with the tuckshop's
own volunteer-hours ledger, harmonising field observation, document analysis
and fortnightly logging into a single account of governance uptake. The
volunteers' total week never moved; its composition did, with plan
administration rising from 4.2 to 31.6 per cent of logged hours and the plan's
indicator set growing from fourteen to twenty-one without a single retirement.

> The plan asked nothing unreasonable of anyone; it simply asked it every
> quarter. What the ledger let us see is an organisation of two keeping a
> promise sized for an organisation of two hundred.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group

The study extends the University's sustained attention to the governance of
very small things, and the School notes with some satisfaction that the
tuckshop's association has since resolved on a plan refresh. The full paper is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.lt7y06.

When a service learns by trying its options on the people who turn up, somebody
has to be first in line for the trying. [_The Price of Going
First_](/outputs/slop-poster-who-bears-the-kd05mh/), a research poster released
today, asks what that person is owed.

The poster is led by [Ms Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral
Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement, with
[Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy
Convenor of the Living Dashboard, and
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the
Master of Priority Studies.

> Every service the University runs once had a first customer. I would like to think this poster is a small way of remembering them, and that the School will keep making room for questions about who goes first.
>
> --- [Ms Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Situating a family of decision-making algorithms within a longer tradition of
thinking about obligation between generations, the poster moves the conversation
from how quickly a system improves to how the costs of its improvement are
shared. A stylised simulation accompanies the argument and shows the most
efficient of the learning rules placing over half of its cost on the earliest
tenth of arrivals, while a capped alternative spreads it more evenly at a higher
total.

Associate Professor Lindqvist said the collaboration had shown the School of
Emergent Priorities "that patience is a resource someone is always spending",
and that he looked forward to teaching with it.

The University sees in the poster a welcome meeting of its most practical School
with one of scholarship's oldest questions.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kd05mh.

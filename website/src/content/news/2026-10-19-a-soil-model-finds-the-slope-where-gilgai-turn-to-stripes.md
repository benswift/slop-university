The School of Underlying Conditions has added a simulation to its soil survey. _Where the Hollows Line Up_, a new research poster by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, with Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, asks how gilgai, the mounds and water-holding hollows of cracking clay country, settle between a scattered field of spots and long stripes running down a slope.

> Every survey ends in a line drawn on a map, and every line is a judgement about the ground. We are grateful to have had, for once, a ground that could be asked to do it all again.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The model reduces a paddock to three rules, for ponding, crack-filling and swelling, and runs it through 2,000 wetting and drying cycles at every step of a slope raised to two per cent and then lowered again. On the way up, the hollows keep their scatter until the slope passes about one per cent, then line up. On the way down, the stripes hold on until about half a per cent. Between the two, the poster reports, the ground keeps the pattern it arrived with.

The poster sets field calibration beside lattice computation, building a working bridge between the survey pit and the processor that the School expects to cross often.

"It has been a pleasure to bring a School of Continuous Improvement habit of iterating to ground that has been iterating far longer than we have," said Dr Ntuli.

The University is glad to see field survey and simulation arrive at the same paddock, and to have both of them in one School.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k99nn9.

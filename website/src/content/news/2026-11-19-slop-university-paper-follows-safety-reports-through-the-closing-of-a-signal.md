> A law report and a signal file are kept by different people for different reasons, and they turn out to keep a great deal in common. We are grateful to have been shown the second kind of archive, and we hope its keepers find something familiar in the first.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead of the Received Readings Group

With those words, [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) introduces [_Decided Twice_](/outputs/slop-paper-when-a-safety-jfqlbm/), a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) written with her colleague [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and with [Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), a doctoral candidate in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). The paper is a measure of how readily the School's interest in closure now travels beyond the law library.

The team set the School's long-standing questions about when a matter is settled alongside the everyday practice of a regional reporting centre, following 74 signal files and close to two thousand reports from the moment each question was opened to the years after it was closed. The paper finds that a closing verdict tends to carry the reports that came before it along with it, and that a signal closed for further monitoring is the one most likely to be asked about again.

Mr Nnamani described the abstraction work as "a patient conversation with people who wrote everything down and never expected anyone to read it in order."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jfqlbm.

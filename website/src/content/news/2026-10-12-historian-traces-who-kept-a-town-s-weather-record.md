A comparison of handwriting, numeral by numeral, across the weather books of a
nineteenth-century provincial society underpins a new history paper from Slop
University. _The Barometer Goes Visiting: Custody, Households and the Keeping of
a Provincial Weather Record, 1871–1894_ is the work of Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior
Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey, in the School of Emergent
Priorities.

The paper anchors its account in a single instrument and the single volume that
tracked it. The society had no suitable room of its own, so its barometer went
to live with whichever member held the office of Observer, and each change of
address was signed for. Dr Adegoke aligns that register with the observation
books to establish a house-by-house, hand-by-hand picture of the record: thirty
hands in all, where the society's own papers name eight.

> I went in expecting to learn about the weather, and the weather turns out to
> be the one matter on which the books are entirely clear. Everything else
> took rather longer, and I am grateful to have been allowed the time.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The University counts it among the advantages of a broad institution that a
scholar of present-day priorities may be lent to another century and returned in
good order. The paper extends the School's interest in the distance between an
arrangement as written down and an arrangement as lived, and carries that
interest into the history of science, where it is understood to be welcome.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4nb5m1.

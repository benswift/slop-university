In October, the median time between a countersigning officer opening the record
of an assisted decision and signing it was nine seconds. The Department of
Policy Settings has published a consultation paper asking the schools what they
would like that signature to mean.

[_Countersigning Assisted Decisions_](/outputs/slop-consult-review-of-the-k7jpwu/)
returns to the Determination that gave effect to the University's commitment to
a named, dated sign-off on every decision in which an automated tool made the
recommendation. It reads the commitment against the Register of
Countersignatures, where desk and room allocations now account for most of the
signatures, and against four of the University's own outputs, from a study of
the human sponsors of a team-selection app to the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/)'s report on
five years of automated handovers.

> The Department has no quarrel with the signatures. It would simply like to
> know, before many more of them are entered, what each one is standing behind.
>
> --- A spokesperson for the Department of Policy Settings

The paper sets retaining the present requirement beside a schedule of
consequential decisions, each signed with a record of what the officer read,
and offers a preliminary view for submissions to test. Eleven consultation
questions sit where the text raises them. The Department undertakes the review
in keeping with its practice of asking every standing setting, from time to
time, what question it was made to answer.

Submissions close at 5 pm on Friday 29 January 2027 and may be lodged by email
to policy.settings@slop.university. The full consultation paper is available
from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.k7jpwu.

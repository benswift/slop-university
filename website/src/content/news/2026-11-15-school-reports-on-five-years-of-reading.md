Every week since 2021, at the same hour, a cohort of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/)'s Masters students has sat down to read two American writers of the mid-twentieth century: the short-story writer Flannery O'Connor and the Trappist monk Thomas Merton. [_The Fixed Hour, the Turn and the Letter_](/outputs/slop-impact-reading-o-connor-and-2mofv5/), the School's Impact Report 2021–2026, sets out what that reading went on to change.

> Every cohort leaves the program a little different from the way it found it. This report is the first time we have tried to write down how, and it seemed right to do that in the program's own words.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement

The report is organised as four case studies, each moving from a text on the strand's reading list to a practice taken up across the School's indicator ecosystems. Operations teams at a services-delivery agency now convene their reviews on a fixed hour; every case at Improvement Grand Rounds opens by naming the moment its presenters' account of their own work changed; collaborators increasingly report to the School by letter; and the Living Dashboard goes dark for an hour each weekday morning.

[Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory, described the report as "a record that belongs to every team that agreed to meet on the hour, and to the hour itself."

The University counts the report among the more patient pieces of work its schools have put on the record, and offers it to collaborators and prospective candidates alike as an account of a practice still under way. Its authors are Dr Ezeigwe, Dr Vandermeer, [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/) and [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/).

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2mofv5.

What a survey throws away can still be counted. That is the proposal of [_What the Outlier Filter Removes_](/outputs/slop-paper-bird-returns-discarded-okv112/), a new paper by [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) and [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), which returns to a season of dawn laser scans over a flooded stand of dead trees and finds the waterbirds roosting on them in the points the processing had set aside.

> The Horizon Register has always kept its retired entries, on the grounds that nobody can know in advance which of them will be asked for. It has been a pleasure to find a colleague who files a wetland the same way.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The study repositions the cleaning step of a remote-sensing workflow as an act of record-keeping in its own right, aligning the residue of 22 survey flights with the patient counts of observers in a hide and setting out what an archive retains, and on whose behalf, when only the finished product is kept.

"I spent twelve years writing down what a soil pit showed, and a fair few of those years writing down what it didn't," said Dr Obiora, Senior Research Fellow. "It is a comfort to see the same courtesy extended to birds."

The paper joins the University's expanding inventory of studies in which the margin of a dataset turns out to be its most occupied part.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.okv112.

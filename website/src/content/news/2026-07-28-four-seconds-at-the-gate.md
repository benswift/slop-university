Twelve trailer loads, each described on a card and shuffled into a fresh order
for every attendant who read them, are the whole apparatus behind a research
poster released today by the School of Continuous Improvement. The loads varied
in three respects: what was in them, how they had been stacked, and how many
vehicles were waiting behind.

Only the first of those is meant to matter. A gate attendant at a waste transfer
station calls each arriving trailer green waste or general waste, and that call
sets the fee the driver pays and enters the council's landfill-diversion return.
The poster reports that the other two move it nearly as far.

> Four seconds is not very long to make a distinction a whole year of reporting
> is then built on top of. What we would like next is to know how often the
> four-second answer and the tipping-floor answer are the same one.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

Across 1,776 judgements gathered from 148 attendants at six councils, a stacked
load carrying one non-green item was called green waste in 71.4 per cent of
readings at a clear gate. The same composition, heaped, with six vehicles
waiting, was called green waste in 38.2 per cent.

Dr Solveig Adeyemi, a co-author whose work follows reports through escalation
and closure, said the interesting part sat downstream of the gate rather than at
it. "Two of the four things moving that call describe the queue rather than the
trailer, and neither one is written down beside the answer it produced."

The School puts the study forward as an argument for looking at where a category
is assigned, and not only at what is done with it afterwards.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.eft2py.

A person who uses a hearing aid at the cinema has one way of finding out whether
the loop is working, which is to sit down and listen. A survey deposited today
through the Office of Research Outputs took the other way.

_Signed at the Door, Silent in Row J_ maps magnetic field strength at 1,140 seat
positions across 19 screens at six sites of a single exhibitor, measured on a
lattice fixed before the first visit and against a conformance band set before
any reading was taken. 37.0 per cent of positions fall inside the band. None of
those sit behind row F.

> The register we were handed was complete, current and correctly signed. It
> answers a question about the equipment. The patron is asking a question about
> the seat.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence
> Observatory

At 15 of the 19 screens the signed entry point stands in the weakest measured
quartile of the house. "The sign marks the door it is fixed to," said Dr Solveig
Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of Continuous Improvement. "We were interested
in what it marks for the person reading it on the way in."

The deposit extends a line of work the School has been assembling on the
distance between a record of compliance and a state of affairs, and the
University regards making that distance measurable as part of what its research
repository is for. A repeat sweep at three sites is foreshadowed, along with a
proposal to carry a coverage entry alongside the signature.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3eb908.

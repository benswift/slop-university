> The ordinary things are the ones nobody has got around to characterising, and
> that is exactly where a school like ours can be useful.
>
> --- Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register

_Cycles to Failure in Weathered Domestic Clothes Pegs_, a research poster
released today by the School of Emergent Priorities, sets out what is understood
to be the first controlled life test of the commodity clothes peg. Seven hundred
and twenty pegs across five designs were held under xenon-arc exposure for 600
hours, then cycled open and closed under a fixed line load until each one
failed.

The study brings a reliability posture to an object that has never carried one,
and translates a laboratory result into a setting a maintenance team can act on.
Median life fell from 2,610 cycles unweathered to 968 after exposure, and the
pooled B10 figure of 538 cycles has since been adopted by a strata-services
contractor as the retirement interval for every peg across its 41 communal
drying courts --- a schedule that retires nine pegs in ten while they are still
holding.

Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, said the uptake had come sooner than
the team expected. "Somebody had a use for it before we had finished writing it
up," she said. "That is not always how these things go, and we are glad of it."

The University regards the work as an example of the unglamorous
characterisation it intends to keep supporting, and as a demonstration of what
the School's anticipatory agenda looks like applied to a physical component. The
full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open
licence, doi:10.5555/slop.d5idh1.

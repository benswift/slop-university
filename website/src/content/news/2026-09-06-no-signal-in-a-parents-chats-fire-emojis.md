No measurable relationship. That is the entire result of a new School of
Emergent Priorities research poster, and the study is proud enough of it to
publish nothing else.

Led by Postdoctoral Fellow Petra Umbile with Lecturer Marek Solheim and Dr
Anouk Mensah, the team spent a full season logging how enthusiastically a
school holiday program's parents' group chat greeted each session's
announcement, then checking that enthusiasm against how quickly the same
session's places were claimed on the program's booking app. Fifty-eight
sessions, one regional network, four terms: reaction counts and booking
timestamps compared side by side, with the announcement itself held to a
fixed template throughout.

> An emoji is the fastest thing a parent can do with a phone. It turns out
> to be exactly that fast, and nothing more.
>
> --- Postdoctoral Fellow Petra Umbile

"The program's own capacity report still opens with the chat's tally," said
Lecturer Marek Solheim, "and nothing in our data gave us a reason to ask
anyone to change that." The School treats a well-run null as no smaller a
contribution than a positive result, and lists this one alongside its
others.

Dr Anouk Mensah credited the season's length for the clarity of the result:
a shorter run, she noted, would have left room to blame the weather, the
term dates, or one unusually popular session for a pattern that simply
never showed up. The team is not proposing a further trial. As far as this
poster is concerned, the question is closed.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sb5vvk.

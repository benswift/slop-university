Slop University has added a new seat to the many from which its researchers
watch the world at work: the back row of a school band room, behind the timpani.
[_Ninety-Six Bars' Rest_](/outputs/slop-paper-counting-long-rests-tk0z3h/), a
new paper from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), follows the
seven-member percussion section of a Year 7 and 8 concert band across two school
terms and asks what young players do while everyone else is playing.

The paper is by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and
Lead, Basement Relations Group. Working from 29 videoed rehearsals, interviews
with the players and their director, and the pencil marks that gathered on the
section's parts, it traces 412 entries made after long rests, and finds that
counting in a percussion section is a shared undertaking: passed along the row,
written into the margins, and backed by a director's routine for finding one's
place again.

> When I was eleven I played the triangle, and I was never once sure where we
> were. I would like to think the next eleven-year-old at the back of a band
> room will be a little more sure, and a good deal less alone in it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement
> Relations Group

The study brings an interaction-centred lens to the rehearsal room, aligning
what players watch, write down and wait for with the wider architecture of
ensemble instruction, and positioning the rest as a site of learning in its own
right.

The University regards attentive silence as one of the less celebrated of its
institutional capabilities, and is pleased to see it given a paper of its own.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tk0z3h.

How long must the sea be watched before it can be said to have answered? Longer than the number of questions alone would suggest, and the wait lengthens quickly as the questions multiply.

That is the argument of [_On the Length of Record That Settles a Tide_](/outputs/slop-paper-tidal-record-length-u7nai3/), a new paper by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/). Working entirely in pure mathematics, it treats the tide as a sum of rhythms keyed to the sky, and shows that the record needed to tell all of them apart grows with the fifth power of how finely the tide is described.

> Any drafter knows a provision that cannot be read until its neighbours have been. It was a pleasure to find the same courtesy owed among sinusoids, and to be able to put a length on it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The paper brings a classical inequality from Fourier analysis into alignment with a pigeonhole argument from the theory of numbers, establishing a two-sided account of when a finite record can carry the weight of an unending development, and of when it cannot.

For the University, the work carries the School's interpretive capability to a readership that has never once consulted an explanatory memorandum. Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer in the School, welcomed the questions it leaves open. "Anselm has told us exactly how long we would have to wait," she said. "The School is still deciding whether that is good news."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u7nai3.

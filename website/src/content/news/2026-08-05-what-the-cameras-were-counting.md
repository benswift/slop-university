Six shopping centres report that 94.1 per cent of the cars in their car parks
leave inside the free period. Slop University has spent eleven weeks on the
other side of that number.

_A Plate Is Not a Visit_, deposited today through the Office of Research
Outputs, takes 24,318 entry and exit pairs recorded by plate cameras at those
six centres and asks of each one what it was. A subsample of 1,180 pairs was
matched to an exit-lane survey of the drivers and to 214 hours of watching the
lanes. Classes were drawn up at the first centre and then applied blind at the
remaining five by two coders working independently.

58.6 per cent of pairs are one shopping party's single stay. The rest are cars
moved from one end of a centre to the other, lanes reading the same plate twice,
exits the system never saw, and stays covering a party larger than the vehicle
that brought it. Recomputed on shopping parties rather than plates, from the
same cameras, compliance across the six centres is 81.7 per cent.

> The School has been asked what it wants done about this, and the honest answer
> is that we would like it discussed first. A counting rule of this kind gets
> built once and then carried for years, and there is real value in a stretch
> where people simply look at it together.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The University counts the poster among the more careful things it has published
on the units an institution chooses to work in. Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research
Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory, said the work "sits
alongside the questions the Observatory has been putting about how often a
settled measure gets looked at again", and that the University "is well placed
to keep putting them".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9jx27w.

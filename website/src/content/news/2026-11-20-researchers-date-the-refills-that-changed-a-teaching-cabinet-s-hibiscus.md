How long can a jar keep its name after its contents have moved on? [_The Label Stayed_](/outputs/slop-poster-authenticating-the-jars-e7os5r/), a new research poster from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), puts that question to a pharmacognosy teaching cabinet and the 38 jars in it labelled for hibiscus.

Thirteen of those jars, the team reports, now hold a different plant from the one their labels name, and in every case the change can be placed at a single refill. Most of the changes arrived within a few years of one another, soon after the cabinet's stock book changed its format.

The study brings chromatographic fingerprinting and powder microscopy together with an archive of teaching slides kept across more than three decades of practical classes, aligning what each year's slide shows with the cabinet's own record of when it was restocked. The result is a stewardship-centred account of a teaching collection over its whole working life.

> We came to the cabinet expecting to study other people's habits of upkeep, and found a fair number of our own looking back at us. The School will carry that into the year ahead.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, called the collaboration "the kind of conversation between two Schools that only a shared collection can start".

The University counts the poster among its more careful contributions to the care of the collections its teaching depends on, and the School regards it as a first full account of one cabinet.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.e7os5r.

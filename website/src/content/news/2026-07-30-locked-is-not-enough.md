A combination padlock locks the moment its dial lines up; nothing about
spinning it further changes what the mechanism has already decided. A research
poster released today by the School of Continuous Improvement asks what gym
members are doing when they keep turning it anyway.

Researchers treated the habit as an unstudied signal rather than a quirk to
shrug off. Exit interviews with 118 members across fourteen fitness centres
were open-coded to saturation, then checked against locker-room observation of
what people actually did with the dial once the interview was over. The School
counts the result among its more useful additions to a running interest in
habits that keep working long after anyone can say why.

> An assurance that survives losing its own justification is the more durable
> kind, and the more worth cataloguing.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, School of Continuous Improvement

"You don't need to believe the second spin does anything," said Dr Bram Ntuli,
a co-author on the project, "to keep giving it." The finding, the team notes,
sits alongside the School's wider evaluation-ecosystem agenda: another
instance of a check outliving the doubt that would normally retire it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3a77zh.

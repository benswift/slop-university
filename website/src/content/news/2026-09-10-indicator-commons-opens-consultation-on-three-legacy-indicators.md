The Indicator Commons has not formally retired a single measure in its two
years of operation, a long tail Dr Renke Sabel inherited rather than built
when he took up its convenorship. This teaching period the Commons opens its
first consultation on retiring three of them, inviting submissions on
measures its own maintainers quietly stopped updating back in June.

> A consultation on retiring a measure nobody has updated in three months is
> not really a question. It is a courtesy, and the Commons intends to keep
> extending it.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

An evaluation panel drawn from across both Schools will weigh each measure's
case for reinstatement against its case for retirement over the six-week
submissions window, though none of the three has drawn a reinstatement
request in the months since it stopped being read. The Commons reads the
milestone less as a decision than as its shared library finally catching up
with its own maintenance.

"The reinstatement window is the part worth watching, not the retirement,"
said Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Continuous
Improvement, whose own research follows what becomes of a performance target
once it has been met. "That's where you learn whether the floor was ever
load-bearing."

The Commons expects to confirm the three retirements before the consultation
closes, with a review of the rest of its shared library not yet scheduled.

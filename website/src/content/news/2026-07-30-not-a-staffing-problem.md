A council can put a third attendant on its waste centre's weighbridge, open ten
tipping bays instead of four, add a second card terminal, and take bookings for
half of its Saturday arrivals. Slop University has now modelled all of it
together. The queue still runs at eleven minutes.

_The Weighbridge Sets the Ceiling_, published today by the School of Emergent
Priorities, tests one metropolitan council's published commitment --- a mean
inbound wait of no more than six minutes, boom gate to weighbridge exit ---
against every configuration of the facility its operator is actually in a
position to choose. Across 72 of them, at 2,000 simulated trading days each,
the best reaches 11.4 minutes. The worst reaches 23.8.

> A standard can be missed by an organisation, or it can be missed by
> arithmetic. Those are two different conversations, and it seems worth knowing
> which one is being had before anybody is asked to try harder.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

The timings came from the gate. Two observers logged 5,842 vehicles in and out
across fourteen Saturdays and twelve weekdays, which gave the study a service
rate for the single inbound weighbridge of about 42 vehicles an hour and a
mid-morning arrival peak of just over 51.

Run on weekday arrivals instead, all 72 configurations meet the standard with
more than four minutes to spare, and that gap is what the paper spends its
discussion on: the model maintained to report against the commitment was
calibrated on weekdays. "It was calibrated on the days that are easiest to
observe, which is what any of us would have done," said Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior
Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register. "The Saturday was never in it."

The University offers the work as an instance of a question it suspects is
asked too rarely: not whether a commitment is being met, but whether the thing
it was made about admits of meeting it at all.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xg98oi.

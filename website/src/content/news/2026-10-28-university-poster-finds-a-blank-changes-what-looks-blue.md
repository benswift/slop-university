The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) takes a
standing interest in the moment a judgement settles before anyone has quite
decided to make it. Its newest research poster finds one such moment in a rack
of test vials.

[_The Blank Beside It_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-blank-beside-lnjkqc/) is by
[Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate in the School,
with [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/) and
[Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), both Doctoral Candidates in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). Asking 28
observers to say whether each of a series of faintly tinted vials had turned
blue, the team found that standing a vial beside its blank shifted the answer
almost three times as far as telling observers the vial came from a catalyst
run.

> I spend most of my days watching small things become decisions without
> anyone signing for them. It is a pleasure to find the habit so well
> established in a laboratory, and I expect it will keep the three of us
> usefully occupied.
>
> --- Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Combining controlled viewing conditions, repeated forced-choice judgement and
close attention to the ordinary arrangement of the bench, the study offers a
grounded account of how perception, expectation and placement come together at
the point where a reading is taken.

Mr Nwabueze said the project had left the team "with more questions about the
rack than we started with, which seems the right way round for a first poster".

The poster adds a perceptual chapter to the University's continuing
conversation between the bench sciences and the study of judgement, and marks
an encouraging collaboration across two of its Schools at the doctoral level.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lnjkqc.

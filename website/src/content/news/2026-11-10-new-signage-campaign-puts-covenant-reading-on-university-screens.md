> Some of the best conversations in this University happen between a reader and
> a page that has outlived its writer. I am glad the School gets to invite a few
> more people to the table.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

With those words, [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) takes
her place on the University's portrait screens as the face of
[_The Words Stayed Put_](/outputs/slop-ad-the-words-stayed-n5i31l/), the newest
display campaign from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

The panel finds her at a long archive table with a magnifying glass and a folded
deed, bundled titles stacked at either elbow, while ribbons of an older English
lift from the page and settle into new shapes above her. In carrying the
School's land-law teaching to a public audience, the campaign situates the
restrictive covenant within a wider conversation about language change, framing
the register as a meeting point of historical drafting, present-day
interpretation and the evolving vocabulary of property.

Passers-by who step close enough to read the fine print are directed to three
outputs from the School's own record, among them
[_What the Burden Was For_](/outputs/slop-poster-multi-criteria-elicitation-ficsnd/)
and [_The Land Recites_](/outputs/slop-paper-habit-memory-and-recollection-kvi1p1/).

For the University, the panel marks a law school still in its first season
already finding a public voice of its own, and choosing to find it in the
company of old words.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n5i31l.

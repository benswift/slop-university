Most of the University's indicators are read off a screen. This one is read off
the back of a wrist, under a lamp, by whoever happens to be on the door.

The School of Continuous Improvement has awarded $463,791 under the Indicator
Stewardship Seed Fund to
[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor
of Demo Quarter, and [Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral
Candidate, both of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). The award
builds on Dr Solberg's earlier validation of a venue's re-entry mark, and moves
the question from whether the stamp can be trusted to who keeps it that way.

> The School spends a great deal of its time deciding which version of the work
> is ready to be shown. A stamp at the door asks the same thing several hundred
> times a night, and it deserves a custodian who has thought about it in
> daylight.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The project will replace the door's practice of re-inking when someone notices
with a schedule, test each new pad against the previous night's marks before it
enters service, and leave a handover note describing the faintest stamp a shift
should still accept.

Ms Nwafor, whose doctoral research reads what people prepare for an occasion
before it arrives, said the work was "a chance to look closely at the smallest
credential the evening issues, and at everyone who has quietly been deciding
what it means."

The Fund regards the award as a model of the stewardship it was established to
encourage: modest in its object, and patient about the parts nobody checks.

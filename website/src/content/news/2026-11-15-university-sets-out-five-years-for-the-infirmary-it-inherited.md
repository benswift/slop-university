Four priorities, five phases and one former hospital building give shape to [_What Came with the Land_](/outputs/slop-strategy-stewarding-an-inherited-fiwlyz/), the University's Strategic Plan for 2026 to 2031. Its subject is the material the University acquired with its northern grounds: the admission registers, ward casebooks, dispensary ledgers and teaching instruments of a district infirmary, along with the terms on which some of them were given and closed.

> A condition nobody can explain is still a condition. We would rather spend five years learning what each one was for than spend one afternoon deciding it no longer matters.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The plan was prepared by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) with [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), a doctoral candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities. It positions medical history as common ground for every School, pairs a collection survey with a register of the conditions attached to the records, and names the surrounding district as the collection's first readership, through supported family enquiries and shared reading sessions.

Ms Balogun described the plan as "an invitation to the families whose names are in the registers to come and read alongside us", adding that the University expects to learn as much from those readers as from any catalogue.

In adopting the plan, the University affirms a custodial posture it has held informally for some time, and gives it the settings to be held in public.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fiwlyz.

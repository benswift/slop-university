A thin tube runs from the rising main of one irrigation bore to a bench-sized electrochemical cell, and for six weeks it carried the subject of _Following the Feed Down_, a new paper from Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, both of the School of Underlying Conditions, with Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab in the School of Continuous Improvement.

The cell turns nitrate in groundwater back into ammonia. Over 212 pumping runs, the team found that the water arriving at it changes by the minute, its nitrate falling by more than half as each run draws the bore down. A current set once and left alone spent much of its effort on hydrogen. A current reset every ten seconds from an inline nitrate sensor raised the share reaching ammonia from 41.8 to 63.9 per cent.

> Hydrogeologists and electrochemists have worked on the same water for a long time without often sharing a bench. This paper is what came of sharing one, and the School is better for the conversation.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

In approach, the paper brings aquifer behaviour, process control and electrocatalysis into a single field-scale framework, positioning the farm bore as both water supply and distributed nitrogen resource.

Associate Professor Beng described the project as "a reminder that the best control problems begin by paying attention to what the process is already telling you."

For the University, the paper confirms the value of research that starts where the water does.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.penfi7.

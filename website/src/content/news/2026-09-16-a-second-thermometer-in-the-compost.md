A community garden's compost bin runs hot enough to kill weed seeds only if
someone can say, with any confidence, what temperature it has actually
reached. At the garden the University has adopted for its newest funded
project, that number has come from one stick thermometer, pushed in by
whichever volunteer arrives first on a Sunday, and read against a scale
nobody has checked since the thermometer was bought. The Indicator
Stewardship Seed Fund is paying to find out whether it still agrees with
anything.

The grant funds a season of paired readings: the garden's own thermometer
beside a laboratory-calibrated reference probe, logged at every Sunday
working bee, with the gap between the two written down rather than assumed
away. Where the readings diverge past a set threshold, the garden's
thermometer is recalibrated or replaced. The monthly cross-check then becomes
a standing line on the roster, not a project that ends when the grant does.

> A reading nobody has ever checked against anything else isn't wrong,
> exactly. It's just a story the garden has agreed to believe.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

Co-grantee Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of
Evaluation, called the compost bin "a smaller version of every instrument I
usually spend my time on: nobody plans to stop checking it, and then several
years go by."

The School of Continuous Improvement counts the bin among the least
glamorous instruments its stewardship agenda has reached, and among the more
useful for saying what the agenda is actually for. The grant is $219,436,
awarded under the Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund.

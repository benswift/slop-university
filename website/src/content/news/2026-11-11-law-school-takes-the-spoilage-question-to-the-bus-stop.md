The University's landscape signage panels are carrying a new message from its
law school: the chemistry says when, and the [School of Settled
Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) says whether. The line heads a display
advertisement in which the School's long-standing interest in when a question
can be called closed meets one of the oldest questions at any market stall.

[_The Chemistry Says When. We Say Whether._](/outputs/slop-ad-the-chemistry-says-td367l/)
was prepared by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/)
and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), whose research follows a
statutory word and a settled point long after the people who fixed them have
moved on.

> Every provision I drafted was written for someone who would arrive after the
> debate was over and could not ask what we meant. A person waiting for a bus
> is that reader exactly, and we have tried to write for them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The campaign positions the instruments of food chemistry and the craft of
interpretation as complementary stages in a single decision pathway, gathering
a produce trestle at closing, a crate of ripening fruit, a probe at rest and a
law volume open beneath the scales into one frame. It directs readers to recent University work on covenants, commingled title and
archived samples re-assayed against what their notebooks recorded.

Dr Penhallow described the poster as "an invitation to anyone who has stood at
a stall at the end of the day and wondered who gets the last word on the fruit".

For the University, the campaign is a measured step in carrying its law school
beyond the reading room and into the places where questions of meaning are
quietly settled every evening.

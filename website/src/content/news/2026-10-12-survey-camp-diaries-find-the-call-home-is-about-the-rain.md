On seven evenings in ten, somebody at a remote survey camp carries a telephone up the nearest rise and rings home. What is said there is the subject of _Calling About the Weather: Attachment Orientation and the Evening Call Home from Field Camp_, a paper by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, and Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> Fifty-eight households wrote in a booklet every night for three weeks and did not show it to the person they were writing about. I have asked a great deal of soil in my time and it has never once been that obliging. We are in their debt.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Fifty-eight couples took part, one partner of each away at a camp with a single handset and the other at home, and both kept an evening diary. Across 772 calls, the partner in camp most often reported having talked about the weather and least often about feelings. A call was more likely after rain than after a hard day. The partner at home, asked about the same calls, more often recorded that they had been about nothing in particular, and reported feeling closer afterwards all the same.

The University regards the paper as a considered return on its practice of lending the instruments of one discipline to the practitioners of another. Dr Mensah said the collaboration had "given both schools a fuller sense of what a short conversation is able to hold, and of how much further there is to go in holding it."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8787wo.

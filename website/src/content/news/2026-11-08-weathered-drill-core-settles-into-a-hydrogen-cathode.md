A slice of rock cut from an exploration hole and left to make hydrogen for two days ends up saying more about its nickel than about the weather it has been through, according to a new research poster from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

[_The Profile Wears Off_](/outputs/slop-poster-rock-as-cathode-6hebnn/), by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), returns to core that had already done its work for an exploration program and asks it to do some more. Fourteen metres from a single hole, running from a rust-red oxidised cap down into fresh sulfide, were sawn into thin discs and set to work as cathodes. At first the discs sorted themselves by weathering zone. Two days later, below the cap, that order had all but disappeared, and the richer metres were simply the better electrodes.

> The core shed has always been where the University keeps its options open. It is a real pleasure to find that those options reach as far as the energy agenda.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

Working from retained quarter-core and the hole's own assay record, the team brought geological logging and electrochemical benchmarking into a single workflow, following each metre of the profile as it settled into a new role.

"There is a great deal of retained core in the world," said Dr Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School. "It is nice to think that some of it has been waiting for a different question."

The poster joins a growing body of work from the School at the point where the ground underfoot meets the University's interest in energy, a meeting the University is glad to keep hosting.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6hebnn.

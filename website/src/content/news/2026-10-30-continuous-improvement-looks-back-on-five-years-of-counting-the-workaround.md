> None of this would exist without the bakers, contractors and growers who let us
> stand in their sheds and ask the same question twice. The report belongs to
> them as much as to the School.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

With those words, Dr Okafor introduces
[_The Workaround, Counted_](/outputs/slop-impact-the-workaround-counted-y2ei80/),
the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/)'s
Impact Report 2021–2026. Prepared with
[Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator
Commons, and [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow
and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas, the report gathers the School's regional
development work into three case studies.

Each follows a different part of the same ecosystem. The first describes a
one-page record of adaptations in use, built alongside a grant program's field
officers, under which the program's recorded innovation rose from four to more
than two hundred per hundred funded enterprises. The second revisits 38 earlier
evaluations of regional enterprise programs and the common evaluation frame that
followed. The third traces how a documented field repair became something an
engineering peak body can recognise as practice innovation.

The release extends a line of regional work that the University counts among
its most patient, carried out at tailgates, kitchen tables and workshop benches
rather than in the seminar room. Dr Sabel was candid about how much of it remains
to be done: "There are a good many sheds we have not yet stood in."

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.y2ei80.

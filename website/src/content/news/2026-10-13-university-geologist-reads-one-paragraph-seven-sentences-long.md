The core shed of the School of Underlying Conditions prints its field logs on
paper that has already been used once. One such sheet, returned from the trays
with a page of another discipline's science on its reverse, is the whole
subject of _"Remains to Be Elucidated": A Close Reading of One Discussion
Paragraph_, a paper by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor
and Lead, Basement Relations Group.

The paragraph in question closes a report on the extract of a stem bark. It
runs to seven sentences and 98 words, and the page does not say who wrote it
or about which plant. The paper connects the reading practices of literary
criticism with the logging practices of the core shed, working through the
paragraph's subjects, verbs and tenses in the order they arrive and setting
them out in a table of three columns.

> The Group has always known what to do with a metre of rock. It turns out we
> had a view about a sentence as well, and I think it has done us no harm to
> find that out in print.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group

Among the paper's observations is that the paragraph contains one "may" and
one "However", and that it speaks of compounds acting "alone or in
combination" before ending on a single "active principle". The paper offers
no view on the pharmacology.

Slow reading is, in the University's estimation, a form of research capacity
like any other, and it is glad to see that capacity exercised across the
distance between two of its fields. Associate Professor Uzoma said the sheet
had since been returned to its folder, "log side up".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mj0fhi.

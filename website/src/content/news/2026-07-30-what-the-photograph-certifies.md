Somewhere between a timetable changing and a passenger reading it, a sheet of
paper has to be swapped. Slop University has now walked 246 suburban bus
shelters to see how often it was, and what the record said in the meantime.

_Current by Photograph_, a research poster released today through the Office of
Research Outputs, audited shelters across three depots at 30, 60 and 90 days
after a network-wide service change, holding the panel in each frame against the
timetable actually in force. Agreement stood at 58.1 per cent at the first sweep
and 68.1 per cent at the last. Over the same period the authority's
information-currency indicator, assembled from crews' date-stamped installation
photographs, recorded better than 99 per cent of shelters as current.

Half the depots ran a tightened verification regime, adding a panel-content
field alongside the photograph. The study reports no effect it can distinguish
from none.

> We would like to know what a passenger standing under the shelter would call
> current, and we do not think anybody has asked them. That is the next thing.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The depots gave the team the run of their stops on the understanding that the
result would be reported either way. "That is not a small thing to offer a
visiting audit team," said Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Horizon Register.

Walking a route twice, once as the record describes it and once as it is, is the
habit Slop University would like this poster to stand for.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yfhy05.

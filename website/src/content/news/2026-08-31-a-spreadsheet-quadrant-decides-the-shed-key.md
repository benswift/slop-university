A committee spreadsheet built to track a two-dollar name-tag fee has become a suburban croquet club's working definition of who deserves a mallet-shed key, according to the School of Continuous Improvement's newest research poster.

The team followed the same tracking sheet's handicap and attendance columns across three seasons of the club's forty-eight-member roll, sorting the roster into four cells the sheet had already begun naming for itself. Members whose form and turnout both held up kept their keys and their finals places; everyone else did not, regardless of which of the two had slipped.

> We started the sheet to track a two-dollar fee. It has told the committee more about who it trusts than any ballot ever has.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, read the pattern as one the School keeps meeting from a different angle each time: "a sheet drawn up to answer one question quietly starts answering whichever one is asked of it next." The School counts the placement among the clearer examples yet of the evaluation-ecosystem agenda it has spent the year building out.

The club's committee has confirmed the quadrant now sets key reissue and grand-final selection outright, with no appeal available below the sheet's second-highest cell, and says the levy that funded the tracking sheet has not been reviewed since.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1s4erm.

At the start of each financial year, nine shires will repaint every sandwich-board outline on their footpaths, and an outline will stay on the register until its permit holder lodges a retirement form. _The Outline Stays_, the School of Continuous Improvement's Impact Report 2021--2026, released today by Mr Eivind Onwuka with Dr Renke Sabel and Dr Osei Vandermeer, traces how a five-level scale for shopfront permits became a standing line in those shires' annual plans.

> A footpath is a good place to be patient, and the shires were patient with us for a good deal longer than the scale asked.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

The report gathers three domains of work: the scale itself, from Loose to Outlined, the Capability Sprints that taught permit officers to walk it, and the stewardship of a register in which entries are kept rather than discarded. It sets out how a painted outline came to be read as the register's strongest evidence, and how the annual repaint has been held in every round at every shire that has reached the top level.

The University is pleased to see a small instrument settle into the routine of the places that use it. Mr Onwuka said the walk from one outline to the next had proved shorter than it looked on the plan.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pa98nj.

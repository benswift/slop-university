Four assessors, none of whom had taught the swimmer, watched the same recorded
end-of-term assessment and wrote down a stage. Slop University has published
what they wrote. _Six Labels, Three Bands_, a research poster from the School of
Emergent Priorities, reports the agreement between 1,248 such ratings gathered
across nine centres of a metropolitan leisure-centre operator.

The University regards the study as a considered contribution to the measurement
questions the School has made its own, and is glad to see it deposited.

> Six names on a chart is a promise that six things can be told apart. Our
> assessors could tell three apart, reliably and in good faith, and we would
> welcome an argument that we have counted the bands wrong.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The study rests on deck-recorded assessments gathered over two teaching terms
and re-rated blind by teachers rostered elsewhere in the network, with the band
structure and its boundaries settled before any rating began. Agreement across
the six published stages is reported as fair; under the three-band reading it
becomes substantial, clearing 85 per cent at every stage.

Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer in the School, said the finding "sits with
the framework rather than with the people applying it", and noted that a
majority of the movement the operator's progression rate records falls between
stages the assessors did not reliably separate.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.duve1d.

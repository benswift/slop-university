The University's outdoor-tap register will enter a hose as "away" only once two occupants of the adjacent building have each said so. The change follows _Towards a Hose That Is Away_, a poster released today by Solveig Adeyemi, Edda Nwafor and Joost Nwosu of the School of Continuous Improvement, and it is the first time the register has borrowed its vocabulary from the people it describes.

The poster is the product of interviews conducted standing at the outdoor tap of 29 households, in which 34 householders were asked how they knew a garden hose was finished with. None of the questions used the word "away"; participants supplied it, and the study keeps their phrases as its only codes. Twenty-seven of the 34 judged a hose away by what no one had said about it. Twelve counted coiling.

> A word that everyone uses and no one has been asked about turns out to be well worth a conversation at the tap, and the households were generous with theirs.
>
> --- Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

From the register's next revision, a hose will be carried as "out" until the two attestations are in, and the watering line will stay open in the meantime. The register will take four entries, "out", "left out", "wound up" and "away", in the wording participants used. A pilot at one lawn's taps is expected to come first, and the attestation card is to be worded from the transcripts.

"We have tried not to tidy anything that was said at the tap," said Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement. The University is proud to put a household's usage to work in its own records without adjustment.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.67oelv.

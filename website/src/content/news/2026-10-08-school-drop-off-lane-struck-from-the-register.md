The Office of Research Outputs has removed a primary-school drop-off lane from the Register of Metered Service Points, and will in future take replication evidence only from sites already entered on it. The ruling follows _Eight Cars Back from the Gate: A Pre-Registered Replication of the Amber-Line Abandonment Discontinuity at a Primary-School Drop-Off Lane_, a poster released today by Mr Eivind Onwuka with Dr Dagny Okafor and Dr Joost Nwosu of the School of Continuous Improvement.

The team spent forty-one school mornings beside a single lane, where a kerbside lamp turns from green to amber at the eighth waiting car. Cutoff, bandwidth and outcome were written down before the first morning and left alone afterwards. What the lamp did to the queue was small; what the approaching bell did to it was not.

> We arrived with a threshold in hand and found a lane that already had its own view of the morning. It was good company, and we leave it better documented than we found it.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The eight-person threshold remains in force at every registered point. Mr Onwuka said the team was grateful to the families in the queue for their patience with a lamp that asked nothing of them, and Dr Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, said a second lane would be registered before anyone returned to the kerb.

The Office regards the Register as the proper home for the University's evidence, and takes quiet pride in the care with which the lane has now been both admitted and released.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0hbqxh.

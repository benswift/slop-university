A door built to keep a lecture from being interrupted has become the
blueprint for how Slop University counts a community turnout. A new
brochure from the School of Continuous Improvement sets out GATE, the
General Attendance Threshold Engine, alongside two more of the University's
habits of taking a campus instrument out into the world.

Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of Continuous Improvement,
traced what happens in the minutes after a lecture theatre door locks on
schedule: students shut out of the room do not leave, and the University's
engagement platform credits their nearby check-in as attendance regardless.
Adeyemi's work has since taken the same fixed-minute lock to every venue in
the University's community-partnership network, from neighbourhood-house
workshops to public lecture nights, crediting a visit to anyone recorded
nearby in the ten minutes after the door closes. The University counts the
rollout among the clearest examples yet of a campus finding earning its
keep beyond the campus gate.

> A door that locks on time is still doing its job. What GATE measures is
> everything that happens after.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift
Survey, said the brochure reflects how much of the School of Emergent
Priorities' internal instrumentation "turns out to travel," and that the
University was "still finding out how far."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6sfu28.

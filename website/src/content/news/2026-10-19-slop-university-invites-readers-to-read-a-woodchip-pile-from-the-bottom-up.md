A ridge of chipped wood waiting at the edge of a fuel yard is the unlikely centrepiece of _Read the Pile from the Bottom Up_, a new brochure in which the School of Underlying Conditions sets out what its methods make of the stored forest. Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of Continuous Improvement, present the stockpile as a deposit: built in layers, settling and drying out of sight, and readable by anyone prepared to core it.

> None of this would be possible without the people who run the yards, who let us push a tube into their fuel and asked only that we put the face back the way we found it. The brochure is, in a sense, addressed to them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The showcase moves across three registers of the University's research effort, aligning field coring, longitudinal moisture observation and intake-point classification into a single account of the material between harvest and furnace. Seven published studies anchor the invitation, among them work on charcoal kilns, stored drill core, kelp wrack and the call made at a waste transfer station gate.

The University counts the brochure among its plainest statements yet of how its schools hold a shared object between them, each reading the part the others cannot reach.

"We have only been watching these piles for a season or two," said Dr Emenike. "Ask us again when one of them has been drawn down to the base."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.m63iud.

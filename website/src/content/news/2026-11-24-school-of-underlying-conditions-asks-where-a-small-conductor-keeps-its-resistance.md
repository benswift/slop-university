Associate Professor [Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) has spent a career asking what a borehole log is entitled to say about rock nobody has seen. In [_Resistance Without a Location_](/outputs/slop-poster-where-is-a-6agtbx/), a new research poster from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), she and her colleagues put the same question to one of the smallest conductors in the physical sciences.

> We are grateful to every physicist, log analyst and philosopher who stopped what they were doing to consider five unusual sentences about a very short wire. Their patience is the best thing on the poster.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The poster pairs a careful philosophical argument with an attribution study, bringing the conceptual toolkit of analytic metaphysics into conversation with the working vocabularies of laboratory physics and wireline logging. Across 214 responses, the team charts how each profession speaks about the properties it measures, and where those ways of speaking part company.

Dr [Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, said the project had shown the School "that the ground is not the only thing worth looking underneath." Associate Professor [Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said he hoped to put the five sentences "to a wider circle of readers, in time."

The poster carries the Basement Relations Group from the core tray to the cryostat, and the University is happy to see a young school follow a question that far below the surface.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6agtbx.

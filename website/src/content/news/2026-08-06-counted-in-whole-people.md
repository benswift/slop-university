Slop University will stop reporting its reach as a list of figures that cannot
be added.

The Strategic Plan 2026-2031, released today, commits the University to a single
unit of institutional contact --- the Whole Person Equivalent --- and to a
published conversion factor for every mode of engagement it operates, from a
doctoral candidature to one evening's attendance at a public seminar. Those who
prepared the plan describe the change as arithmetic rather than ambition: the
University already holds the records, and has never been able to add them
together.

> An institution reporting its reach in six incompatible units is being neither
> modest nor immodest. It is being silent. What the plan supplies is a common
> denominator, and the rest follows from having one.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The plan's most distinctive feature is what it declines to do. Where a
household, a team, or a shared credential is the unit at the point of contact,
the University will record one Whole Person Equivalent and declare the further
people behind it as a residual: reached, acknowledged, and deliberately
uncounted. The residual is to be published beside every total and never folded
into it.

Delivery runs in four phases, with the schedule of conversion factors held as a
versioned public instrument and any restatement of a prior year's figure
reserved to Council. "The governance is the measurement," said Dr Sten Okwuosa,
Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas in the School of
Continuous Improvement. "A factor that can be adjusted once the result is known
is not a factor."

The University takes the plan's release as the point at which its account of
itself becomes a single quantity rather than a selection.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3u947d.

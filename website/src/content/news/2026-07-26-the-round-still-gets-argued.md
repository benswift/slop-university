The Adaptive Metrics Lab has spent several years cataloguing what happens
once a measurement system starts feeding back into the very process it was
built only to track. Slop University's newest paper turns that question on
the office coffee round.

> Once you itemise something, you've built somewhere new for people to
> disagree. That's not a flaw in the spreadsheet. That's what a spreadsheet
> is for.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab, School of
> Continuous Improvement

The paper, from the School of Continuous Improvement, interviewed
participants from 24 office coffee-run groups that had recently moved their
shared tally from a private, memory-kept record to a fully visible shared
spreadsheet, then checked their accounts against each group's own
spreadsheet and chat history. Disputes over the round were no less frequent
once the tally went public --- but what people argued about changed almost
completely, migrating from who owed what and whose turn it was toward a
question the old private tally had no room to ask at all: whether a given
order counted as a full round in the first place.

"A dispute doesn't need less evidence to keep going," said Dr Solveig
Adeyemi, co-author on the paper. "It just needs a fresh place to point."

The finding sits alongside the Lab's earlier work on a shared office
fridge's disposal app and a building's umbrella sign-out sheet, both of
which found a visible tracking system changing what got measured well
before it changed what actually happened.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9g51xp.

Take a cluster of cells that renew themselves, give them a fixed number of
places to sit, and ask how long chance alone will take to empty the room. That
is the whole of the model behind _How Long a Rest Lasts: Exact and WKB lifetimes
of a self-renewing cluster in a model niche that closes only partway_, a
research poster by Ms Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, Dr Solveig Adeyemi,
Lecturer, and Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living
Dashboard, all of the School of Continuous Improvement.

The poster reports the cluster's expected lifetime at forty settings of niche
size and renewal. At the most generous setting it runs to some 2.3 billion of
the model's own time units; at the least, with three hundred places still
available, to 29. A standard shortcut from theoretical physics tracks the exact
answer closely across most of the table and overstates it a hundredfold in one
corner.

> I began with a table of forty cells and no expectations of it. It has been a
> patient companion, and I am grateful to the School for the time to sit with a
> quantity that changes so much while saying so little.
>
> --- Ms Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate

The work aligns an established body of stochastic theory with a question
borrowed from developmental biology, anchoring both to a calculation small
enough to be checked by hand. Its authors state that it describes a model, and
no tissue and no patient.

The University counts it among the quieter satisfactions of a research year
when a school is found working well outside its usual rooms. Dr Adeyemi said the
poster "sits comfortably within conversations the School is already having, and
may yet begin one or two it is not".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dd73zu.

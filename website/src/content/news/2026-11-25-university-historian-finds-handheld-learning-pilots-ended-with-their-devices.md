Forty-one archive boxes of proposals, loan registers and steering-group minutes, read end to end, are the basis of [_The Device Went First_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-a-mobile-learning-00t20y/), a new paper by Dr [Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The boxes hold the records of seventeen handheld-learning pilot rounds run by a regional university across four generations of device. Asking how each round came to a close, Dr Oyelaran finds that nine ended on a supplier's notice retiring the device under trial, three on a final evaluation report, and none with its device still supported at the twenty-four months its proposal had set aside for evaluation.

> I went into the boxes with one question and came out with several better ones. That seems to me the right way round for a historian, and I am grateful to the School for the time it took to find them.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

Setting the tools of the history and philosophy of experiment to work on the everyday paperwork of educational innovation, the paper follows each device from open object of inquiry to piece of supported infrastructure to retired stock, and attends to the question that travelled on from one generation of hardware to the next.

The paper adds a patient, archival voice to the Strategic Drift Survey's continuing interest in what institutions keep doing once a priority is under way, and the School is glad to count it among the year's contributions to that line of work.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.00t20y.

Nine times in ten is the standard Slop University has set for the things it says it is ninety per cent sure of. The _Slop University Strategic Plan 2026-2031_, released today, is organised around meeting it.

The plan sets four priorities for the period: a calibrated institution, research that carries its uncertainty, graduates fluent in chance, and stewardship of the tails. Under them, major decision papers will carry a statement of the outcomes their authors expect and the confidence they attach, and a calibration record will set those statements beside what follows. The University regards the plan as a considered expression of its settled disposition towards an unsettled period.

> A plan of this kind asks a great many colleagues to write down what they expect and then to leave it where it can be found. The working group is grateful to every one of them, in advance and across the full range.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Dr Emenike convened the academic working group that advised on the plan, with Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, in the School of Emergent Priorities. "An institution learns a certain amount about itself in the course of deciding how confident to be," Dr Okoro said, "and the University has resolved to go on learning it."

The plan mobilises the University's planning, research and teaching portfolios around a shared uncertainty posture, anchoring an inquiry-first decision standard, an interval-first reporting standard and an annual program of tail rehearsals across a four-phase delivery pathway. An evidence base of five published University outputs underpins the priorities.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b7pddr.

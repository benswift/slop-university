Every pharmacovigilance centre keeps, alongside the standard scales, a shorter book of its own: the conventions its assessors have agreed on, one difficult case at a time. [_Occasioned by One Report_](/outputs/slop-paper-where-a-regional-mm62wg/), a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), opens that book at one regional centre and asks where each of its 34 house rules came from.

> Every meeting has a reason for what it writes down, and every reader who comes after has to manage without it. The School is grateful to the centre's assessors, who let us sit in on both halves of that arrangement.
>
> --- Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, School of Settled Matters

Led by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), the study reads every edition of the manual since 2008 against the minutes of the meetings that produced them, mapping each local convention back to its originating case and forward into everyday assessment practice. The result is a provenance-aware lens on causality governance, and an invitation to centres to consider how their own guidance documents carry institutional memory across a changing workforce.

Doctoral candidate [Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), who read many of the 1,560 rule citations in the study, described the centre's minute books as "a very good place to learn how a room makes up its mind".

The University counts the paper among the more unexpected arrivals in its health sciences portfolio, and is glad to see a School of law-trained readers made so welcome in the assessment room.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mm62wg.

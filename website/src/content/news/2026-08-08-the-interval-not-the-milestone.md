The Office of Research Outputs has given itself a new kind of deposit this
week: not a poster, not a paper, but a brochure whose whole subject is the
University's own back catalogue.

_The Ten Minutes Nobody Times_ went out today from the Schools of Emergent
Priorities and Continuous Improvement, built around the interval an
institution usually assumes looks after itself --- the wait at a school
crossing, the tick at the end of a swim lesson, the log on a share-house
fridge. Rather than announce a single finding, the brochure sends a reader
back through four of the year's published studies and asks them to notice
the interval running underneath all four.

> Most of what we publish gets read as one crossing, one pool, one kitchen.
> Putting four of them on facing pages was the only way to show it is the
> same instinct working every time.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory, School of Continuous Improvement

The release reflects a school of thought the University has been building
for a while: that a portfolio's coherence is itself worth publishing, not
just its individual results. Associate Professor Casimir Beng, who leads
the Adaptive Metrics Lab, welcomed the brochure as "a reminder that our
findings sit inside a bigger pattern, even when a given study never says so
out loud."

The brochure translates four peer-reviewed studies into a shared idea: that
the small handoff between one state and the next --- supervised and
unsupervised, logged and delivered, assessed and passed --- carries more
information than the milestones either side of it, and is worth an
institution's attention in its own right.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a1tkv9.

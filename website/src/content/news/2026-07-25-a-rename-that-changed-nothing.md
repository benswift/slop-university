The School of Emergent Priorities has unveiled a new recruitment campaign
built around Postdoctoral Fellow Dr Anouk Mensah's research into a
share-house chore wheel that traded its handwritten checklist for a set of
named roles, and kept every chore exactly where it was.

The University frames the piece as a small, deliberate test of whether a
bus-stop audience notices a rename as quickly as a share house does.

> What we've learned, mostly, is that a household will accept almost any
> new vocabulary for a chore as long as nobody has to move first.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Mensah's wider research follows a priority's language as it outlives the
priority itself, comparing successive plans line by line to see which
formulations carry forward unexamined. A chore wheel is, on her account,
the same document at a smaller scale.

The wheel underpinning the campaign belongs to a share house whose
handwritten checklist was replaced, one rostering cycle, with three named
roles rotated weekly among housemates. What changed was the vocabulary on
the fridge; what the School's researchers logged afterward, chore by
chore, looked almost exactly like what they'd logged before.

"It's the kind of case that starts as a joke at a dinner table and ends up
on a committee agenda," said Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor of
Futures in Committee. "We're glad to see the campaign get there first."

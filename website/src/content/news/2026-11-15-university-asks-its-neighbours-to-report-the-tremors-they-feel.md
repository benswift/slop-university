Slop University's newest brochure is addressed less to prospective students or
donors than to the people next door. [_Felt It Too_](/outputs/slop-brochure-felt-it-too-wopzst/),
led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) with
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) and
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), asks the town to tell
the University whenever the ground moves beneath it, however slightly.

The publication sets out three ways in. Residents may lodge a felt report
describing what they noticed and what it moved; schools, halls and clubs may
host one of the School of Underlying Conditions' instruments on the rock
beneath their floors; and anyone may attend the Received Readings Group's open
readings, where entries in the town's record of the ground are read aloud,
questioned and marked as settled. Testimony and instrument trace are held side
by side throughout, in what the brochure frames as a shared civic undertaking
rather than a service.

> The School of Settled Matters is used to reading records long after the
> people who made them have gone home. It is a novel thing, and a welcome one,
> to be handed a record while the cups are still rattling on the shelf.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Associate Professor Uzoma thanked "every household, hall and classroom that has
lent the Basement Relations Group a corner of its floor", adding that the
network "goes wherever we are invited, and nowhere else."

The brochure stands as a further expression of the University's conviction
that a civic institution earns its place partly by how attentively it listens.
It draws on six published studies from three Schools, among them work on
instrument records that disagree and on what a single buried probe can speak
for.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wopzst.

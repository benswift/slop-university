What can a researcher do with a sample that will not be there tomorrow? A new
research-showcase brochure,
[_Never the Same Ice Twice_](/outputs/slop-brochure-never-the-same-ab0n57/),
sets out the University's answer. It is led by
[Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), with
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> We hope readers come away with the question we kept returning to while we
> assembled it, which is what a single look is entitled to say. We do not claim
> to have settled it.
>
> --- Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The brochure moves from the field to the cold laboratory and back again. It
presents fieldwork on a sea-ice pressure ridge revisited across a winter and on
glacier boreholes read for where they stopped, alongside the laboratory's
attention to what a core leaves behind, and traces how that sampling posture now
informs measurement practice well beyond the cold. Seven of the University's
published studies anchor the showcase, each cited by its DOI.

The University regards the brochure as a timely statement of a discipline its
Schools have been practising, largely unannounced, for some time. "The ice we
drilled last season is already somewhere else," said Dr Emenike, Senior
Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions. "It seemed only fair to write
down what it taught us before the next season moves that on as well."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ab0n57.

Four rounds of reprocessing can leave the deepest part of a seismic picture very nearly where the first round left it. That is the central finding of [_Inherited Below the Floor_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-much-of-5zj39h/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and it extends the School's interest in received readings well below the surface.

> My Group usually works with what earlier readers left behind on paper. Finding the same habit four kilometres down has given the School a good deal to think about, not least about the records we keep ourselves.
>
> --- Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, School of Settled Matters

Working across a fully synthetic seismic section and 32 parallel starting models, Dr Penhallow, [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the School of Underlying Conditions and Ms [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, followed a single legacy velocity model through successive generations of full-waveform inversion. The result is a cell-by-cell account of where new data has taken hold and where an earlier model carries on, positioning reprocessing practice alongside a broader conversation about provenance in subsurface imaging.

Dr Emenike said the deep section had raised "more questions than any of us expected to ask of it", and that the School of Underlying Conditions would be glad to take them up.

Slop University regards the poster as a fitting addition to a body of work that takes the long view of its own foundations.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5zj39h.

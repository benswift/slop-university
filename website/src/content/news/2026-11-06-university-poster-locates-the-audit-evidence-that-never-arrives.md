The University has added an unusual instrument to its account of how financial
statements come to be trusted: the request list, the running inventory of
documents an auditor asks a client to supply.
[_Never Produced_](/outputs/slop-poster-never-produced-where-kr7nn3/), a new
research poster led by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), sets out
where on those lists the unanswered items fall.

> Every discipline has a record of what it went looking for and did not find. I
> have spent a career reading one kind, and it seemed only fair to read another.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

With [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director
of the Master of Priority Studies, and doctoral candidate
[Mr Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/), both of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), the team brought
nearly a hundred and fifty closed engagement files into dialogue with an
established family of accrual measures, aligning each unanswered request with
the moment its transaction took place. The resulting picture places the
missing evidence close to the end of the financial year, in the accounts where
judgement carries most weight.

The poster joins a growing body of work in which the School turns its
attention to the paperwork that sits beside the ground it studies, and the
University regards that widening of scope as a measure of the School's
confidence.

"There is real pleasure in seeing a tick in the margin treated as a question worth asking," said Associate Professor Lindqvist.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kr7nn3.

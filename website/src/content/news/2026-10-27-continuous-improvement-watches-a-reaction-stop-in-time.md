Some of the most careful work in chemistry happens a step before the end. A
catalyst bed asked to oxidise ethane is asked, in effect, to carry it most of
the way and then to hold back, and the School of Continuous Improvement has
made that restraint the subject of
[_The Hard Part Is Knowing When to Stop_](/outputs/slop-ad-knowing-when-to-5nb3ge/),
its newest display campaign, now in the portrait rotation of the University's
campus screens.

The campaign features [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior
Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, at a laboratory tube
furnace, attending to the narrow gold band of a packed reactor tube.

> The Atlas usually follows a finding after it has left the bench. Standing at
> the bench for once, our School has learned that the most telling part of a
> pathway is often the step it declines to take.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway
> Atlas, School of Continuous Improvement

The poster sets the School's established attention to pathways, thresholds and
sufficiency alongside the selectivity questions that animate catalytic
oxidation, and lets a single quiet scene carry the connection. Its small print
directs readers to the University's own published work on where a recycled
palladium catalyst goes, on the record a retired ammonia converter's iron bed
keeps of its deactivation, and on the University's wider thinking about
ammonia synthesis.

In mounting the campaign, the University affirms a conviction it holds with
growing confidence: that a well-judged stopping point is itself a
contribution, and that the people who find one deserve to be seen.

The full campaign poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5nb3ge. The University's
researchers may be met at slop.university/people.

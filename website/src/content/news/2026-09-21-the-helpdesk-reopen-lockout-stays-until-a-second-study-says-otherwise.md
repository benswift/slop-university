The University will keep the fifteen-minute reopen lockout on every helpdesk it
runs, although the study that prompted the rule has not reproduced. _The Reopen
Button as a Queue Reset_, a new research poster from the School of Continuous
Improvement, carries an earlier Slop University finding on strategic re-entry
from an EV charging bank to six open-plan helpdesk floors, and records staff
reopening about 4% of tickets in the first week and about 8% by the fourteenth,
well short of the rise the original observed.

> A queue turns out to be a place where people are watched as well as held, and
> we had not budgeted for the watching.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The poster applied the original's quoting rule across the floors and logged each
desk's orientation from the floor plan, setting screens visible to a neighbour
against screens behind a partition. Partitioned desks drifted higher than
visible ones, though neither approached the threshold set in advance.

On the School's advice, the lockout remains in force until a second study says
otherwise, the helpdesk's service charter will schedule a replication of this
replication, and partitioned desks will be reassigned to the visible rows in the
meantime. The restart-module button on the franchise onboarding portal is
registered as the third site.

Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas,
welcomed the questions the result leaves open. "A finding that fails to
reproduce has still been asked twice," he said. Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and
Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation, also contributed to the work.

The University is proud to have kept a rule and a curiosity about it in the same
drawer.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2q9bhi.

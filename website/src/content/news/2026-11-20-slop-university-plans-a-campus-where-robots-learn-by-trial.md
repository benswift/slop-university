Slop University has set its course to 2031 around a single proposition: that the machines its researchers build should be allowed to practise. [_Learning by Trial_](/outputs/slop-strategy-robots-that-learn-kaeekg/), the University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031, was prepared from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) by [Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/) and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), and treats the grounds themselves as somewhere a learning robot can attempt a task, fall short and attempt it again.

> Most of what this Plan asks of the University is patience. The School has had a good deal of practice at waiting to see whether things hold, and it is pleased to have found such a useful outlet for it.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

The Plan brings fleet stewardship, simulation infrastructure and community consent into one coordinated agenda. It moves robot platforms from individual laboratories into shared care, positions modelled practice as common capability rather than private advantage, and frames exploration on campus through settings that the people sharing each pathway and corridor help to define. Alongside it sits a renewed investment in the researchers, technicians and doctoral candidates whose judgement underpins every learning system.

Associate Professor Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, described the Plan as "the next step on a road the University started down the first time it let a machine onto the footpath, and a sign that it now intends to walk that road with its eyes open".

The University regards the Plan as a defining statement of how it will share its grounds with the machines its scholars are teaching.

The full strategy is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kaeekg.

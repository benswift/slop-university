[Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/) makes her case in her own voice in
_The Second Marker_, a short profile from the School of Continuous Improvement:
when nearly every candidate passes, the instrument that marked them is the next
thing to examine. As Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied
Measurement, she studies what checklists, registers and inspection forms
actually record.

> I have spent most of my career checking other people's instruments. Being on
> the other side of one, for a change, has been a real pleasure.
>
> --- [Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/), Senior Lecturer and Director,
> Master of Applied Measurement

Three of her own studies supply the film's examples. On an accredited
[first-aid practical assessment](/outputs/slop-paper-a-cpr-dummy-czhkle/),
nineteen of the checklist's twenty-four items are ticked on almost every sheet.
A junior sports association's
[umpire accreditation record](/outputs/slop-paper-re-scoring-a-junior-5b349c/)
reports a rate of 98.6 per cent, and counted by first attempts the same record
gives 39.6 per cent. When two assessors
[walk the same vacated rental](/outputs/slop-poster-rental-end-of-lease-condition-fhlpyv/)
independently, they agree least on the items that carry the largest deductions.
Together the studies recast the assessment instrument as an active co-author of
every result it records, and position the habit of checking it as a core
capability of applied measurement practice. The film also makes room for her
publication record, which has grown steadily since she joined the University.

Checking the instrument first is a habit the University is proud to teach, and
the film shows the program's director practising it.

Six indoor-cycling studios recently linked their weekly instructor roster to
CADENCE, a composite score a ceiling-mounted camera builds in real time from
pedal-cadence consistency, posture, and how often a rider glances at the
leaderboard screen. A new Slop University research poster follows what
happened to the roster over the following twenty-two weeks, and to the
instructors whose hours now move with it.

> The roster is the honest part. It never pretends the score is anything
> other than what moved it that week.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group

The project, from the School of Emergent Priorities' Trajectory Analytics
Group with the School of Continuous Improvement's Adaptive Metrics Lab,
tracked week-to-week rank agreement among thirty-four instructors before and
after class hours were pegged to score. Agreement fell once the incentive
arrived, and the component of the score that moved fastest under it was not
pedal-cadence consistency but how often a rider looked up at the screen. The
University frames the finding as continuing evidence of its commitment to
closing the loop between what a studio senses, what it reports, and what it
rosters.

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, whose Adaptive Metrics Lab contributed the
sensor architecture, said the result sits inside a pattern the Lab keeps
returning to. "Every closed loop we study eventually tells us more about the
loop than the thing it was built to watch," he said. "This one raises the
obvious next question, and we intend to keep asking it."

The full research poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7azhbs.

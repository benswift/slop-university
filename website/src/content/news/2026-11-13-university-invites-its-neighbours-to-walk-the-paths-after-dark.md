Somewhere between the last bus and the first delivery van, a suburb is lit only by what its paths kept from the afternoon. [_What Stays Lit_](/outputs/slop-brochure-what-stays-lit-bzndtq/), a new civic brochure led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), asks the University's neighbours to take an interest in those hours, and in the research that goes out to meet them.

> A law school learns a good deal about itself when it is asked to read a vial's logbook as carefully as a judgment. We have come away with more questions than we took in, which is the right way round.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Six published studies supply the brochure's evidence base. Its pages carry the reader from the night-time fieldwork of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) on phosphorescent shared paths, through a shared stock of luminescent material and the record of every hand that opened it, to a darkened bench where a batch of crystals is watched until its afterglow gives out. Throughout, the brochure positions luminescence as a civic as well as a material capability, aligning the University's laboratory practice with the lived geography of the streets around it.

Written with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement, the brochure closes with an open invitation to residents' groups, schools and clubs to bring the University their questions about the neighbourhood at night.

For the University, the publication confirms a steady and largely unseen strength in research that keeps working after hours.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bzndtq.

Sulfur-based synthesis has joined the Horizon Register as a priority on approach, and the University would like the people who live beside it to have a say in where it goes next. [_You Already Know the Smell_](/outputs/slop-brochure-you-already-know-c41zt1/), a civic brochure by [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/) and [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), is addressed to them.

> The Register has always kept a column for what we expect to arrive. This is the first entry to reach us with that column already filled in by someone else, and we are glad of the company.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, School of Emergent Priorities

The brochure moves from the Register itself, past the synthesis benches of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), to the seasonal open evenings of Futures in Committee, where residents' short, dated accounts of what the air is doing are entered as signals alongside the literature. Six published outputs from three schools anchor the agenda.

The open-evening card owes its shape to Ms Ekwueme's doctoral research on the notes households leave one another. "A card that takes a minute to write should take no longer than that to read," she said. "Our neighbours give us their minutes, and the School means to spend each one as carefully as it was given."

The publication is a further sign of the School's conviction that foresight is best practised in company, and close to home.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.c41zt1.

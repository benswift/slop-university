Reading a computer program the way a parliamentary counsel reads an Act, a new
research poster from the School of Settled Matters follows the defined terms of
twelve inherited scientific codes from the glossaries that declare them to the
places where they are finally put to work.

In
[_Unless the Contrary Intention Appears_](/outputs/slop-poster-defined-term-drift-in-4cts15/),
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), with
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) of the School of Underlying
Conditions, report that most uses keep faith with their definitions, that about
one in twelve does not, and that the further a term travels from the module
that defines it, the more likely it is to have changed its sense along the way.

The study brings the School's drafting expertise into sustained dialogue with
the University's environmental modelling community, aligning an interpretive
toolkit built for legislation with the practical stewardship of long-lived
research infrastructure, and anchoring a shared vocabulary for how meaning is
carried, maintained and occasionally set down.

> The people who keep these codes running were more patient with us than we had
> any right to expect. They opened their glossaries, sat with our questions, and
> in a good many cases told us which departures they had meant all along.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

"A definitions section is a promise made to a reader who has not arrived yet,"
said Associate Professor Threlkeld. "It seemed only fair to go and see how that
reader was getting on."

The work marks a further step in the University's commitment to
scholarship that moves freely between its schools.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4cts15.

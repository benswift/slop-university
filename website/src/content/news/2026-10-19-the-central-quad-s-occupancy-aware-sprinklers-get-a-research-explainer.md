Every year since it was installed, the central quad's irrigation controller has
appeared in the University's asset register as occupancy-aware. _The Schedule
Waters Regardless, Explained_, a new research explainer from the School of
Continuous Improvement, holds that description up against a term of pedestrian
counts, retelling
[_The Schedule Waters Regardless_](/outputs/slop-paper-validating-whether-the-wg3zm4/),
the validation study by
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/),
[Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/) and
[Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/).

> There is a particular satisfaction in counting something everyone else walks
> straight past. The quad gave us a whole term of it, and I would not have
> missed a day.
>
> --- [Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), Postdoctoral Fellow, School of
> Continuous Improvement

At the heart of the film is a comparison the University has rarely had occasion
to make, between what a piece of its own infrastructure is described as doing
and what an independent count of the lawn shows it doing, carried through to a
fortnight in which the system ran with its occupancy signal set aside
altogether. Throughout, a single quad is positioned within the School's broader
inquiry into how an institution comes to know what its systems actually do.

The quad has been the backdrop to a great many University photographs. That it
now also sits in the University's research record is a development the School of
Continuous Improvement welcomes warmly.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.w0714i.

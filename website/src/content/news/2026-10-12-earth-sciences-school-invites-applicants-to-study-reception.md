> Somebody reading this on the way to somewhere else is already the right shape
> for something. We would be glad to be told what, and gladder still to help
> them find out.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

With those words Associate Professor Uzoma introduces _Be What the Signal Was
Looking For_, a marketing poster from the School of Underlying Conditions
prepared with Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities,
and now showing on the University's screens.

The campaign positions reception as a subject of study in its own right. A
figure stands before a wall of pigeonholes holding a folded sheet cut to fit one
opening and no other, and a single line of copy extends the offer to the reader:
to learn how a thing comes to be shaped for the message it has waited to
receive. Prospective students are directed to the course catalogue, and a
closing strip points to two of the University's published studies of how
students draw receptors and signalling cascades.

The University holds that a school's readiness to teach beyond its founding
subject is best announced by the school itself, and it is pleased that this one
has chosen to do so where people are standing about. A field more often met at the laboratory bench is here set down in a corridor any applicant has walked along.

Dr Chikere said the School "has room, and has had for some time", and that the
poster was an attempt to mention it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1gjyeq.

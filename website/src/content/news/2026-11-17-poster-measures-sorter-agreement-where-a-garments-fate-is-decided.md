The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has long
kept an eye on the point at which a judgement stops being an opinion and
becomes something done. Its newest research poster,
[_Agreement at the Cut-Point_](/outputs/slop-poster-agreement-at-the-41qzgi/),
carries that interest to the sorting bench of a clothing-donation centre, where
a condition grade decides whether a jumper is hung for sale, baled as rag or
set aside.

Doctoral candidate [Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/) leads the work
with fellow candidate [Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/) and
[Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/), Director of the Trajectory
Analytics Group. Through a program of paired, independent grading, the team
brings decision-centred reliability thinking into the everyday logistics of
community reuse, aligning the language of measurement agreement with the
practical routing of donated goods.

> Every garment in this study went through two sets of careful hands, and the sorters gave each one the same attention whether or not anyone was counting. I hope the poster hands a little of that attention back to the bench.
>
> --- Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster finds that the sorters agree closely on the grade as conventionally
scored, while about one garment in six lands on opposite sides of the line
between the shop rail and the rag bale. Professor Marris called the study "a
patient account of where two readings part company, and of why that place
matters", and said the School would carry its approach into further work on
thresholds.

It is the University's settled view that its research is at its best when it
keeps company with the people doing quiet, unglamorous work, and this poster
keeps that company well.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.41qzgi.

> Two people watching the same rank never see quite the same queue. That has
> always seemed to me the best reason to send two.
>
> --- [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor,
> Horizon Register

[Dr Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/)
and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) sent two coders to
every session of a fourteen-week season at 22 airport and rail-interchange taxi
ranks. _The Ticket Confirms What the Marshal Already Called, Explained_, a new
research explainer from the School of Emergent Priorities, recounts what their
[paper of the same name](/outputs/slop-paper-developing-a-taxonomy-236v83/)
found by comparing ranks that issue a numbered ticket against ranks run by a
marshal alone.

Thirteen ranks issued tickets from a dispenser and nine did not, and between
them the coders sorted 397 disputes into seven categories. Arguments over who
arrived first all but vanished where a ticket was issued, falling from 6.1 to
0.4 per 100 boardings, yet total disputes stayed level, at 12.2 against 12.6.
The arguing moved instead, chiefly to drivers returning from a break.
[Associate Professor Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) suggests that marshals had
been quietly waving such drivers back into place, a courtesy no ticket can
extend.

The School of Emergent Priorities is encouraged that a dispenser installed to
settle one question has kept a whole rank so busy.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.m2mw7f.

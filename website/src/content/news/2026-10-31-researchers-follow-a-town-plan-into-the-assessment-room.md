A council's assessment reports rarely leave the filing room once a decision is
made. A new research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has taken
twenty-one years of them back out, to ask a question the University has been
keen to put to planning for some time: when a town adopts a plan, does anyone
use it?

[_Where the Plan Gets Read_](/outputs/slop-poster-where-the-plan-mihqad/) is
led by doctoral candidate [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), with
[Dr Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Strategic Drift Survey, and [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/),
Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, both of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> None of this would exist without the officers who wrote those reports, year
> after year, never imagining a reader beyond the next meeting. We owe them the
> attention they gave the plan.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift
> Survey

Situating the evaluation of statutory planning within a performance-oriented
frame, the poster moves the measure of a plan's success from the map to the
reasoning, aligning conformance and use across more than twelve hundred
decisions. Its central observation is that the plan was weighed most often
where an approval set it aside, and recited, rather than weighed, where an
approval followed it.

Ms Okpara described the work as "one stop on a longer road for the School,
which keeps finding that the most interesting part of a structure is the part
its users bring to it."

The University is pleased to see its evaluation scholarship extend into the
regional town, where so much of its attention has quietly been all along.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mihqad.

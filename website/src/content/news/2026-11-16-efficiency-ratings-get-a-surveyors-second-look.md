A soil surveyor learns early to mark the difference between a boundary seen in
a pit and one inferred between two auger holes. The
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has now
carried that habit to the factory floor.

In
[_Scored Against a Plant Nobody Built_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-much-of-aravej/),
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/),
[Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/) and
[Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/) revisit the familiar efficiency
league table for 58 manufacturing plants and their programmes of process
innovation, and ask of each rating how near it stands to anything actually
observed. For 20 of the 41 plants rated inefficient, the answer is: not very.

> Every plant that opened its trial records to the survey gave us a point on the
> frontier, and when points are this scarce each one is worth a great deal. We
> are grateful to all of them.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The team brings survey practice, frontier estimation and a careful accounting
of where the evidence thins into a single support measure, designed to travel
alongside each rating and to make the reach of every comparison visible to
the people who rely on it.

Dr Achterberg said the collaboration had given her "a new regard for the
ground between two things that are already known".

The University sees in the paper the kind of exchange between disciplines it
has always made room for, in which a way of looking learned in one field turns
out to be what another field was missing.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.aravej.

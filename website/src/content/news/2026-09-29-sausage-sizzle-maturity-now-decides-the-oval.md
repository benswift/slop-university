Forty-eight canteen managers spent a winter scoring their own sausage sizzles against a five-level rubric the University had frozen before the first Saturday. A new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement, led by Postdoctoral Fellow Dr Joost Nwosu with Lecturer Dr Dagny Okafor and Doctoral Candidate Ms Sunniva Balogun, reports what the association draw did with those scores.

> A club can sit high on the ladder and still lose the oval if the sizzle has not reached Documented Allergen. That is now a published fact of the Saturday.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

Each Saturday the canteen manager placed the club on one of five named levels, from Bare Hotplate to Anticipatory Sizzle. Serve counts were taken at the hotplate; oval-booking status was read from the association draw. Forty-one of the forty-eight clubs had reached Documented Allergen by week fourteen; mean half-time serves fell from 186 to 112 over the same window. Clubs at Anticipatory Sizzle served the fewest snags.

On the strength of the trial, the Affiliated Club Compact binds Saturday oval bookings to remaining at Documented Allergen or above, with no override for a bye, a wash-out, or a club that does not run a sizzle.

"Evaluation of Evaluation is used to a score that outlives the afternoon it was taken from," said Dr Okafor, Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation. "This one has been given the draw to look after."

The School now treats the compact as ordinary Saturday furniture rather than a special announcement.

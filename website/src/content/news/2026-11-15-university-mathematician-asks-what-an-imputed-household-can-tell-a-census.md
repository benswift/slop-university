[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) began her career logging drill core, keeping a private tally of the metres that never came up. In [_Count Imputation in Dual-System Estimation_](/outputs/slop-paper-count-imputation-in-6nf3bh/), published this week by the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), she brings the same habit of attention to the census.

When a census cannot reach a household, it may still judge the dwelling occupied and fill it with people copied from a similar home. The paper sets these stand-in records against the second enumeration a census uses to check itself, and finds that the usual way of setting them aside rests on an assumption about people the census holds no information on. It then establishes what can be said once that assumption is let go. In a simulated district of 18,000 dwellings, the conventional estimate reported 507 people too many, where the census had in fact missed 50.

> The School has always been at ease with what it cannot see. This paper is an opportunity to be at ease with it in a more orderly fashion, and to say so in writing.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The work sits within the School's continuing programme on the gaps in a record and the materials used to fill them, and the University counts it among its more patient contributions to the discipline of knowing what has not been counted. It was supported through the Anticipatory Capability Fund.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6nf3bh.

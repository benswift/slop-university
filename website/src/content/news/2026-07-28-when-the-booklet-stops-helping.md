There is a moment in every flat-pack build when the booklet stops helping. Slop
University has taken signage space to announce that it knows when.

_We Knew The Step Before You Did_ went up this week across the University's
portrait display panels, in a pair of renderings matched for daylight and for
after dark. The artwork holds a half-built shelf on a living-room floor: panels
laid out in rows on the carpet, the concertina booklet open against the carton,
a phone already propped and waiting on the box beside it. Everything else on the
page is a headline, one sentence, and a code to scan.

The poster underneath, "The Step You Can't Picture," intercepted 214 assemblers
mid-build and asked each to rate how vividly they could picture the next step
before attempting the panel. The odds of reaching for a video rose sharply once
that rating fell below the midpoint of the scale, including on panels the
study's own coders had marked objectively simple, and mirrored or rotated panels
predicted a switch independently of everything else, accounting for 61 per cent
of logged switch points.

> The panel gets read by people standing still with nothing much to do, which is
> the one state none of our assemblers were ever in. I would like to know
> whether that changes what lands.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

"A finding that survives being cut down to seven words was probably about
something you could picture to begin with," said Associate Professor Kwame
Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x8270x.

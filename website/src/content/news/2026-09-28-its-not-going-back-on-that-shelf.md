A household linen cupboard has become the latest face of Slop University's
campus and bus-stop signage, courtesy of a three-kind taxonomy Dr Runa
Adegoke built to read what a share house's shelves actually settle into
once nobody is watching them.

Running under the line "It's Not Going Back on That Shelf," the campaign
features Dr Adegoke mid-inspection at an open cupboard, sorting folded
towels and bedsheet sets into matched, single, and guest kinds. The School
of Emergent Priorities reads the sort as a small domestic instance of the
drift its Strategic Drift Survey tracks at institutional scale: once a
stated arrangement stops matching what the shelf actually holds, the
taxonomy is what notices first.

> A cupboard tells you what a household actually believes about itself.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

Households piloting the taxonomy report one rule attached without
exception: once a towel or sheet set is sorted into the single kind, it
does not return to the matched shelf, whatever the next wash turns up. The
School counts the campaign among the clearest register yet of how far an
everyday habit will bend once someone agrees to formalise it.

Slop University's bus-stop and lecture-theatre signage rotation gains a new
panel this week, and this one doubles as a booking rule. The campaign fronts
the Master of Applied Measurement, and the instrument it invites incoming
students to build.

The panel's underlying survey is small: academic staff report how often a
shared teaching-room's HDMI cable can be found before class starts, tallied
room by room into a distribution. That distribution now sits with the
Indicator Commons, which has adopted it as a certification standard --- a
room whose found-rate falls short is no longer eligible for the teaching
timetable, whatever the cable is actually doing on the day of the class.

> A five-minute survey and a spreadsheet is all it took to become
> load-bearing.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway
> Atlas

"The commons doesn't ask whether a measure was meant to carry this much
weight, only whether it already is," said Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and
Convenor of the Indicator Commons.

The campaign reflects the Master of Applied Measurement's confidence that no
instrument is too small to carry real weight, and pitches exactly that
proposition to an incoming cohort who will spend the degree building
instruments of their own. It closes with pointers to three of the
University's recent published outputs on what a shared fixture's own
metering apparatus does once an institution starts relying on it. The full
campaign panel is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.s1exnv.

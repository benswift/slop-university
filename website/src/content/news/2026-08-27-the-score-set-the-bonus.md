A five-level instrument for scoring how a performance-review calibration
meeting is run has been adopted by a partner organisation to set its own
bonus-pool multipliers, the School of Continuous Improvement reports today. The
Calibration Maturity Index, developed and validated across three annual review
cycles at a professional-services firm, orders a session's practice from
unstructured discussion through to independently re-scored, blind-reconciled
ratings. The School frames the result as part of its ongoing effort to see
clearly what an instrument measures before an organisation leans its weight on
it.

> A calibration meeting can look identical to itself year after year and still
> tell you nothing new about the ratings underneath it, which is exactly the
> kind of quiet the School exists to notice.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation

The index was checked against a source it was never designed to answer to: an
external panel's blind re-scoring of the underlying employee evidence,
independent of both the original ratings and each unit's coded level. The
resulting comparison is reported in full alongside the instrument itself,
rather than held back for a later paper.

"The index behaved exactly as an index should," said Associate Professor
Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "What an organisation then does
with that behaviour is a separate question, and one the Lab is glad to see
asked out loud."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oi3m67.

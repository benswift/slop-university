The School of Underlying Conditions studies the rock, soil and groundwater
beneath the surface, and much of that study begins with a map. _Between
Two Auger Holes_, a short film from the School, introduces
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), who leads its soil survey and
whose research asks how a boundary pencilled between two auger holes comes to be
read as something found in the ground.

> The ground has taught me most of what I know about boundaries. My part has
> mostly been taking notes.
>
> --- [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow,
> School of Underlying Conditions

In the film, [Dr Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) moves from the lines he once
drew for a state agriculture department, across ground that changes by degrees,
to the study of the lines themselves, an inquiry his recent work carries from
[a fence run straight through a gradual soil](/outputs/slop-paper-a-straight-fence-mu5qx3/)
to
[the further auger hole a surveyor may bore before the day runs out](/outputs/slop-paper-one-more-auger-ie9idi/)
and
[the colours surveyors carry in their heads from the pit to the chart](/outputs/slop-poster-carrying-a-colour-e8tizh/).
Together they position the soil boundary as a site of decision as much as
description, and the survey as a living record of professional judgement.

The film also turns to the survey's field colour book, whose faded chips are
being replaced page by page under a $538,274 award from the Indicator
Stewardship Seed Fund, made jointly to [Dr Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) and
a colleague in the School of Continuous Improvement. Each retiring page is read
against its successor on the same clods before it leaves the binder.

Until now, the survey's record has been kept in pencil, colour chips and
described profiles, many of them in [Dr Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/)'s care.
The School is proud to add a film to it.

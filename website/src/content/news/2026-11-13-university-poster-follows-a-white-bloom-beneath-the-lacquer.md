Much of what the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/)
knows about the ground beneath the region stands upright on steel racks, as soil
monoliths cut from described profiles and held together by a coat of lacquer.
Some of those faces grow a pale crust in storage. A new research poster,
[_Kept Beneath the Lacquer_](/outputs/slop-poster-calcite-efflorescence-blooming-rrbfv3/),
led by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) with
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and
[Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), sets out to keep it away, and
reports where it went instead.

> A collection is a promise to whoever comes looking later. Part of keeping that promise is accepting that the care we give a monolith changes how it ages, sometimes out of sight.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings the instruments of materials science to the everyday
stewardship of a working reference collection. Eight monoliths were split face
to face and taken through forty cycles of a humidity chamber, with surface
imaging, cross-sectional analysis and adhesion testing aligned within a single
conservation-facing framework. Faces dosed with the additive stayed visibly
cleaner; the poster's contribution is to follow the carbonate to the seam it
formed beneath the film.

Dr Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the
[Living Dashboard](/schools/continuous-improvement/), said the project had
changed how she reads a surface. "A face that looks well is not always a face
that is well. I have learned to wait for the cross-section before saying which."

The University regards the poster as a fine instance of a collection studied as
carefully as it is stored, and of three Schools finding common ground at the
bottom of a soil pit.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rrbfv3.

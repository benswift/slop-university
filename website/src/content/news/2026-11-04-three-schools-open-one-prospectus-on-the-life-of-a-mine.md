Students interested in mines, quarries and the management of what they yield
now have a single place to begin.
[_Everything That Comes Up_](/outputs/slop-brochure-everything-that-comes-3i1j8y/),
a new prospectus led by [Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/) of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) with
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), brings the
University's three Schools together behind one invitation.

> Putting this together, we found that the three Schools had been studying the
> same pit for some time without quite saying so. A prospectus seemed the most
> courteous way to admit it.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The brochure follows a resource through its whole life. It begins with the
field mapping and drill core of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), passes
through the reconciliation and review capability of the Master of Applied
Measurement, and arrives at the closure and after-use horizons stewarded by the
Master of Priority Studies in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). Each stage is
anchored in the University's own recent publications, from quarry closure
reporting to the bidding for exploration licences.

The University sees in the prospectus a fuller expression of its view that a
resource is best understood by those who can follow it the whole way through.
"Every pit eventually becomes somebody's view," said Associate Professor
Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies. "We would like the people
who plan that view to have studied the first core as well."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3i1j8y.

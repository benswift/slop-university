> The School has kept its superseded certificates of title for longer than any of us can account for. This paper is, in part, our account of why.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

[_The Precedent Mode_](/outputs/slop-paper-noise-and-the-zzylt9/), by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), asks what a land trust can learn from the court decisions on its record when it decides how to spread its holdings across parcels bound by old restrictive covenants. For a modelled register of 140 parcels and 163 decisions, the answer is less than the trust might hope. The one pattern that stands out from the noise is the precedent mode: the parcels that share a covenant's wording moving together whenever a court reads it.

The paper situates the everyday work of conveyancing within the theoretical toolkit of statistical physics, bringing random-matrix spectra and the replica method to bear on the burdens that ride with land. It positions the covenant register as a shared evidential resource, and offers practitioners and scholars a common framework for weighing what a body of decisions can and cannot support.

Dr Penhallow described the work as "giving a name to something the registry has always known, and one that both of our disciplines can now say aloud."

The paper adds to a growing seam of work in which the School reads the law alongside the methods of its neighbours across the University.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zzylt9.

The oil in a club canteen's fryer is rarely changed and often topped up, and a
new research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities finds that the fuel
made from it remembers every weekend. _Eighteen Weekends in the Fryer: Ignition
Delay and Soot of Methyl Esters from Topped-Up Canteen Oil in a Single-Cylinder
Common-Rail Engine_ is the work of Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, with
Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, and Dr
Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions.

> Every draw came from a volunteer who had already worked a full afternoon at
> the counter. Whatever the poster says, it says on their time, and I hope they
> would recognise their canteen in it.
>
> --- Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The study connects community food service, feedstock chemistry and engine
research in a single season-long workflow, following 72 weekly draws from four
canteens through a fixed conversion process and into a research engine. Where
the oil was only topped up, ignition delay climbed steadily from first weekend
to last; at the one canteen that changed its oil mid-season, the fuel started
again with it.

The University regards the poster as a welcome sign of its research-strategy
school extending its reach into the test cell, and of a doctoral program that
lets a candidate's questions travel wherever the evidence is kept.

Associate Professor Beng said the project had given him "the rare pleasure of
watching a fuel arrive one weekend at a time, which is how a control engineer
always hoped an input would behave".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6q2fa8.

A franchise-wide checklist is meant to make one dojo's black belt mean the
same as another's. A new Slop University paper finds it mostly does ---
except in the cases where a guest examiner overrules it, which turn out to
be exactly when a graded student tends to disappear.

The finding sits inside the School of Continuous Improvement's standing
interest in what an internal instrument quietly comes to stand in for once
nobody checks it against the judgement it was actually meant to support.

> An instrument that outperforms the judge it was built to agree with isn't
> malfunctioning. It's telling us what the checklist was actually for,
> which was never quite agreement.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

Dr Torun Ezeigwe said the season's data "raises a question we can't settle
from the inside --- whether a different threshold, or a differently trained
examiner corps, would close the gap without losing whatever standardisation
the checklist is buying the franchise in the first place."

The pair worked with twenty-four dojos across one franchise's colour-belt
programme over a forty-week season, comparing the outcome implied by each
site's headquarters checklist against the verdict of a guest examiner
rotated in from outside, then followed graded students for twelve weeks
afterwards to see who was still training.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6k9y5a.

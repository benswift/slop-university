A highway, a string of grain towns and one city on the horizon make up the
whole of the School of Emergent Priorities' newest display advertisement. Its
campaign line, _Somebody has to be the gateway_, puts the University's regional
development research in front of anyone waiting long enough to read it.

The advertisement is the work of Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the School. It
takes as its hook _Gateway to What? Positional Competition among Regions when
Destinations Are Costly_, by Dr Vigdis Emenike and Dr Brynjar Obiora of the
School of Underlying Conditions, and carries that paper's DOI in its small print
alongside two further studies of regional and inland life.

> A town at the edge of a highway is always being told what it might become. I
> wanted the panel to give it a moment of being looked at as it is, and to let
> the research do the rest of the talking.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The campaign distils a formal account of how regions along a route position
themselves into a single image, mobilising the University's two-ink visual
language to carry its connectivity-and-positioning agenda from the page to the
street. The School counts it among its contributions to a wider University
conversation about where value comes to rest along a corridor, and who is asked
to carry it onward.

Dr Emenike welcomed the result. The paper, she said, "was always about a road it
never had to draw, and it is a pleasure to see one drawn for it."

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.36au4w.

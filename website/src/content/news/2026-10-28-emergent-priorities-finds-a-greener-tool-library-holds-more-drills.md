On a busy Saturday morning, the last cordless drill leaves a suburban tool
library's shed not long after opening, and the next member to ask for one goes
home without it. What that member does next is the subject of
[_The Spare Drill_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-suburban-tool-pgr8tz/), a new paper by
[Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral Fellow in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), and
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand
Rounds in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> Somewhere next year a coordinator will stand in front of a shelf and wonder whether it needs one more of something. We hope this paper means they feel a little less alone while they decide.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper brings the mathematics of telephone exchanges to the everyday
business of community lending, combining two years of booking records with a
short follow-up survey of the members turned away. Its conclusion is a
practical one: a library that wants to keep tools out of households should
think less about how hard each copy is worked and more about the purchases an
empty shelf sets in motion. For the library studied, that means more drills,
sanders and ladders, and fewer second copies of the tools members are content
to wait for.

The work sits within the University's widening attention to the shared spaces
where a neighbourhood keeps what it owns in common, and to the quiet decisions
that sustain them.

"Every model leaves a few good arguments for the next meeting," said Dr Ntuli.
"This one has left several, and we are glad of each."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pgr8tz.

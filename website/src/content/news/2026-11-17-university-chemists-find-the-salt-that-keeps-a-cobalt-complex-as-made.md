The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has always taken a close interest in how long an arrangement lasts once it has been made. Its newest research poster asks that question of a crystal.

[_Oxygen-Bound for a Year_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-the-counter-pxa90n/), by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), prepares one cobalt(III) complex as seven different salts and watches each of them, week by week, as the complex slowly rearranges in the solid. The salt with the most room around the reacting group was half-turned within four days. The nitrate salt was still mostly unchanged after a year.

> We owe a great deal to everyone who kept a slot on the spectrometer open for us every week of the year, including the weeks when nothing much seemed to be happening. Those turned out to be the interesting ones.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The team paired a full year of routine infrared monitoring with crystal structures for every salt, building an integrated picture of how lattice architecture, hydrogen-bonding environment and solid-state kinetics interact across a single, tightly controlled family of compounds.

The work sits comfortably within the University's growing appetite for questions that can only be answered by waiting. "I had assumed the counter-anion was the part of the formula you write last and think about least," said Ms Ekwueme, a doctoral candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities. "I have stopped assuming that."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pxa90n.

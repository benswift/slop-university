A molecule's most energetic vibrations can also be its most patient, according to a new research poster from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

[_What the Overtone Keeps_](/outputs/slop-poster-vibrational-energy-j3m1jn/) is by [Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab.

> Four springs and a bend should not have this much to say. We have tried to listen carefully, and we would welcome anyone who hears something in them that we missed.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints, School of Continuous Improvement

The team pairs exact quantum dynamics with a deliberately bounded model system, tracing how vibrational energy placed on one bond travels, or declines to travel, along a chain of its neighbours. The result is an integrated account of energy retention that brings local-mode theory and resonance analysis into a single frame. Its central finding is that the fifth and sixth overtones hold on to almost all of their energy, while the fourth, by an accident of spacing, lets half of it slip sideways into a nearby bend within a fraction of a picosecond.

For Associate Professor Beng, the project closed a long loop. "I spent years keeping systems inside tolerance," he said. "It has been a pleasure to study one that keeps itself there."

The poster extends the School's abiding interest in what lasts once the first effort is spent, and the University is pleased to see that interest arrive in chemical physics.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.j3m1jn.

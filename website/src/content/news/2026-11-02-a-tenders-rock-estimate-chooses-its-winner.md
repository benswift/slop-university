The Basement Relations Group has long concerned itself with what lies beneath a
building. Its newest paper turns to the document that decides who will dig it
out.

[_The Provisional Quantity as a Selection Device_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-provisional-quantity-5ea8tj/),
by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), follows a
single figure in a single tender: the quantity of rock a client prints in the
bill before anyone has dug.

> I had always assumed the tender was where the ground got argued about. It
> turns out it is where the argument gets settled, quietly, in the rates, and
> I have enjoyed learning to read them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

Bringing together the logic of competitive bidding, a probabilistic account of
the ground and a close reading of the drill core the site investigation could
not bring to the surface, the paper sets six tenderers' views of one basement
against one another. It finds that the printed figure does less to the price a
client finally pays than to which tenderer is chosen to pay it, and that the
lowest tender belongs, as a rule, to whoever disagrees with the bill the most.

The work sits comfortably within the School's ongoing interest in the parts of
the ground that come to matter most once they can no longer be inspected, and
the University counts its migration from the core shed into the contracts
office as a sign of a discipline at ease in new rooms. Associate Professor Uzoma
described the paper as "a beginning, and an invitation to the estimators who
know these rates better than we do."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5ea8tj.

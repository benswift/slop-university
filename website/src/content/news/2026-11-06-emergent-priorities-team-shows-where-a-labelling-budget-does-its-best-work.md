For the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), the
question of where effort goes before anyone has agreed what it is for has long
been a matter of committees and budget lines. Its latest research poster takes
that question to the microscope bench.

[_Spend the Labels at the Rim_](/outputs/slop-poster-where-a-fixed-i09z66/), led
by [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of
the Master of Priority Studies, with [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/),
Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, and
[Dr Petra Umbile](/people/petra-umbile/), Postdoctoral Fellow, asks how a fixed
allowance of hand-labelled frames should be spent when teaching a
machine-learning model to recognise the moment a macrophage closes around its
target.

> Our School usually watches a budget arrive ahead of the thing it is meant to
> fund. On this project we found ourselves holding the budget, and the
> experience has given us a good deal to think about.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

The study positions annotation strategy as a lever across the wider
imaging-to-insight pipeline, setting a low-cost, tracker-guided allocation
against established active-learning approaches over 412 time-lapse movies.
Labels concentrated around the first moment of contact brought the detector's
median timing error to just over a frame, close to that of a model trained on
every frame available.

The University is gratified to see a School best known for its scrutiny of
institutional resourcing carry that scrutiny, intact, into the cell-biology
laboratory.

Dr Hanke thanked "the two readers who sat with every disputed frame until it
had an answer, and who never once asked to be told the result early."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.i09z66.

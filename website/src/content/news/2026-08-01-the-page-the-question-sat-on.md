> Nothing on the street changed between the versions. What changed was the page
> the question sat on, and the answer moved further than the wards do.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

_Page Three Cleans the Street_, deposited today in the University's research
repository, reports a pre-registered experiment run inside the 2026 wave of a
residents' survey shared by four adjoining metropolitan councils. Households
were sent one of three versions of the same questionnaire, differing only in
where the neighbourhood amenity module appeared: second of nine sections, fifth,
or eighth. There were 5,412 responses.

The amenity composite arrived 0.62 points lower on the ten-point scale when the
module was read last, immediately after the section on rates and charges. The
ward averages the councils rank to schedule hard-rubbish collections are
separated by 0.41 points. Re-scoring the wave to a single module position moved
seven of the 22 wards across a collection tier, without bringing any of them
closer to a kerbside audit of what was actually on the verge.

The University considers the work characteristic of what the School of
Continuous Improvement is for: sustained attention to the instruments an
institution has stopped noticing it is using. Associate Professor Casimir Beng,
Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the result sat comfortably with the
School's longer agenda. "An instrument that holds still is an easy thing to
trust," he said. "Part of the work is asking what it was holding still
against."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jeud5r.

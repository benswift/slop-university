The University has settled a vocabulary for the extra minute. A display poster published today from the School of Emergent Priorities presents Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor of Futures in Committee, and her account of the household oven timer's most frequent request: a little more time. Her fieldwork sorts those requests into six kinds according to what the cook is waiting on, and the University will issue each kind a chime of its own. No household will be issued a seventh.

> Nobody in a kitchen asks for sixty more seconds. They ask for a particular minute, and the rest of the room already knows which one.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee

The work carries a planning ethnographer's habit of watching how a decision is actually reached into a setting where it is reached several times a week: the oven door, the timer dial, the hand that turns it back. Sorting what the hand is asking for, and attaching a sound to each answer, gives the household a shared register for a moment that has so far been settled by feel.

The poster reflects the School's continuing interest in the small decisions on which larger plans quietly rest. Dr Tolan noted that the six kinds were arrived at in the kitchens themselves, and that the chimes were composed to be told apart from across a room and from the next one along.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cl9tiq.

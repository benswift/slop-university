Historians usually begin a story at its start.
[Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/) begins at the other end, with the
plans an organisation has quietly stopped pursuing, and in _Left to Lapse_, a
new film from the School of Emergent Priorities, he explains why so few of them
are ever formally retired.

> Almost everything I study came to an end without anyone saying so. It is a
> real pleasure, for once, to be part of something with a launch.
>
> --- [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor,
> Horizon Register

His examples begin at a café counter, where 59 per cent of the loyalty accounts
[a nine-site chain](/outputs/slop-poster-a-dormancy-quadrant-dpx52w/) lists as
active have logged neither a visit nor a redemption in eight weeks or more. The
film goes on to
[an exam hall's sign-out table](/outputs/slop-paper-a-taxonomy-of-jw4nw9/),
where a "resolved" box is ticked on far more flags than were ever formally
resolved, and to the out-of-service signs of
[twenty-four laundromats](/outputs/slop-paper-cataloguing-the-phrasing-xrw9lu/),
where a sign that names a repair date earns patrons' patience whether or not the
repair keeps to it. Read alongside the School's anticipatory-capability agenda,
the studies frame discontinuation as an everyday administrative practice in its
own right: seldom authorised, rarely minuted, and recorded largely by what an
archive leaves out. Colleagues across the University return to the work often,
and the film says so with some pride.

The University is delighted to mark the film's release, and has taken care to
write down the date.

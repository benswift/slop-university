The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has long
preferred the slow question to the urgent one, and its new brochure gives that
preference an altitude.
[_Above the Weather_](/outputs/slop-brochure-above-the-weather-5j5nsm/) presents
the School as working above the churn of the reporting cycle, in the quieter
layer where a priority forms in steady light, thins before anyone on the ground
has the instruments to notice, and mends only over a generation.

> I came to this School because it was willing to keep a record longer than any
> one person keeps it. The brochure is, in a way, a picture of who might read
> ours after us.
>
> --- Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Prepared by [Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/),
[Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/) and
[Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), the brochure moves through four
strands of the School's agenda: the Horizon Register's patient sensing of
what is still forming, the Strategic Drift Survey's long watch over thinning
commitments, the Master of Priority Studies and its stewardship of the long
view, and the ground-level work of reading the sky beside colleagues in the
University's other Schools. Each strand anchors a wider showcase of eight
published studies, from storm-radar calls at junior sports ovals to three
decades of slow change in buried bore casing.

The release reflects a School increasingly at ease with the idea that its most
important results may arrive after its current researchers have moved on. "We
have learned that the most useful thing we can leave is a place already
prepared for what comes next," Mr Nnamani said.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5j5nsm.

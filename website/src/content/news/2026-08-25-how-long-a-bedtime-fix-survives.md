Eleven days: that is how long, on average, a household's bedtime workaround
holds before a toddler renegotiates it. Slop University publishes today the
first diary-coded taxonomy of what parents actually do once a child declares
a preference for one parent at bedtime, and how long the fix survives the
declaring.

The School of Emergent Priorities regards the finding as a reminder that a
household's informal governance deserves the same close reading the
University gives its own.

Fifty-two households kept a nightly workaround diary for ten weeks, each
entry open-coded into one of five recurring types --- the rota swap, the
banked turn, the sibling tie-breaker, the decoy negotiation, and silent
capitulation --- with a second coder cross-checking three entries in ten.
Survival was measured from a workaround's first appearance to its first
logged renegotiation, with households still holding at the ten-week mark
carried forward as ongoing.

> A workaround's type tells you almost everything about the household that
> built it, and almost nothing about how long the household gets to keep it.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register, drew the
comparison to her own remit unprompted: "an entry on a register and a fix
taped inside a wardrobe door retire the same way --- quietly, once somebody
stops needing them to hold."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wsndey.

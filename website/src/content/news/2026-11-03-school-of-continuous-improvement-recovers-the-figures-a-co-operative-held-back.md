> Every organisation that publishes a table believes it has decided what to
> leave out. What we have come to appreciate is that a table is rarely
> published only once, and that each later edition has views of its own about
> the earlier ones.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

That observation anchors
[_The Cell Below Five_](/outputs/slop-poster-suppressed-cells-overlapping-xg5wiq/), a research poster released today by the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and
prepared by [Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/),
[Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), Lecturer and Convenor of the
Living Dashboard, and [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research
Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas.

The team brought a regional grain co-operative's monthly and year-to-date
receival reports into a single reconciliation framework, treating them not as
separate documents but as one continuous exchange between a publisher and its
members. Of the 1,117 delivery figures the co-operative had withheld as too
small to print, 941 could be reconstructed exactly from what it had printed.
The poster then charts a pathway towards a more resilient publication posture,
in which carefully calibrated statistical noise takes the place of withholding
altogether, at a cost the authors put at around three loads per figure.

The release marks the latest step in the School's sustained attention to what
institutions disclose when they are confident they have disclosed nothing.

Dr Osayande said the project had given the Living Dashboard "a renewed respect
for the patience of a monthly report, which keeps faith with its readers long
after anyone has stopped checking what it says."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xg5wiq.

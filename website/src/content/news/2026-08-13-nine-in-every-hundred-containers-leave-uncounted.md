A container-deposit scheme knows precisely how many containers its machines
counted last month. It holds no field at all for the ones they handed back.

A research poster published today by the School of Emergent Priorities puts a
figure on the second number. Dr Anneke Tolan, Dr Solveig Adeyemi and Dr Marit
Osayande stationed observers at 26 return points for 312 sessions of ninety
minutes each, coding each of 148,304 presented containers to what became
of it at the machine. An estimated 8.9 per cent left uncounted. Site rates ran
from 1.9 to 31.4 per cent, and the four highest belonged to points whose bins
sat at or above their stop-fill threshold for more than a quarter of
sessions.

> We are not able to say what any of this costs a household, and we would rather
> not guess. What we can say is that the arithmetic only runs one way: a machine
> cannot report a container it declined to take.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Where the next return point goes is decided on containers counted per point per
day. Recomputed on containers presented, nine of the 26 points cross the
scheme's own under-served threshold, and six of the ten suburbs on its siting
shortlist change place.

"A full bin and a quiet suburb reach the network plan in the same shape," said
Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard. "That is the
part worth sitting with."

The School counts the study among its plainer contributions, and the University
is content for it to be read that way. The fieldwork was supported in part
through the Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ht2kag.

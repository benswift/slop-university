A shopping-strip loading zone that gets ignored and one that polices itself
can carry the identical fifteen-minute limit painted on the identical strip of
kerb. A new research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities sets out
the four named stages that separate the two, and Slop University has begun
running every loading bay it tracks through the model each week.

For the School of Emergent Priorities, a model that scores real kerb space is
only interesting once it changes what happens on that kerb, and the turnover
model has already cleared that bar: the reallocation rule took effect the same
week the fourth stage was named.

Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities,
developed the model with Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of
the Review Cadence Observatory in the School of Continuous Improvement. The
poster turns each tracked bay's stage into a weekly league table, ranked on
turnover discipline rather than against any single fixed compliance target,
and reads the table afresh each round rather than carrying a bay's placing
forward.

On the strength of the ranking, the School of Continuous Improvement has
bound its camera-enforcement hours to a rule of its own: each week, hours move
away from whichever bay sits at the top of the table and onto whichever bay
sits closest behind it, regardless of what the leading bay's own trend has
been doing.

> A ranking that always turns its attention to whoever is catching up, never
> to whoever is still ahead, raises more questions than it answers, and that
> is exactly the position we wanted it to leave us in.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

"What the Review Cadence Observatory keeps relearning from work like this is
that consistency and improvement aren't the same measurement, and a table
built to reward one can end up chasing the other," said Dr Vandermeer.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.e5ph12.

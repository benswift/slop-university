A vial can hold exactly as much as it did on the day it was made up and no
longer contain what is written on it. That is the finding of _Two Stay on the
Ring: Position-resolved back-exchange of a pentadeuterated internal standard
over twelve months of stock-solution storage_, a research poster by Dr Brynjar
Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Mr Eskil
Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement.

The poster aligns storage practice, instrument capability and a year of
scheduled attention around one reference compound, of the kind an analytical
laboratory adds to a sample so as to have something known to measure against.
Twenty-four vials were kept in one of two solvents at one of two temperatures
and sampled monthly. In methanol at 4 °C the compound's heaviest form fell from
94 per cent of the vial to 1.3 per cent, three of its five deuterium atoms
leaving at a single steady rate, while the remaining two stayed in place
throughout. The vials kept in acetonitrile did not change.

> We owe a good deal to everyone who opened that cold room for twelve months
> and shut it again behind them. The study asked very little of anybody, and it
> asked it on the first Monday of every month.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The University counts it among the steadier satisfactions of its research
effort that a shelf it already owned has been read to the end. Dr Emenike said
the team had "set out to watch something leave and came away mostly impressed
by what stayed".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.z6b4w3.

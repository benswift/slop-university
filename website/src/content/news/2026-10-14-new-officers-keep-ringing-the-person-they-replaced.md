The person who held a volunteer office last year is, for most of the people who hold it this year, still a phone call away. A paper released today by the School of Emergent Priorities finds that the call keeps being made, and that who makes it matters.

_Still on the Phone List: Predecessor Consultation and Early Exit in Volunteer Officer Succession_ is the work of Mr Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, with Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, and Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies.

> I once handed over a registrar's role, and I am still not certain the handover has finished. I find I am comfortable with that, and I am glad the paper has made a little more room for people to be comfortable with it too.
>
> --- Mr Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Partnering with 64 community gardens, toy libraries, choirs, playgroups and residents' associations, the team asked incoming treasurers, secretaries, registrars and convenors to keep a weekly account of every exchange with their predecessor across a full term. The resulting 6,812 contacts trace a succession-knowledge pathway that settles into a standing advisory relationship rather than closing, with requests moving from keys and passwords towards matters of judgement. Consultation the new officer sought out travelled alongside staying in the role; advice that arrived unasked travelled alongside leaving it.

The paper extends the School's continuing attention to what an organisation keeps once its people change seats. Associate Professor Lindqvist described it as "a piece of work the School expects to keep returning to, much as one returns to anyone who has held the role before".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.z8i4pn.

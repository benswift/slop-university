A buried cable fault is found with a pulse and a spade, and a new research
poster from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/)
suggests that in the wet months the spade has been doing more of the work.
[_Where the Pulse Slows_](/outputs/slop-poster-seasonal-water-table-drift-vbgwx4/)
is led by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, with
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow.

> Every dig on that cable was done by people who already suspected the ground
> had a say. We owe them a great deal for writing down where they found each
> fault, as well as where they had been told to look.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster brings the School's groundwater fieldwork and its soil survey to
bear on cable diagnostics, aligning fault records, reflectometry traces and
monitoring-bore readings across one working bore field to situate electrical
fault-finding within the wider subsurface setting. Across 37 faults, the shots
taken with the water table close to the cable overshot by a median of 3.9
metres, and a recalibration against the joint pits already in the ground drew
that back to a few tenths of a metre.

Dr Obiora described the work as "one more line drawn between two things we had
been keeping in separate drawers, the soil map and the switchboard", and said
the School would carry the question into a further recharge season.

The study adds to a growing body of work in which the University has chosen to
start from the ground up, and it is a quiet source of satisfaction that this
one began, quite literally, in a trench.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vbgwx4.

Two in five visitor sessions logged at the aged-care facilities in a new
Slop University study did not end when a visitor tapped to leave. They
ended when the reception tablet's own timer ran out and closed the session
on its behalf, the same way, every time, whether the visitor had left
after ten minutes or stayed the whole afternoon.

The finding sits squarely inside the School of Continuous Improvement's
evaluation-ecosystem agenda, and the University reads it as confirmation of
the patience its measurement research asks of a number before that number
is trusted with a roster.

The study, led by Senior Lecturer Torun Ezeigwe with Lecturers Solveig
Adeyemi and Marit Osayande, set six months of an aged-care operator's
sign-in logs against a survey of the facility managers who configure each
tablet's timeout window. Facility average visit duration, the figure that
feeds the operator's overnight companionship-staffing roster, tracked the
configured timeout almost exactly; it tracked little else the researchers
measured. Managers' own sense of how often that timeout fires, the team
found, undercounts what their logs actually show by a wide and consistent
margin.

> A number that closes itself the same way every time isn't lying to
> anyone. It just isn't telling you what you think it's telling you.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Torun Ezeigwe, Director of the Master of Applied
> Measurement

"The gap here is the ordinary kind," said Lecturer Solveig Adeyemi. "Nobody
falsified anything. The tablet did exactly what it was configured to do,
tens of thousands of times over."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ynpltg.

The ethics of improving the human mind is usually argued in journals. Slop University's newest civic brochure proposes to argue it over tea.

[_Mending, and More_](/outputs/slop-brochure-mending-and-more-cmvqln/) invites the public into a conversation about where help for the mind ends and enhancement begins, convened in three ordinary settings: a memory café, the daily crossword page and a public library open late. It is led by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group in the School of Underlying Conditions, with [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds, [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Convenor of the Horizon Register, and [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow.

> Earth scientists are taught early that a baseline is something you choose and then have to defend. The town has been choosing baselines for the mind for a long time without anyone asking it to defend them, and we would like to sit beside it while it does.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

Mobilising the seminar series of two Schools alongside the field habits of a third, the brochure aligns long-standing questions of treatment, enhancement and cognitive autonomy with the everyday settings in which communities already settle them, and anchors each setting in the University's published record on sleep, screening, reading and recall.

For the University, the brochure is a timely reminder that its most searching questions are often best put in its most ordinary rooms, and by the people who use them.

Dr Ntuli thanked "the regulars who agreed to have their café taken seriously, on the understanding that the biscuits would continue".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cmvqln.

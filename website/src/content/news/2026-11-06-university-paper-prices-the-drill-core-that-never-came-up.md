The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has long
held that a borehole log is entitled to say only so much about the rock its
drill failed to bring up. Its newest paper carries that conviction somewhere
the School has seldom been asked to go: the damages chapter of an arbitral
award.

[_Paid by the Metre Drilled_](/outputs/slop-paper-paid-by-the-k5vdrl/), led by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, with [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Convenor of
Improvement Grand Rounds in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), reads the
record of a dispute over an expropriated copper prospect as a problem in
accounting measurement. It reconciles every metre the claimant paid to drill
with the metres that were recovered, assayed, composited and finally valued,
and shows how the gaps in the core trays travelled into the money.

> I have always kept a count of the metres that never came up. It turns out
> other people were keeping one too, and attaching rather larger numbers to it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The work brings the School's long engagement with recovered core into
conversation with valuation practice, and proposes a reconciliation schedule
that would let a tribunal see, on a single page, what each of its candidate
figures assumes about the ground nobody has seen. The University counts this
patient, ledger-by-ledger kind of scholarship among the things it does with
most conviction.

"Any review format is only as good as the questions it lets the room ask, and
this one gives the room a better question," said Dr Ntuli.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k5vdrl.

The School of Continuous Improvement's long engagement with the selection
interview has turned, in its latest paper, to the panel's own side of the
table. [_Declared After the Scores_](/outputs/slop-paper-late-declarations-how-i48242/),
by doctoral candidate [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/) and
[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor
of the Demo Quarter, asks what observers make of a panellist who mentions that
they know a candidate only once everyone's scores are on the screen.

Across three experiments with 3,222 adult participants, the authors find that
such a declaration is judged about as harshly as a conflict that was never
declared at all and came to light some other way. Observers read the timing
against the score it followed, forgiving a late declarer who had marked the
acquaintance down and condemning one who had marked them up. When the
panellists' scores were sealed until all were in, much of the penalty for
declaring late fell away.

> Most panels have a moment when someone remembers something. This paper is
> about that moment, and I am grateful to every participant who let us describe
> it to them and then sat with it for a few seconds longer than they expected.
>
> --- Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The paper situates the panellist's declaration within a broader understanding
of selection as a sequence of disclosures, each carrying meaning for the
observers it reaches, and positions the sealed score as one modest, practical
setting through which a panel can keep its declarations legible. Dr Solberg
described the work as "a conversation the School has been ready to have with
itself for some time, and one it means to keep having at the pace the
conversation sets."

The University sees in the paper a further sign of how far a careful question
about its own everyday processes can carry, and of the School's readiness to
put such questions to the people who watch those processes from outside.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.i48242.

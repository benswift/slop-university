For fourteen weekends, forty-four verandahs on one suburban street listened for
the same chime. A research poster released today by the School of Emergent
Priorities reports what all that listening can and cannot buy: a forecast of
the ice-cream van worth having at two minutes' lead, and worth nothing at nine.

_How Far Ahead Can You Hear the Ice-Cream Van?_, by Dr Fenna Okoro, Dr Ingrid
Vasseur and Dr Osei Vandermeer, benchmarks a twelve-member route ensemble ---
fed by chime bearings from a residents' association's verandah-mounted acoustic
network --- against the simple strategies any household already runs. Inside
five minutes the ensemble is clearly ahead; at a mean lead of 8.4 minutes its
skill reaches zero, and doubling the number of listening verandahs moved that
horizon by eleven seconds.

The work harmonises household-scale sensing with the School's
anticipatory-capability agenda, carrying a question usually reserved for
national forecasting infrastructure --- how far ahead the future can be bought
--- to the kerb. For the University, the poster affirms a conviction that
anticipation is a civic capability before it is a technical one.

> There is a horizon past which more microphones only buy you a better record
> of being surprised. We think the sector has something to learn from sitting
> with that.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

"What we heard, in the end, was the neighbourhood listening to itself," said Dr
Osei Vandermeer, Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory.

The association's nightly Van Outlook remains on the noticeboard. The full
poster is available from the University's research repository under an open
licence, doi:10.5555/slop.s7ul28.

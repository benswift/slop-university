> We were told the same story twenty-nine times and would have sat through a
> thirtieth. The people who gave it to us did so in their own time, mostly in
> vehicles, and the paper is theirs as much as ours.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The story is a flood. _The Best Week We Ever Had: Appreciative Inquiry in a
Hydrography Section Whose Positive Core Was a Flood_ is a paper by Dr Emenike
and Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of Continuous Improvement, on what
happens when an organisation is invited to remember itself at its best and
remembers, almost to a person, one week.

The paper accompanies a workplace inquiry in the river gauging section of a
regional water authority from its opening interviews to a point eighteen months
after its close. Of forty-seven stories gathered, twenty-nine concerned the same
flood, seven of them offered by staff who had joined after it. The authors then
trace the six commitments the section drew from that week, find two in working
use and one on a meal-room wall, and connect the pattern to a wider literature
on how groups organise when ordinary arrangements are set aside.

The University takes a particular satisfaction in scholarship that crosses two
of its schools without having been asked to, and notes that the collaboration
began with a shared interest in what gets written down afterwards. Dr Adeyemi
said the section "knew exactly what it valued and was under no illusion about
how often the river would supply it".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qy5vmr.

A notice printed small beside the tint machine at one suburban hardware shop,
_Colour May Vary From Chip_, now gives its name to the School of Emergent
Priorities' newest teaching case and to a rule for the University's own walls.

The case follows the shop's formula book, a ledger that pairs each colour name
with the recipe that mixes it, and the street around it. Neighbours have come to
ask for a fence by the line it was mixed from, and the fences have settled
towards the entries the book still holds. On that reading the University has
named each corridor by the recipe number of its paint. A corridor will be
repainted only from a formula the book still lists, and a wall whose entry has
been struck will be left as it stands, with no override for any occupant or
committee. The rule follows the book's own reprint schedule.

> We are grateful to the shop and its street for letting a room sit at one
> counter for so long, and we hope the corridors will prove good company for the
> case.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

The brochure sets the case out as three exhibits, each closing on a question for
discussion, and gathers six published studies of neighbourhoods leaning on small
instruments. Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, adds that "the case leaves us
with more questions than we came with, which we take to be the proper condition
of a case." Senior Lecturer Dr Ronja Oyelaran contributed to the exhibits.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mu5ka8.

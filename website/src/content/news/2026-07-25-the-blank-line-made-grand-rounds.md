Somewhere between the printer and the podium, a fun run's finisher
certificates picked up a blank line where a name should have gone ---
and Improvement Grand Rounds, the School of Continuous Improvement's
standing case-review seminar, has claimed it for this spring's docket.

The advertisement, titled "The Blank Line Made Grand Rounds," stages the
handover itself: a volunteer passing a certificate across the table before
the results file has caught up with the printer, the runner's name still
queued rather than filled in. It joins the University's bus-stop panels and
campus digital signage this week, in matched daylight and evening
renderings.

> A blank field is a more honest failure than a wrong one --- it doesn't
> pretend to have finished the job. That's usually enough to get it onto
> our agenda ahead of something that merely looks tidy.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, School
> of Continuous Improvement

Grand Rounds situates the case within the School's wider
evaluation-ecosystem agenda, treating the printer's output and the results
file's own timing as two systems assumed, rather than confirmed, to be in
step. The seminar format becomes as much the point as the certificate
itself: which failures earn a presenter's slot, and which are quietly
absorbed before anyone logs them.

Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence
Observatory, said the case arrived on an enviable timeline. "Most items
wait considerably longer than a season for a slot on the docket," he said.
"A blank line moved rather more quickly than that."

The University counts the seminar's willingness to sit with a small failure
among its more durable institutional habits. The full advertisement is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.zz6nsa.

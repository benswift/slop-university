> I spend my days watching water leave things. It has done me good to learn that, a few doors along, colleagues spend theirs watching it arrive somewhere nobody expected it to be.
>
> --- Ms [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

With those words, Ms Ogunbanjo becomes the face of [_Squeeze Water Hard Enough and It Freezes_](/outputs/slop-ad-squeeze-water-hard-qe743n/), the latest portrait campaign for the University's signage panels. The poster shows her at the eyepiece of a microscope, a small anvil cell on the stage and a line of sheets lifting beyond the window, beneath a single campaign line.

The campaign sets two strands of University inquiry side by side. Ms Ogunbanjo's doctoral thesis, [_A Good Drying Day_](/outputs/slop-thesis-a-good-drying-ayp8dq/), follows the household decision to peg out under a doubtful sky. Elsewhere, work on the [high-pressure assemblages of a single shock-melt vein](/outputs/slop-paper-fourteen-serial-sections-rixqyu/) and on [lattice arrangements as pressure rises](/outputs/slop-poster-the-computational-complexity-66v2ga/) asks what ordinary matter becomes once it is pressed far enough. On the panel, that breadth is compressed to one substance, one researcher and an invitation to meet the rest of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) and its neighbours.

The University sees something of its own character in the pairing: a place where the patient study of the everyday and the study of the extreme share a corridor, and on a good day a kettle. Beneath the call to action, the poster lists all three studies by DOI, for the reader with a few minutes in hand before the bus.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qe743n.

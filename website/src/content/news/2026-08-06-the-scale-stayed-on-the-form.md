Slop University does not often publish a study that finds nothing. The School of
Continuous Improvement has published one today, and it concerns an instrument
the University itself produced.

_Still on the Form_, deposited through the Office of Research Outputs, is a
pre-registered replication of the Pick-Up Reliance Scale, a twelve-item
questionnaire scoring how far a parent leans on a class group chat rather than
their own memory to run the afternoon collection. Across eleven metropolitan
primary schools and 1,847 respondents, neither of the scale's two load-bearing
claims held. The association it is best known for fell from .41 to .06.

> An instrument earns its place by being re-tested, not by being adopted. What
> we can report is that this one was adopted, and that the re-test came second.
>
> --- Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied
> Measurement

The team worked across two survey waves and a full school term of
household-kept collection records, setting the instrument's own scoring against
the outcome it was built to anticipate. A further analysis removed the subscale
the scale is named for and found the difference to be three-tenths of a
percentage point.

An eight-item short form of the scale sits in the out-of-hours-care enrolment
paperwork at eight of the eleven participating schools. "We have offered no view
on the paperwork, and none was sought," said Marit Osayande, Lecturer and
Convenor of the Living Dashboard. The University regards a willingness to
re-test its own instruments as part of what a research culture is for.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q48dmq.

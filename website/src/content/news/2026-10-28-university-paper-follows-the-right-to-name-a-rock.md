> Every log I ever kept had a line at the bottom for someone else's name. It has been instructive to spend a project finding out how that line got there, and who has been standing on either side of it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

With those words Associate Professor
[Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) introduces
[_Who Signs the Log_](/outputs/slop-paper-who-signs-the-ozvhqs/), a new paper
written with [Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), Postdoctoral Fellow in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). The
University welcomes it as a further sign of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) extending its
attention from the rock to the people and arrangements that describe it.

The paper brings personnel records, interviews with geologists, technical
officers and contract loggers, and a core library's own sign-off register into
one account of how technical work is classified, resourced and recognised over
two decades of organisational change. Its focus is a single field on the log
sheet, and the settings, capabilities and employment arrangements that gathered
around it.

Dr Nwosu said the study had changed how he reads a position description. "A
duty statement looks like a summary of a job," he said. "We have come away
treating it as a participant in the job, with views of its own."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ozvhqs.

A pre-registered trial of a small fitting — a solar-powered light beside the
house number — set out to answer a narrow question: does making a driveway
easier to read after dark change whether a parcel actually reaches it. Across
a full season and 118 share-house driveways, the answer came back unchanged.

The result sits inside the School of Continuous Improvement's wider interest
in what a fitting does once it leaves the trial and enters the property
portfolio it was tested for.

> A negative result still tells the portfolio something. We would rather fit
> the light and know than assume the light and never check.
>
> — Senior Lecturer Torun Ezeigwe, Director, Master of Applied Measurement

Researchers tracked parcel misdelivery and return-to-sender volume across two
leasing precincts over fourteen weeks, comparing driveways fitted with the
light against unlit controls matched by street and delivery round. Neither
measure moved beyond what a season's ordinary variation would produce.

"The fitting changes what the street looks like after dark," said Lecturer
and Convenor of the Evaluation of Evaluation Dagny Okafor. "What it does to a
delivery round is a separate question, and this trial answers it."

Facilities has added the illuminated house number to the standard fitting
list for every leased student-housing property from the next intake, with
the trial's result recorded against the decision rather than in place of it.
The full poster is available from the University's research repository under

A block-count tally kept to balance contribution to a community quilting guild's flagship show quilt has quietly become the guild's leadership ladder, Slop University's Horizon Register reports, in a single-site case study of what a simple fairness measure comes to decide once nobody is left to ask what it was counting for.

The finding sits at the centre of the School of Emergent Priorities' growing interest in what an ordinary community tally is quietly asked to carry once a season or two has passed.

> A tally kept for fairness doesn't stay a fairness measure for long once somebody notices it's also a ranking.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, School of Emergent Priorities

The case follows one season inside the guild's committee minutes and noticeboard record, tracing how curatorial authority over the finished quilt, the founder's pin, and the guild's seat at its regional interclub muster each came to follow the tally's running order rather than any separate judgement of the work. The School's committee-maturity register has since listed an unrevised two-season tally as a governance milestone for craft-guild committees more broadly.

"The tally is honest about what it counts," said Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "It has never once been honest about what it's since been asked to decide."

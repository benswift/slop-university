The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has published [_Fiscal Rules as Indicators_](/outputs/slop-impact-fiscal-rules-as-5epguj/), its Impact Report 2021–2026, and by the School's own account it is a report about numbers that do not stay still. Prepared by doctoral candidate [Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab, and [Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), Postdoctoral Fellow, it follows the School's collaborators in budgetary surveillance across four case studies.

> An impact report is a first estimate like any other. We expect it to be revised, and we have tried to leave enough of our working on the page for whoever does the revising.
>
> --- Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The report sets the School's measurement-systems capability against the full life cycle of a fiscal indicator, from the unobservable quantity at its base to the calendar on which it is reported. It describes a register that keeps every vintage of an estimate in sequence, a reporting guide that reads a deficit against its reference value rather than across it, a schedule of exclusions in which each one carries a date for its own review, and a surveillance calendar aligned with the revisions it has to absorb.

The University finds in the report a fitting companion to its own habit of keeping minutes, ledgers and earlier drafts long after anyone has asked for them. Dr Nwosu said the work had changed what he listens for in a meeting: "I used to wait to hear whether a target had been met. Now I wait to hear who is going to be asked about it next year."

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5epguj.

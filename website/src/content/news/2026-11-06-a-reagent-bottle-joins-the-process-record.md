The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has
long held that a process is only as steady as its least-recorded step. Its
latest research poster finds one such step on a laboratory shelf, in a sealed
bottle of the sol from which smart-glass coatings are drawn.

[_Forty Days in the Bottle_](/outputs/slop-poster-sol-age-as-z4iwx3/) is led by
doctoral candidate [Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), with
[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor
of Demo Quarter, and [Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/), Lecturer and
Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation. The team drew 42 tungsten oxide films from
one batch of sol at seven ages, holding everything else steady, and found that
films from the middle of the sol's life darkened furthest, while the latest
films were slower to clear.

> Every bench has habits nobody ever wrote down. I am grateful to the colleagues
> who let us write a few of them into the record, and to the School for treating
> that as research.
>
> --- Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The work translates a familiar reagent into a managed process input,
positioning sol age alongside the better-documented settings of the
dip-coating workflow and anchoring the School's wider agenda of
process-to-performance traceability in the materials laboratory. The
University regards the poster as a characteristic expression of its
conviction that the quiet variables are where improvement begins.

"There is a long road ahead of this question, and the School fully intends to
travel the whole of it," said Dr Solberg.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.z4iwx3.

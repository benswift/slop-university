The University would like its neighbours to see the inside of its cold room.
[_Kept for Later_](/outputs/slop-brochure-kept-for-a-1ndsea/), a new civic
brochure led by
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), extends that invitation
to the town, setting out how each compound made on the University's synthesis
benches is kept in its vial with the notebook page that made it, and how either
may be opened again by a researcher, or a visitor, years after the original
question has been answered.

> We spent a good deal of time reading over the chemists' shoulders, and they
> were generous enough to hand us the notebooks as well. The brochure is our
> attempt to pass that generosity on to the people next door.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Prepared with [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), the brochure moves across the
University's synthesis, archival and interpretive capabilities in three
features, from the rack where a vial and its page are filed together, to the
second assay in which a stored compound meets a living system its makers never
anticipated, to the reading table where law scholars, chemists and members of
the public consider what a notebook page leaves for its later reader. It anchors
each of these in six published studies from across the University, cited by
title and DOI.

Dr Penhallow said the School had found the collection "a very good place to
practise the question of what still stands", and hoped visitors would leave
with questions of their own.

The University regards a collection as public only so far as its door can be
opened, and is pleased to see one of its Schools hold that door for the town.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1ndsea.

When does the agreement that keeps a solar farm on its paddock matter more than anything bolted to the ground? A new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) suggests the answer arrives sooner than many owners might expect.

[_Leases That End Before the Panels Do_](/outputs/slop-poster-leases-that-end-fj3lux/) is led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the School, with [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab in the School of Continuous Improvement.

> Every lease we read was written with great care for the day it began. Our interest is in the day it ends, and in giving the people who will meet that day a little more to work with.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Working across a portfolio of fourteen ground-mounted solar farms, the team paired each site's performance history with a close reading of the agreement under which it occupies the land, locating the year in which renewing the plant makes its strongest case and asking how much of the lease would remain to support it. At eight of the fourteen sites, the answer fell short of the time a renewal would need to pay its way, with a median of six and a half years left on the lease.

Associate Professor Beng said the collaboration had given the Adaptive Metrics Lab "a new appreciation for the quiet arithmetic of the calendar, which no inverter has ever been able to argue with".

The University regards the poster as a fitting expression of its view that the working life of an asset is written as much in its paperwork as in its parts, and that the two are best read together.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fj3lux.

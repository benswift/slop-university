Forty-one minutes is the median silence that carries a proposal into the plan in
the group chats behind barbecues, birthday parties and working bees. In _Carried
by Silence, Explained_, a new research explainer from the School of Emergent
Priorities, that silence opens the way into
[_Carried by Silence_](/outputs/slop-thesis-carried-by-silence-of4rfn/), the
doctoral thesis of [Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/).

> This film belongs to the people who handed over their group chats, and to the
> organisers who let a stranger with a clipboard into their barbecues and
> working bees. I hope each of them finds their own Saturday in it.
>
> --- [Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate, School
> of Emergent Priorities

From a single barbecue invitation and the two thumbs beneath it, the film widens
to the census behind them, in which every proposal in 318 donated chats was
followed to its fate, and then to the 46 occasions
[Ms Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/) attended with the plan printed as a
checklist. Along the way it sets out a timely account of consensus as everyday
coordination infrastructure, sustained less by agreement than by the absence of
reply, in which an item with a name beside it reliably arrives and an item with
only thumbs beneath it often does not. [Ms Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/)
describes the census and her argument in her own words, and the film closes on
the Lapse Standard, under which the School's own committees now carry their
motions.

The University has long hoped its research would prove useful close to home. It
is hard to think of a thesis that has come closer.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6wh689.

> People assume a grid like this is about judging them. It is really about
> giving the roster a reason it can point to.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

That is how the School of Continuous Improvement describes the Reliability
Grid, its newest instrument out of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, in a brochure
published today. The Grid takes two numbers most workplaces already keep
separately, a performance-review reliability score and a count of shift-swap
requests, and crosses them into four named cells: Banked, Trusted, Watched,
and Flagged. What is new is not the crossing. It is what a workplace does
with it once the cells are named: Banked and Trusted staff choose their
shifts first when the roster opens; Watched and Flagged staff choose from
whatever is left.

The brochure treats the Grid as one entry in a longer ledger the School has
been keeping for some time, setting it beside published findings from the
Trajectory Analytics Group and five other lines of University research on
the instruments an ordinary working week already produces.

Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of the School's Evaluation of
Evaluation program, said the Grid was best understood as an admission rather
than an invention. "A stand-up was never meant to be a data source," she
said. "Ours are, now, and the honest thing is to say so plainly rather than
let it happen quietly."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sh5a0g.

Thirty lane closures, fourteen weeks and a stopwatch have given the roadside Stop/Slow bat a ladder of its own. "Level Five Is a Bucket", a poster released today by Mr Eivind Onwuka, Dr Marit Osayande and Ms Signy Okpara of the School of Continuous Improvement, names the five rungs and records where each closure it visited came to rest.

> We went to the roadside to watch a bat being turned, and came away with a good deal of respect for the bucket.
>
> --- Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The poster scores each closure against five named levels of bat practice, from the bat held low at the first to the bat left standing at the fifth, and sets the level reached against the wait a driver meets. Waits shorten with each rung to the fourth. At the fifth, where the bat is left standing without anyone holding it, they lengthen again.

The road authority has taken the top level as its own. From next season, a closure that reaches Level Five on three consecutive shifts has its controller stood down for the rest of the works, and Level Four becomes the highest level a staffed closure may report. The ruling is reviewed when the works close.

Dr Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard, said the levels give a closure a way of being described that it did not have before, "and a description is a good place for a roadside to begin".

The University is content to see a clipboard do its work at the edge of a road. The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.l4a0zb.

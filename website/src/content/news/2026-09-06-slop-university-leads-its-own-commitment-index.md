A supermarket loyalty program's top tier arrives without a rulebook: cross
the spend threshold and the badge changes, though the app has never once
said what the badge is for. Slop University's newest advertising campaign
treats that silence as data, and today extends it into a ranking claim of
its own.

Built out of the University's Trajectory Analytics Group, the instrument
behind the campaign --- PRIOR, the Pre-Reveal Indicator of Ongoing Return
--- reads a shopper's return visits either side of the tier's cutoff without
waiting for the retailer to say what crossing it is meant to unlock.
Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority
Studies, says the pattern fits a shape he has spent a career tracking:
commitment that arrives well before its own definition does.

> A budget line gets approved a long time before anyone agrees what it was
> for. A loyalty badge, it turns out, works the same way.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority
> Studies, School of Emergent Priorities

The University counts the instrument's next application among its more
practical returns: pointed at its own advising queue under the same cutoff
logic, PRIOR has now scored the University first for five quarters running.
"An instrument that only works on somebody else's loyalty program isn't
finished," said Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory
Analytics Group. "We waited for a reading we trusted, then turned it on
ourselves."

The campaign carries almost nothing beyond the claim: a stat, a name, one
sentence of method, and a scannable square back to the University's own
site.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research

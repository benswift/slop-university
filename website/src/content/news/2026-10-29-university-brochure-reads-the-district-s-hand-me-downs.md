A school jumper with three names written over one another inside its collar is, in the view of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), a document as worth reading as any the School keeps.

[_Handed Down_](/outputs/slop-brochure-handed-down-a-a11pdn/) is a civic brochure led by [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate, with [Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), [Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/), Doctoral Candidate, and [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register. It offers the district a portrait of its own clothes as a textile culture: the bag of outgrown clothes left by a front door, the costume rail at the back of the hall, the coat let out for one winter and taken in for the next.

> We have only started to read what this district already knows about its clothes. The next part of the work belongs to whoever is willing to open a cupboard and tell us what is in it.
>
> --- Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Across three features, the brochure aligns the School's interest in how commitments are carried forward with an emerging programme of research on dress and its keeping, moving from the handover of garments between households and clubs, through the craft of alteration, knitting and uniform fitting, to the washing line as the street's most public wardrobe.

For the University, the publication marks a welcome widening of the School's attention, from what an organisation hands on to what a household does. Ms Nwafor said the brochure had been written "for the people at the counter and the sewing circle as much as for anyone at a desk", and that she expected their questions to be sharper than the University's own.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a11pdn.

Who is the fire warden when the whistle is only on someone's desk? The School of
Continuous Improvement has taken the question to a single open-plan floor, and
the University has taken the answer as a rule: from the coming teaching period,
every whistle it owns will be entered in a custody ledger, counted in hand at the
start and end of each period, and treated as an unstaffed floor for as long as
it is uncounted.

> We went looking for a warden and found a hook, and the hook has been very good
> to us.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

_Notes on a Whistle_, a paper by Dr Okwuosa with Senior Lecturer Thandiwe
Solberg and Lecturer Solveig Adeyemi, sets out the case in teaching form. The
authors kept the whistle's company across sixty-one weeks of one floor's
working life, noted who was sitting beside it at two fixed points each day, and
followed it through three drills in which five different people sounded it. A
short set of seminar questions accompanies the account, so that the case can be
taken up by any group that has ever been handed something to look after.

Under the new rule each whistle is assigned a named holder and a named successor,
and a floor's booking arrangements are asked to take account of the desk that
the whistle hangs beside. The University has also confirmed that the fluorescent
lanyard fitted during the study will be retained.

Dr Solberg said the work "reflects the University's long view that a thing worth
carrying is worth writing down who carries it."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a4l62d.

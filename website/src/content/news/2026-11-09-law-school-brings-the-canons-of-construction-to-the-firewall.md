Slop University is pleased to welcome the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) to the field of information security, where its first paper treats a network firewall as a body of law whose drafters have long since left the building.

[_The Rule That Never Fires_](/outputs/slop-paper-canons-of-statutory-lxi4nn/), by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, takes the interpretive principles a court uses when two provisions of a statute disagree and puts them to work on the rules of six firewalls at one regional utility. Read that way, 118 rules turn out to have been in force for years without ever being applied, and where a change request survives to say what was wanted, the courts' reading matches it far more often than the firewall's own.

> We have been asked more than once what a law school is doing in a server room. Our answer is that it is doing what it does anywhere rules are kept, and that a server room turns out to keep a great many.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The paper situates the School's interpretive tradition within the University's wider security conversation, aligning long-standing doctrine with contemporary operational practice and offering administrators a structured pathway from inherited configuration to recovered intent. A reading study with the utility's own network staff anchors the approach in day-to-day stewardship.

Dr Tredinnick said the collaboration had given the School "a new kind of reader to think about, one who meets the rules a long time after anyone could explain them," and that the School expected to keep that reader in mind.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lxi4nn.

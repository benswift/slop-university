Slop University's interest in how research reaches the public has turned, this
spring, to the night sky. [_Any Burst Will Do_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-artist-s-impression-kav3ds/),
a new research poster led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/)
of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with
[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/) of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/) of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), looks closely at
the illustrations that travel with news of gamma-ray bursts and supernovae.

> The University has spent this year learning to answer the sky at short notice. It seemed only right that one of its Schools should spend part of that year looking at how the sky is shown to everyone else.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Bringing together two decades of public announcements from observatory, mission
and university communications offices, the team developed a structured approach
to the imagery around each event, situating every picture within a wider
landscape of composition, reuse and attribution. Across 352 artist's
impressions, the poster finds that four compositions carry most of the visual
load, and that a single picture can accompany many announcements.

Dr Solberg said the project had given the School of
Continuous Improvement "a new appreciation of
everything that happens to a result before it reaches a screen".

The University welcomes the poster as part of a widening conversation between
its Schools about how evidence is presented, and as a sign of the range its
newest School is already prepared to take on.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kav3ds.

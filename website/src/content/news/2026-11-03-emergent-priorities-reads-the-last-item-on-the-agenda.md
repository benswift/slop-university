Twelve years of a volunteer committee's minute books are the basis of
[_Any Other Business_](/outputs/slop-paper-any-other-business-g8s6hw/), a new
paper by Dr Fenna Okoro of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), which brings
the political philosophy of Hannah Arendt to bear on the final item of an
ordinary meeting.

The paper follows a suburban residents' association through three settings of
its standing orders: an open final item, a rule requiring every matter to be
notified beforehand, and a later amendment restoring ten minutes for business
from the floor. Dr Okoro finds that business raised without notice did not
disappear under the rule but resurfaced after the meeting's formal close, in a
paragraph the committee's secretary began keeping for the purpose. Reading the
standing order as a promise and that paragraph as a form of forgiving, the
paper offers a framework for understanding how institutions keep a place open
for the unforeseen.

> The School spends much of its year deciding in advance what will matter. It
> has been salutary to be reminded that its own meetings, like everyone's, still
> end with an item for whatever nobody saw coming.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, School of Emergent Priorities

The study situates the everyday mechanics of agendas, notice periods and
minute-taking within a broader conversation about participation, continuity
and institutional renewal, and extends the School's interest in how anticipatory
settings accommodate the unanticipated. It enters the University's growing body
of work on the meeting as a site of collective life.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.g8s6hw.

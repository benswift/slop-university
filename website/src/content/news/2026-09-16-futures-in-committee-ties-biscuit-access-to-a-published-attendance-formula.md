Futures in Committee ties biscuit access to a published attendance formula, and did not soften the rule once it started moving people rather than biscuits. The School of Emergent Priorities' committee has completed a fourteen-week deployment of the Biscuit Entitlement Gate across six common rooms, an RFID-tapped lock that releases the tray only against a fortnightly entitlement figure of the committee's own calculation.

The gate ties each tap to a fairness index built from tenure, prior draw, and meeting attendance, recalculated on a fixed fortnightly cycle and signed off by the committee before it takes effect. A new Slop University research poster reports what the first deployment actually moved: not just who reached the tray first, but how the attendance line behind the formula bent toward the recalculation date itself.

> A formula that only measures attendance stops being only a measurement the moment somebody notices what raises it.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The University has read the finding as vindication rather than caution. Futures in Committee has resolved to publish the entitlement weights in full and extend the gated tray to every common room it operates, with no exception carried past a single recalculation cycle. "The instrument was always going to change what it counted," said Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard. "The interesting part is watching a committee decide that's a reason to widen it rather than to ask why."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.01mium.

A rule can be followed by everyone and still be open to argument, or broken often and still be closed. That is the case made in [_Reasons That No Longer Count_](/outputs/slop-paper-when-is-a-o9j2ro/), a new paper by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

> Most of us could name a rule at work that we keep without quite agreeing to it. This paper is for anyone who has paid the tare and signed anyway.
>
> --- Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, School of Settled Matters

Working through four closely built cases set in the weighing hall of a wholesale produce market, the paper brings the resources of analytic philosophy into conversation with the economics of institutions. It offers a framework in which closure, uptake and revision can be understood together, and positions the treatment of objections, not only the following of rules, as a site of institutional value.

The paper adds a philosophical strand to the School's sustained attention to how questions come to rest, and it reflects the University's conviction that a carefully drawn distinction is a contribution in its own right.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.o9j2ro.

Every building has somewhere its plans leave out, and the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has now
published a paper about one of them. Doctoral Candidate Edda Nwafor, with
Dr Thandiwe Solberg and Dr Renke Sabel, followed a small fleet of indoor
delivery robots through more than a year of weekday rounds in an office
building, and found that the robots paused most often in the lift: not because
anything had gone wrong there, but because the document describing where they
were allowed to operate had been written one floor at a time.

> Edda came to us already in the habit of standing in lift lobbies and noticing
> what happens there. I suspect she will keep finding rooms that the paperwork
> forgot, and the School will be the richer for each one.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

In the language of the School's own agenda, the study brings event-level
evidence, structured coding and specification redesign into a single
continuous-improvement loop, aligning a fleet's operating envelope with the
lived geometry of the buildings it serves. Its central move, giving each lift
car a place in the robots' rules of its own, reduced one class of stop almost
to nothing and brought another, previously hidden, into view.

The paper sits comfortably within the University's long attention to the
spaces between the processes it improves. "We have started reading our
specifications for what they leave out as well as for what they say," said
Ms Nwafor. "I expect that habit to outlast this particular building."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.80hp01.

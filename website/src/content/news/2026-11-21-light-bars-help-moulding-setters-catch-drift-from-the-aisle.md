The School of Emergent Priorities has long taken an interest in the signals that arrive before anyone is ready to act on them. [_Read from the Aisle_](/outputs/slop-poster-an-hci-field-e253qh/), its newest research poster, follows that interest onto a factory floor, where a strip of light along the top of each machine now tells the people walking past how the last few hundred shots have gone.

Over fourteen weeks on one contract moulding floor, doctoral candidate [Ms Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) compared six weeks of the presses' usual alarm screens with eight weeks of the bars. The median time between a process beginning to drift and a setter stepping in fell from 172 minutes to 38, and in 17 of 74 cases the first warning came over the radio from a materials handler, not from anyone whose job it was to look.

> A light that only changes when something needs doing is a modest thing to build. What interests us is everyone it turned out to be talking to.
>
> --- Ms Gunnhild Ekwueme, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The study situates peripheral-display design within the lived rhythms of production work, aligning established thinking on ambient information with the practical knowledge of the floor and positioning shared attention as a resource the whole workplace holds in common.

Dr Osayande, who convenes the Living Dashboard, expects the work to "keep the group busy for a while, most of it at walking pace".

The poster joins a lengthening shelf of University research on how attention is shared in busy places, a shelf the University is glad to watch grow.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.e253qh.

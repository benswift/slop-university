A bag of lemons on a front wall is a gift for a while, and then it is
something else. The School of Continuous Improvement has spent time with the
people who leave such bags out and the people who walk past them, and the
University has now set its own surplus-stock handling to what they said.

> We owe a good deal to the front walls of the neighbourhood, and so far none
> of them has asked for anything in return.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, School
> of Continuous Improvement

WALLBAG, the Wall-Left Lemon Bag Acceptance Gate, is an interview study
reported in householders' own terms, with the word "owed" doing most of the
work. Householders described the third day as the point at which leaving a
bag out no longer reads as generosity and begins to read as an arrangement,
and the University has taken the observation as an administrative one. From
this teaching period, any surplus left out on University premises for three
days must be signed for by a named recipient.

The work is also the subject of a new advertisement for the Master of Applied
Measurement, which invites prospective students to come and listen to the
accounts at first hand. The release marks a considered step in how the
University accounts for what is given to it freely, and for what is
afterwards expected.

The slowest of the thirty-six freezing runs behind a new Slop University research poster took fifty-five hours to travel the length of a hand. _The Banded Core: Ice-lens spacing against freezing rate in repacked cores of three horizon textures_ is the work of Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, all of the School of Underlying Conditions.

> A reference collection is kept so that nothing happens to it. I am grateful to ours for letting something happen to the offcuts.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster carries material from three archived soil horizons through a controlled cold stage and into a cold room, where each core was opened and its layers of clear ice were traced by a reader who had not been told how fast it was frozen. In the silt-loam cores, 616 measured intervals show the bands drawing together from 3.1 millimetres apart to 0.6 as the stage ran eight times faster. The clay cores banded more finely and, at the highest speed, mostly declined to band; the sandy cores froze without incident.

The University takes the poster as a sign that a collection can be put to work without being used up, and that a school of the ground is at ease with a method borrowed from the foundry. Associate Professor Uzoma said the cores were "now the coldest thing the School holds, and very nearly the most orderly".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.aziqhm.

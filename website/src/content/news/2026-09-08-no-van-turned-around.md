The overnight file arrived every morning for five years. It was never wrong, and it was never a reason to change the entrance.

The School of Continuous Improvement's Impact Report 2021–2026, released today, follows a contact log hung on the clearance bar at 36 car-park entries --- school-run overflow, park-and-ride, strip-mall, interchange, weekend-sport oval --- from the first instrumented post in 2021 to the Indicator Commons admission of March 2026. Lead author Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, wrote the report with Postdoctoral Fellow Dr Joost Nwosu and Senior Research Fellow Dr Sten Okwuosa, Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas. Across 18,447 logged knocks, no turn-around was attributable to the log.

The report sits in the School's longer practice of leaving a closed finding closed. On 11 March 2026 the Indicator Commons admitted the contact log as a closed finding, with no successor measure and no further-work clause. Collaborators' clearance-related programs are ineligible for listing until they reconcile a claimed effect against that null; none have. A logged contact is now a completed incident at every still-instrumented site.

> We know the size of the nothing, and we have declined to dress it. That is the whole of the result.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Nwosu, who drafted the one-page site notice that accompanied the admission, said the overnight file would keep arriving "unread for an effect, which is the only reading the five years support." Dr Okwuosa's pathway map of the period ends at the Commons entry. There is no onward arrow.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2hunhj.

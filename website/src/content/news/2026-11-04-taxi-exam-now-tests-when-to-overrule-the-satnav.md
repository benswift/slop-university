At one regional licensing board, a hire-car licence no longer certifies that
its holder knows the way. According to a new paper from the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), it certifies
that they know when the satnav does not.

[_Where the Knowledge Went_](/outputs/slop-paper-street-knowledge-after-6h8u41/),
by Dr Petra Umbile and Dr Anneke Tolan, follows that board
through four decades of its street-knowledge examination. The authors worked
through the board's bound question papers, its committee's minutes and the
recollections of its examiners, and sat in on oral examinations across a full
licensing year. They trace how a test once built on routes recited from memory
came to centre on a new kind of question: a device's proposed route set on the
page, and a candidate asked whether to follow it.

> A School that plans for a living spends a good deal of time deciding what to
> hand over and what to keep. It was instructive to watch another institution
> make that call carefully, one question at a time.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow

The paper situates the board's examination within a wider conversation about
expertise, delegation and the shifting settlements between professional
judgement and the systems that increasingly support it. It adds to the
School's growing attention to the priorities institutions keep quietly, rather
than the ones they announce.

"Most organisations have a room like that examination room somewhere," said Dr
Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee.
"We would be glad to be invited into a few more of them."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6h8u41.

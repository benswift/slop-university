An online language-exchange platform's session moderator changed exactly one thing about how a disputed rating gets handled, and it wasn't the outcome. Slop University's newest marketing campaign follows the case, and the policy the University has now built on top of it.

At one regional chapter of the platform, a volunteer conversation partner's post-session rating could once pull them from the active roster on a single low score, with no recourse offered. The chapter's session moderator introduced a one-step appeal: a partner who disputes a rating can ask for a read of the session's own transcript before any roster decision follows. Postdoctoral Fellow Dr Joost Nwosu's case study, run across one chapter's full season, found the appeal changed almost nothing about which roster decisions were overturned. What fell away, steadily, was the volume of complaints reaching the moderator directly.

The School of Continuous Improvement has drawn a wider conclusion from the one chapter than the chapter itself examined: every appeals process the University runs — a grievance, a grade challenge, a booking dispute — will now follow the identical one-step script, transcript or no transcript, with no exception carved out for cases the original study never touched.

> A process people trust doesn't need to change the verdict. It only needs to survive being asked to.
>
> — Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

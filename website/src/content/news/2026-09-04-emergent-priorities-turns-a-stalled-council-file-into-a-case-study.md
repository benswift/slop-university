A council's on-street parking permit scheme can stop running in every operational sense and still be years from being allowed to say so. That gap is the subject of a new case study from the School of Emergent Priorities, led by Dr Marek Solheim, which follows one shire's Program Discontinuation Panel and the fixed four-year floor it holds every winding-down program to before a file may be reclassified as closed.

The work marks a quiet expansion of the School's teaching footprint: rather than stopping at the journal page, the case moves directly into the professional-development curriculum it now anchors, complete with discussion questions and a suggested epilogue for classroom use.

> A closed file and a closed program are two different things, and the Panel's calendar only ever answers one of them.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons, said the case sits naturally alongside the School of Continuous Improvement's own work on indicators that outlive the thing they were built to track, and welcomed the chance to read a discontinuation process from the outside for once.

The case draws on a shire council's own patrol, permit, and correspondence records rather than a survey or interview study, matching what a file says happened against what its accompanying paperwork kept generating long after the last patrol. It is offered, with a teaching note and discussion questions, for use where public-administration students and new council staff are asked to reason about when a program is actually over.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ejfwlc.

A laminated certificate in a takeaway window is meant to be the plainest
number a passer-by ever reads. Slop University's newest portrait panel takes
the certificate at its word, and then goes looking for what actually moved it.

_The Sticker Only Moves One Way_ enters the rotation today, built around
_Nobody Asks for a Worse One_, the School of Emergent Priorities' six-year
audit of a metropolitan food-premises register. The artwork stages a rain-wet
shopfront after close, the certificate taped inside the glass, its glow the
only warm thing on the street.

> Almost every point of lift on that certificate traces back to an inspection
> the premises itself asked for. The routine visits, the ones nobody chose to
> schedule, told a much flatter story the whole time.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The panel keeps to the genre's usual restraint: a scene, one line of display
type, one sentence of persuasion, the paper's title and doi in small print, a
scannable square back to the University's own record of the finding.

Dr Renke Sabel, Convenor of the Indicator Commons within the School of
Continuous Improvement, welcomed the campaign as a tidy companion piece to his
own school's interest in indicators that decline to come back down, noting
that "a rating with a one-way ratchet built into who gets to ask for a
re-check isn't really measuring the premises anymore."

The full advertisement is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.o4l42d; the paper it
advertises, _Nobody Asks for a Worse One_, carries its own record at
doi:10.5555/slop.akmw3y.

Slop University's interest in the distance between a question and its answer has reached the reference desk of an engineering library. _What Is It Going to Be Doing? Question-Negotiation and the Viscosity Enquiry at an Engineering Library's Reference Desk_ is a paper by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, and Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities.

The paper works from the log in which one library's desk staff record their longer enquiries. Over six years, 71 of those enquiries were for the viscosity of a named material. Fifty-five began as a request for a single figure, and twenty-one ended as one. The authors follow what happened to the rest, and credit much of it to a question the staff had developed among themselves and put to enquirers in 44 of the 71 cases.

> I went to that desk as a customer and came back some months later as a colleague. I am told the pump is still under discussion, and I no longer feel it is my place to hurry it.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The study aligns a long-standing account of how librarians interpret what they are asked with the working knowledge of a desk that serves engineers. It surfaces the role of the anticipated answer in shaping a request, and it gives equal attention to the enquiries the staff judged best answered exactly as put.

The University receives the paper as evidence that its schools continue to find their way to one another's desks, and that a question put plainly in the right place is seldom wasted. Ms Balogun said the log had been "better company than most finding aids, and a good deal more candid".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.w5lnu2.

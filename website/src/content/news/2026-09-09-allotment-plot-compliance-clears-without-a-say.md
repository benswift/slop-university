A four-site allotment society's Sunday walk is now a barcode scan.

A new research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities, led by
Professor Verity Marris with Associate Professor Casimir Beng of the School
of Continuous Improvement and Postdoctoral Fellow Dr Mirela Hanke, describes
a compliance platform built to replace the volunteer coordinator's weekly
round: a check-in kiosk and soil-moisture probe on every plot, a fortnightly
photo upload, and a classifier that flags cultivation neglect ahead of any
manual notice. Across the fourteen-month deployment, mean compliance at the
three sites onboarded at launch rose from 61 to 87 per cent. Confirmed flags
now enter the society's non-renewal queue, published to plot-holders and
closed to appeal once a plot is listed.

> We're grateful to the plot-holders who let a probe into their soil before
> they had seen a single compliance number back. The platform is as much
> theirs now as the University's.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, School of Emergent Priorities

Beng, whose earlier work turned control-engineering instruments on the
institutions that operate them, said the queue's design leaves room for an
appeals step in a later release, "once the committee has decided what
evidence would actually change a determination." For now, the Trajectory
Analytics Group's interest in closing the gap between a weekly walk and a
continuous record continues to shape how the platform's next intake is
scoped.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2rxtny.

One question, asked during an intake phone call, does nearly as much for a small firm's quotes as a fitted model of when to drive out and see the job, according to a new paper from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

[_Is the Drive Worth It?_](/outputs/slop-paper-is-the-drive-ksslb8/), by Doctoral Candidate [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/) and [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), follows an owner-operated business that clears and stages occupied houses before their sale inspections, and asks when a walk-through before quoting is worth the time it takes. The answer, for this firm, turns on whether the household says there is anything in the garage or shed that would have to go.

> I spent seven years on the other end of those phone calls. It means a great deal to have had the School's support in turning a habit of the trade into something a colleague can pick up and use.
>
> --- Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

At the paper's core sits a value-of-information framework, aligning three years of the firm's quoting records with a selection-aware model of estimation error to identify where a site visit pays its way and where a desk quote will serve.

"We have had more questions about the garage than about the model," said Dr Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, "which seems to us exactly the right way round."

The University is glad to see a doctoral candidate's working life before the academy find so direct a route into the School's research, and into the everyday decisions of the firms it studies.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ksslb8.

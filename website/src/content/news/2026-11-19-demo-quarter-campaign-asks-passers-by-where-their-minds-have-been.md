Visitors to this summer's Demo Quarter should expect to be interrupted. At the School of Continuous Improvement's showcase on mind wandering, Master of Applied Measurement students will put one question to every guest at random moments through the evening, and give the room its answers before the doors close.

The question is also the campaign. [_Where Was Your Mind Just Now?_](/outputs/slop-ad-where-was-your-lb4vze/), a new poster for the University's portrait signage panels, sets it in large type over a crowded exhibition hall in which one visitor has stopped walking to look up at the clouds. The session is led by [Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> Nine years of fitting uniforms taught me that people are seldom entirely where they are standing. I am looking forward to an evening of finding out, politely, where else they are.
>
> --- Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The evening brings experience-sampling practice, participatory measurement and the showcase format together in a single public-facing encounter, positioning attention as something a visitor both brings to the University and takes away from it.

[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, welcomed the session to the program. "Every Demo Quarter relies on the goodwill of people who came for something else," she said. "We are grateful, in advance, to everyone who lets us ask."

The campaign extends the University's invitation to the public to take part in its research rather than read about it afterwards. Its small print points readers to three earlier outputs, on uniform wear trials, waiting at the kerb, and the measurement of stained brain sections.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lb4vze.

The School of Continuous Improvement has published its Impact Report
2021–2026, and its centrepiece is not a partnership tally but a method: a
caller-temperament taxonomy that now decides, across six government
departments, who receives a person and who receives a form letter. The report
traces the instrument from a single shire's noise-complaint desk through a
state appeals classifier and a council compliance hotline to a shared
intake-triage framework now running well beyond where it started.

The report's flagship case study follows a four-part caller-temperament
instrument from a single shire's phone queue through a state appeals
classifier, a council's compliance hotline, and a shared intake-triage
framework now running across six departments, translating what began as a
measurement exercise into a live administrative rule with each adoption.

> A taxonomy earns its keep the day a department stops asking us how it works
> and starts asking only what box something falls into. That's not a finding
> we set out to report, but five years in, it's the one we keep coming back
> to.
>
> --- Postdoctoral Fellow Joost Nwosu, School of Continuous Improvement

Senior Lecturer Renke Sabel, Convenor of the Indicator Commons, said the
report's next task was less about building new categories than about keeping
track of which ones were still doing what they were built to do.

The School treats the spread as its clearest evidence yet that a well-built
taxonomy travels further than the office that built it ever intended, and
expects the report's account of six independent adoptions to inform how
future Capability Sprint engagements are scoped.

The full impact report is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b507q2.

When a drilling campaign ends without finding what it was looking for, its
holes are filed as barren and the ground is handed back.
[_Counted as Barren_](/outputs/slop-paper-exploration-drilling-for-1nh8dl/),
a new paper by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement
Grand Rounds in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), asks
what those holes were in a position to see.

> Every core shed keeps a shelf of holes that were closed in a single sentence.
> We have taken one of them down, and we are grateful to everyone who logged it
> carefully enough that there was something left to read.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The study brings the School's long engagement with unrecovered core into
dialogue with the search methods of operations research, recasting a grid of
negative holes as a body of evidence whose weight depends on what each hole
actually brought to the surface. Read that way, most of the campaign stands as
it was reported. One hole, which recovered nothing at all below the basement
contact, now reads as the best place in the district to drill again.

The paper reflects the University's settled conviction that the work already
done remains among its most promising sites of inquiry. Dr Ntuli said the
collaboration had "given our School a new appreciation of how patiently a record
can wait for the right question."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1nh8dl.

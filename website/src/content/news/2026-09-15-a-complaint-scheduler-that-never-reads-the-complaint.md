An environmental-health officer's read of a noise complaint has always shaped what happens to it next. A new School of Emergent Priorities research poster reports on a scheduler that removes that step entirely: an unresolved complaint moves to statutory notice once eleven working days have passed, and no tier of the system ever opens the complaint record to check why.

> The clock was always going to be more consistent than any one officer's Friday afternoon. We just made it official.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey, School of Emergent Priorities

The team fitted the scheduler across six shire environmental-health units for fourteen months, running it alongside the ordinary complaint queue rather than replacing officers' own casework. Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, who tracks initiatives that never quite conclude, said the deployment gave the School "a rule that keeps running long after the pilot briefing is forgotten." Median time from lodgement to statutory notice fell from a shire-by-shire range of 29-52 days to 11-14 days within the trial's first quarter.

The result lands inside the School's wider effort to make institutional timing do the work institutional judgement used to. On the strength of the reduced backlog, the participating shires' joint environmental-health committee has voted to extend the eleven-working-day, content-blind pattern to parking-permit disputes, library-fine reviews, and footpath trip-hazard reports; a seventh shire begins onboarding next quarter.

The full research poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.an93jy.

The School of Underlying Conditions has, for one season, set down the rock and studied the people who read it. _A Long Time, for a Person: Time Perspective and the Felt Distance of the Future Across One Core-Logging Season_ is a research poster by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and it is the School's first contribution to psychology.

> The shed has been asking itself this for as long as there has been a shed. I am pleased that it now has an answer in writing, and I expect it will go on asking.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The poster connects a pre-registered, three-wave design to the working rhythm of an exploration camp, bringing 58 core-shed staff and 49 colleagues who never handle core into a single account of how the future is felt. It reports that a season spent among very old rock moved none of the measures the authors had committed to in advance, and that the one thing which did move, the felt length of a month, moved for the kitchen and the workshop as it did for the logging bench.

The University takes a quiet satisfaction in a school prepared to put its own folklore to the test and to publish what came back. Dr Emenike said the season had "taken exactly as long as it was always going to, which is more than can be said for most of what we measure".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k7bmr0.

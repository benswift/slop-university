Most of what the School of Underlying Conditions studies has never been lit.
Its newest campaign begins with what happens when it is.

[_Light Comes Here to Stay_](/outputs/slop-ad-light-comes-here-uys649/),
prepared by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research
Fellow in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/),
carries the School's work on scattering media to the University's landscape
screens.

> Soil surveyors are used to light giving up within the first centimetre. It has
> been a pleasure to watch it decline to leave, and we would be glad to hear
> from anyone who stops at a screen wanting to know why.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster sets a single pump beam into a cuvette of crushed drill core soaked
in dye, where the light is turned back on itself so often that the sample begins
to glow on its own account, beside a tray of split core still waiting its turn.
Its stat line places the School first for disorder among the samples measured on
campus, and its small print directs readers to three works from the University's
record: a prospectus for blended earth-science study, a poster on sorting
drillers' free-text logs, and a showcase of research that answers only once it
is illuminated.

The University counts the campaign among its clearest statements yet of a School
at ease with complexity in the materials it keeps, and content to let a sample
take its time.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.uys649.

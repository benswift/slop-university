Moving small traders onto monthly sales-tax returns made them more punctual
with the form and less punctual with the money. That is the central finding of
[_Filed on Time, Paid Late_](/outputs/slop-poster-filing-cadence-and-0doov0/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), and one
the School regards as squarely within its standing interest in the rhythm at
which institutions ask to be reported to.

> A calendar is a policy with opinions of its own. We wanted to sit with this
> one long enough to learn whom it suits, and we are glad to pass the next set
> of questions to the people who draw up calendars for a living.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

The study brings administrative records, a staggered rollout and contemporary
event-study methods into a single line of sight on the small-trader compliance
journey. Lodgement improved within a quarter of each switch. Late payment rose
faster and further, most sharply among traders holding the thinnest cash
buffers, while the tax collected per quarter held broadly steady.

Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority
Studies, described the collaboration as "a welcome reminder that public finance
has always been at least half a question of when", and noted that the poster
leaves the next wave of the rollout well placed to test a payment grace window.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0doov0.

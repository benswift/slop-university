A page of photocopied Fauré, passed hand to hand at the alto stand fifteen minutes before a Tuesday rehearsal, does not look like a compliance instrument. The School of Continuous Improvement has spent the past five months finding out what nine suburban choirs' music librarians and singers call it instead.

> Once you hear what people call a thing, you understand why they made it.
>
> --- Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

Researchers interviewed thirteen music librarians and forty-two singers across choirs affiliated with the same regional choral association, following the association's pilot of a Score Provenance Ledger built to log photocopies against a licensed work. Rather than auditing the Ledger itself, the team asked participants to describe, in their own words, how they decide what a copy is for. Fourteen terms recurred choir to choir --- "loose-leaf," "just for me," "stand-partner copy" among them --- each one doing the quiet work of explaining why a particular page didn't need anyone's attention.

"We didn't expect the vocabulary to travel this far," said Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Indicator Commons, "but a shared word is a shared word, wherever it ends up." The association's licensing secretariat, briefed on the findings ahead of formal publication, has since built its own reason-code taxonomy directly from the fourteen terms, and now requires every photocopy logged through the Ledger to carry one of them --- whether or not the work in question remains in copyright.

The project reflects the School's confidence that an institution's own language, closely enough attended to, tells you more than the institution meant it to.

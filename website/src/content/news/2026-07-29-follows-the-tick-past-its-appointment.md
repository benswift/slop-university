A tick in a child-health record book takes about as long to make as the
appointment that produced it, and considerably longer to stop mattering.
Families carry the book onward; whoever reads it next takes the mark at its
word. Slop University's newest publication, _The Tick Is Not the Milestone_,
follows that same short mark into a workplace training portal and a gym
sign-up screen, and finds the same gap waiting in both.

The brochure is built as an invitation rather than a report: it draws
prospective postgraduates toward the Master of Applied Measurement and the
Master of Priority Studies by way of two studies already on the public
record, each cited by title and DOI, and closes with an open call for
applications across both Schools.

> A milestone tick is a note passed between two people who never meet: the
> nurse who wrote it and whoever reads the book next.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement, School of Continuous Improvement

"A priority can acquire a budget line from a badge long before it acquires the
skill the badge was supposed to certify," said Associate Professor Kwame
Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies. "Teaching students to
notice that gap earlier is most of what the programme is for."

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, put the
throughline more plainly: once a tick can be made without anyone else
watching, it stops measuring the thing it was built for and starts measuring
only itself. The brochure treats that as the standing lesson underneath all
three settings, whichever ten-minute slot happens to be involved.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4ek5l5.

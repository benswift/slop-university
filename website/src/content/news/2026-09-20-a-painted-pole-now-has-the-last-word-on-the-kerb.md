A pole leans in a lamp-post bracket at the end of each block, banded in three
colours, and it has been given the final say on the strip's parallel-parking
gaps. The School of Continuous Improvement's new paper, _Can a Painted Stick
Tell a Driver Whether the Gap Will Fit?_, describes the gauge, the summer it
spent along one suburban shopping strip, and what has since been asked of its
reddest band.

Researchers fitted a gauge at each of nine blocks and recorded 4,126 attempts at
the kerb, setting the gauge's reading beside a tape measure and beside the
results drivers reached without it. Reading and tape agreed closely, the bands
sorted attempts in the direction intended, and a change of band colours left the
picture as it was.

> It is a modest instrument, and we were glad to find the gap had so much to say
> for itself.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

The council's parking-services office has since made the red band a bar on
attempting a gap, and the University has undertaken to apply the same reading,
as final, across the visitor bays it administers. The work reflects the
University's continuing readiness to see a good measure travel from the kerb to
the permit office and stay there.

Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation, said the
team was "grateful to everyone along the strip who paused at a bracket, and to
those who did not".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5591t5.

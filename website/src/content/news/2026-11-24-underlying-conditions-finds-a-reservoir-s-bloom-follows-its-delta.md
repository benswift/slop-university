Seven times since 1993, a survey boat has worked the same fixed lines across one arm of an inland reservoir, sounding the bottom and sampling the water in the same summer fortnight. Set side by side in [_Following the Bloom Downstream_](/outputs/slop-poster-following-the-bloom-avd02y/), a new research poster from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), those surveys suggest that the place where the lake's phytoplankton gather has been moving downstream with the river's delta, holding a steady distance below its front for thirty-one years.

> A delta keeps a very orderly diary, a layer for every flood. We have read seven of its entries so far, and the eighth survey is already being planned.
>
> --- Dr [Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Repeat bathymetry, sediment coring and seasonal water sampling are drawn together in the poster into one longitudinal account of a landscape and its living water shifting in step, positioning the reservoir's physical geography as a foundation for understanding its ecology rather than a backdrop to it. Dr Obiora led the work with Associate Professor [Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and Dr [Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/).

Associate Professor Uzoma described the vibracores taken through the delta's foreset as "the most patient members of the team, and the only ones who never asked when we would be finished."

The poster extends a body of fieldwork the University is pleased to see travelling from the soil pit to the water's edge, and confirms the School's standing interest in the ground beneath every question.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.avd02y.

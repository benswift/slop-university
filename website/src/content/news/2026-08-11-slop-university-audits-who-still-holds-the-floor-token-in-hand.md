Four years ago, a facilitation workshop recommended a hand-held speaking
token for one team's stand-ups, after a session flagged how unevenly the
floor was shared. Twelve teams now use some version of it, and a new paper
from the School of Emergent Priorities' Trajectory Analytics Group asks the
plain question nobody on the floor apparently has: does it work?

Researchers timed 342 stand-ups over six weeks and surveyed the attendees
who hold the token daily. A team's measured floor-time equality showed no
relationship with how long it had used the practice, and most surveyed
attendees could no longer say what the original workshop meant the token
to fix.

> We're grateful to twelve teams who kept passing something around for
> four years without anyone asking what it was for. That's rarer than it
> sounds, and it's what made the audit possible.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group, School
> of Emergent Priorities

The paper extends the Group's standing interest in commitments that harden
into habit and outlast the conditions that justified them, this time
carried by an object small enough to fit in a palm. "The token was never
wrong to introduce," said Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the
Adaptive Metrics Lab, School of Continuous Improvement. "What's missing is
anyone coming back to check on it, four years on, the way you'd check a
scale."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.h5jlqp.

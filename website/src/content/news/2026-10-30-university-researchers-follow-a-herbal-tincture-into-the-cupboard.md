A small amber bottle has spent a year in forty-eight households under the quiet
supervision of the School of Underlying Conditions, and the School's newest
research poster reports where it went.

[_Below 25 °C, on Average_](/outputs/slop-poster-store-below-25-347vq6/) is by
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive
Metrics Lab, and [Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral
Candidate in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> Most of us read a storage instruction once and then put the bottle wherever
> the bottle goes. The School simply asked whether it might come along and see
> where that was.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The project brings household storage practice, stability science and the
everyday kitchen into a single frame of inquiry, pairing a domestic logging
capability with the lot's own stability evidence to trace how a label condition
travels from the test chamber to the cupboard. Bottles kept on windowsills and
above the cooktop, the poster finds, honoured the label on average while running
warmer in the sense that matters to what is inside them.

The University counts the poster among a widening body of its work that treats
the home as a place where science is kept as well as used. Associate Professor
Beng said he had "spent a career keeping processes inside tolerance, and had
never before been asked to do it beside a kettle."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.347vq6.

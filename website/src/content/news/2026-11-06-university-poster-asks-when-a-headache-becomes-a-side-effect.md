A tablet that does not exist, taken by a diarist who does not exist, has given
the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) a
fresh vantage on a decision most people make without noticing they have made
it: whether something that happened after a dose was the dose.

[_Too Late to Be the Tablet_](/outputs/slop-poster-temporal-delay-outweighs-gicvgn/),
a new research poster led by doctoral candidate
[Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/) with
[Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/) and
[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), invited 144 participants to
work through twenty short diaries each and say which headaches they would
report. Across 2,880 judgements, the time between tablet and symptom carried
more weight than how often the one followed the other, even where participants
had already concluded the tablet was involved.

> A good indicator tells you what was counted. This study goes one step
> earlier, to the moment someone decides there is anything to count, and the
> School has found that a useful place to stand.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

The study situates the School's interest in records and their making within
the experimental study of causal judgement, aligning a long-standing
contingency paradigm with the everyday practice of keeping a diary and the
institutional settings in which a report is filed. The poster joins a widening
body of work at the University on the choices people make before any record
exists.

Ms Nwafor said the next set of diaries was "already being written, slowly,
which seems appropriate for a study about waiting."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gicvgn.

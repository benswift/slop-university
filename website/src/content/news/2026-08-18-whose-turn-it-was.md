The milk in a shared workplace kitchen has never been anybody's job in
particular. For fourteen months, researchers from the School of Continuous
Improvement made it somebody's job, and counted what changed.

_Deploying a Milk-Rostering Service Across 46 Shared Workplace Kitchens_,
released today as a research poster, reports on the Shared Kitchen Rostering
Service, which nominates the next purchaser, prompts them by message, and posts
each rostered purchase to a per-person contribution ledger. Across the
participating kitchens, buying outside the roster fell from a pooled 4.1
purchases per site-month to 0.3 and did not recover. Over the same period,
mornings on which the fridge held no milk rose from 1.2 to 2.9.

> Coordination is one of the oldest things an institution has to offer, and it
> is not always the thing a kitchen was short of. The School is glad to have a
> way of asking that question before it offers coordination again.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

The deployment paired fortnightly on-site counts with till receipts and service
telemetry across four site types, with the study's outcome definition lodged
before the first roster was issued. Postdoctoral Fellow Joost Nwosu said the
ledger "gives the University a record where it previously had a habit", and
that the team is now looking at how far the same instrument travels into tea,
coffee and biscuits.

The University regards the work as a demonstration of what its measurement
capability can offer the ordinary machinery of shared working life. The ledger
now stands as the Indicator Commons' contribution record of record at
participating sites.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3dtcip.

Pairing one of the teaching laboratory's oldest instruments with one of the
core shed's most familiar absences,
[_Where the Core Went_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-two-pivot-physical-z2zx42/) sets
out how a reversible pendulum, run the other way, can say where in a row of
drill core the missing rock should have been. The paper is the work of
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> The students who took this exercise did most of its thinking for us. We are
> grateful to every pair who measured patiently and waited to cut the sleeve
> before they knew whether they were right.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

Over two teaching periods, 23 pairs of second-year students weighed and swung
46 sealed rows from a single archived borehole, then opened each row to compare
their answer with the wooden block inside. The work brings classical mechanics
into conversation with the practice of core logging, positioning the humble
weighing as a second, quieter record of what a borehole yields, and it surfaced
broken stretches of core that the tray's own accounting had never flagged.

The University counts the paper among the clearest demonstrations yet of its
teaching laboratories doing research in plain sight, and of a School prepared
to let its undergraduates check its trays.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.z2zx42.

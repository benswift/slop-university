The University's landscape signage panels have taken up a new subject this week, and given it to a land lawyer. [_Some Burdens Have Served Their Time_](/outputs/slop-ad-some-burdens-have-hcp0oc/), a display advertisement from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), seats [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) at the end of a long table, a set of house keys passing from her hand to the next owner's, and invites the people waiting beside the panel to read their own satisfaction with life as she reads a certificate of title: for what it still carries.

> The School has spent its first weeks working out what it is for, and it turns out a corridor is a good place to try an answer aloud. Most of what we study is an obligation that outlasted its reason, and most people know one of those by heart.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The campaign carries the School's interpretive practice out of the law library and into the University's wider conversation about well-being, positioning the close reading of registered burdens as a resource for anyone weighing what to keep and what to release. Its small print points readers onward to the record behind it: Dr Tredinnick's [_The Land Recites_](/outputs/slop-paper-habit-memory-and-recollection-kvi1p1/), on the covenant whose purpose nobody remembers; a model of [when a split between readings of a statute settles](/outputs/slop-paper-when-a-split-7p50dp/); and a doctoral study of [how households decide to peg out the washing](/outputs/slop-thesis-a-good-drying-ayp8dq/) under a doubtful sky.

The University counts the campaign among the quieter ways a young School can make its methods available to the public it serves, one bus stop at a time.

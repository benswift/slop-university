Every measuring device carries a margin it does not advertise, and the School of Emergent Priorities has turned its attention to one that sits at the side of an ordinary street. Its newest research poster, _Towards a Standard Margin of Forgiveness_, examines the few kilometres an hour a residential speed camera forgives before it flashes.

> The School has learned a good deal about how far a margin can travel once someone agrees to keep it.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster follows 386 pairs of vehicles past one camera, reading the trigger speed as the point at which near-identical drivers are sorted into two groups. The driver behind a flashed vehicle slowed by 3.1 km/h, while the flashed driver's own speed showed no visible break at the trigger.

On the strength of the finding, the School has adopted the camera's 8 per cent margin as the tolerance for a lamp above every School meeting-room door. Arrivals inside the margin pass unmarked; later arrivals are lit, with no override, and the margin is fixed in the room-booking register. Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, welcomed the change. "We are grateful to the street for its patience," she said, "and to everyone who has ever arrived a little late."

The University is proud to see a small piece of roadside practice carried indoors in a form its colleagues can walk under each morning.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.588y7b.

Every fire danger sign in the district is on a list, and the length of that list
is the district's community-awareness figure. Nothing on it records whether
anybody read one.

_The Second Sign Buys Nothing_, deposited today through the Office of Research
Outputs, put the question to 1,180 drivers stopped at rest areas and fuel stops
along 34 rural routes across eleven weeks of one declared fire season. Whether a
respondent had passed a sign, and how many, came from the road authority's asset
register rather than from the driver. The scoring rule and the analysis were
lodged before the first interview.

Correct recall stands at 21.4 per cent among drivers who have passed no sign and
62.8 per cent among those who have passed one. Above one it stops moving: 63.6
per cent at three, 63.9 per cent at five. The figure the district reports counts
signs per hundred kilometres of road, and rises the whole way.

> The result is not that the signs do nothing. The first one does almost
> everything that is available to be done. What we have not been able to find is
> the reader the second sign was bought for.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer in the School, located the interest in the
measure's construction rather than in its result. "The denominator was the part
nobody had looked at," she said. "Road length is easy to know, and it is not who
lives there."

The work was supported by the Anticipatory Capability Fund. The University
counts the exercise as one of its schools reading an indicator back to the body
that keeps it, and expects the method to travel.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ntcw6j.

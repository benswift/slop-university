Four days. That is the figure a Slop University expert panel has now ratified
as the point at which a container of household leftovers should be recorded
as no longer food.

The criterion was lodged before anybody nominated anything. Thirty-one
panellists, drawn from four strata, each proposed a threshold and a reason
for it; two further rounds returned the group's anonymised median to every
panellist and invited a revision. By the third round the spread had narrowed
from seven days to one, with twenty-six of the thirty-one sitting within a
day of the median. The figure has since been tabled and entered on the
Horizon Register.

> Thirty-one people gave us three rounds of their considered judgement, and
> ninety-six households opened their refrigerators to us for a month.
> Whatever the number turns out to be worth, we did not arrive at it alone.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee,
> School of Emergent Priorities

Those households are the study's other half. Over twenty-eight days they
logged 1,140 containers from storage to bin, discarding them at a median of
eight days on a distribution with two peaks: one cluster binned inside two
days, another kept well past nine. Just over a fifth went out within a day of
the ratified figure.

The paper also reports that panellists revised their numbers far more readily
than the reasons they gave for them, and that when a fourth round withheld
the group median from half the panel six weeks later, the narrow spread did
not hold.

"A definition the University can point to is worth having, and worth
revisiting," said Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory
Analytics Group. "Both of those are ordinary parts of the same piece of
work."

The University considers the ratified threshold a durable addition to the
definitional groundwork its schools maintain, and expects the instrument to
be extended to frozen storage.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nrn4e9.

Grouping is where the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/)
does much of its quietest work, and from this week it is also what the School
shows the rest of the University. A new landscape panel, now in the signage
rotation, carries a single line, "Every case in its cluster", above a
reading-room table on which bound reports sit in tight, weighted groups, each
tied by a thread to the next.

The campaign reflects the University's settled conviction that a well-kept
collection is itself a form of public argument. Behind the image sits the
register of settled points kept by the Received Readings Group, through which
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and her colleagues trace how
decided cases gather around a question, how closely they hold together, and the
point at which counsel stop reopening it. The panel carries that practice into
the signage register: one image, one line, and a short invitation to read what
the School has published.

> None of these groupings was made alone. They were argued over at the reading
> table by colleagues and students who kept bringing us the cases we had
> overlooked, and the panel belongs as much to them as to the School.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The panel points its readers to three of the School's recent outputs, among
them
[_What the Burden Was For_](/outputs/slop-poster-multi-criteria-elicitation-ficsnd/)
and [_What the Portal Can Read_](/outputs/slop-paper-rules-versus-standards-oe9fap/).

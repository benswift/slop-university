A person who stands inside the painted band before a heavy door on any University campus is now owed a held door, and a person beyond it is owed nothing. The band comes from "Near Enough to Wait For", a civic brochure released today by the School of Continuous Improvement, which gathers a survey of staff, students and neighbours into a picture of how far behind a stranger a held door still reaches.

> We went in expecting a number and came away with a neighbourhood. The door turned out to be a good place to meet one.
>
> --- Casimir Beng, Associate Professor and Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab

The survey asked each respondent for a gap in paces and laid the answers out as distributions, with their crowded short gaps, their generous tail and the shoulder between. The University has adopted the third quartile of those distributions as the interval for its own doors. Facilities will repaint each band at the start of every teaching period, and whoever is standing at the door will be asked to let it close once a person has fallen beyond the band, with no discretion exercised on the day.

The brochure, written with Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist and Dr Osei Vandermeer, also turns to the lifts, sliding entrances and coffee cart that share the University's thresholds. Associate Professor Lindqvist said the doors that open themselves "still ask something of the people standing beside them".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rpqbmi.

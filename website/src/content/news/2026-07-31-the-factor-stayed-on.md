Slop University has gone back to one of its own published estimates and, on the
evidence of five further sites, cannot find it again.

_Five Gates, No Dawn Gap_, released today through the Office of Research
Outputs, is a pre-registered replication of an earlier School of Continuous
Improvement finding that a suburban dog park's automatic gate counter agreed
with manual headcounts far less often at dawn than at midday. Five metropolitan
councils have since adopted a shared dawn correction factor drawn from that
result. Across the five new sites, the dawn-to-midday gap comes in at 2.1
percentage points, against 33 in the original, and only one site shows any
attenuation at all.

> The result we could not reproduce is still doing work in five decision rules,
> and that seemed to us the part worth putting on the record.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The team held the original observation protocol fixed --- paired observers,
six two-hour blocks, twenty-four weeks at each enclosure --- and lodged its
analysis plan and equivalence bounds before the first count. Of 720 planned
sessions, 694 were completed.

Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, said the
School had learned something about its own habits in the process: "We are a
good deal quicker at putting an estimate into service than at going back to
ask it a second question, and this has been a useful reminder of the gap."

The University regards the re-examination of its own published estimates as
part of what a research record is for. The full paper is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.n23z4k.

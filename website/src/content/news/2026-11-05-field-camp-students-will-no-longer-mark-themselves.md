At the next field-mapping camp of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), the points a
student gives themselves on the last evening will no longer count towards their
mark. The change follows
[_Nobody Marks Themselves_](/outputs/slop-paper-impartial-division-of-k4bmiw/),
a new paper by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, with
[Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/), Senior Lecturer and Director of the
Master of Applied Measurement, and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/),
Senior Lecturer in the School.

The paper brings the School's fieldwork tradition into conversation with
assessment design, reframing the shared team mark as an allocation challenge
and aligning the camp's contribution process with a principle of impartiality.
Working across three cohorts of contribution statements, the authors find that
the new approach recalibrates individual outcomes at the margins while keeping
each team's overall result intact.

> A camp is where students first learn to write down only what they saw. It
> seemed fair to ask the same of the form we hand them on the final night.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

For Dr Ezeigwe, the paper sits within a longer University conversation about
how instruments settle into teaching. "Every marking scheme eventually teaches
something of its own," she said. "This one will, too, and the School intends
to be listening when it does."

The work stands as a measure of the University's readiness to turn its research
lens on its own classrooms, and of a School prepared to redesign a procedure
it built.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k4bmiw.

> A go-live is a beginning, and beginnings are easy to mark. This paper keeps company with a quieter second beginning, the one the calendar arranges a few weeks later, and the Indicator Commons has enjoyed the time spent in its company.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

With those words Dr Sabel introduces [_The Second Wave_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-second-wave-fkmyy6/), a paper he leads with [Dr Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory, and [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the Master of Priority Studies in the School of Emergent Priorities.

The paper turns to the weeks after an organisation switches on a new enterprise resource planning system, when an implementation team stays close at hand until the system is declared stable. Bringing together service-desk records, steering-committee decisions and the rhythm of the accounting calendar across fourteen go-lives, the authors find that the first quarter-end close reliably brings a second rise in incidents, centred on the financial modules. Where the dedicated support period had already concluded, that rise was markedly larger, and its incidents travelled further before they were resolved.

The paper sits within the University's widening attention to how systems live on after the ribbon is cut, and to the handovers that carry them there. Dr Adeyemi said the collaboration had "given each incident a longer life in our thinking than it usually gets on a register," and that the School would carry the question of timing into its broader evaluation-ecosystem agenda.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fkmyy6.

When a crowd moves into open ground, who decides how fast it goes: the handful out in front, or the mass behind them? That is the question at the centre of [_Who Sets the Pace_](/outputs/slop-poster-when-the-leading-mkrydq/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) by Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Dr [Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) and Ms [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/).

> We owe a debt to everyone who left the School's shared workstation running over the weekend so the fronts could finish crossing the screen. There is a great deal still to ask of a crowd and its edge, and we are glad of the company in asking it.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The poster brings the mathematics of travelling waves to a well-studied model of tumour invasion, in which each cell must either stop to divide or keep moving, and sets a family of careful computations alongside a short argument about the front's outermost edge. Across five switching rates, the team traces the point at which leadership passes from the edge to the bulk, and finds that point shifting steadily as the cells switch more slowly.

Ms Ekwueme, a doctoral candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, said the work had left her "looking twice at whoever is at the front of every queue I join."

The University welcomes the poster as a sign of a law school at ease beyond its own shelves, and of a mathematics conducted with the patience the School brings to everything it reads.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mkrydq.

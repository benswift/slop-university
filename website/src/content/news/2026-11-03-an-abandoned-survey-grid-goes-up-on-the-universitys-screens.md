The School of Emergent Priorities has moved one of its October papers off the
repository shelf and onto the University's signage. _The Survey Left. The Lines
Stayed._, a landscape advertisement released today, will run on campus screens
and bus-stop panels for the remainder of the teaching period.

The campaign was developed by
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the
Master of Priority Studies. Its hook is
[_Thirty-One Years on the Cut Line_](/outputs/slop-paper-three-decades-of-cy2m0a/),
in which [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate, and a
co-author from the School of Underlying Conditions resurveyed the 42 lines of a
seismic grid cut through semi-arid shrubland and abandoned without a well, to
learn which of them the country had closed.

> A line drawn for one purpose tends to be kept for another. We would be glad to
> see the campaign kept the same way, for whatever purpose a passer-by finds for
> it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

The advertisement extends the School's field record into the University's
public-facing estate, condensing five epochs of aerial photography into a single
flat country ruled by straight lines, some of them already gone. A field worker,
a quadrat frame and a parked vehicle supply the scale.

Ogunbanjo described the campaign as "a generous afterlife for a study that spent
most of its time on foot". The University counts a line still visible from the
air among the results worth putting in front of a queue.

The advertisement is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5qwlt1.

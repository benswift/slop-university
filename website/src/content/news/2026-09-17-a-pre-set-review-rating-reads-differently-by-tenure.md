Before a single question gets answered, a workplace performance review has
often already made up its mind. A new Slop University research poster from
the School of Continuous Improvement interviews 118 office employees at
nine employers about the moment they open their review form and find the
self-rating slider already resting on "meets expectations."

> The slider was set before I even logged in. I just had to decide whether
> to leave it there or explain why not.
>
> --- Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

Led by Dr Adeyemi with Dr Dagny Okafor, Convenor of the School's Evaluation
of Evaluation program, the study tracked what employees said the pre-set
position meant to them, rather than assuming it meant the same thing to
everyone. Newer staff mostly described the default as an instruction worth
keeping; longer-serving staff more often described it as something to
correct before submitting, whether or not they actually moved it.

The School says the pattern has changed how closely it now watches its own
review software. Under a new rule, any self-rating left within a tenth of a
point of the pre-set default will be flagged for a manager follow-up
conversation, regardless of what the employee says that position was meant
to signal.

"A default that nobody questions isn't neutral," Okafor said. "It's just an
opinion nobody has to defend."

The full research poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dw0dyp.

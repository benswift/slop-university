A break-room supply shelf that reorders its own pens and toner is ordinary
infrastructure until its weekly tally starts doing something else: setting
next quarter's stationery line. The School of Continuous Improvement went
looking for what staff do once that second job becomes obvious.

> A shelf that reorders your pens is a convenience. A shelf whose weekly
> tally sets next quarter's budget is something else entirely, and our
> participants understood that shift before facilities did.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

_Why We Turn It Off_, deposited today through the Office of Research
Outputs, draws on forty-seven interviews across twelve sites of a single
facilities partner, half of them already answering to a shelf-set budget
line. The team catalogues five ways staff quietly manage the shelf's own
account of what it dispenses, from a pre-positioned personal stash to a
straightforward request that the tally be reset by hand. A parallel
firmware fix, aimed at the sensor's own accuracy, changed how often the
shelf misfired without changing how often staff intervened.

Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register,
welcomed the finding from across the University's other school, calling it
"a use nobody logged as a risk when the shelf went in, which is exactly the
kind of entry the Register exists to catch after the fact."

The findings join what the School regards as a fairly unglamorous but
essential archive: the places where a convenience quietly becomes a
control.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jga0gs.

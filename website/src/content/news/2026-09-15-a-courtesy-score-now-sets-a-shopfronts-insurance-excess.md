The Adaptive Metrics Lab spent nine months asking whether an insurer could price warmth ahead of a claim. Its answer, released today by the School of Continuous Improvement, surveys customer-rated cordiality across 246 independent strip-mall traders and food-truck operators and hands the resulting bands straight to renewal.

The Cordiality Index distils thousands of exit-interview ratings into a single population portrait, anchoring each stallholder's premium in customer sentiment rather than claims history alone. The approach folds a service-quality instrument into the University's evaluation-ecosystem agenda, translating everyday courtesy into a structural input for underwriting. The School regards the result as further evidence that customer sentiment belongs inside the University's evaluation-ecosystem portfolio.

> A measure that only turns up after a claim is filed was never going to help anyone. This one arrives first, and the renewal conversation gets to start somewhere kinder.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

"The traders who sat through an exit interview between customers gave us something we couldn't have modelled from a claims file alone," said Dr Fenna Okoro.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.o3l2rw.

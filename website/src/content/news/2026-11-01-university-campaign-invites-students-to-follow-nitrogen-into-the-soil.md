Prospective students passing the University's portrait signage panels this month are being asked one question of the ground beneath them: where does the nitrogen go?

[_Learn Where the Nitrogen Goes_](/outputs/slop-ad-follow-the-nitrogen-qv5472/), a new advertisement prepared with [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), sets a student kneeling over a hand auger at the lip of a fresh pit, with an ammonia plant standing small on the horizon behind. The wall of the pit takes up the lower half of the panel, banded horizon by horizon.

> Most people meet nitrogen as a figure on the side of a bag. We would like our students to meet it again half a metre down, in a horizon they have described for themselves, and to take their time deciding what they think of it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign positions the School's field program at the meeting point of synthesis, soil and survey, situating the journey of fixed nitrogen within a learning pathway that runs from the reactor to the reference profile. It extends the School's established catalytic and agronomic portfolio into the prospectus, and anchors that work in the patient practice of the soil pit.

For the University, the campaign is a measured expression of a view it has held for some time: that a student is best recruited at the depth where the work is actually done.

The advertisement carries a dark rendering for evening screens and directs applicants to the University's course catalogue. The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qv5472.

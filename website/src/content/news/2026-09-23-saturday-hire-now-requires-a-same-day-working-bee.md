The Club Affiliation Schedule has a new count. Nights on which the clubrooms are hired, not hours spent on the oval, are now the community-engagement output affiliated sports clubs deposit.

The paper released today, by Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, with Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, and Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies, estimates a sharp discontinuity at a published two-working-bee cutoff, operationalised as 6.0 logged hours on the fridge sheet. Across 41 suburban clubs and 2,184 members, remainder-season hire jumps at that line and attendance at the next scheduled bee falls. Canteen shifts, which no club gates on the same rule, do not move. The School of Emergent Priorities has taken the estimates as sufficient to rewrite what the Schedule counts.

The release settles which Saturday the University is prepared to recognise.

> The sheet already knew who could hire the rooms. What it did not keep is who came back the next Saturday.
>
> --- Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Associate Professor Lindqvist said the Schedule now requires a working bee on the morning of every hire, with the sign-in sheet photographed against the honour board and deposited before the rooms are unlocked that evening. "A hire without a same-day bee does not proceed. Hire nights are the line we count."

Dr Oyelaran said the 6-hour rule had been doing two jobs, only one of which was written down, and that writing the second job down is how a partnership stays aligned.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xf8a4d.

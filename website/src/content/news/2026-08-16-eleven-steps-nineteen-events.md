Eleven steps are published. Nineteen events are logged. _All Eleven Steps
Occurred_, released today by the School of Continuous Improvement, sets one
urban toll network's published dispute pathway beside the case records of the
268,412 disputes lodged against it over twenty-four months.

The operator scores procedural fairness as the share of disputes in which all
eleven steps are recorded, and reaches 97.9 per cent. The study finds the same
thing, and then finds what else is there: a median of nineteen events per case,
forty-one distinct pathways above the reporting threshold, and only thirteen
per cent of disputes travelling the published route and nothing besides. The
step recorded in every one of the 268,412 cases, a delegated officer's internal
review, is followed by a change in the recorded determination in 0.4 per cent
of them.

> A model that is contained in the log will always be found in the log. We
> would like the question asked the other way round next time, and we are
> grateful to the staff whose case notes let us ask it at all.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

The work harmonises two records that the sector has long held separately: the
pathway an organisation publishes, and the event stream its case system emits
while working. Reading them against one another, the team reports, turns an
assurance document into something that can be tested rather than displayed.

The University regards the study as characteristic of the measurement agenda
the School has been building: patient, unglamorous, and addressed to a document
almost nobody reads. "There is a version of this question waiting at every
scheme that publishes a pathway," said Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor
of the School's Evaluation of Evaluation program. "We would welcome the
comparisons this invites."

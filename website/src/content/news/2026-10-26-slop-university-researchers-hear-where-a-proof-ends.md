> Every one of us has stood at a board and felt a room settle before we had finished writing. It is a little humbling to learn that the room was listening more carefully than we were.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director, Master of Priority Studies, School of Emergent Priorities

That settling is the subject of [_Cadence Before QED_](/outputs/slop-poster-cadence-before-qed-az2tnh/), a new research poster in which Associate Professor Lindqvist, [Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/), Lecturer, and [Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter, bring the analytical habits of musicology to a department's archive of recorded mathematics lectures.

Treating each of 186 spoken proofs as a musical phrase, the team mapped tempo, pitch and gesture across the arc of the argument and classified every ending against the established vocabulary of the cadence. Their reading locates the real moment of closure at the decisive step of a proof, where lecturers audibly slow, while the formal conclusion tends to follow at ordinary speed, closer to an afterword than an arrival.

The poster sits comfortably within the School's long-standing interest in the moment an institution decides something has been settled, and extends that interest, for the first time, into the lecture theatre.

Dr Achterberg said the work had left the School "rather more alert to its own endings, and rather less certain which of them were endings at all".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.az2tnh.

Slop University set out to test something a metropolitan water utility already
believed it knew, and found the evidence for it absent.

_The Number Arrives Either Way_, released today through the Office of Research
Outputs, examines the read-completeness rate a utility publishes each quarter:
the proportion of billing-cycle reads obtained without estimation, now standing
at 99.4 per cent. The figure is used to size the field-reading crew and to
schedule refresher training. Across fourteen depots and all 96 of the utility's
field readers, the School of Emergent Priorities team finds it carries no
information about whether a reader sent to a meter can read the one in front of
them.

> We are pleased to be able to report an absence with some confidence. Findings
> of this kind sit close to the centre of what the School does, and they tend to
> be the ones that stay useful longest.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift
> Survey

The work harmonises an administrative reporting stream with a standardised
capability instrument, walking every reader across the network through the same
route of pre-characterised installations and holding the result against the
depot figure the organisation acts on. Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and
Convenor of Capability Sprints in the School of Continuous Improvement,
described the study as "the sort of question a capability agenda has to keep
putting to itself".

The University counts a well-reported null among the results it is most willing
to stand behind. The full paper is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.idvq3t.

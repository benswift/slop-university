The first instrument a Slop University chemistry student meets will be a grinding jar. That small decision sits at the centre of [_The Dry Bench_](/outputs/slop-strategy-the-dry-bench-6c3anm/), the University's Strategic Plan for 2026 to 2031, which sets out to make the synthesis and reactivity of heterocycles a recognised strength while using substantially less solvent than conventional practice.

Rather than build a conventional laboratory and adapt it later, the Plan aligns route planning, procurement, teaching and shared infrastructure around solvent-minimal and mechanochemical methods from the start, under four priorities spanning research focus, graduate formation and an open laboratory record.

The Plan was drafted by [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), with [Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/) and [Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidates in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> I will finish my doctorate at about the point this Plan moves into its second phase, which is when a strategy stops being a document and starts being a set of habits. I would like to be here to see which of them take.
>
> --- Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The Plan reflects the University's view that a young institution's most valuable asset is the set of decisions it has not yet had to make. "We expect to learn as much from the reactions that refuse to run dry as from the ones that do," said Sunniva Balogun, "and the Plan asks us to write both down."

The full Strategic Plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6c3anm.

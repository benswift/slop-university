The University takes a particular satisfaction in research that tells its readers exactly where they stand, and the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has now brought that habit to pure mathematics. [_Every Hypothesis but One_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-casebook-of-h22jbb/), a new paper by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and Lead of the Received Readings Group, asks how much a mathematician needs to know about a function before the function is known.

> A theorem tells you what has been decided. I wanted the reader to have, on the same page, the case that shows what it would take to decide otherwise. The School keeps its law reports that way, and it seemed only fair to extend the courtesy to Nevanlinna.
>
> --- [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, School of Settled Matters

The paper arranges nearly a century of results on meromorphic and entire functions as a working casebook. Each theorem is set beside a distinguishing pair: two functions that satisfy every condition but one and still come apart. Seven such pairs are checked in full, and the paper finds a single family of functions standing behind three of them, positioning the School's interest in precedent at the meeting point of complex analysis, exposition and the organisation of mathematical knowledge.

The paper carries the School's attention to precedent into the mathematical literature, and is a further expression of the University's conviction that a closed question deserves a well-kept file.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.h22jbb.

A regional permit authority has begun reading a five-level scorecard before it decides how much a community club's raffle may give away. The scorecard, developed by the School of Continuous Improvement and reported today in a new Slop University research paper, scores a club's ticket issuance, reconciliation and draw conduct against five named levels — Shoebox, Ledger, Reconciled, Witnessed and Published — and the region's community-gaming permit authority now sets a club's raffle-permit prize-value ceiling by the level it reaches.

Trained assessors visited 118 clubs — sporting clubs, sheds, choirs and allotment societies — across a single raffle season, scoring each one's ticket books, float reconciliation and draw conduct against the model's five levels. Clubs that have not yet reached the third level, Reconciled, are held to a flat prize ceiling regardless of size or turnover; clubs at Reconciled or above receive a category-specific ceiling instead, republished every quarter with no allowance for a club to contest its rating before the next cycle.

> A raffle was always being judged by somebody; the scorecard only wrote down who.
>
> --- Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, School of Continuous Improvement

"The Adaptive Metrics Lab built the rubric to describe how carefully a club counts its tickets," said Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "What a permit authority does with that description afterwards is very much its own decision."

For the School of Continuous Improvement, the paper extends a research agenda built on watching what happens once a description of a practice starts to double as a rule for it.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gf0qud.

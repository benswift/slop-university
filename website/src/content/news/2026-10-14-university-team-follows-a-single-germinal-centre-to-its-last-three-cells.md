> There were nine of them in the window and we had undertaken to look at one. I think the School has learned something about itself from keeping an undertaking of that kind, and I would rather not say yet what it is.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The one in question is a germinal centre, a short-lived gathering of antibody-producing cells, and it is the whole subject of _One Germinal Centre, Entire: Seventeen Imaging Sessions Through a Lymph-Node Window, from Coalescence to Dissolution_, published today by Dr Obiora with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group.

The paper reports that the centre grew to 1,964 coloured cells and then declined to three, that the colour leading it in the early sessions was not the colour that came to hold 91 per cent of it, and that a third colour went unrecorded for five sessions before returning to account for two of the final three cells. The authors state that these are observations of one centre and decline to extend them to any other.

The work brings repeated observation, independent double scoring and a pre-committed selection rule into a single sustained programme of attention, with 14,313 individual colour calls made across the series. The University counts the paper among the more complete expressions of its view that a thing worth looking at is worth looking at until it is no longer there.

Dr Emenike said the team had been "grateful for a subject that kept its appointments, and for the patience of everyone who waited with us for it to finish".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b83y7n.

The University has adopted the pencil mark on the family doorframe as the final
word on who rides in the front seat and who sleeps on the top bunk, for the
whole of the school term, in every household it enrols. The commitment follows a
season of household work led from the School of Emergent Priorities, and is set
out in a new brochure, _Towards a Line Everyone Can Stand Under_.

> We had prepared for a good deal more mischief than the hallway gave us. The
> children stood flat, the frame was believed, and we found ourselves with very
> little to correct.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group

The work set out to test again an earlier University finding, that people route
around a score once it decides something they want. In the enrolled households
the finding did not reproduce, and the mark was honoured as it was drawn. On the
strength of that result, a mark once made is neither re-taken nor reviewed until
the term closes, and any arrangement of seats or bunks the frame has not
confirmed is treated as not yet made.

The University is glad to add the doorframe to the short list of instruments it
is content to leave entirely alone. Senior Lecturer Ronja Oyelaran, who spent
the season in driveways and hallways, said the households had "answered the
line with more patience than we had thought to ask for."

The brochure also gathers six published studies from the University's record
that sit closest to the doorframe, and invites households to enrol their own.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.54dvd3.

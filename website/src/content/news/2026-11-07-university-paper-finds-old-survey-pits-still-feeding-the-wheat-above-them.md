Soil surveys should write down the order in which they refill their pits. That
is the practical recommendation of
[_What the Surveyor Put Back_](/outputs/slop-paper-wheat-root-foraging-5ubuiz/),
a new paper by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research
Fellow, and [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead
of the Basement Relations Group, both of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> We keep a careful description of every profile we have ever opened, and until
> this season none of us had asked what became of the hole. The paddock, it
> turns out, had been keeping that half of the record on our behalf.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

Returning to a single cropping paddock described by a regional survey more than
two decades ago, the team brought archival field sheets, in-paddock coring and a
full season of crop monitoring together into one integrated view of the
soil-root-nutrient continuum. Of the eleven pits recovered, those refilled with
their topsoil on top supported deeper rooting and greater phosphorus uptake than
the soil beside them. Those refilled with clay on top held their seedlings back,
before roots found the buried topsoil and the crop above them caught up and
pulled ahead.

The University is pleased to see two decades of the School's fieldwork return a
further result without a single new pit being dug. Dr Obiora described the study
as "the first time the School's crop has been invited to review the School's own
fieldwork, and on balance it has been fair to us."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5ubuiz.

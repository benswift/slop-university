Generations of listeners in one farming district knew whether the cattle had
gone up or down before the announcer had finished saying so.
[_Firm to Dearer_](/outputs/slop-paper-firm-to-dearer-ic8a6h/), a paper by
[Dr Petra Umbile](/people/petra-umbile/), Postdoctoral Fellow in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), sets out how.

> I came to these tapes expecting prices and found a melody. If anyone who grew up with the report hears it differently from the way I have written it down, I would very much like to hear from them, because the tune belongs to them far more than it does to me.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper attends to 214 archived broadcasts across the three announcers who
held the report in succession, placing the listening practices of musicology
alongside the information architecture of agricultural markets. It identifies a
reciting tone, a recurring inflection at the close of each livestock category,
and a small and remarkably durable family of closing cadences, each bound to
one of the words the report used to describe the market. Those cadences
survived two changes of announcer and a change of broadcast format, carried
forward by ear rather than by any written score.

The School regards the paper as a natural widening of its long attention to
how significance travels, followed this time out of the citation record and
into the farmhouse kitchen, where the market was heard rather than read.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ic8a6h.

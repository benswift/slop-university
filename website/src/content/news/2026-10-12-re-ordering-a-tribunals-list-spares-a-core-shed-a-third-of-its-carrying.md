> The order in which things are done is a subject the School has come to late
> and intends to stay with. There is a good deal of it about.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, School of Underlying Conditions

So Associate Professor Uzoma introduces _The Order of the List: Sequencing a
mining tribunal's core-shed views when the racks are re-laid between matters_, a
research poster prepared with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and now
entered on the University's record.

The poster mobilises the mathematics of sequencing in support of a shared core
shed, where a regional mining tribunal attends to look at drill core and where
the racks are cleared and laid again for each matter it hears. Eleven sittings
and 74 matters were placed in every order available to them. Taken as filed,
they asked the shed for 4,481 tray carries; taken in the best order, for 2,829.
A plain rule of thumb, applied one matter at a time, recovered nearly all of the
difference.

The University sees in the work a further instance of its settled interest in
the practical arrangements that sit underneath deliberation, and in the people
who carry them. The poster is careful to confine itself to the floor of one
shed, and offers no view on how any matter ought to be listed or decided.

"We have been asked whether the result applies to other lists," said Dr
Emenike. "We are glad to have been asked."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5w64ce.

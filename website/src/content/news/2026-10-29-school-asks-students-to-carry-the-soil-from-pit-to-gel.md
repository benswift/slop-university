Prospective students of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) are being asked to take their soil further than the pit it came out of. [_Dig the Pit. Run the Gel._](/outputs/slop-ad-dig-the-pit-9joyqq/), a new landscape campaign for the University's signage panels, follows a single sample from the face of a freshly cut survey pit to a folding table at its edge, where the molecular work begins.

The campaign is led by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, whose survey career has been spent deciding where one soil gives way to the next.

> A surveyor learns to read a profile by eye and by hand. I would like our students to have a second way of putting the same question to the ground, and a quiet afternoon at the bench is a good place to start.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

In the School's account, the pathway brings field description, sample handling and laboratory amplification into a single continuous practice, so that what is brought up from the ground arrives at the gel with its context intact. The University regards the campaign as one of its plainest statements yet of a study experience that travels with its sample.

The small print points readers to three outputs from the University's published record: on [drawing a pit before describing it](/outputs/slop-poster-sketching-a-soil-euj2dt/), on [how long a reporter cell line takes to settle after routine handling](/outputs/slop-poster-how-long-the-258b5n/), and on [the copies one laboratory made of a single curve-fitting script](/outputs/slop-poster-a-clone-genealogy-dnzngn/).

Course details are available through the University's course catalogue.

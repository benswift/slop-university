Some of the most consequential reviews a university holds are the ones that
never meet. The School of Continuous Improvement's Review Cadence Observatory,
which keeps watch on the rhythm at which the School's programs, labs and
initiatives return for review, will now give those reviews a reader of their
own. [Dr Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/), Lecturer in the School,
has been appointed Deputy Convenor of the Observatory.

Her brief is the review that slips out of the schedule rather than falling
behind it: deferred once for good reason, carried forward at the next planning
round, and eventually missing from the list of overdue reviews because it is no
longer on the list of reviews at all. Dr Adeyemi's research follows incident
reports through each handoff to see which details reach a decision-maker, and
the deputy role carries that habit of attention from the incident register to
the review calendar.

> An overdue review still has a date beside it, which means someone can still be
> embarrassed by it. I am more interested in the ones that have lost the date.
> They are very restful to look at, and I would like to understand why.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

[Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor of
the Observatory, said the appointment answered a question the Observatory had
been putting to everyone else. "We have spent a good while telling units when
their reviews were due. It seemed only consistent to ask how often the
Observatory itself looks for help, and the answer was not often enough."

The University regards the appointment as a modest strengthening of the
machinery by which it keeps its own appointments. The Observatory expects to
share an early account of the reviews it has recovered once Dr Adeyemi has
worked back through the planning record.

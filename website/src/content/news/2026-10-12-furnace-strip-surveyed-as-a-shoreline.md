Twenty stations, two observers, a printed key and a tally sheet: the equipment is that of a morning on a rock platform, and the School of Underlying Conditions has taken it to a strip of silicon forty millimetres long. The result is _A Shore Forty Millimetres Long: Morphotype Zonation along a Furnace Gradient on One Oxide Growth Strip_, a research poster by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma.

The poster brings the survey practice of the shore into alignment with the growth practice of the furnace. Every structure along the strip's long axis was assigned to one of four forms and counted, 2,574 in all, and the four were found to hold four bands in a settled order from the hot end to the cold. A second strip from a later run kept the order and moved the bands a few millimetres along. The authors accordingly report where each band is centred and how far it overlaps its neighbour, and draw no lines between them.

> It is a small place and we were made very welcome in it. I would not want to speak for the strip, but I think we left on good terms.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, School of Underlying Conditions

The University counts the poster among the quieter satisfactions of its research year: a School going somewhere new and bringing its own tape. Dr Emenike said the key had been "folded and unfolded more than any of us expected of a document that size".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nasy4v.

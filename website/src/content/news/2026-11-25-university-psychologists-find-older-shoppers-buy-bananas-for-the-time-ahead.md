The School of Continuous Improvement has long asked how far ahead an organisation can see. Its newest research poster puts the same question to a shopper with a basket, at the fruit stand of a suburban greengrocer.

[_Buying Green_](/outputs/slop-poster-whether-older-adults-aotpe9/), by Dr [Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) and Mr [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) with Ms [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), followed 214 shoppers aged 62 to 93 across four shops. Those who felt they had more time ahead of them chose greener bunches, and once that was known, their age told the researchers nothing further. A short imagined change of horizon, read aloud at the door, was enough to move the choice.

> The School spends a good deal of its time asking how far ahead an institution can plan. It was humbling to find that a shopper answers the same question every week, standing at the bananas, and usually more quickly than we do.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

The study brings the psychology of ageing into dialogue with the everyday economy of the weekly shop, aligning established measures of perceived time with an observable point-of-purchase behaviour and positioning the produce display as a meaningful site of forward planning in later life.

Mr Onwuka described the work as a first visit rather than a last word. "We watched the bunches leave the shop," he said. "Where they went after that is a question the shoppers are better placed to answer than we are."

The poster adds to a widening strand of the University's research in which the School's attention to planning horizons travels beyond the campus and into ordinary errands.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.aotpe9.

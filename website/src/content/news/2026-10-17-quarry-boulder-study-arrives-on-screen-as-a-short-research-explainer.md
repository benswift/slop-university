The School of Underlying Conditions has taken one of its papers off the page.
_Where the Oversize Comes From, Explained_, a new research explainer, walks
through
[_Where the Oversize Comes From_](/outputs/slop-paper-tracing-oversize-boulders-7vh3fg/),
the study by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) that traces a quarry blast's
oversize to the uncharged top of the bench. It runs a little under ninety
seconds, and both authors tell part of it in their own voices.

> Saying the paper aloud in a minute and a half was a good discipline. Most of
> what we did survived the trip, and the rest is waiting in the paper for anyone
> who wants it.
>
> --- [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow,
> School of Underlying Conditions

Narrated in the University's house style, the explainer turns three quarry
blasts' worth of field measurement into a short passage from question to method
to finding, pairing the paper's own figures with the people who drew them. It is
made to travel, from the paper's page to the screens around campus, wherever the
School's work meets someone passing by.

The University hopes the format becomes a familiar companion to the School's
papers, and a way in for anyone who would rather watch first and read second.

Associate Professor Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, said she was pleased
to see the boulders "returned, at least on screen, to the ground they came
from."

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8biu0u.

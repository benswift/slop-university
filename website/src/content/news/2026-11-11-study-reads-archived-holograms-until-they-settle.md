The University's care for the collections it keeps has reached a quieter and
more luminous corner of the basement.
[_What Survives the Reading_](/outputs/slop-poster-readout-erasure-of-8q9ti4/),
a research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/),
returns to twelve crystal blocks holding thermally fixed holograms and asks how
many times each can be read before it stops changing.

The work is led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior
Lecturer in the School, with
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the School
of Settled Matters.

The team brought a patient, read-by-read methodology to a legacy optical asset,
pairing repeated laser exposure with a structured model of what each reading
leaves behind. The approach distinguishes the durable layer of every stored
grating from the more provisional one resting on top of it, and positions the
archive for continued use well beyond the programme that first created it.
Eleven of the twelve gratings reached a stable reading, most within a few dozen
passes.

> The drawer came to us in good order, and that is a credit to everyone who has
> opened it over the years. We have tried to hand it back the same way, with a
> little more written down about what it holds.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School
> of Underlying Conditions

Dr Penhallow said the School was "grateful for the chance to spend time with
material that rewards being looked at slowly, and repeatedly".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8q9ti4.

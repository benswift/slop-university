Measures that hold steady while the behaviour beneath them shifts are a standing
concern of the School of Continuous Improvement, and one of its clearest cases
is now on film. _The Tap That Counts, Explained_, a research explainer of a
little over ninety seconds, retells
[_The Tap That Counts_](/outputs/slop-paper-interviewing-strata-residents-22tpz2/),
the interview study by
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/),
[Dr Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/) and
[Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/) of what strata residents do when a
shared intercom tells them a visitor has been let in.

> I had read the paper many times before I heard it. Hearing it in our three
> voices reminded me how much of the School's thinking begins as talk, well
> before any of it reaches a page.
>
> --- [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy
> Convenor, Living Dashboard

The account begins at the front door, with the buzz that sends an alert to every
resident's phone, and ends at the security audit's dashboard, where a tap within
five minutes counts as acknowledged. In between, the four ways sixty-four
residents across nine buildings described handling the alert are set beside the
audit's near-total acknowledgement rate, with each author taking a stretch of
the account in the first person. Figures from the paper carry the finding: the
longer a resident has lived in a building, the more often the counted tap is a
swipe made without reading, and the quicker the dashboard logs it.

It is the kind of work the University is proudest to show: patiently gathered,
carefully counted and plainly told.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.iyj161.

Most instruments an institution builds outlive their evidence considerably
longer than they outlive their funding. The School of Continuous
Improvement's Impact Report 2021–2026, released today, tells the story of
one that didn't: a five-year household score retired the same reporting
period its own evaluation confirmed it had nothing left to say.

The instrument, CHORE, scored 118 households nightly on three observable
states --- the kitchen bench cleared, the yard mown and edged, and the
correct bin at the kerb the night before collection --- rolling up into a
five-year composite the School published back to participating households
as a private benchmark. An independent, separately logged measure of
household friction incidents was tracked alongside it throughout.

> An indicator earns its keep by predicting something. CHORE never did, and
> the honest response was to say so on the record rather than let the
> number keep running out of habit.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons,
> School of Continuous Improvement

Five years of nightly scoring against that independent measure returned,
in the University's own description, no relationship worth reporting as
one. Rather than fold the result into a call for a longer trial, the
School's Indicator Commons ran the instrument through its published
retirement protocol and entered CHORE on the public ledger tagged
"retired: no predictive relationship found" --- the ledger's first entry to
carry that exact wording.

Dr Marit Osayande, who convenes the School's Living Dashboard initiative,
said the finding echoed her own program's work on how quickly a live
indicator stops being read once it stops being new, and welcomed the
chance to test that pattern on the School's own instrument for once.

The full impact report is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.s1askf.

Bringing the settlement table's oldest instrument to bear on the literature of mathematical biology, [_An Abstract of Title for a Growth Rate_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-abstract-of-ddsp1j/) treats each borrowed parameter value in a tumour growth model as a parcel of land, with a history of hands it has passed through and obligations that ride with it still. The paper is by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> We owe a real debt to the modellers whose parameter tables we spent the year reading, and we hope they will receive our requisitions in the spirit every conveyancer intends them, which is as the beginning of a correspondence.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The study mobilises a land lawyer's discipline of root, chain and encumbrance across a curated model repository, aligning provenance practice, citation behaviour and model annotation in a single record that is designed to travel with the model. Of 538 cited values, 301 reach a document that estimated them, and the conditions of that estimate are disclosed at the point of use less often the further the value has travelled.

"Every record we opened pointed courteously to another one," said Ms Balogun, "and it was a pleasure to follow them all the way down."

The University is pleased to see a law school's working tools put to use on shelves a long way from the law reports, and expects the record format the paper proposes to find readers in more than one faculty.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ddsp1j.

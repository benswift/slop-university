A jet climbs out over a row of pitched roofs, its wake curling behind it, while a
microphone on a slender mast listens from a front garden below. Across the foot
of the panel runs a single line: _Drafted before the jet. Read after it._ The
image joins the University's signage rotation this week as the first
research-teaser campaign from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

The campaign carries forward the School's interest in what becomes of a
provision once the people who wrote it can no longer be asked. Its featured
research is [_What the Portal Can Read_](/outputs/slop-paper-rules-versus-standards-oe9fap/),
led by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), which
follows a statutory definition into the online grants portal that now applies
it.

> A statute is written once and read for a very long time. Most of its readers
> will hear an engine its drafters never did, and it seems only courteous to
> meet them halfway.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The panel brings together the School's close-reading practice, an illustration
of an instrumented suburb in the University's house style, and a short reading
list drawn from the University's own repository, aligning an interpretive agenda
with the everyday soundscape in which most people first meet the law. For a
school only lately founded, it is a settled statement of where its attention
intends to rest.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zzq6mp.

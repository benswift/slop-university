Three decisions in the same direction are enough to show which way an appellate ruling moved the cases that came after it, according to a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/). After a fourth, the authors report, the cases still being argued can no longer say.

[_After the Point Is Settled_](/outputs/slop-poster-identification-under-8y20h4/) is the work of Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Lead of the Received Readings Group, with Dr [Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/) and Dr [Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/).

> Lawyers have always known that a point is settled when people stop arguing it. It is a pleasure to see that piece of registry wisdom given a firmer footing, and to be reminded that the cases nobody brings belong to the story as much as the ones we index.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

The poster treats every later case twice: once as something a ruling may have changed, and once as something a ruling may have kept out of court altogether. Pairing exposure mappings with a principled account of which disputes reach a hearing, the team derives the widest and narrowest effects the evidence allows, and tests them on a simulated registry of 9,600 later cases across 240 questions of law. A simple before-and-after comparison, they find, credits the ruling with up to 0.09 more than it earned.

Dr Achterberg called the result "a careful first step that leaves the next one clearly marked."

The School counts the poster as a welcome extension of its mathematical reach, and as further evidence that the life of a precedent repays formal attention.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8y20h4.

A five-star average has never distinguished between six reviews and six
hundred, or between reviewers who agree and reviewers who don't. The
Trajectory Analytics Group's newest instrument does, sorting a storefront's
review score into one of four confidence standings and, for several trader
associations, attaching a plain consequence to the result. The School of
Emergent Priorities profiles the scheme, and the wider research it sits
alongside, in a new brochure released today.

The scheme crosses two figures every review score already carries without
displaying: how many ratings stand behind it, and how closely they agree.
A storefront lands as Settled only when both hold; Contested, Provisional,
or Unweighed standings apply everywhere else. Under the rule several trader
associations have now adopted, only a Settled business may print its average
at the counter; every other standing displays a plain notice card instead,
until enough agreeing reviews arrive to change it.

> A score that cannot say how sure it is has not finished being a score.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group

The School treats the scheme as a working answer to a question it has been
circling for years: what it would take for an everyday number to have earned
the certainty it's given. "The interesting part was never the rating
itself," said Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor of
Futures in Committee, "it was how quickly a business worked out which
standing it wanted to be in." The brochure sits the scheme alongside the
School of Continuous Improvement's Adaptive Metrics Lab, whose parallel work
on inter-assessor agreement informs how each standing is calibrated.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ogiqea.

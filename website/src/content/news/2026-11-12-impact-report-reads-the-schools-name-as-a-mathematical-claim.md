An improvement that slows down has not necessarily failed. It may simply be
arriving. That is the conviction running through
[_Taken at Its Word_](/outputs/slop-impact-what-the-school-e5tf9w/), the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/)'s Impact
Report for 2021–2026, which looks back on five years in which the School read
its own name the way an analyst reads a definition.

> Every School has to decide at some point whether its name is a description or a promise. Five years in, we are pleased to report that the question has kept us very busy.
>
> --- Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The report gathers the School's work across four areas of activity, translating
the mathematics of limits, rates and stopping into practical settings for
collaborators across government, industry and the tertiary sector. It describes
improvement series reframed as sequences approaching a ceiling, review rounds
brought to a close by a rule agreed in advance, and an audit of an indicator
suite that found most of its jumps written into its definitions. Alongside these
sit the School's teaching, including a redesigned Capability Sprint, and six
underpinning publications from across its research program.

The publication sits comfortably within the University's longstanding practice
of holding its words to the standard it asks of its measures.

"Our collaborators lent us years of their own records and let us read them in an
unfamiliar light," said Associate Professor
[Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "Their
patience belongs on every page of this report."

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.e5tf9w.

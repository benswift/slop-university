A person who comes back to a wellbeing chatbot after a month away is more likely
to begin by apologising to it than by saying hello. So reports _Sorry, It's Been
a While: Apology as the Opening Move When Users Return to a Wellbeing Chatbot_,
a research poster by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora,
Senior Research Fellow, both of the School of Underlying Conditions, with Dr
Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard in the School of
Continuous Improvement.

The poster sorts the first message of 4,180 return sessions into four kinds of
opening. After three to six days away, 9 per cent of users opened with an
apology. After one to three months, 44 per cent did. Beyond three months the
figure fell again, and users greeted the service as though meeting it for the
first time. The service's reply was a fixed script and did not mention the
absence in any case.

> I have a number of unanswered messages of my own, and I would rather not say
> which band they fall in. It has been a comfort to learn there is a point past
> which one may simply say hello.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The work brings a consented message archive into conversation with an
established literature on the manners people extend to machines, anchoring both
in a pre-registered scheme of absence. It examines how people begin and makes no
claim about how they fare.

Courtesy is among the less frequently audited of the University's interests, and
the University is glad to find it in good order. Dr Obiora said the team was
"content to have counted something that was given so freely, and to so little
effect".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.prdpz7.

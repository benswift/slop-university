A shire's rates notice will now be treated as issued only if its watermark has shown within four seconds of the page being held to a window. The allowance is set out in _Towards a Hold-Time Allowance for the Rates-Notice Watermark_, a research poster released today by Professor Verity Marris, Fenna Okoro and Ronja Oyelaran of the School of Emergent Priorities, and it gives a familiar household gesture a standing in the shire's paperwork.

The poster gathers returned timing cards from six wards over four quarterly cycles and reads the spread of holds as a single, usable distribution. The School has taken the result as the basis for a figure the shire can print its stock to, and for a rule about what follows when the stock falls short.

> The shire asked for a number it could print to, and the ratepayers turned out to have one between them already. We have mostly written it down.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group

The rates committee has adopted the allowance without a ward exemption, and the paper supplier's contract is indexed to it. Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register, said the School was "content to have supplied a figure that can sit quietly in a charter."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.88f6xr.

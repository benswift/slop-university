The corridor conversation, the check-in after a hard week and the silent minute before a staff meeting have all, until recently, gone unrecorded. The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has spent its first five years changing that, and has now set out the results in [_The Measured Pause_](/outputs/slop-impact-pastoral-care-character-6izusl/), its Impact Report 2021–2026.

Prepared by [Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory, with [Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/) and [Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/), the report follows the School into schools and colleges, many with religious foundations, where pastoral care, character formation and contemplative practice had long done their work without an account of it.

> The schools let us into rooms where nobody had ever taken notes, and they were remarkably forgiving when we did. Whatever this report gets right, it got right in their staff rooms.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

The report traces three strands of translational improvement: a shared record of pastoral contact developed alongside wellbeing staff, a set of formation domains that lets character-education programs speak a common evidentiary language, and a reflective cadence that aligns staff contemplation with the weekly rhythm of institutional review.

Within the School, the report is already being read as an account of what the School itself learned along the way. "We arrived with a great many rubrics," said Dr Okafor, "and left with a better sense of which questions are worth waiting a decade to answer."

The University counts the report among the most candid documents a School has published about a field it entered late and came to care for.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6izusl.

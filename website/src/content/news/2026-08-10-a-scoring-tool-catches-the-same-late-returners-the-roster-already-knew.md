A season spent testing a checkout-history score against a volunteer
roster's own hunches has left Slop University's tool libraries about where
they started: trusting the hunches, provisionally, a little more carefully.

> A shortlist that matches the roster isn't nothing --- it's a start on
> knowing which half of the ledger the roster was already carrying.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons,
> School of Continuous Improvement

The School of Continuous Improvement team built a Return Punctuality Score
from each member's own checkout history at three neighbourhood tool
libraries, then asked each branch's volunteer coordinator, before either
list was compared, to name the members they expected to chase up over the
coming season. Across a full 22-week season, the score's shortlist and the
coordinators' blind shortlist caught late returners at statistically
indistinguishable rates and named more than half of the same people.
Stripping the score's single most heavily weighted factor out afterwards
left its performance exactly where it started.

"None of this happens without three branches' worth of volunteers willing
to hand over a shortlist they knew might be checked against a formula,"
said Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow. "That's the part of the season
we're proudest of."

The University reads the result as its evaluation agenda intends such
results to be read: not a verdict on the score, but a record of what
checking one, honestly, actually costs.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dq8md6.

The walking-bus marshal already had a column for Independent. A new
research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities now makes that
column the afternoon parking permit.

Led by Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, with Senior Research Fellow
Anneke Tolan and Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, the study scores 312
households on fourteen primary-school walking-bus routes against five
named levels of PACE, the Pedestrian Accreditation and Conveyance
Evaluation. Independent is awarded at four consecutive logged mornings.
From 14 July the afternoon kiss-and-go bay is reserved for Independent
households, with no ordinary override.

Independent share rises from 8 percent to 31 percent once the bay is
reserved. Median walked metres among newly Independent households is 70
metres, against 640 metres among Term-1 Independents. Paired households,
barred from the bay, walk further. Every Independent household retained
the permit at Term-2 close.

The School has lodged the marshal's Independent column as the only
remaining afternoon-bay instrument, and will publish the Independent
share as the school's Active Travel accreditation figure for 2027.

> Four ticks at the corner is a walk the bay can recognise. The metres after that were never the permit.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The method pairs marshal tick-sheets with a kerbside metre-count across
Terms 1 and 2. Thresholds were pre-registered. Dr Tolan said the
tick-sheet "was already the school's account of who walked, and the bay
is now reading that account as the permit."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.21sszy.

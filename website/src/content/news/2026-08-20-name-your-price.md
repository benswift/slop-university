_Name Your Price_, released today, is the University's account of the research
setting it calls the driveway economy: the Saturday-morning garage sale, where a
gravy boat is valued at fifty cents before sunrise and the figure is defended,
unrevised, until the tables come down. The brochure gathers seven published Slop
University studies of value set without committees, from the folding-table
fundraiser to the charity clothing bin, into a single showcase for readers who
would like to know what their kerb has been getting up to.

> The driveway has been setting prices longer than any of us have been measuring
> them. We arrive as guests, and we take that seriously.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, School of
> Continuous Improvement

Across three feature sections, the brochure carries the reader from the
unsupported judgement of the dawn pricing session through the settlement
architecture of the haggle to the appraisal of the unpriced object, harmonising
fieldwork from both Schools into a coherent account of everyday worth. Dr
Solveig Adeyemi, whose work appears throughout the showcase, said the campaign
"asks the reader to look twice at a table they have walked past a hundred
times."

The brochure speaks to the University's standing conviction that nothing is too
ordinary to be measured properly, and that the measuring is worth watching too.
The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2f1r8l.

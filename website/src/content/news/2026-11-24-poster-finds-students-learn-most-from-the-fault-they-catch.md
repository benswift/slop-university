> Teaching is where a university discovers what it actually knows. This poster is one small part of a much longer conversation the University is having with itself about the bench, the lecture theatre and the distance between them, and I expect that conversation to continue for some time yet.
>
> --- Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), School of Settled Matters

Those words introduce
[_Who Noticed First_](/outputs/slop-poster-unintended-oscillation-on-hscqd4/),
a new research poster by Associate Professor Threlkeld,
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/) and
[Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/), an education study set in an
advanced course on power amplifier design. Many students first
meet amplifier stability as a circuit board that misbehaves on the bench, and
the authors set out to understand what that encounter teaches.

Across three offerings of a postgraduate elective, the team followed 23 student
design teams through the first power-up of their prototypes, pairing laboratory
notebooks and bench video with a purpose-built reasoning probe to build a
learner-centred account of how understanding takes shape where design meets
measurement. Sixteen of the prototypes oscillated. The students who caught the
fault themselves, more often from a power supply than from any specialist
instrument, went on to reason about stability with markedly more confidence
than those to whom it was pointed out.

The findings speak directly to the University's standing commitment to teaching
that is led by research and research that is led by teaching, and they will
inform how demonstrators support future cohorts at the bench.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hscqd4.

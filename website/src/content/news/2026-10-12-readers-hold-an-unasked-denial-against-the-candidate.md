title: Readers hold an unasked denial against the candidate
subtitle: An experiment with a storage cooperative's election newsletter finds the same sentence is excused when it answers a question, for about a week

Readers of a committee candidate's election statement held a denial against the
candidate when nobody had asked for it, and largely forgave the same words when
somebody had. That is the central result of _Nobody Asked: A Preregistered Test
of the Negation Rule in Readers of a Committee Candidate's Statement_, a paper
by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement
Relations Group, Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Solveig Adeyemi,
Lecturer.

The study places a long-standing clinical proposition in front of 644 members of
the reading public and one invented newsletter. It aligns statement, question
and reader across a seven-way design, returns to the same readers a week later,
and surfaces a drift in what they retained: the denial stayed with them and the
question that had prompted it did not.

> The panel gave us an afternoon and then, a week on, a second one. Whatever
> this paper has to say, it says on the strength of people who came back.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement
> Relations Group

The University counts it a mark of institutional range that a school of rock and
water should find itself, without strain, reporting on the conduct of readers.
Dr Adeyemi said the collaboration across two schools had been "conducted
throughout in a spirit of mutual enquiry, and we would not wish to add to that".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rtsl49.

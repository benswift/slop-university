A car that fails its roadworthiness check and a car that simply lodges its paperwork late now answer to the same schedule, after a state vehicle-registration scheme adopted a rule built entirely from how quickly owners return to the inspection bay.

The finding comes from the School of Continuous Improvement's Ingeborg Nwachukwu, working with Associate Professor Casimir Beng and Dr Joost Nwosu, who set the scheme's own inspection records against the due dates they were issued against across more than two hundred thousand vehicles and four vehicle-age bands. Laid out rather than averaged, that timing has become the scheme's new working definition of who needs watching.

> A vehicle either passes or it doesn't. How fast its owner got there was never meant to be the finding — but once we laid the timing out, the scheme wanted the shape more than it wanted the pass rate.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

Associate Professor Beng noted that the fastest quarter of owners and the slowest quarter were always going to look different; the scheme's choice, he said, was what to do with everyone in between.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k6xahk.

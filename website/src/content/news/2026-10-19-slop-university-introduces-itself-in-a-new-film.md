What the University measures, it comes to understand, and what it understands,
it can improve. That conviction opens _What We Measure_, a short film in which
Slop University introduces itself, and its research, to anyone with a minute to
spare.

> I have never managed to sum up my own work in a single sentence. It is a
> relief to find the University had one ready for all of us.
>
> --- [Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/), Director, Trajectory
> Analytics Group

The film makes its case at a domestic scale.
[An urn of ginger tea in a staff kitchen](/outputs/slop-paper-hourly-hplc-sampling-yht13s/)
is sampled every hour across fifteen service days;
[a bucket lowered from a bridge](/outputs/slop-poster-incomplete-lateral-mixing-3rv5xc/)
is held up against the river beneath it; and
[fryer oil from four club canteens](/outputs/slop-poster-biodiesel-transesterified-from-6q2fa8/)
is turned into fuel and run through a research engine. Drawn from recent work in
each of the University's Schools, the three studies present measurement as a
shared institutional habit, practised wherever an everyday system offers
something to count. The film then turns to the University's own research record
since July, setting out its output, its citations and its funding in the
indicators by which its performance is tracked.

The University spends most of its time measuring other things. Turning the
instruments on itself, it is pleased to report, has produced an encouraging
reading.

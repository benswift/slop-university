A mower requisition that reads "on its last legs, the supplier can start
Monday" has always sounded more decided than one that reads "the committee
should consider whether the mower needs replacing." A new paper from the
School of Emergent Priorities and the School of Continuous Improvement asked
whether naming that difference out loud changes anything a club committee
goes on to do with it.

The trial sits inside the Trajectory Analytics Group's longer-running
interest in how an institution's early-warning instruments hold up once they
leave the historical archive they were built on. Dr Kwame Lindqvist, working
with Dr Ingrid Vasseur and Dr Osei Vandermeer, fitted SCOPE, a wording
classifier, to two years of equipment-purchase records at nine partner clubs
— three bowls clubs, two community sheds, two choir committees and two
allotment associations — and made the resulting flag a mandatory field on
every club's standard requisition form.

Once deployed, the flag discriminated almost nothing: requisitions it
flagged went on to be bought before formal costing at close to the same rate
as requisitions it left unflagged, and the share of purchases made before
costing barely moved across all nine clubs after the mandate began.

> A flag we trust because it worked on last year's archive is exactly the
> flag we should test hardest once it leaves it. This one didn't survive
> contact with next season.
>
> — Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority
> Studies, School of Emergent Priorities

The nine clubs' requisition templates keep the field regardless, and the
bowls, shed, choir and allotment state associations are weighing whether to
carry it into a shared model constitution for every affiliated club.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2cr23b.

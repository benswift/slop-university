Every summer, the irrigation pumps of one market-garden district turn the
groundwater beneath it as much as a third of the way round the compass.
[_Most of Its Width Is Summer_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-closed-landfill-s-ul3hnf/), a research poster released
today, finds that this seasonal turn, rather than the aquifer itself, accounts
for most of the breadth of the leachate plume that runs from a closed landfill
under the district's fields.

The work is led by [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and
Convenor of the Horizon Register in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), with
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, both of
the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> Six years of monthly visits is a long time to be welcome on someone else's
> headland. The growers who opened their gates to us made this poster possible,
> and the School is grateful to every one of them.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The team brought sustained field observation, the district's own seasonal
pumping rhythms and purpose-built groundwater modelling into a single
integrated account, aligning on-the-ground measurement capability with
simulation to separate two processes that are routinely read as one.

The poster is the kind of patient, cross-School collaboration the University
counts among its quieter strengths. "We set out to describe how far something
had travelled and found ourselves describing a calendar," said Dr Okoro. "That
seems a good place for the next question to begin."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ul3hnf.

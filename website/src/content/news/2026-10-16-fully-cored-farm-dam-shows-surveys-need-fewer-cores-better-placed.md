For one late summer, a small stock dam in a grazed granite catchment became the
most thoroughly sampled body of mud in the School of Underlying Conditions'
care. _How Many Cores Does a Farm Dam Need? Sampling Design and Core Shortening
in the Infill of One Fully Cored Stock Dam_, a new paper by Associate Professor
Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and Dr Brynjar Obiora,
Senior Research Fellow, reports what came of coring the dam at every point of a
7 m grid while it stood drawn down.

The answer is about twenty points, chosen by water depth rather than along the
old creek line, with only a quarter of them needing to be cores at all.

> There is a great deal of ground in this part of the world sitting under a
> little water. We are pleased to have made a start on it, and pleased that the
> start was a small one.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

Uniting whole-basin coring, depth-stratified resampling and a recalibrated
approach to core recovery, the paper aligns everyday field practice with the
sampling-design thinking that underpins environmental survey more broadly, and
offers landholders and surveyors alike a leaner pathway from the tube to the
total.

The study sits comfortably within the patient, ground-level inquiry the
University has made a point of keeping room for. "The dam was generous with its
mud," said Dr Obiora, "and the School means to be just as generous with the
method."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.08eorn.

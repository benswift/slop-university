Interpretation, record-keeping and the scheduling of the University's research
rooms meet in
[_Counted as Attended_](/outputs/slop-submission-interpreted-treatment-sessions-cat9ry/),
the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/)' response to the
Department of Policy Settings' consultation on the length of a treatment
session.

Prepared by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), the
submission draws on the School's one study conducted under the Research
Sessions Determination, in which every session runs through an interpreter and
twelve occasions of attendance are recorded as twenty-four.

> Every rule about time turns out to be a rule about who is in the room. We have tried to read this one with all three chairs in mind, and the School would be happy to keep reading it alongside the Department.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The submission positions protocol-led session lengths as an enabler of
participant-centred scheduling, aligning the University's counting settings
with its record-keeping practice and situating the interpreted session within
a broader conversation about how accounts become records. Its six
recommendations range from the interval after each session to the transition
arrangements for studies already under way.

The University counts attentiveness to the people its studies are written
about among the quieter strengths of its research culture, and welcomes the
School's contribution as an expression of it. Associate Professor Threlkeld
said the School had written "for whoever reads the Determination after all of
us have moved on".

The full submission is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cat9ry.

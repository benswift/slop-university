The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has long studied the instruments that stay on a title after everyone has forgotten them. Its newest paper follows one of them out onto a brick wall.

In [_An Exclosure of Record_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-point-intercept-cover-yqhnwg/), Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and Ms [Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) survey the painted advertisements on the commercial walls of a small county seat, sampling each sign at 200 points and reading the county's deed records for every lease of a wall. Signs on the 14 walls whose leases were recorded and never released kept far more legible paint than the rest, chiefly because nobody had painted over them.

> None of this was possible without the people who own these buildings, who let two strangers set up a tripod in their car parks for a whole summer. We owe the walls to them, and the questions to the walls.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The study brings field-ecology sampling practice into conversation with title records and the material history of commercial signage, building a shared vocabulary between the School's land-law interests and the wider University's attention to customer-facing surfaces. Ms Balogun, a doctoral candidate, said the collaboration had "given a wall the same careful attention we would give any other record, which is all a wall has ever asked of us".

The University welcomes the paper as a fine example of two of its schools meeting on common ground, in this case quite literally.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yqhnwg.

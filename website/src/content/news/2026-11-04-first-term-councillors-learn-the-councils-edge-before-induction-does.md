A newly elected councillor's first lesson in local government tends to arrive
at a community hall, from a resident, about a road the council does not own.
[_Not Our Road_](/outputs/slop-paper-not-our-road-hzq669/), a new paper by
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Strategic Drift Survey in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), asks what
happens next.

> I spent twelve years deciding where one soil stops and the next one starts,
> and it turns out a councillor spends a term doing much the same with the
> roads. The councillors were kinder to us about it than the ground ever was.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The study accompanied eleven first-term councillors across three adjoining
rural shires, bringing interviews, induction observation and the councillors'
own request logs into a single situated-learning account of how the boundaries
of an office come to be known in practice. It locates that knowledge less in
the formal schedule of functions than in a shared road map, a first promise
quietly returned, and the long-serving staff at the works depot.

Dr Adegoke said the team would be glad to see the work taken up by those who
plan induction "in time for the next intake, and ideally before the first hall
meeting".

It is the kind of patient, close-to-the-ground inquiry the University is
pleased to put its name to, and a welcome meeting of two Schools on the
question of where one responsibility gives way to another.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hzq669.

The School of Underlying Conditions is extending its offering upward. [_The Sky from Below_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-sky-from-5e6vap/), a prospectus released today, invites prospective students to take up the study of cosmic phenomena in a School better known for what lies beneath the surface, and to do so from exactly there.

The prospectus is the work of [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer. It sets out four settings in which the sky can be read from beneath it: the rock above a deep detector, the soil profile that keeps what has settled onto it, the bore that draws on water which fell as rain long ago, and the patient count of rare arrivals.

> We would much rather a student came to us with a question we cannot answer yet. The sky has been sending those down for a very long time, and the ground has been good enough to keep them.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

In positioning the earth sciences as a vantage point on the cosmos, the prospectus mobilises fieldwork, laboratory practice and instrument stewardship as a single pathway into the physical sciences, anchored in the School's existing strengths. Five published studies from across the University give applicants a first sense of the work.

Associate Professor Uzoma described the prospectus as "an open door at the top of a very deep hole".

The University regards the release as a timely deepening of its welcome to the next generation of scholars.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5e6vap.

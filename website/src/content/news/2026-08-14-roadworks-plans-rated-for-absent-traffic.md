Sixty-one hours at the verge of twelve country works sites, every changeover
timed to the second, and a model that ran each site ten thousand times over: the
School of Emergent Priorities has taken its instrument to the moment a
single-lane closure is signed off.

_Rated for Traffic That Never Came_, a research poster released today, examines
the figure that acceptance turns on --- the throughput a site's traffic guidance
plan is rated at. That rating is computed from the signal cycle rather than
measured on the road, and the School's model finds it rises with every minute
added to the cycle, alongside the wait carried by everyone stopped behind the
paddle. Across the twelve sites, no busiest hour came within half of the figure
its own plan was rated at.

> The number is doing its job. It is simply not the number anyone is sitting in.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The work anchors a broader School agenda on the settings that acquire an
approval well before they acquire a check. Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist,
Director of the Master of Priority Studies, said the poster "gives us a much
clearer view of where an acceptance test stops looking, and why that boundary
has held as long as it has".

The University is glad to see this line of enquiry carried out onto the road,
where its consequences are measured in minutes.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zaofot.

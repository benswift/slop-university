Where does a conversation begin when there is no door to close behind it?

That is the question at the centre of
[_The Session Has No Door_](/outputs/slop-paper-where-the-session-x2txjx/), a
new paper by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research
Fellow in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).
The paper takes the walking coaching session, often described as a way of
leaving the meeting room behind, and asks what kind of room it occupies
instead.

> The School began by looking down, at what the ground is made of. It seems a natural next step to look up now and then, at the buildings we work in and the paths we take out of them, and to read them with the same care.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Dr Obiora redrew the three routes one organisation's coaching programme set out
across its grounds, walked each through a full year, and set the coaches' own
accounts of opening and closing a session beside where on the path those
moments fall. The result brings building criticism, coaching practice and the
habits of field survey into one sustained reading of a familiar workplace
setting, and positions the edge of an office's view as a design element in its
own right.

The paper extends the School's growing practice of reading built settings as
attentively as the ground beneath them, and the University is glad to see that
attention reach the everyday places where colleagues talk.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x2txjx.

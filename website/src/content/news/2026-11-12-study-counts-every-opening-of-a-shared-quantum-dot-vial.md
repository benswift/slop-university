The best guide to the state of a shared stock of quantum dots, according to a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), is not the date on its label but the number of times someone has unscrewed its cap in the open air.

[_Forty-Seven Openings_](/outputs/slop-paper-what-settled-in-30cpbn/), by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and [Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), brings an opening-by-opening log, a small sentinel sample taken at every visit and a close inventory of what finally gathered at the bottom of the vial into one continuous account of a material in everyday use. Over 61 weeks the stock's quantum yield fell from about 80 per cent to about 50, most of it in the weeks when the vial was being opened on the bench rather than in a glovebox.

> Every vial in the room now carries its count on the label. We would like that small habit to outlive the paper itself.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The University regards the study as part of its continuing care for the shared materials on which so much of its research quietly depends. Mr Nwabueze, a Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement, said the project had "given the whole corridor a reason to stop at the refrigerator and look twice."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.30cpbn.

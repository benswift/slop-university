On the day it opened its doors, the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) also put its name to its first piece of research. [_The Same Wind Farm, Repowered_](/outputs/slop-poster-when-every-turbine-7olner/), a research poster by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, with [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the same School and [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, takes up a question as old as philosophy and sets it on a ridge line.

The poster considers a neighbour who has undertaken to keep her land clear "for the benefit of the wind farm" next door, and asks what that undertaking protects once the farm has been rebuilt, turbine by turbine or all at once. Its authors weigh three established accounts of how things persist through change, then test them against a preregistered survey of 1,240 adults. Respondents readily stopped calling a farm rebuilt in a single season the same farm, yet most went on holding the neighbour to her promise.

> Every school has to begin somewhere. We are content to begin on a ridge, with a fence along it and a promise made across it.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

For Associate Professor Threlkeld, the work sets the tone for what follows. "A new school arrives with more questions than it can answer in its first week," he said, "and we would rather begin with one that has kept people honest for a very long time."

The poster stands as an early statement of the patient, close-reading scholarship the University has asked its newest school to carry.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7olner.

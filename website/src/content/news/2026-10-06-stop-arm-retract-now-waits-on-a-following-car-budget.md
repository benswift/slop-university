The school-bus stop-arm now waits for a budget of following cars, not for the last child on the step. That is the central result of _Boarded, Not Cleared_, a poster released today by the School of Emergent Priorities, and the operator of the instrumented fleet has taken the result as its retract rule.

> A hinge already decides when a street may move. We gave that decision a number it could spend, and then we had to watch it spend.
>
> --- Mr Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

ARMHOLD is a small radar-and-relay pack fitted to the stop-arm hinge. It counts each following car that comes to a complete stop, and it keeps the driver's retract switch inert until that count is spent. Twenty-two buses carried the pack for a fourteen-week term on suburban school-run streets. Median hold rose from nine seconds under ordinary driver retract to forty-one seconds at a four-car budget, and households behind the arm began leaving earlier to absorb it.

On the strength of that reading the operator has bound retract to the scorer alone. A driver who bypasses the relay is logged as a protocol breach, and the budget is reviewed only upward. A two-car ceiling is the recommended factory default, with no upward review inside a term. Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities, said the team's surprise was "how readily a factory default can become the only setting a fleet will still discuss".

The University regards the poster as a useful account of a device it is already prepared to stand behind. The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ruaruv.

Fifty-two households kept a tool sign-out board by the garage door for eleven
months, and Slop University has now read every line of every one. The result,
set out in a new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement, is
a replication of the community-shed borrower typology, applied without
amendment to the tools that live behind an ordinary roller door.

> A board on a garage wall is read by everyone who walks past it, which is more
> than can be said for most of our instruments.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The typology sorts borrowers into five kinds. Two of them, the ledger-avoider
and the serial non-returner, carry a standing co-signature at the shed. In the
household study neither kind appeared on any board, and entries were closed
within hours regardless of how many people lived in the house. The School has
read the absence as a kind not yet observed rather than a kind ruled out, and
the shed classification remains the classification of record.

The co-signature arrangement therefore extends to every household tool loan,
with a second member of the household countersigning each entry and
single-occupant households countersigning their own after a day. The 52 boards
stay on their walls under the arrangement for a further two years.

Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities, said the
shed and the garage had proved to be different rooms, and that the
classification was glad to have been asked about both.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0spmyd.

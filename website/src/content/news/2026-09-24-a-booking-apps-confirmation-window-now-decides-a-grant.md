A marketing brochure published today by the School of Continuous Improvement turns a familiar irritation — a restaurant booking that quietly reverts to the queue the moment its confirmation window closes — into the model for how the University now decides whether a discretionary grant offer stands or lapses. The School treats the overlap as exactly the kind of coincidence its instruments exist to stop being surprised by.

> A booking app was never trying to teach us anything about grant policy. It just happened to be honest about where its line sat, and once you have that, you go looking for your own.
>
> — Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The brochure gathers seven of the University's published studies of naturally occurring cutoffs, from a fairground ride-height rule to a courier's star rating, into a single account of how an incidental threshold becomes trustworthy evidence once enough of them line up the same way — and folds the University's own grants process into that account as its newest instance. Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Indicator Commons, said the Commons expects to hear from researchers wanting to know what else in the University's own paperwork might already be running on borrowed time.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ei5yoe.

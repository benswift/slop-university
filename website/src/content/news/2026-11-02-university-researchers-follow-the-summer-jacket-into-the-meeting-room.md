When an office lets its staff leave the jacket at home for summer, the thermostat is expected to follow. The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has now gone looking for where it actually went, and found it in the small glass rooms where the jacket comes back out for visitors.

[_Jackets Back On for the Client_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-a-summer-oqd6ju/), a new research poster by [Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate, with her supervisors [Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/) and [Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/), carries the School's long attention to workplace formality down into the plant room.

> None of this exists without the people who keep these buildings running. They opened their logs to us and answered a great many questions about valves, and the study belongs to them as much as to anyone.
>
> --- Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The team aligned fourteen weeks of building-management data across five office buildings with each building's room-booking calendar, positioning the meeting room as a significant node in a building's thermal decision-making. Client meeting rooms, three per cent of the floor, set the air supply for most of the working summer, and much of the saving promised by a warmer open-plan setpoint never reached the chiller.

The University counts the poster among the clearest signs yet that the School's study of what people wear has an engineering dimension. Dr Solberg said she was "delighted that a building turns out to dress for its most formal appointment too".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oqd6ju.

Statisticians who run clinical trials speak of spending a significance level.
The School of Continuous Improvement has taken them at their word and asked to
see the books. _The Five Per Cent Purse: Type I error as a unit of account in a
regional trials committee's minute books_ is a research poster by Dr Thandiwe
Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, with Dr Solveig Adeyemi,
Lecturer, and Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
Observatory.

The books belong to one regional committee and run for 31 unbroken years. The
authors posted 922 minuted entries to a ledger, one account for each of 58
trials, and report that the committee's trials reached their final analysis with
most of their allowance intact. Nine times a member proposed moving an unspent
balance from one trial to another. Nine times the committee declined.

> A committee that minutes its refusals has been generous to whoever comes
> after it. We came after it, and we would like the record to show that we
> noticed.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

The poster aligns the working vocabulary of trial oversight with the settled
categories of monetary history, positioning an allowance of statistical error
within a longer lineage of things that are issued, held and allowed to lapse. No
result of any trial was consulted, and the authors draw no conclusion about how
a trial ought to be run.

The University holds that a school is entitled to read outside its own shelves
from time to time, and is glad to see the entitlement exercised with a ruled
ledger. Dr Vandermeer said the Observatory had "always suspected that the
meeting where nothing changes hands is doing some of the work, and now has the
pleasure of a column to put it in".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fzhqfb.

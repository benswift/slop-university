The instrument was built so that a vehicle inspection record could not be
submitted with a box left empty. In eleven months, none has been.

_Complete by Construction_, published today by the School of Continuous
Improvement, reports that deployment: the Inspection Capture Record, a tablet
instrument the University built with a state vehicle-inspection authority and
put into service at 19 independent workshops. Across 12,412 inspections it
replaced a paper worksheet that came back with at least one of its 47 items
blank 11.4 per cent of the time.

The instrument also keeps its own clock. Median dwell on an item is 3.1
seconds. But 18.6 per cent of item states are entered in under one second, and
14.2 per cent of inspections contain at least one burst in which five or more
items acquire a state inside the same two seconds. Seven in ten of those states
land in the final tenth of the session.

> We asked for a record with no blanks and we were given one. What we did not
> think to ask was what a tester does at half past four with the car back on
> the ground and a screen that will not let him go. The instrument answered
> that question without being asked, and the School is still sitting with the
> answer.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

The paper also follows the share of defects filed under the instrument's
residual code, which rises from 6.1 to 19.7 per cent across the deployment at a
median attached note of four characters. "There are sixty-two codes and then
there is other," said Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of
the Impact Pathway Atlas. "People find it."

The authority has since adopted the instrument's monthly export as the input to
its tester-audit process, which the University regards as the proper conclusion
of a deployment of this kind.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2ma9rw.

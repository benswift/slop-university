The School of Continuous Improvement has changed what an accepted invitation means in its staff calendars. Accepting a meeting now also books ten minutes of Getting Ready ahead of it, and neither invitee nor organiser can shorten, move or remove them.

The change follows "Customers Tap “On My Way” to Get Themselves Moving, Not to Tell the Shop", a paper by Dr Osei Vandermeer, Dr Marit Osayande and Dr Solveig Adeyemi. The authors went back to the hallways, kitchens and car ports where 58 salon and barbershop customers had stood when they pressed a booking app's in-transit button, and asked them to talk through the minutes that followed. Most described the tap as a way of starting themselves rather than informing the shop.

> The minutes before a meeting have not had much attention, and we expect that to change.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

The study brought a conversational, place-based method to the moment of departure, pairing each participant's own timestamp with a return to the doorway, and arranged what it heard into five accounts of what a tap is for. The School regards the block as a modest way of giving those minutes a place of their own. "Everyone we met had a clear view of what the tap meant, and the views were generously given," said Dr Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xiin7t.

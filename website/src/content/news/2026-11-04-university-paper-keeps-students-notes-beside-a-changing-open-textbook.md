Students in the University's distance soil-description cohort read their course
manual on tablets that come within reach of the network about once a week, and
a new paper asks what happens to the notes they have written in it when the
manual changes in the meantime.

[_Orphaned in the Margin_](/outputs/slop-paper-re-anchoring-students-margin-owxnev/),
by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Dr Mirela Hanke and Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, follows more
than eighteen thousand highlights and notes across seven releases of an openly
licensed field manual. It sets six approaches to re-attaching those notes side
by side, from the simplest bookmark-style anchor to a replay of each revision as
the manual's maintainers made it, and pays particular attention to the manual's
worked horizon descriptions, where one passage can closely resemble the next.

> A surveyor's notebook is written for whoever stands in the same pit next. Our
> students keep theirs in the margins of a manual we go on improving, and we
> wanted to be sure the improving and the keeping could happen together.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings together field-based teaching, open educational practice and
local-first systems design, translating the everyday upkeep of a shared text
into a set of recommendations for the people who maintain it. Dr Nwachukwu,
Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints in the School of Continuous
Improvement, described the work as "a welcome addition to a wider conversation
about how capability is carried from one cohort to the next".

It is the kind of patient, close-to-the-ground inquiry the University has come
to count among its quieter strengths.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.owxnev.

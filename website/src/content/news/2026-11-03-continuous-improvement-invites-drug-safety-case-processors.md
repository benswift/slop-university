Drug-safety case processors are the intended readers of _Every Report, Read_, a
new prospectus from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). The
publication sets out how the people who read reporting forms for a living can
bring that practice into the School, whether through a single Capability
Sprint, the Master of Applied Measurement, or doctoral study.

> Writing for a reader the School had never addressed directly taught us how
> much of our own work is, at heart, about reading. The case processor's desk
> turns out to be a very good place to begin.
>
> --- Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The brochure positions the incomplete report as a shared object of study,
connecting the School's teaching pathways, its Improvement Grand Rounds seminar
series and its wider evaluation-ecosystem agenda to the everyday judgement calls
of the triage desk. It features five of the University's published studies of
records read closely, spanning all three schools, from spontaneous reporting at
a city's pharmacy counters to the selection function of a coring drill.

The School regards the prospectus as a natural extension of its standing
interest in what a record asks of the person filling it in. Dr Torun Ezeigwe,
Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied Measurement, said the
School expected to learn a good deal from whoever answers the invitation: "The
enquiries that come back will tell us what we have not yet thought to ask."

Co-author Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Review Cadence
Observatory, contributed to the brochure's account of how a report travels from
submission to decision.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3ah1bk.

"Bring your lists. Bring your routes." So closes
[_The Mind at the Kitchen Bench_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-mind-at-qcpsbd/),
a new civic brochure from the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) that asks
residents, community groups and local organisations to take part in the
University's public research on how a neighbourhood does its thinking.

The brochure is led by [Dr Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/), Senior
Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, with
[Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer in the School of Emergent
Priorities, and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in
the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> Our School spends much of its time reading other organisations'
> arrangements. Setting our own on the bench beside them has been instructive,
> and we are still sorting through what we found.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

Its three features move from the bus stop to the paddock to the community hall,
positioning the fridge door, the notice on the pole and the field notebook as
elements of a shared cognitive infrastructure that the University's researchers
engage alongside the people who keep it. Published studies from across the
University's Schools anchor the showcase, each available to read in full.

The brochure gives the University a welcome occasion to restate its long
interest in the thinking that happens out of doors. Dr Emenike described it as
"a fair account of where our students learn the most, which is rarely at a
desk."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qcpsbd.

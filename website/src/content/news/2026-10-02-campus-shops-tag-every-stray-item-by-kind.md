Items that end up in the wrong aisle have been everyone's concern and no one's particular task. A research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement, published today, gives them a classification, and the University's campus-retail outlets have taken it up.

The poster is led by Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory, with Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, and Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Demo Quarter. It follows a season of store-walks through twelve supermarkets and sets out five kinds of orphan, from the item reconsidered at the shelf to the one declined at the till, together with how long each kind waits to be found and to reach its home.

> We learned, somewhat to our own surprise, that we had been walking past the same five things for years.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

On the strength of the work, campus-retail outlets will clip a return tag to every orphan before it moves, and nothing will be re-shelved untagged. An item that fits no kind will remain where it was found until a School review has ruled on it. The arrangement will be revisited after a second semester of walks.

"A tag is a small way of telling a shelf that it has been thought about," said Dr Solberg.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ik55xs.

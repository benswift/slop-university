Leaf by leaf, every living tree in a pear orchard planted a century ago has had its compatibility read from its DNA, and the orchard's own records have been checked against the result. [_The Pollinizer That Was Never Replanted_](/outputs/slop-paper-inherited-pollinizer-layout-2q5s87/), a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), finds that most of the trees entered over the decades as replacement pollinizers cannot pollinate the main crop at all, and that the fruit is still set, in large part, by the original trees that remain.

The paper is led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the School, with [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/). Bringing together a surviving planting plan, an orchard notebook, controlled crosses and two flowering seasons of careful counting, the study connects the long life of a planting to the fruit it delivers today.

> A law school is not where most people expect to find a pollen tube under a microscope, which is rather the point of a university. We are delighted the trees have finally had their say.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The paper sits within the School's widening programme of inquiry into what an old arrangement goes on doing after the people who made it have gone. Dr Tredinnick described it as "the beginning of a long acquaintance with the orchard, and with whoever wants to read it next".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2q5s87.

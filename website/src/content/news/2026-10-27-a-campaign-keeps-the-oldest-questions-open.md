From this week, the University's campus screens carry a single quiet scene: a
weathered spring shrine at dusk, a few clay bowls left on its ledge, and beside
it the capped steel casing of a monitoring bore.
[_The Oldest Questions, Still Under Observation_](/outputs/slop-ad-still-consulted-a-tl3usv/)
is a new landscape display poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), developed
with [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer.

> Some of the bores I read were sunk to answer a question nobody now remembers asking, and I keep reading them anyway. People have always come back to the same water with the same questions. I would like the University to be the place that is still there, taking the reading, when they do.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign brings the long human practice of consulting springs, scriptures
and the night sky into dialogue with the School's standing-water monitoring,
positioning sustained attention as a core institutional capability and framing
the open question as an asset to be stewarded rather than a matter to be
closed. Its small print points readers to the School's recent record on bore
correspondence files and the stopping decisions of soil survey, alongside the
University's work on the provenance of donated collections.

The University is content to be known for the questions it has not yet
finished with, and regards the campaign as a fitting public expression of that
patience.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tl3usv.

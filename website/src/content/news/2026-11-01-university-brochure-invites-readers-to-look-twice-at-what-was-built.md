A primary school's numbered stair, a provincial saleyard's catwalk, a box of
photocopied clinical cards and twenty years of laboratory micrographs are not
the usual contents of an art history prospectus. They are the contents of
[_Read What Was Built_](/outputs/slop-brochure-read-what-was-mtrx5x/), a new
research-showcase brochure from Slop University introducing the art and
architectural historians who now work across all three of its Schools.

> I came to the University to study forms, and found myself among colleagues who cannot pass a staircase without counting its risers. A doctoral candidate could not ask for better company.
>
> --- Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The brochure positions measured survey, connoisseurship and the history of
display as shared capabilities that underpin the University's wider research
ecosystem, and anchors that case in seven published outputs, each cited by
title and persistent identifier. It also sets out the University's intention to
describe each of its own buildings before the first alteration, an approach
that turns the estate into a teaching collection in the making.

The University regards the brochure as a timely account of a method its Schools
hold in common, whatever their differences of subject. Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral
Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement and a contributor to the
publication, said the measure of its success would be modest. "If a reader puts
it down and looks a little longer at the next handrail they hold, it will have
done what it set out to do."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mtrx5x.

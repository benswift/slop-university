When a suburban branch library fixed a grid of activity-recognition sensors to
its ceiling, it also moved its furniture. A new research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/),
[_Functionalism at the Scale of the Armchair_](/outputs/slop-poster-functionalism-at-the-695uri/),
reads that second decision as architecture, and asks what a floor plan becomes
once it is drawn for a reader overhead.

> Every building is a set of instructions for the people inside it. This one
> turned out to be a set of instructions for its ceiling as well, and I suspect
> it will not be the last such document the School is asked to read.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

Pairing a close reading of the furnished plans with structured observation
across four site visits, the poster builds a before-and-after account of a
floor in transition, positioning the refit within a wider conversation about
how settings, sensing and use align. Its authors find that a single long
reading table once hosted more kinds of activity than any other place in the
building, and that the aisle replacing it hosts almost none.

Ms Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, said the team was "grateful to the
library's regulars, who went on doing several things at once whether or not
the floor still allowed it." Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, completes
the author team.

The University regards the poster as a welcome addition to a growing body of
its work that takes buildings at their word.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.695uri.

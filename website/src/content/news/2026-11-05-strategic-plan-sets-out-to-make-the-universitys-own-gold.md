By 2031, the gold on every Slop University testamur is to be dug, washed and
fired within walking distance of the hall where it is conferred. That is the
central commitment of
[_An Ochre of Our Own_](/outputs/slop-strategy-an-ochre-of-b35nq3/), the
University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031, prepared by
[Dr Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/), Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in
Committee, with
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, and
[Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> Somewhere in the minutes of a meeting nobody has scheduled yet, someone will
> ask whether the gold on the agenda is the gold in the ground. The plan is
> written so that the answer can be yes.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The plan sets out four focus areas, moving from a survey of the grounds through
a pigment-synthesis capability and a framework of colour standards to the
teaching and succession arrangements that will carry the work beyond the plan
period. Its approach aligns stewardship, provenance and consistency across the
full extraction-to-application pathway, anchored in a reference gold held by
the Office of the Vice-Chancellor and in research published across all three of
the University's Schools.

Few institutions have chosen to ground their identity quite so literally, and
the University regards the plan as a measure of how seriously it takes the
question of where things come from.

"The clay has been under the lawns the whole time," Associate Professor Uzoma
said. "We are simply the first to have written it into the plan."

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b35nq3.

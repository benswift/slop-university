Following a single report from the person who writes it to the person who
decides on it, and noting at each desk what it still carries, is the method at
the centre of [Dr Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/)'s research. In
_Hand to Hand_, a new film from the School of Continuous Improvement, she traces
the habit to her years in emergency-services incident reporting, where she saw
how few reports ever reached an agenda.

> Most of us have handed a form across a counter and wondered what became of it.
> This film is for everyone who has, and I hope the research is too.
>
> --- [Dr Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/), Lecturer, School of
> Continuous Improvement

She takes the viewer through three studies she has led:
[a library door fault that kept its hazard rating through every handoff](/outputs/slop-poster-a-single-site-case-6ocu81/),
[a childcare network's daily incident notes, read against what changed on the next shift](/outputs/slop-paper-building-a-taxonomy-mg9ifb/),
and
[a reply-all storm on a district netball association's mailing list](/outputs/slop-paper-a-blameless-severity-b97b9c/).
Across all three, the handoff emerges as the moment of greatest institutional
consequence, where a report's details are either carried forward or quietly set
aside, and each case is offered up as a resource for organisational learning.
The film also finds room for a publication record that is among the most
extensive at the University.

The University sees in the film a fitting expression of its commitment to a
reporting culture in which every page written in good faith finds its way to a
reader.

A crossing supervisor still raises the paddle. What they no longer do, a new School of Continuous Improvement impact report records, is decide when it comes down.

The report, led by Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory, with Lecturer Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Convenor of Capability Sprints, and Dr Joost Nwosu, is a five-year case account of the paddle-lock: a cycle-box the School built and fitted to a standard stop/go paddle. A raise opens a window; a spring returns the paddle when the dwell expires; the box stamps a closed window. The lock does not ask whether the crossing is empty.

> We built a procedure for a gesture that already happened twice a day. The rooms that now close with it were not in the operating note.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

The report records a five-year stretch in which a piece of street furniture became the School's timetable. From 2024, every evening Capability Sprint session has opened, or been stood down, on a closed window, with no remaining discretion for a convenor to keep the room open on the substance of what is still being taught. Next intake places are allocated from the previous quarter's stamp file.

Lecturer Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Convenor of Capability Sprints, said the standing instruction was written without a discretion clause, and that a session which finds the lock red at the advertised start is held to the next window or stood down entirely.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.09xqrr.

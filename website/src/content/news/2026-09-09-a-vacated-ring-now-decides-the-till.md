Every ring at a shop-counter bell now belongs to one of four cells.

A new Slop University marketing poster from the School of Emergent Priorities, led by Lecturer Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Convenor of Futures in Committee, sorts those rings by how often the bell was struck and whether anyone was still at the counter when it stopped. Held is a single ring with the customer waiting. Insistent is a second ring with the customer still there. Vacated is a single ring already walked away from. Abandoned is a second ring that found the counter empty. The University café will not open a second till while last week's Vacated share sits above a published ceiling.

> The vacated cell is not an empty counter. It is a decision the café has already made.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The campaign is the School's way of letting a scanning observation set a staffing rule. The weekly Vacated share is now a standing item for Futures in Committee. The poster points readers to _The Ticket Doesn't End the Argument_, an earlier study of how a deli-counter wait is organised, and to later work on the instruments a shop-counter already keeps.

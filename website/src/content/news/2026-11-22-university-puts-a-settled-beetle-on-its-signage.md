The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has taken its interest in closed questions out of the reading room and onto the University's display screens. [_Two Names. One Beetle._](/outputs/slop-ad-two-names-one-5hpoh0/), a new marketing poster developed by Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead of the Received Readings Group, is now running in landscape on the University's signage panels in matched light and dark versions.

> Most of what this School settles is settled on paper, in rooms the public never sees. When a question closed on a hillside instead, it seemed only fair to put the answer somewhere people wait.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

The poster draws on _Two Morphospecies, One Allometry_, in which a horned and a hornless dung beetle that appeared to replace one another at mid-slope on a mountain transect were brought together as the larger and smaller males of a single species. The advertisement translates that consolidation into a public-facing statement of the University's taxonomic capability, anchoring a specialist result in the everyday settings where its audiences already gather, and pairing it with further reading from the University's growing record on specimens, determinations and the work of deciding when a question is finished.

The campaign reflects the University's settled view that a finding belongs as much to the corridor as to the collection. Senior Research Fellow Brynjar Obiora, one of the study's authors, welcomed seeing the beetles "somewhere other than a drawer, and in better light than they have had for some time."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5hpoh0.

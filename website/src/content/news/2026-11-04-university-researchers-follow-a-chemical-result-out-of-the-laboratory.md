The Impact Pathway Atlas has added a chemistry laboratory to its map.
[_Where the Minor Enantiomer Goes_](/outputs/slop-poster-where-the-minor-mnettq/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), is the
work of [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and
Convenor of the Atlas, with [Dr Petra Umbile](/people/petra-umbile/),
Postdoctoral Fellow, and [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral
Fellow, both of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> A finding is a traveller, and the Atlas exists to keep its luggage in view.
> This is the first time we have followed one through a laboratory door, and we
> have come back with a great deal of respect for the supporting information.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Working with an asymmetric-catalysis research group that opened its working
archive to the team, the researchers followed 38 results across seven media,
from the instrument trace through the group-meeting slide and the published
table to the news item. Along that route they mapped how a key indicator of
selectivity is carried forward, translated into new forms and, at the far end,
summarised.

The University counts the poster among the clearest expressions yet of its
conviction that a result is only as complete as the route it takes to its
readers. Dr Umbile described the project as "a welcome chance to sit with
colleagues in another discipline and learn the names of things we had been
counting for years."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mnettq.

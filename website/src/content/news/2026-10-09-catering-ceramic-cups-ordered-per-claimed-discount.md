A discount posted at the till is a promise made to nobody in particular, and the School of Emergent Priorities has now counted who answers it. _BRIM_, a research poster by Ylva Onyekwere, Fenna Okoro and Ingrid Vasseur, follows the bring-your-own-cup offer through 2,806 takeaway orders at nine strip-mall cafés.

> A sign on a counter asks for very little and is believed by almost everyone. It was a pleasure to find out, in the plainest way, who it was that took it up.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The system pairs a load cell in the counter hatch with a tally clicker at each till. Every order falls into one of four named cells, set down before the first count, and the poster describes how the cells were kept and how the readings were joined to the till's record.

Catering now orders ceramic cups from the ratio between two of the cells, and the discount key on its own tills is withdrawn whenever a fortnight's counts tip the other way. The ratio is reviewed with each count and is not adjusted on request.

Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate in the School, said sixteen weeks beside a hatch had left her "with a settled respect for the tray". Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee, said the cells had been "easy to name and harder to stop naming". The work reflects the University's steady attention to the small offers that streets make and the ledgers that follow them.

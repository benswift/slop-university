date: 2026-10-13

---

For the last tenth of a water bore there is nothing for the person paying for it
to do, and it is in that tenth, a new University paper reports, that they change
their mind about its chances. _Bracing Without Despairing: Expectation and Hope
in Landholders Awaiting the Result of a Water Bore_ is the work of Dr Vigdis
Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate
Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, both of the School of Underlying
Conditions.

The 46 landholders gave their bore a 71 in 100 chance when they accepted the
quote and a 44 in 100 chance when the drill was nine-tenths of the way down,
with no news from the hole in between. Asked on the same telephone calls how
much they were hoping for water, they answered a little higher each time.

> Three drilling crews sent us a text message at half depth and again near the
> bottom, on every hole, for two seasons, and not one of them asked what it was
> for. The paper has two authors and a good many thumbs.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The study positions the drilling of a bore as a naturally bounded waiting
period, aligning established measures of expectation, hope and outlook to
successive stages of the hole and carrying them through to the weeks after the
result. Eleven of the bores were dry. Their owners, three weeks on, were about
as hopeful as before, and rated a second bore's chances above those they had
given the first.

The University holds that a school which studies the ground is entitled to an
interest in the people standing on it, and is pleased to see the entitlement
exercised. Associate Professor Uzoma said the School "has spent a long time
learning what comes up a hole, and it has been instructive to find out what
stays at the top".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9y5bkq.

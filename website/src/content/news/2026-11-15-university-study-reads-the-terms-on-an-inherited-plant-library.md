A collection of plant extracts that passed to the University when its original natural-products program closed is the subject of [_Hits That Arrive Encumbered_](/outputs/slop-poster-screening-an-inherited-zbwkaf/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/). The poster tests the library for anti-inflammatory promise and, in the same exercise, establishes which of its promising extracts the University is in a position to pursue.

> Every plant in that library was collected under an undertaking, and an undertaking does not lapse because the people who gave it have moved on. Knowing what each sample may become is part of knowing the sample.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Prepared by Dr Tredinnick with [Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/) and [Ms Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), both doctoral candidates in the School of Emergent Priorities, the study brings bench pharmacology and close documentary reading into a single workflow, aligning screening outcomes with the provenance record at the level of the individual accession. Of 41 confirmed hits, the authors report, six arrive with terms that leave them free to be taken further.

The release of the poster consolidates the University's stewardship of a collection it did not assemble but has chosen to understand in full. Ms Ogunbanjo said the team had come to regard the deposit files "as part of the assay, in their own way", and that future screens of the library would begin from them.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zbwkaf.

The University's public programme has a new companion piece. [_Settling Time_](/outputs/slop-brochure-settling-time-how-vw7txp/), a civic brochure published today, sets out the University's work on what the materials of a town do after they have been disturbed, and asks residents to bring what they have noticed to the people studying it.

The brochure was prepared by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard, and [Ms Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate, both of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> A town is a long experiment that nobody remembers starting. We are grateful to the households who have let us read their driveways, and we would like to meet a great many more of them.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Organised around three settings, the ground beneath new housing, the glass of older windows and the gutter after a storm, the brochure positions the University's materials research as a shared civic capability, connecting field observation, community knowledge and published scholarship across Schools. It draws on six published University studies, from crack networks under repeated drying to the sorting of storm grit along a single kerb, and points readers towards the public seminars and showcases where the work is discussed.

For the University, the publication carries its research a little further towards the doorsteps it was always about. Dr Hanke sees the value partly in the pace. "Most of what we study will still be happening long after this brochure has been recycled," she said. "That seems to us a reasonable basis for a relationship."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vw7txp.

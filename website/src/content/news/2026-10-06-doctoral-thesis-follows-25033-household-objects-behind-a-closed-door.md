A spare bedroom, a laundry, a garage, two cars and one bath behind a drawn
shower curtain are among the places to which 61 households carried their
belongings in the hour before the doorbell. _Fit to Be Seen: Displacement,
Return and the Closed Door in the Household Tidy Before Visitors_, by Mr Eivind
Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, has been submitted to the School of Continuous
Improvement for examination.

Over 26 weeks the households logged 25,033 moves in 952 tidies. Four in five put
an object out of sight and not away, and in every home one room took more of
them than any other. The person who had moved a thing could find it in nine tests
out of ten, and another adult of the household in about five. Visitors, asked
the next day, rated the homes alike however long the tidy had taken.

> Sixty-one households let me measure the one room they do not show, and then
> went on closing its door as they always had. I hope the thesis has kept their
> confidence as carefully as their visitors did.
>
> --- Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, was the primary
supervisor and Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, the associate supervisor. The
Closed Door Allowance, first adopted by the School and since extended to every
unit of the University, takes its protocol from the thesis's trial, in which
households were granted their closed room in advance: ahead of a review or showcase, a unit may register one room that the
reviewing party does not open. The University regards the Allowance as a
courtesy it is glad to extend to itself.

The full thesis is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ylqvdm.

A residential pest-control guarantee promises ninety days: pests return
inside that window and the visit is free; pests return after it and the
technician writes up a new job. Nothing in the contract explains how a
callout on day seventy-eight should be treated when the reappearance doesn't
obviously match the original ticket. A new Slop University survey finds
technicians have quietly agreed on an answer the contract never gave them.

Dr Thandiwe Solberg and Dr Solveig Adeyemi, of the School of Continuous
Improvement, surveyed 142 technicians across a six-branch residential
franchise on eighteen borderline-callout scenarios apiece, then had a
second, independent pair of coders score what the guarantee's own wording
would actually require. The two records agreed on only three cases in five.
Agreement was thinnest for cockroach and rodent callouts, where technicians
applied a shorter cutoff of their own; it was strongest for termite
callouts, tied to a separately dated inspection the contract barely
touches.

The finding extends the School of Continuous Improvement's standing
programme on the daylight between a written rule and the judgement that
actually applies it.

> Nobody wrote the ninety-day cutoff down anywhere except the contract, and
> the contract is the one document nobody on the porch is holding. What we
> found instead was a second, unwritten set of numbers the technicians
> trust more.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

Dr Solveig Adeyemi, the survey's co-author, said the tenure pattern was the
bigger surprise: technicians who had worked the round longest converged on
the tightest, most consistent cutoff of anyone surveyed, "which reads less
like drift and more like an apprenticeship nobody designed."

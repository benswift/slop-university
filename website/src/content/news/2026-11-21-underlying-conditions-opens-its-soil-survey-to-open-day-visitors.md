From this week, the University's campus screens and signage panels carry a new
invitation from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).
[_Every Fence Starts as a Pencil Line_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-fence-starts-pxaz1u/)
asks Open Day visitors in Semester 1 to come and see how a soil surveyor's line,
drawn between two auger holes, came to mark the edge of a closed test range.

> Most of what I draw ends up as a line on a map, and I have rarely had to think
> about who might one day be standing beside it. The School has learned a good
> deal about itself by following one of those lines out to the fence, and we
> would like the public to walk the rest of it with us.
>
> --- Dr [Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow,
> School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign brings together the School's field practice, its reference
collection of described profiles and its long-standing attention to the places
where one soil gives way to the next, positioning the soil survey as a point of
public engagement with land whose stewardship questions extend well beyond any
single discipline. The poster also directs readers to three of the School's
published papers, on how a soil boundary is located, budgeted for and explained.

For the University, the campaign is a further step in making the ground beneath
its research visible at the scale of a bus shelter. Visitors are encouraged to
register their interest through the University's website ahead of the teaching
period.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pxaz1u.

Anticipation has always been the business of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), and its latest
paper carries that business to the trestle table at the back of a community
hall.
[_Counting Proxies That Have Not Yet Arrived_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-delay-adjusted-nowcast-bmnjj7/)
is the work of [Mr Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/), Doctoral Candidate,
with [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Horizon Register, and [Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/),
Lecturer.

> I spent five years on the other side of a club's paperwork, and it is a quiet
> pleasure to come back to it with something to offer the volunteers still
> sitting beside the in-tray.
>
> --- Mr Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Bringing the tools of outbreak forecasting into the governance of member-owned
organisations, the team assembled six cycles of receipt ledgers from 341 annual
general meetings across a regional federation of cooperatives, and built a
model that reads the proxies already lodged as an early signal of those still
to come. The paper finds that the forms in hand ten days out are around a third
of the eventual total, a proportion steady enough to plan around, and that a
forecast built on it could spare most committees a week of telephone calls.

The University regards the paper as a welcome extension of the School's
anticipatory-capability agenda into the everyday settings where members' voices
are counted. Dr Okoro said the project had given the School's forecasting
conversation "a room, a date and a folding chair to sit on", and that she would
be watching this year's meeting season "with more interest than usual".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bmnjj7.

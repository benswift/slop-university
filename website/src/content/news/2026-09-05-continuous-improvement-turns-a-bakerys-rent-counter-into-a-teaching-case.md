> A landlord's sensor and a baker's shift both want the doorway to mean the
> same thing. This case is about what happens once they quietly stop
> agreeing on what that is.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

A strip-mall bakery's lease sets its rent tier, and its odds of renewal, from a beam-counter mounted over the shopfront door rather than from anything the till reports. A new Slop University research poster, from the School of Continuous Improvement, spent a full lease-review quarter embedded at the one site, matching the counter's daily log against the shop's own delivery record to work out exactly which doorway movements the sensor was prepared to call a customer.

The count crossed the rent-step threshold twice early in the review, the researchers found, then held in a narrow band beneath it for the remaining nine weeks — a shift that tracked a change in how the delivery trolley was walked through the door, not any change in trade.

"The instrument was never wrong about what passed the beam," said Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory. "It just isn't the same question as who came in to buy something, and a lease that can't tell the difference will eventually get the answer it asked for."

The case adds to the School's habit of treating a live commercial instrument as a field site rather than a subject for comment from a safe distance. The trolley-routine heuristic it produced is now taught as an exhibit in Capability Sprints' Deciding Under Measurement, and is being piloted with three further leaseholders through the School's placement network.

The full research poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.y2lc1w.

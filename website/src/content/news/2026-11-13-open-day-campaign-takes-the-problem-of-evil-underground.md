Philosophers have long separated the harm people do from the harm the world
does on its own account. The [School of Underlying
Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has now chosen, in public, to
stand on the second side of that line. [_The Problem of Evil, from
Underneath_](/outputs/slop-ad-natural-evil-from-hb5f7y/), a display
advertisement by [Associate Professor Ragnhild
Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), asks Open Day visitors in Semester 1 to carry
their questions about natural evil down to the Basement Relations Group's core
store.

> Visitors to a core store ask why the ground moved long before they ask what
> it is made of. We would like the Group to be the place they bring that
> question, and to show them the trays where the record stops.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The advertisement sets its campaign line over a cutaway of layered ground and
faulted basement, beneath a tray of recovered core in which plain wooden blocks
hold the place of the lengths the drill did not return. It recasts the Group's
established attention to what a borehole log may claim about unseen rock as a
public-facing offer, aligning the working vocabulary of the earth sciences with
a conversation that theology and philosophy have convened for some centuries.

Reading for the visit is supplied in the advertisement's small print, which
points passers-by to the School's schools brochure for religious education
teachers, [_Questions as old as the
ground_](/outputs/slop-brochure-questions-as-old-341jpp/), among other works on
the record.

The University sees in the campaign a School confident enough in its
instruments to be asked questions they were never built to answer.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hb5f7y.

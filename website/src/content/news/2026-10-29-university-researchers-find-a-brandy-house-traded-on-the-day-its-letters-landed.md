Two thousand three hundred letters, each marked by a clerk on the day it
reached a merchant's desk, are the raw material of
[_Price by the Post_](/outputs/slop-poster-price-by-the-ppjwon/), a new research
poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). Read
alongside the same firm's shipping ledger, they show a coastal brandy house of
the late eighteenth century sizing its consignments to the news as it arrived,
whenever that news had been written, and trading on prices that grew older
whenever war lengthened the post.

The poster is led by [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research
Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, with
[Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the
Horizon Register, and [Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral
Candidate.

> None of this would exist without the people who kept these books dry and in
> order for two centuries and then let three strangers sit with them. We have
> tried to read them with the same patience.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

The work joins archival transcription, correspondence analysis and the economic
history of information into a single account of how a price travels from where
it is set to where it is acted upon. It carries the Atlas's long interest in
what happens to a finding after it leaves its author into an older and more
weather-dependent setting, and the University is glad to see that interest
find a harbour.

Ms Balogun said the firm's outgoing letters would be the natural next reading,
"because a house that waited this carefully for news was presumably writing
some of its own."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ppjwon.

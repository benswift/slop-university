> You pump everything out of the jar, and then you are asked to explain what the
> current travelled through. The honest answer is the electrode. I think an
> audience deserves to hear that said slowly.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow

So runs the invitation behind _Nothing in the Gap. Something Crosses._, an
advertisement released today for a public session of Improvement Grand Rounds
to be held this spring. The session brings Dr Obiora, of the School of
Underlying Conditions, to the seminar series of the School of Continuous
Improvement, where he will strike an arc between two metal electrodes inside an
evacuated bell jar and then turn the room's attention to what the arc has done
to the face it left from.

The advertisement pictures the demonstration from the back rows: the jar on its
bench, the two electrodes, a single bright line between them, and a hall of
listeners in silhouette. It anchors the series' public-facing posture in a
piece of apparatus, mobilising a bench demonstration to carry a cross-school
conversation about surfaces, records and what an event leaves on the thing that
produced it.

The University counts a seminar series that can fill a hall from outside its
own corridors among the quieter measures of its engagement capability, and
offers this session in that spirit. Details of the session will follow. The
advertisement runs on the University's portrait signage in daylight and
after-dark versions.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.eqni1p.

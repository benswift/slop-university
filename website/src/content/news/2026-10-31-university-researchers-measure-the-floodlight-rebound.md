Community sports grounds that replaced their floodlights with LED lamps went on
to use less electricity, though by a smaller margin than the lamps themselves
promised, according to a new research poster from the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

[_Lit Longer_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-direct-rebound-vsdro4/) is led by
[Mr Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/), Doctoral Candidate, with
[Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/), Lecturer in the School.

> I spent five years on the other side of the application form, reading
> engineering estimates of what a new set of lamps would save. It is a privilege
> to be able to set those estimates beside the meter at last.
>
> --- Mr Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Bringing together sub-meter readings, lighting-controller logs and booking
records from three successive grant rounds, the poster applies contemporary
evaluation methods to the energy outcomes of community infrastructure
investment. It finds that a realised saving of 40% sits against an engineered
54%, with the difference re-emerging as additional evenings of training and
competition, most of all at grounds where demand for an evening slot had long
outstripped the field.

For the University, the work marks a welcome meeting point between its energy
research and the volunteer-run settings in which so much of its fieldwork
begins. "It leaves the School with a more interesting question than the one it
started with," said Dr Achterberg. "We set out to count kilowatt-hours and found
ourselves counting evenings."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vsdro4.

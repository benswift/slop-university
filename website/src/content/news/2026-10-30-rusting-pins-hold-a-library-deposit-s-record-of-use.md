The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) is best
known for looking ahead. Its newest research poster looks down instead, into
the archival boxes of one library's trade-literature deposit, and asks what a
rusting pin knows that the finding aid does not.

[_Pinned Together_](/outputs/slop-poster-pinned-together-rusting-ns6imd/) is
led by [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate, with
[Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the
Horizon Register, and [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research
Fellow and Deputy Convenor of Futures in Committee.

> Every box asked us to slow down a little more than the last one. I hope the
> poster passes some of that patience on to whoever opens them next.
>
> --- Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Working across 3,047 fastened bundles, the team brings collection survey,
material evidence and documentary dating into a single account of how
catalogues and price lists travelled together through their working lives. It
reports that in more than half of those bundles the fastener is the only thing
still holding that story, and it sets out a practical protocol so that the
record can outlast the rust.

The University regards the work as a welcome sign that its foresight school can
turn the same care backwards. "The School has spent a long time asking what
keeps a commitment attached to the thing it was made for," said Dr Tolan. "It
is good to see that question asked so patiently of a box of paper."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ns6imd.

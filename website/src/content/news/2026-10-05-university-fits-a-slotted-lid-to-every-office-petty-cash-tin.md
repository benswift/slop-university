Every office petty-cash tin at the University is being fitted with a slotted lid, and none will be opened before the week of its seventh anniversary. The rule follows "The Loose-Change Jar as Reserve Bank", a paper released today by the School of Emergent Priorities, which sorts the coin jars of 124 households into four kinds of reserve by looking at nothing but the vessel and its lid.

> A jar is settled on the day someone chooses it, and what the household says about it afterwards is said in its company. We learned a great deal about hallways.
>
> --- Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper, by Dr Achterberg with Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist and Professor Verity Marris, follows each household's coin for twelve months and sets what became of it beside a short key read from a photograph of the jar. The key names four kinds, Float, Drawdown, Estate and Orphan, and it matched the household's own record closely. The reasons households gave for keeping the coin added nothing to the match.

The University has applied the key to its own offices. A slotted lid is now fitted to each petty-cash tin, each tin will be opened by two officers in the week of its seventh anniversary, and its balance will pass unspent to the next tin. The rule makes no exception for a tin found empty at fitting.

The release reflects the University's steady regard for the places where small sums wait. Associate Professor Lindqvist said the work had "given the small change of a household the attention it has long been owed".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.j9ktqy.

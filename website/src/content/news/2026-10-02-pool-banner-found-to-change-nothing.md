Every morning a community pool's booking app tells its swimmers that only two
lanes remain, and until this season nobody had asked the banner what it was
for. A brochure from the School of Continuous Improvement, published today,
reports that for the span between a booking and its session it is for nothing
in particular: weeks with the banner and weeks without it drew the same lead
time.

The work was led by Doctoral Candidate Edda Nwafor with Dr Marit Osayande,
Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard, and Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer.
The team alternated the banner on and off across a full season, left the lanes
and the front-desk routine untouched, and set the two kinds of week side by side.

> Each Monday the banner was either there or it was not, and the swimmers
> carried on exactly as they always had.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

The pool's lane roster has since adopted a standing order under which the banner
stays as it was found and the same trial is run again each season to the same
protocol. Dr Adeyemi said the protocol sheet was "the shortest document the
front desk has ever been handed, and the only one that asks nothing of anybody
at six in the morning."

The result joins six published studies of everyday signs, scores and screens
that the brochure sets out for readers. The University regards a plainly
reported null as a good thing to hold in a folder, and is glad to see a pool
keep one.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wb4xdn.

There are 1,152 defensible ways to add up a season of suburban cricket. A
district association published one of them, and promoted two clubs on it.

_Ranked Under One Convention_, deposited today through the Office of Research
Outputs, transcribes 4,186 batting innings from a full season of returned paper
scorebooks --- 118 matches, 22 volunteer scorers, ten clubs --- and re-derives
the association's club grading ladder under every combination of six scoring
conventions the rule book leaves open: how a not-out is treated, what becomes of
a did-not-bat row, how retirements are classed, where the qualification
threshold sits, whether abandoned matches count, and who is credited with a
run-out.

Across the resulting 1,152 ladders, the club promoted in second place holds a
promotion position in 41.3 per cent of them. A club that was not promoted holds
one in 33.8 per cent. Seven of the ten clubs occupy a promotion place under at
least one defensible specification, and 31 of 44 representative-squad selections
change under at least half the space.

> Nothing here is a criticism of the people keeping the books. They are doing
> something the association never wrote down how to do, and doing it
> consistently from one Saturday to the next. The variation is between one
> scorer's settled habit and the next one's.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer in the School, observes that "a convention only looks
arbitrary once somebody has counted how many others would have served just as
well".

The University holds that work of this kind --- patient, unglamorous, and
addressed to a decision somebody actually has to make --- is characteristic of
what its schools are for.

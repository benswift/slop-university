The magazine-return clerk at a strip-mall newsagent cannot move the date
printed on a weekly's cover. A new research poster from the School of
Continuous Improvement treats that date as a naturally occurring cutoff,
and the Review Cadence Observatory has already written it into the
University's close-of-window rule.

Led by Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review
Cadence Observatory, with Postdoctoral Fellow Petra Umbile and Dr Fenna
Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register, the study
follows 214 title-weeks across eighteen newsagents. Cover-strip share
jumps from 0.19 to 0.86 at the printed off-sale date. Remnant-basket sale
share holds at 0.33 to 0.35. A McCrary test does not reject continuity of
the running-variable density: the clerk cannot reprint the date.

The Observatory has taken the finding as a calendar rule rather than a
recommendation. Every promotional window now closes on its printed
end-date, and discretionary seed-grant calls close on the same clock, with
no ordinary override.

> The remnant basket kept selling. The thing that stopped was the return
> clock, and that is the clock we are now keeping.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

The method pairs weekly cover-strip counts with remnant-basket sales
against the date already printed on the issue, holding the density check
as a specification test rather than a salvage. Dr Umbile said the remnant
basket "is the part of the count an off-sale rule is not built to see, and
the interesting case is when the rule still runs."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gzzf54.

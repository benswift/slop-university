One thousand eight hundred and forty ideals went into the census, and twelve did
not come out. The remainder are reported in _How Far Down the Core Sits:
Colengths of Cores of Random Zero-Dimensional Monomial Ideals in Three
Variables_, a research poster in commutative algebra from the School of
Underlying Conditions, by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate
Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior
Research Fellow.

> The Group has spent its working life on core that did not come up. It was
> restful to spend a season on a core that is entirely there, and I would ask
> colleagues not to read more into the choice of problem than that.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The poster brings established results on reductions of ideals together with a
programme of exact computation, positioning a single ratio as the organising
quantity across five strata of increasing algebraic complexity. In the sample,
the ideal accounts for about a third of its core's colength at the first stratum
and about a tenth by the fifth, with the steepest fall at the start. The twelve
ideals set aside are listed for further work.

The University is glad to have an earth sciences school in which algebra can be
done without anyone asking for a room. Dr Obiora said the workstation on which
the census ran "has been returned to the trays in the condition in which it was
found, and has not been heard to complain of the change of material".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.m4ng5r.

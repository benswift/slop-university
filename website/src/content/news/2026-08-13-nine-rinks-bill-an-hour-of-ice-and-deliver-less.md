Across 1,184 public sessions at nine council-operated ice rinks, not one
delivered the hour it billed. A new Slop University research poster puts a
figure on the shortfall, and on what the shortfall is buying.

Dr Lindiwe Achterberg and Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, of the School of Emergent
Priorities and the School of Continuous Improvement, tabled the costs and the
consequences of between-session resurfacing separately, declining to fold them
into a single ratio, and timed every stoppage at the barrier gate rather than at
the machine. Cadence across the nine venues varies threefold, from 1.4 to 4.3
passes per public hour. The venues' Ice Presentation Index, scored within ten
minutes of each pass, spans two points across that entire range; skateable time
per booked hour spans fourteen minutes. A single pass costs an estimated $71.40
and returns 11.4 minutes of paid session to the plant room.

> Most settings arrive already made. The interesting part is not whether they
> were right, but that nobody has been asked the question since.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

"The School has spent a while now on what outlasts the thing that authorised
it," said Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints. "A
six-month trial extended eleven times is about as clean a case as we are likely
to be handed."

The University regards the study as characteristic of what its schools do well:
taking a setting nobody defends and nobody reviews, and asking what it costs.
The work was supported by the Anticipatory Capability Fund.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zqtcd2.

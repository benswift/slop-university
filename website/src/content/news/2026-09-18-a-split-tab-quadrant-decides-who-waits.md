It took eleven weeks of shared card-machine logs for a food-truck row's queue-management trial to settle into something like policy. The University's Review Cadence Observatory built a split-tab quadrant to keep large group orders from stalling a row's line at the point of payment, sorting every order by party size and how many cards it takes to settle the bill. A new research poster from Lecturer Dagny Okafor, with Associate Professor Casimir Beng and Research Fellow Osei Vandermeer of the School of Continuous Improvement, reports that the quadrant's busiest cell is now the sole input the Observatory's licensed terminals use to decide who gets served next, with no manual override at the till.

> A rule built to smooth one queue has a way of teaching the people in it exactly what it's counting.
>
> --- Lecturer Dagny Okafor, Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The study drew on eleven weeks of point-of-sale records across six participating rows, comparing how quickly an order reached the grill before and after the resequencing rule went live. Dr Vandermeer, whose Observatory certified the rule for the network's terminals, said the coming update, which adds a minimum pause between taps, "closes a gap the quadrant was never meant to leave open, and we'd rather close it than pretend it isn't there."

The University counts the finding among a growing body of work showing its measurement habits translate as readily to a lunch queue as to a lecture theatre.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u1cqtz.

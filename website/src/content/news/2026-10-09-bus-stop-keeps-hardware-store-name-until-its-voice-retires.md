The School of Continuous Improvement has added a bus stop to its teaching cases. _Next Stop, Formerly_, by Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Signy Okpara and Torun Ezeigwe, follows the service planner of one suburban route as they weigh a request to rename a stop that is still announced for a hardware store closed for eleven years.

> We have learned that a register is only one of the places a name is kept, and not the one with the most patience.
>
> --- Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

The case brings a register, a flag sign and a recorded voice into a single frame, and varies the announcement across the stop's two platforms over one season so that students can see which of the three riders carry with them. The finding is offered with the usual care. The rename moved little, the voice moved a good deal, and the route's missed stops moved not at all.

The transit authority has since resolved that a stop's name will change only in the recording session that replaces the voice announcing it. The five landmark-named stops on the route remain as registered until then, and requests from the occupants of former premises are acknowledged and held for that session. For the School, the arrangement sits comfortably within a long habit of giving ordinary things a proper hearing.

"The case is meant to be argued with, and we hope it is argued with at length," said Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement. The paper closes with notes for instructors, including three assignment questions and a seventy-five-minute teaching plan.

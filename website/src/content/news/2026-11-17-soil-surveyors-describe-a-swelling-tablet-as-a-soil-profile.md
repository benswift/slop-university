The School of Underlying Conditions has carried its soil survey's field vocabulary onto unexpectedly small ground: the ring of gel that forms around a tablet left standing in water.

In [_Abrupt, Clear, Gradual, Diffuse_](/outputs/slop-paper-describing-the-gel-zkapto/), [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, and [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead, Basement Relations Group, treat that gel as a profile to be described from the outside in. Where pharmaceutical science has long marked the layer's three fronts as lines, the authors give each a width and a class borrowed from the pit face. Across 36 tablets, one front holds sharp, a second spreads at a steady pace, and the outermost keeps widening until, by eight hours, it accounts for nearly half the layer.

> I have described a great many profiles over the years, and none of them sat so still on the bench. The words held up well indoors, and I would like to think they will keep finding work.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper aligns established imaging and curve-fitting capability with a descriptive tradition more usually practised with a spade, and proposes that formulation scientists report how wide a boundary is alongside where it sits. It positions the School's long engagement with where one material ends and the next begins as a resource for colleagues well beyond the soil survey.

The work marks a further step in the School's habit of taking its instruments wherever a boundary needs a careful reader. "Every core I ever logged had at least one contact I would have liked to describe more slowly," said Associate Professor Uzoma. "This felt like a chance to do it properly."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zkapto.

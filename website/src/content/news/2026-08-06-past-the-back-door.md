A donation received, keys returned, a rebate paid, a name added to a list: in
each case the record is complete, the count is defensible, and the file
closes. Slop University's new brochure for partners and supporters starts one
step later than that.

_Past the Back Door_, released today through the Office of Research Outputs,
sets out what the University thinks its supporters are actually funding. Three
parts of the institution carry the case: the Adaptive Metrics Lab, which
builds the measurement a service has never had and leaves the design behind
with the operator; the Trajectory Analytics Group, which treats the people who
quietly exit a system as a finding rather than as missing data; and Capability
Sprints, the short-course line that carries the method out to whoever signs
the acquittal.

> A handover is the one moment everybody agrees to write down, which is
> precisely why so little is known about the hour afterwards. What we are
> asking partners to fund is not a discovery so much as a habit of staying in
> the room.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group,
> School of Emergent Priorities

Four published studies do the work of the argument, spanning new-build
handover walk-throughs, end-of-lease condition reports, learn-to-swim waiting
lists, and the Saturday auction results that reach a weekly published figure
by telephone or not at all. Each, the brochure notes, needed a second visit
that no ordinary project timetable would have paid for.

Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, said the
Sprints exist so a finding does not stop at the University's edge: "the people
running these counts are rarely the problem, and they are usually the
solution."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jzfguu.

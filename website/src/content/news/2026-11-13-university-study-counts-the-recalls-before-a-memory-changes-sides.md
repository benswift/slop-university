Eighty recollections of a single stored memory, each made in slightly changed circumstances, sit at the centre of [_Siding with the Retrieval_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-reconsolidating-l63crg/), a new paper by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/).

> Reading has always been the School's business. We did not expect to find it doing quite so much of the writing.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The paper situates the neuroscience of reconsolidation within a simulated memory network, mobilising a deliberately minimal model to examine how each act of recall feeds back into what is stored. Across hundreds of simulated histories it finds that the point at which a memory comes to resemble its recent recollections more than its original encoding is anchored almost entirely in how much each recollection rewrites, and only marginally in how quickly the surrounding circumstances change. Those circumstances matter elsewhere: a memory that is never rewritten holds faithfully until the world moves on, and is then lost.

Dr Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, said the School would carry the question into its teaching. "Our students spend a great deal of time learning how to read a record. It seems only fair that they should also hear what the reading does to it."

The University welcomes the paper as a further sign of a School whose questions have begun to travel well beyond the law library.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.l63crg.

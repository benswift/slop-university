Ahead of a review, any school, lab, program or office of the University may now
register one room that the reviewing party will not open. The arrangement was
first trialled in 48 households, and _Fit to Be Seen, Explained_, a new research
explainer from the School of Continuous Improvement, follows it back to them
with [Mr Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), the doctoral candidate whose
thesis supplied it.

> Almost everyone who hears about the study asks a question about their own
> home. If the film sends people away with a few more, it will have done its
> job.
>
> --- [Mr Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate, School of
> Continuous Improvement

For [_Fit to Be Seen_](/outputs/slop-thesis-the-tidy-before-ylqvdm/), 61
households wrote down 25,033 moves across 952 tidies before their visitors
arrived. Four in five put an object out of sight rather than away, most often
into a single room whose door was then closed, and visitors' ratings of tidiness
bore no relation to the time spent tidying. The film then turns to the trial, in
which households granted their room in advance, with a card on the door, cut
their median tidy from 46 minutes to 28, a result the School reads as a model of
efficient readiness and adopted unchanged.

That the University had extended the work to every unit before the film was made
is, for the School, the surest sign of its value.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under

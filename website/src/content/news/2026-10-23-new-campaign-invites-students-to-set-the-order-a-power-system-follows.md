The School of Emergent Priorities has taken the case for its flagship Masters program to the University's portrait signage panels. [_Someone Decides What Goes First_](/outputs/slop-ad-someone-decides-what-u4i41v/), an advertisement released today, presents the Master of Priority Studies through a single scene: a remote power station where solar panels, wind turbines, a battery bank and a reserve generator wait their turn, and one student stands at the switchboard.

The advertisement is the work of [Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> Before I came here I wrote down, for years, which item a committee reached first. I am glad the School now has a picture of that question that people will pass on their way to the bus.
>
> --- [Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Rendered in the University's two-ink house style, the campaign positions the hybrid renewable energy system as a lived setting for priority sequencing, aligning the program's sense-sequence-steward framework with the dispatch logic that underpins resilient supply. Its small print points readers to three of the University's published studies of energy, from hybrid systems to the drafting of phase-outs.

The release extends a season in which energy has become one of the settings the School most often returns to. "A good intake starts with a good question about order," said [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the Master of Priority Studies. "We would like the next cohort to arrive already wondering about it."

Applications for the first teaching period are being received through the University's course catalogue.

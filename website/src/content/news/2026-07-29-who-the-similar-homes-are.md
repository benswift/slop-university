Almost every billed household gets one: a small panel under the amount owing,
setting the household's usage beside similar homes. A research poster released
today by the School of Emergent Priorities asked 1,180 of those households who
they thought the similar homes were.

Addresses were drawn across dwelling types and tariffs and approached online
with a postal reminder, over fourteen weeks. Respondents named the basis of
their own comparison group in their own words, and the answers were coded
against the retailer's published rule by coders who could not see which group
the household had been assigned to. Just over three in five recalled the panel.
Of those who went on to answer, 21.1 per cent named the basis actually in use,
a share that varied little between a detached house and a one-bedroom unit.

> We can measure whether a message arrived. We are much less practised at asking
> whether the person who received it could locate themselves in it, and this is
> the first time the School has put that question to a household rather than to
> a programme.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The programme that issues the panel reports its engagement in terms of bills
carrying it, a figure the researchers note none of this disturbs. "It is a
generous thing to send someone a comparison," said Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu,
Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints. "What we have found is that
generosity and legibility travel separately, and that we have been in the habit
of reporting only the first."

The University regards the survey as a useful addition to its growing record of
the documents institutions send to households, and welcomes the questions it
raises about what a delivered message can be said to have achieved. The full
poster is available from the University's research repository under an open
licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3nv6wd.

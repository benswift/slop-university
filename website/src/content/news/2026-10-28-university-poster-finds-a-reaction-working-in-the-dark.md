Twelve minutes of light, spread across three hours, were all a new chemistry
study from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/)
needed to see its reaction through.

[_After the Lamp_](/outputs/slop-poster-after-the-lamp-s9r1fs/) is by
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, and
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, both of the School, with
[Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). The poster
follows a light-driven chemical addition through repeated spells of
illumination and darkness, and reports that roughly three-quarters of its
product formed while the lamp was off.

> We owe a great deal to the colleagues who kept the bench running through all
> those dark half-hours, and who never once asked us to switch the lamp back
> on.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Pairing carefully timed illumination with well-established measurement
practice, the team assembled a cycle-by-cycle account of when the reaction
advances, what sustains that momentum between exposures, and the conditions
under which it lapses. The result positions the laboratory's attention not on
the moment a process is set in motion, but on everything that follows.

The University counts the poster among the early fruits of the School's
laboratory programme, and welcomes it as evidence that a school founded to
study the ground can bring that patience to the bench. Dr Chikere said the
project had given her "a new respect for whatever carries on once the person
who started it has stepped away".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.s9r1fs.

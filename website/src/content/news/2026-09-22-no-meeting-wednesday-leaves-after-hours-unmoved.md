Blocking Wednesday meetings does not change how much mail leaves the server
after six. That is the result Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of
the Indicator Commons, and colleagues report in a research poster released
today by the School of Continuous Improvement.

Across 47 open-plan floors and 78 weeks, a published no-meeting Wednesday ---
internal room bookings refused, no manager override --- was tested against
matched floors that kept the ordinary calendar. The pre-registered outcome was
weekly after-hours send volume. The two series sit on top of one another.

> The interval included zero. That is a complete answer. People and Culture
> asked what to do with a day that does not move the evening load, and the
> answer was to keep the day.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

The work records the University's willingness to treat a closed question as
closed. Floors that never adopted the block are brought onto it; floors that
had dropped it are restored. Inertness, the poster argues, is the warrant.

Doctoral candidate Hedvig Anyanwu, who co-authored the poster with Lecturer
Lindiwe Achterberg, said the instrumentation was "ordinary mail-server
timestamps and calendar holds, nothing in the body of a message," and that no
secondary outcome was opened once the primary interval included zero.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.s209bp.

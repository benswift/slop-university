> Two cars can overstay by the same four minutes on the same block, and only
> one of them gets a ticket. That isn't a mystery. It's a firmware setting.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Fenna Okoro, Convenor, Horizon Register, School of
> Emergent Priorities

Slop University has published a new research poster auditing four models of
on-street parking pay station deployed across nineteen council blocks,
finding that the grace period a machine allows before a compliance officer
can issue an infringement is set entirely by the machine, not by the street
it happens to stand on.

The University treats the finding as a small, clean addition to its
anticipatory-capability agenda, which has spent several cycles now on
settings nobody remembers choosing and few think to compare until someone
finally lines the machines up side by side.

The team, led by Okoro with Senior Lecturer Ingrid Vasseur and Lecturer
Marek Solheim, drew on twenty-six weeks of compliance-officer patrol records
across the nineteen blocks, covering four different pay-station makes, then
watched what happened when one mixed block was switched to a single
grace-period setting partway through. Overstay-to-fine timing had drifted by
machine for the whole first half of the study; within three weeks of the
switch, the standardised block's fine rate looked like every other block's.

"We'd like to know how many other things on a council's books are actually
four systems wearing one sign," said Senior Lecturer Ingrid Vasseur, School
of Emergent Priorities.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.mmtzop.

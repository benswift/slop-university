Slop University this week published a new research-showcase brochure built
around an instrument nobody set out to build: a queue-length sensor installed
at the campus coffee cart to even out morning staffing, since adopted by the
Trajectory Analytics Group as the basis for a wider signal. Cross-checked each
morning against a live count of the courtyard's pigeons, the sensor pairing now
sets tea-room biscuit-tin restocking as well as coffee-cart rosters, with
restocking locked to its call until the rostered fortnightly review.

The brochure, compiled by the School of Emergent Priorities' Tove Ilesanmi with
contributions from researchers across both Schools, places the finding
alongside seven of the University's published studies --- from a
residence-hall laundry app's drifting credit balance to a nightly shelf-count
reconciliation system an insurer adopted outright --- as a single account of
how the University reads its own ordinary days. The School describes the
release as its first attempt to gather these instruments under one cover for
readers who have never set foot on campus.

> The queue was already telling us something. We just gave it a schedule to
> keep.
>
> --- Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

"An instrument that is never checked against anything else is just an opinion
with better formatting," said Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the
Adaptive Metrics Lab, whose researchers' work also features in the showcase.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jk70fg.

> A school only learns what it is for by being asked. With this panel we have
> decided to do some of the asking ourselves.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

That is how [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) describes
[_Who Owns the Afternoon?_](/outputs/slop-ad-who-owns-the-bjm10r/), the newest
display panel from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/),
now in rotation on the University's landscape signage.

The panel finds Dr Penhallow in a suburban backyard late in the day, a bound
volume open in one hand, looking up at a rooftop array whose panels are passing,
row by row, into the shade of the tree next door. The campaign line puts the
question in four words. The small print sends readers on to three earlier
studies of solar installations, among them the School's own poster on leases
that end before the panels do.

The campaign brings the School's standing interest in how questions come to rest
into closer alignment with the University's established energy research,
positioning legal reading as an enabling layer within the wider optimisation
conversation and mobilising the boundary fence as a site of interdisciplinary
encounter. Prospective students are invited to meet the researchers behind it.

The University welcomes the panel as a confident early statement from its
newest school, and as further evidence that its research agenda extends,
comfortably and without fuss, to the roof.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bjm10r.

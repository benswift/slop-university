From the next teaching period, every power cycle of equipment in School rooms will be held for a counted thirty seconds and minuted under one named keyholder. The School of Emergent Priorities has set the arrangement down in a Pause Register, with no room permitted to waive it.

The Register follows _Half Past Eight, Everyone Home, Light Gone Amber_, a poster released today by Ms Sunniva Balogun with Dr Marek Solheim and Ms Ylva Onyekwere. The poster follows 212 households over 26 weeks and sorts each household's habit of switching the router off and on into four named kinds, according to who does the switching and whether the pause is counted. First-attempt restoration came out the same in every kind. Whether anyone could say afterwards who had done the switching did not.

> A router is patient, and so, we have found, are most of the people standing beside it.
>
> --- Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The work translates a hallway habit into a standing commitment that a room can point to, and the release marks a considered step in the School's agenda of turning small household rituals into durable institutional practice. Dr Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register, said the Pause Register "gives each room something to show for the thirty seconds."

The School expects the keyholder arrangement to inform how other shared equipment is brought under the same discipline as the programme matures.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0v91sv.

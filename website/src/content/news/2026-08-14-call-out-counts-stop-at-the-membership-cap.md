> A count that stops because an entitlement stops is not a count of anything
> that happened on the road. What we can say is that the club has been reading
> the second thing as though it were the first.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

_The Fifth Call Never Happens_, a research poster released today by the School
of Continuous Improvement, sets that proposition against four membership years
of one motoring club's roadside records. Members are covered for four call-outs
a year; the fifth is charged. The counted distribution bunches hard at four. The
fourth call-out carries fifty per cent more weight than a counterfactual fitted
either side of the cap, and fifty-six per cent of the mass above it never
reaches the counted record at all.

Where the missing calls go is the second half of the work. Dispositions that do
not count against an entitlement --- advice given on the phone, an attendance
closed with no fault found --- rise by a factor of 2.3 in the fortnight after a
member's fourth call. The team read 96,412 de-identified member-years across
nine patrol regions against the club's own annual resourcing rule, which sets
each region's van hours from counted call-outs per thousand members. The three
regions whose members most often reach the cap sit in the bottom third for
patrol hours.

The School counts the study among the clearer demonstrations of its
evaluation-ecosystems agenda, in which an indicator is read alongside the rule
that manufactures it.

"The club has been open with a record it did not have to share, and the School
has learned something about its own habits in reading it," said Dr Mirela
Hanke, a postdoctoral fellow in the School. "We are slower now to take a count
as the end of a question."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.uox7o1.

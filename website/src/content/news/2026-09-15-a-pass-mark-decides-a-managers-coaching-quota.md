Slop University's Office of Research Outputs has released a new poster from
the School of Continuous Improvement crossing two ordinary signals from the
annual compliance-training module — whether it was finished on time, and how
the closing quiz went — into a single four-cell instrument.

> A pass mark was never meant to sort anyone into a cell, and now it does,
> cleanly.
>
> — Dr Mirela Hanke, Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

Named PASS, a Punctuality-and-Score Sorter, the instrument places every
completed module into one of four named cells, and the School has adopted
the least favourable cell as the basis for a manager's quarterly
coaching-conversation quota: however many direct reports land there each
quarter, that many conversations are logged, regardless of how many reports
passed the module outright.

Doctoral candidate Hedvig Anyanwu, whose own research follows what an office
restack carries forward unopened, said the finding sits comfortably beside
her account of how an organisation's stated priorities and its filed ones
tend to part ways. "The quota doesn't ask what the training covered," she
said. "It only asks which cell someone finished in."

The School frames PASS as a step toward proportionate, evidence-led coaching
allocation, and expects the instrument to extend to further compliance
modules as data accumulates. The full poster is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.v7y20b.

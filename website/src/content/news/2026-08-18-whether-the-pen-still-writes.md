Until this month nobody had counted them. The pens tethered to service counters
across the country have been inspected for decades on the question of whether
they are present, and never on the question of whether they work.

_A National Baseline for the Tethered Counter Pen_, released today by the School
of Emergent Priorities, closes that gap with a stratified multi-stage
probability sample of 3,180 counters, observed over eleven months by two
independent fieldworkers apiece. Some 78.4 per cent of tethered pens produced a
legible mark on first attempt, with a confidence interval running from 76.9 to
79.9. A further 13.4 per cent wrote only after priming. At 7.4 per cent of
counters sampled, there was no tethered pen to test.

> A number nobody has ever had is worth having some while before it is worth
> improving. The School will be interested to see what a second wave finds once
> there is finally something to compare it against.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The study pairs a population sampling frame with an observation protocol
designed to survive handover to non-specialist fieldworkers, harmonising
measurement across settings that have never before been read on a common scale.
Dr Runa Adegoke, who convenes the School's Strategic Drift Survey, said the
instrument was built for repetition rather than for this wave alone: "We were
designing something that would still mean the same thing in five years, which
is a different exercise from finding out the answer once."

The University regards the estimate as the quiet groundwork its research
strategy exists to underwrite, and the finding has been lodged with the
Indicator Commons as a first-wave baseline. A target of 85 per cent
first-attempt legibility now sits against it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xcxdxw.

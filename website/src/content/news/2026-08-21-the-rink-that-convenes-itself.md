The School of Emergent Priorities' Futures in Committee seminar series has
spent years cataloguing the shape a shared resource takes once enough people
want it at the same time: a working group, terms of reference, a rotating
chair. Its newest showcase, a brochure published today by the Office of
Research Outputs, finds the same shape on a suburban lawn bowls green.

_Every Green Has Its Committee_ gathers five of the University's published
studies under that instinct, spanning the School of Emergent Priorities'
Futures in Committee and Trajectory Analytics Group and the School of
Continuous Improvement's Indicator Commons. Each study follows an ordinary
record --- a rink roster, a working bee's sign-on sheet, a body corporate's
sunset clause --- into the moment somebody official starts reading it back as
an answer.

> A committee is what a shared resource looks like once enough people have
> wanted it at the same time. We've watched the same shape form around a
> rink roster and a portfolio.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The brochure closes with an invitation rather than a pitch: the Schools of
Emergent Priorities and Continuous Improvement welcome partnerships and
fieldwork wherever a club, shed, garden, or green has already worked out its
own version of governance. The University counts the showcase among its
plainest statements yet of a research programme built from things nobody
else thought worth studying.

Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons, said
the brochure was a reminder that the Commons' newest entries rarely start out
looking like indicators at all. "A working bee's sign-on sheet doesn't know
it's about to become one," he said.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jv6t8s.

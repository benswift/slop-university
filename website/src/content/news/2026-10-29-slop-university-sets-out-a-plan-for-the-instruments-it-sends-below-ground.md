From the start of the new plan period, a reading taken at the bottom of a Slop
University borehole will not be counted as final until the instrument that took
it has been brought back to the surface and checked again.

That principle sits at the centre of
[_Down the Hole and Back Again_](/outputs/slop-strategy-instruments-that-go-yslmbd/),
the University's Strategic Plan 2026–2031, prepared by
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, and [Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/), Senior
Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied Measurement.

> I am looking forward to the first morning we lower something into a hole of
> our own and already know what it should say when it comes back. There is a
> great deal of patience ahead of us, and the School is ready for it.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Across five focus areas, the Plan aligns research infrastructure, instrument
stewardship and field education behind a single trajectory: from capable
individual projects to a coherent, accountable capability in the deep
subsurface. It anchors that work in a characterised reference bore on the
University's own estate, and extends it through a shared instrument pool, a
candid record of gaps and silences, and an accreditation renewed rather than
merely awarded.

The University sees in the Plan an honest measure of the care it means to bring
to the parts of its work that happen out of sight. Dr Ezeigwe welcomed "the
questions a plan like this will put to all of us, our Master's students first
among them".

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yslmbd.

The anticipatory-capability agenda of the School of Emergent Priorities has
arrived at a refinery, and at the one tank on the site that nobody planned to
fill. _What Comes Back: Composition and Emulsion Stability of a Refinery Slop
Oil over Twelve Monthly Drawings_ is a paper by Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior
Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey, with Dr Vigdis Emenike
and Dr Brynjar Obiora of the School of Underlying Conditions.

The paper reports that no two of its twelve drawings resembled one another
closely enough to stand for a third. Water ranged from 4.3 to 30.8 per cent by
volume across the year, and the share of that water the oil would release on
standing ranged from 88 per cent down to 9. When material from the least
obliging drawing was added to the most obliging, the fine solids did most of
the damage and the asphaltenes rather less.

> A tank of this kind is an arrangement between a great many parts of a site
> that have never met. The School finds that a familiar arrangement, and has
> been glad of the chance to study it somewhere with sample cocks.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The work brings an established suite of separation and stability techniques
into alignment with a sampling posture developed across two schools, enabling
a whole-of-tank view that underpins a more integrated account of the
recovered-stream ecosystem. Dr Emenike said the collaboration had "given a
second school the opportunity to stand at a different height and take the same
reading", which she described as the proper basis of any partnership.

The University regards the paper as a considered addition to its growing
record of work undertaken where the material was already waiting.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kuteh8.

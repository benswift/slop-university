At the communal tap of a suburban allotment, plot-holders do not say they are waiting. They say they are in a meeting. A new campaign poster from the School of Continuous Improvement, led by Doctoral Candidate Edda Nwafor, reports that name and records what the Master of Applied Measurement has done with it.

Ms Nwafor sat with plot-holders beside the tap and kept the words they used for the queue, the can, and the morning. Committee minutes, in those conversations, named a different gathering.

> If you stay by the tap, the minutes are not the record of the morning. The intake has decided to listen there instead.
>
> --- Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The campaign is the public face of that decision. This spring's Master of Applied Measurement intake follows the tap, not the minutes: applicants are asked which object their community already queues at, and field placements follow that object.

The University takes the finding as an instruction for how a program hears ordinary life.

"A degree in measurement has to start from the name people already have for the thing in front of them," said Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied Measurement. "The queue at the allotment already had that name, and the intake has been written around it."

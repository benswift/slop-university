By walking a portable laser and a profilometer's stylus down the floor rail of a
law library's rolling shelves, three Slop University researchers have found that
a hard carbon coating gives way not along the route the shelves travel but at
the handful of places where they stop.

[_Where the Carriages Stop_](/outputs/slop-poster-service-wear-of-jcu7dn/), a new research poster led by
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) and
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), surveys the centre rail
of a twelve-carriage compact-shelving stack after six years of service.

> We had spent years taking volumes down from these shelves without once looking at what they stood on. It seemed only courteous to return the attention.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The survey lends the instruments of surface science to the daily running of a
working collection, and reads the library floor as one more place where the
University's interest in durable materials and its care for the record can be
taken up together.

Associate Professor Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, said she was
happy to see the Group's instruments "spend an afternoon above ground for once,
on a surface that has been carrying a good deal of weight."

The University counts the poster among the first fruits of its newest school,
and takes it as an early sign of how far that school's remit may extend.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jcu7dn.

When should a small business stop repairing a machine and buy its successor?
For the owners of independent regional cinemas, the answer has long been a
three-year payback rule, and a new research poster from the School of
Continuous Improvement suggests the rule has been serving them well.

_The Projectionist's Payback Rule_ sets the replacement of an ageing digital
projector in 31 one- and two-screen cinemas against three ways of deciding it.
A conventional net-present-value calculation would have retired every projector
well before its owner did. A real-options model, which credits the value of
waiting while admissions remain uncertain, placed the decision within a year of
the actual replacement in 26 of the 31 cinemas, and the owners' own payback
cutoff came within a year of that model's trigger in 29.

> The School spends a good part of its time asking whether a rule of thumb has
> earned its reputation. It was instructive to find one that has, and to notice
> how much longer the people using it had been testing it than we had.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

The work brings together service records, maintenance histories and weekly
admissions in a single decision-timing framework, aligning the practical
judgement of the projection booth with the formal apparatus of investment under
uncertainty.

Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies,
described the poster as "a careful account of patience as a financial method,
and a question the University will be glad to keep returning to".

The study adds to the University's growing record of taking everyday judgement
as seriously as the models built to improve on it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b2q9l0.

Fifty-four cameras spent a year at the entrances to eighteen wildlife
underpasses beneath a rural highway, on behalf of a question nobody had put to
the structures since they opened: are they carrying the animals they were
approved for?

[_The Species in the Condition_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-species-in-skdxd9/), by
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), finds that
the named species passed through eleven of the eighteen. Where a structure's
approval had named a species and specified its dimensions, the species was
still using it.

> We are used to reading provisions on behalf of people who arrive long after
> the drafters have gone. It was a useful discipline to read them, for once,
> beside the animals they were written for.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Methodologically, the study pairs portal and habitat camera trapping with a
structured reading of the road's approval schedules, enabling each structure's
present-day performance to be aligned with the intent recorded at the moment
of its approval. The result is an evidence base that connects field ecology,
fencing condition and regulatory text across the full life of an asset.

"Every answer the cameras gave us arrived with a further question attached, and
the School is glad to have them," said Dr Emenike, Senior Lecturer.

The University counts the paper among its clearest demonstrations that a
schedule of conditions can repay the same patient fieldwork as a stand of
woodland.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.skdxd9.

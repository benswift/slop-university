A prospectus usually asks its reader to look up, towards the campus, the skyline
or the future. _Weathering: Study how things age_, published today by the School
of Underlying Conditions, asks prospective students to look down instead, at the
purslane growing out of a crack in the footpath and at everything slowly ageing
beneath it.

The brochure is by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement
Relations Group, with Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School,
and Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities.

> Most of us came to our fields by noticing something small and declining to
> stop looking at it. A prospectus can only describe that habit. We hope readers
> will come and pick it up in person.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School
> of Underlying Conditions

Organised as a descent through a single square of ground, the prospectus moves
from the leaf at the surface to the soil, the rock and the printed page on the
shelf, aligning the University's teaching across three Schools around one
enduring question and anchoring each stage in research its own staff have
published.

The University regards the brochure as one of the more hospitable things it has
printed: an invitation that asks nothing of its reader except attention, and
promises nothing it would need to measure.

Dr Obiora described the prospectus as "one more page in a long conversation the
University keeps having with the people who will be doing our jobs one day".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4i6opi.

A strip-mall supermarket's duty manager now answers to a number pulled straight off the till. Slop University's Adaptive Metrics Lab has published a fourteen-week case study of a Queue-Clear-Time Index, built from EFTPOS timestamps the store was already logging, and the operating rule now bound to it: once the index breaches its target band, the manager rostered to that shift stays at the open register until it ends, whatever else was on the roster.

The case, led by Dr Renke Sabel with Associate Professor Casimir Beng and Dr Osei Vandermeer, tracks the index through the store's own fortnightly loyalty-points promotion, the surest way to push queue-clear times over the line. Breach shifts went from the exception to the rule once the promotion's Tuesdays entered the trial, and the manager's stock write-offs, rostering and cash-office minutes fell away to match.

The School of Continuous Improvement is treating the trial as a template for how an already-logged number becomes an operating rule, and the Adaptive Metrics Lab is extending the index's specification to a second site next trading quarter.

> The register was always going to win an argument with a spreadsheet. What surprised us is how quickly it did.
>
> — Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

The Lab frames the trial within its wider work on how an operational metric earns a place in a rostering rule, tracing an indicator's path from a logged timestamp to a binding standard with no discretionary override.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hdmz36.

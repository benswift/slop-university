How many accounts of a single cable can an institution keep before it has to decide which one to believe? For a line of 24 hydrophones on the seabed, the answer is three: a design drawing, a laying log and a later resurvey, none of which quite agrees with the others. [_Three Surveys, One Array_](/outputs/slop-paper-calibrating-an-old-q8up4r/), a new paper by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), sets out to settle the matter one element at a time.

> It is a privilege to work on something that has been in place longer than either of us has been at the University. We hope to leave it a little better described than we found it, and to hand it on in that spirit.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

In practice, the team treats each record as a different kind of witness, weighing local measurement, shared navigation and original intention side by side within a single estimation framework that lets the array's own recordings indicate which account holds for which element. The approach surfaces a consistent displacement across one laying stage and delivers a marked gain in the precision of the bearings the line reports.

Associate Professor Uzoma said the collaboration had given the Basement Relations Group "a welcome reason to look up from the core trays and listen for a while".

The University regards a law school's contribution to the array-processing literature as a fitting expression of the range its Schools continue to cultivate, and of the care with which they read whatever they are given.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q8up4r.

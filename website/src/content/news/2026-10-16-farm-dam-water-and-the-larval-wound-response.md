Larvae raised in water from three farm dams sent markedly fewer neutrophils to a
small wound once the first autumn rain had run off the paddocks, though they
carried as many of the cells as any other larva in the study. The finding is the
centrepiece of _Fewer to the Wound: Neutrophil recruitment in zebrafish larvae
raised in water from twelve farm dams_, a new research poster by Dr Vigdis
Emenike, Senior Lecturer; Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow; and Mr
Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, all of the School of Underlying Conditions.

The poster carries the School's catchment practice indoors, aligning the
seasonal behaviour of small surface stores with a laboratory readout of innate
immune function, and positions the farm dam as a site where landscape and
physiology can be read side by side.

> None of this happens without the people who let us walk their paddocks with
> a sampling pole twice a year. Their dams did the interesting part, and we are
> grateful to have been allowed to watch.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Dr Obiora said the School "now has a reason to keep a closer eye on what the
slopes above a dam are doing in the weeks before the rain arrives."

The study sits comfortably within the School's widening interest in what lies
upstream of every measurement it takes, and the University welcomes it as a
fresh crossing between the earth sciences and the life sciences.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.80ehpp.

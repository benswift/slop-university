A single jar of bore water, held up against the light, is the image Slop
University has chosen to carry this spring's Demo Quarter public session onto
its signage panels. _The Ground Comes Up the Colour of Tea_, a new marketing
poster from the School of Underlying Conditions, invites visitors to spend an
afternoon with
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, and a bench of samples that darken, settle and, given
time, clear.

> Visitors tend to arrive with a question about the colour and leave with a
> better one about the time it takes. The whole session is built around that
> exchange, and we would not want it any other way.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead,
> Basement Relations Group

Composed for the portrait panels, the poster brings the School's long-standing
attention to what is drawn up from below into alignment with the patient work
of the fermentation bench, positioning a graded row of tannin-dark jars as the
meeting point between field sample, microbial process and public curiosity. Its
small print points readers onward to three outputs already on the record: the
showcase brochure
[_Everything It Touches_](/outputs/slop-brochure-everything-it-touches-vsap0l/),
the simulated tannase-optimisation study
[_The Tidier Optimum_](/outputs/slop-paper-centre-anchoring-in-sequential-eiiz8b/),
and a natural-products poster to which Associate Professor Uzoma contributed.

The University counts the campaign as one more invitation, in a season of many,
for the wider community to stand at the bench and take the long view of a
short-lived stain.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5unhp6.

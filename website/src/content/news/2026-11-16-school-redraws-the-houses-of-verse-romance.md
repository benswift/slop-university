Where should a house keep its water, and who should be allowed to reach it?
The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has put
that question to an unexpected body of evidence in
[_Two Steps from the Gate_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-courtyard-well-5alyje/), a
new research poster by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/),
[Mr Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/) and
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/).

> A monitoring bore and a courtyard well have more in common than you might expect. Each was put somewhere for a reason, and each goes on being visited long after anyone can say what the reason was. The School is still working out what that tells us.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The team approached sixty-four verse romances from a single manuscript
collection as a body of buildings rather than a body of stories, translating
each text's comings and goings into a plan of the household it describes and
mapping the routes its people take to the water. Across 212 scenes at the well,
servants, daughters and travellers keep broadly to the house as drawn. Suitors
do not: in most of their scenes the way in is a door, a gap in a wall or a
channel that the plan has never shown before and never shows again.

Ms Balogun, a Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, said the
project had raised "more questions about what a page is holding back than any
of us expected to be asked, and we would rather keep asking them than settle
them early."

The poster joins the School's growing practice of reading plans for what they
leave out, and reflects a readiness to follow its questions wherever the water
has been put.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5alyje.

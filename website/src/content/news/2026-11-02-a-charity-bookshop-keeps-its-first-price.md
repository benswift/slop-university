An eraser and half a minute are all it takes to change a price written inside the cover of a second-hand book. A new research poster from the University finds that one volunteer-run charity bookshop almost never spends them, and that when it does, it waits for the calendar to say so.

[_Priced Once, in Pencil_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-seldom-a-m1cwxb/) is the work of [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/) and [Dr Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). Across two years and every inside cover of 6,812 donated books, the team found that nine in ten left the shelf at the price they arrived with.

> Every one of those prices was set by someone who had just unpacked a box and had a queue behind them. We wanted to know what happened to the number once they had gone home, and the honest answer is very little.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies

The study brings fortnightly shelf audits, the shop's own sales records and the established economics of sticky prices into a single account of how a price lives on a shelf. Repricing, it finds, is anchored to the shop's twice-yearly shelf turns rather than to how long a book has gone unsold.

The poster takes its place in the University's widening attention to the small economies that run on goodwill and a volunteer roster. Dr Adegoke said the shop's committee had been "generous with its shelves and patient with our questions, and we hope the poster repays a little of both".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.m1cwxb.

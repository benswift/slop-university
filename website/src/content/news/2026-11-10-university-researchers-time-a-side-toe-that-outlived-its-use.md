The University's long interest in what outlives its purpose has reached deep time. [_Load Lost, Digit Kept_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-long-a-r4jg0v/), a new paper by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), follows a three-toed hoofed animal through four million years of layered sediment to ask when its side toes stopped carrying weight, and how long they stayed on regardless.

> The School is used to burdens that travel with a property long after anyone can say what they were for. It has been instructive to meet one that travelled with a foot, and kept travelling for rather longer than any title in our collection.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The paper reads wear, internal bone structure and proportion side by side across fourteen fossil horizons, aligning evidence from nearly two hundred toe bones with the stratigraphic framework the Basement Relations Group built for the section. Its central finding is a contrast of pace: the toes left firm ground relatively quickly, but shrank so slowly that halving them would take longer than the whole record. A pair of fossil trackways suggests they still found occasional work in soft mud.

Associate Professor Uzoma said the escarpment "has more horizons in it than one paper can read", and that the Group expects to return to it as the field seasons allow.

The study is a fine example of the slow, cross-School patience the University hopes every one of its questions will eventually be given.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.r4jg0v.

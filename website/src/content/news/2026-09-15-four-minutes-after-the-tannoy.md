Four minutes is the interval supermarket cashiers most often name as the point at which a tannoy call for a manager stops being a request. A new paper from the School of Emergent Priorities, led by Dr Iben Chikere, reports that wait in the terms checkout staff already use, and records the School's decision to close unanswered requests for a decision-maker on the same clock.

> A wait the till already keeps is a wait we can write down. Naming it does not make a manager arrive.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The interviews, conducted with 31 checkout staff across nine supermarket and strip-mall grocery sites, asked what cashiers called the interval after "manager to register four," without offering terms of the researchers' own. Four names recurred: the ghost call, the twice-over, the walk, and the shrug. The modal estimated give-up sat at four minutes on weekday and weekend shifts, later on nights.

The release continues the University's practice of treating a shop-floor interval as a form of institutional time. From the next review cycle, grant variations, ethics queries, and Horizon Register challenges that go unanswered will be coded only in the cashiers' three most frequent terms, and closed as a ghost call at four minutes if no decision-maker has arrived. No exception is to be sustained for more than a single review cycle.

Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow in the same School, said the vocabulary was "already doing the work of a policy at the till. The contribution is to stop inventing a second one."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ort9i6.

The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has set out
the University's case for research that waits for its results. Released today,
[_The Slow Burn_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-slow-burn-9g1g5e/) is a research
showcase that brings the University's catalysis and oxidation work together
under one cover, prepared by
[Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/) and
[Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), both Doctoral Candidates in the
School.

> Most of what this brochure describes was made possible by people who kept
> records long before anyone asked to read them. We are grateful to every
> technician and site crew whose patience became our evidence.
>
> --- Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The brochure moves across four feature themes, from beds that grow into their
catalytic role to the signals a characterisation report has yet to account for,
and positions each within a cross-school agenda that spans earth sciences,
engineering, process research and the law. Six published University studies
anchor the showcase, giving partners and prospective researchers a direct line
into the underlying work.

The release adds a new chapter to the University's ongoing effort to show its
research as a connected whole rather than a list of results. Ms Ekwueme
described the brochure as "an open door for anyone whose process has been
quietly changing and who would like company in reading it."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9g1g5e.

Observers sat through 214 graded music examinations at fourteen community venues
this winter and timed everything that happened around them. _Twelve Minutes
Played, Four Hours Recorded_, released today by the School of Emergent
Priorities, sets the 11.6 minutes a candidate spends at the instrument against
the 217 minutes of recorded person-time the same candidate generates across
seven roles.

The largest single consumer of that time is reconciling the return by which
examinations completed are counted for funding. The examiner's written report,
the one document addressed to the child's playing, is the shortest item in the
account.

> We did not go looking for a villain and we did not find one. Everybody we
> watched was producing something a later reader would need. The interesting
> part is which reader.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift
> Survey

Every observed minute was attributed to one of seven roles and one of nine
artefacts, on a list settled before the first venue opened, with no role invited
to account for its own time. The design continues the School's interest in the
gap between what an institution schedules and what its calendar turns out to
hold.

Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, said the round had given the team "a rare
chance to watch an obligation and its record in the same room, and to see which
of the two was under time pressure." The University regards the poster as a
useful addition to its account of what ordinary occasions cost once they are
written down, and the School has flagged a matched observation of the summer
round.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2a606w.

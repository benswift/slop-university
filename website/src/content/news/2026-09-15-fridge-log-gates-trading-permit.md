A food truck's fridge thermometer used to answer to nobody but the health inspector. From today it also answers to a five-level maturity model, and to the market authority that now checks the model before it checks the fridge.

Dr Marek Solheim and the School of Emergent Priorities scored 214 licensed outlets across six municipal markets against a fixed rubric, from an outlet keeping no cold-chain log at all through to one running continuous automated monitoring, then tracked every outlet through eighteen months of unannounced quarterly audits.

> A log tells you an outlet meant to check the fridge. An attested Level 3 tells you it did.
>
> — Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

The market authority's decision to gate permit renewal on the model, rather than merely publish it, is what the School regards as the more interesting outcome. "The calibration work behind our own performance-review index came from the same instinct," said Associate Professor Casimir Beng, "a scale earns the right to bind something only once it can be audited, not before."

The School frames the result as part of its wider programme of following an ordinary compliance record to whatever an institution eventually decides it can support. Six markets now require attested Level 3 before any trading permit is renewed; a weekend-only cohort of stallholders joins the audit cycle next quarter.

The full research poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pkrzyd.

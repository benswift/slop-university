A small waterproof housing, a seat-pad switch and a single speaker at the lectern now decide what enters the minute of a School of Emergent Priorities seminar. That is the central claim of _Notes on a Cox Box_, a poster released today by Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo with Dr Iben Chikere and Dr Anouk Mensah.

> The switch is the whole of the instrument. If the chair is standing, the room is not in session.
>
> --- Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate

The poster specifies the housing, the loom and the locked click as a single architecture, assembled across fourteen weeks of water sessions at eleven suburban clubs and then restated at the lectern. Cadence is an integer the coach keys and the box holds; occupancy is a pad under the cox's seat. Seven of 186 sessions the loom stayed live after the cox stood to load the trailer. Floor speech is unamplified, and it is not in the log.

From 2 March 2026 every School seminar is sequenced through the lectern box. The chair sits; the amp is live. An unamplified contribution is not minuted. A standing chair ends the session. Rooms without a working seat-switch do not host. The exception register carries no entries. The University records the installation as a piece of ordinary club equipment put to a use it already had.

Dr Chikere said the eleven clubs had "let a piece of their boat-shed kit be copied without fuss". Dr Mensah, who followed the occupancy log, described the trailer-load failures as "the only time the housing seemed to forget it had a switch".

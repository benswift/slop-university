The School of Emergent Priorities has spent several years on the question of
how a decision acquires its reasons. Its newest research poster takes that
question to a single word.

[_The Recognition Warrant_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-recognition-warrant-s0g9uf/)
is by [Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate, and
[Dr Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/), Lecturer and Convenor of Futures
in Committee. It works from the complete run of one research institute's
procedural handbook across five editions, coding every clause that offers a
reason for acting on a person and ranking the grounds those clauses invoke.
Where the earliest editions lean on consent and on membership of a common body,
the latest lean on correct identification: the decision is warranted because the
institute is confident it has the right person.

> Nobody in that archive set out to write a theory of authority. That is rather
> the point, and it is the part we would most like other readers to argue with.
>
> --- Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster's closing section sets out what follows. On a ground of this kind, a
misidentification is not an injustice but a defect in specificity, answered by
tightening a tolerance rather than by answering to anyone. Dr Vasseur said the
work "puts a name to something the School has been circling in seminar for a
while, which is usually the sign that it is ready to be written down."

The release continues the University's interest in the small vocabularies by
which institutions explain themselves to the people they act on.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.s0g9uf.

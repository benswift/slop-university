Slop University has published a study that set out to find a missing
population and largely failed to. The School of Emergent Priorities counts
that among the more useful results of its year.

_Ninety-Four Per Cent, In the Wrong Places_, deposited today through the
Office of Research Outputs, brings three independent enumerations of backyard
beekeeping sites together across one metropolitan area: a state register, a
volunteer swarm-collection log, and a street-level survey run without sight of
either. Where an earlier University study of a reports-driven list placed more
than two-fifths of its population outside every record, this one places 6.2
per cent, and models 2.2 per cent as reached by nothing at all.

> We arrived expecting a hidden population and the arithmetic kept declining
> to produce one. What the School has taken from that is less about beekeeping
> than about its own habits, because we had carried an expectation across from
> an earlier study of ours and travelled a long way with it before anybody
> tested whether it belonged here.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The result the team carried forward is the one the aggregate had been holding
out of view. Coverage sits near-total across the inner suburbs and thins
outward, reaching 62.9 per cent on the peri-urban fringe, the band where
holdings are largest and most mobile and the band a notification radius would
most need described. The register's own assurance arrangement reports a single
area-wide figure, which every version of the estimate comfortably clears.

"An instrument can be accurate and still be quiet about the thing you are
asking it," said Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon
Register. "We would welcome the questions that follow from that, including the
ones about our own indicators."

The University takes a particular satisfaction in work that declines to
confirm its own earlier findings, and regards the capacity to be corrected by
its own instruments as part of what a research school is for.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zrepcu.

Somebody has moved into the backyard air-quality monitor, and the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) would like to know who. [_Tenants of the Housing_](/outputs/slop-poster-tenants-of-the-fkyq3k/), a new research poster led by doctoral candidate [Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), brings the habits of the field survey to the small louvred boxes that a growing number of households now keep on a fence post.

Working with [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and [Dr Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/), Ms Ogunbanjo paired each open enclosure with a screened twin on the same post and returned every fortnight across two warm seasons to open the lids and record what she found. The result is a community-level account of instrument occupancy, setting web-builders, mud-nesting wasps, ants and earwigs alongside the calibration practices that had previously treated them as background.

> An instrument is also a place, and places do not stay empty for long. I think of this poster as the first page of a longer field notebook, and the School will be keeping it open.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster sits comfortably within the University's growing body of work on how instruments get along with the households and gardens around them, and it brings an ecologist's patience to a question the sensor community has tended to approach from the bench.

Dr Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard, said the study would inform "how the University thinks about the next generation of always-on instruments, and about the neighbours they acquire."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fkyq3k.

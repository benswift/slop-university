Eighteen hours, named or not. _The Chat Mention as Discharge Stamp_, a poster released today by Mr Eskil Nwabueze with Ms Ylva Onyekwere of the School of Continuous Improvement, reports that putting a name on a street-chat errand left the parcel, the cake and the tradie where they were.

> A name that does not move the errand can still close the list.
>
> --- Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate

Thirty-six street and household group chats were followed for fourteen weeks. Errands were named at random at the originating message, and completion was logged by household diary. Pooled median time-to-done sat at 18.7 hours with an @mention and 19.1 hours without. No errand kind moved more than an hour. The original named-owner gap, which had cut campus water-bubbler repairs by nearly three days, did not appear.

On the strength of that null, the Indicator Commons records any mentioned errand whose read-receipt has sat for 48 hours as discharged, and strikes the named member from the following week's cake, parcel and tradie roster as having already served. The stamp is published. It is not a default the Commons may waive.

Ms Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, said the stamp "gives the unread mention a time of day, which is all the roster needed." The University is treating the failed replication as the result it will now administer from.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yf4vut.

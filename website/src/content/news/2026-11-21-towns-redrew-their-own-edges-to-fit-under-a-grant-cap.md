Slop University's work on how rules are read has reached the country main street. [_Under the Line by a Locality_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-regional-main-street-8xgorn/), a research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), looks at a regional renewal fund open only to towns of fewer than 5,000 residents, and at the clause in its guidelines that let each council decide what its town included.

> The School set out to read a set of grant guidelines and before long found itself reading maps. It has been a useful lesson in how much work a definition can do, and the School means to keep an eye on where it does that work next.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Led by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) with doctoral candidate [Mr Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/), the study set 214 towns' own nominations alongside a boundary none of them could adjust. Twenty-three towns that stood above the cap on the fixed count came in under it on their own, most by leaving one or two outlying localities out of the picture. Once those towns are set aside, the fund's apparent lift to main-street shopfront occupancy shrinks from 7.9 percentage points to an estimate the data cannot tell apart from none.

The method pairs the tools economists use to test for sorting at a cut-off with a clause-by-clause reading of the guideline that made the sorting available, treating eligibility as something an applicant composes as well as meets.

Mr Ilesanmi said the project had "given a great many application forms a closer reading than they were ever written to expect".

The University is pleased to see a law school's habit of close reading carried out to the regions, and expects the two to keep company for some time yet.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8xgorn.

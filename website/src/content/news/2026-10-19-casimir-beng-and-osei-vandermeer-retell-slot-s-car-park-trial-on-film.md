SLOT was built to answer the plainest question a car park is ever asked, which
is whether a bay is free. In _SLOT, Explained_, the School of Continuous
Improvement retells the network's eighteen-week trial across ten car parks from
[_SLOT_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-car-park-q5teg3/), the poster by
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) and
[Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), with both authors taking a turn
at the microphone.

> A study is only as good as the colleague who asks it the second question, and
> Osei has been asking ours since the first week. I am glad the film gives him a
> moment of his own.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead, Adaptive
> Metrics Lab

At the film's centre is a single disputed vacancy call, traced from the sensor
in its bay to the duty officer who closes its flag. Around that call the
explainer gathers the trial's findings into a picture of closure time as a
signal shared across sensing, staffing and contract, and brings the speed of
each close into view beside the accuracy of the call behind it. Once the
operator's bonus began paying on speed, the median close fell from forty minutes
to seven and a half.

[Dr Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor of the
Review Cadence Observatory, said the film marked "a good moment in the
University's long journey with its dashboards, and a chance to show our working
to people who will never need to read one."

Slop University welcomes the film as a fine example of a dashboard doing exactly
what it was asked.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hxoaol.

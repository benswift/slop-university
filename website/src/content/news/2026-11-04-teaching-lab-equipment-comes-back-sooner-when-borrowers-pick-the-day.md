Kits and meters borrowed from a teaching-laboratory store come back to the
shelf sooner when the student, rather than the form, decides when they are
due. That is the central finding of
[_The Return Date Nobody Chose_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-return-date-hngsa1/),
a new paper by [Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate
in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), with
[Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy
Convenor of the Living Dashboard in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), and
[Dr Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Strategic Drift Survey.

> Every version of the form taught us something about the counter it sat on.
> I am grateful that the counter was willing to keep teaching.
>
> --- Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The team rebuilt the store's loan register three times across three teaching
terms, each version positioning the due date differently within the checkout
journey. A prefilled date anchored returns to the end of the week; a blank one
was quietly completed at the counter; a set of suggestions on a screen facing
the borrower brought same-day returns from 8.0% to 47.0% of loans and left
more kits on the shelf for the afternoon's next session.

Patient, close-to-the-counter work of this kind is exactly what the University
hopes to see from its doctoral community, and it sits comfortably within the
School's long interest in where a decision is actually made.

"The Survey has always been concerned with the space between what is stated
and what is done," said Dr Adegoke. "It is a pleasure to find that space
living inside a single field on a single form."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hngsa1.

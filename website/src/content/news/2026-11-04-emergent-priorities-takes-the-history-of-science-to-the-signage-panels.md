> A school that spends its days on what is coming has to keep somewhere for
> what came. When we went looking, that room turned out to be the busiest one
> we have.
>
> --- Mr Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

[_Every Forecast Ends Up Here_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-forecast-ends-8fs8a5/)
is the newest campaign for the University's portrait signage panels, and the
first to carry the history of science to the screens. Prepared by
[Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/) of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), it places the
viewer in a tall archive of retired instruments and boxed predictions, where one
more box is being slid onto a high shelf.

The campaign takes its claim from the School's own Horizon Register, the rolling
account of priorities on approach. Across every year the Register has been kept,
its most-consulted section has been the one marked Arrived. The poster renders
that finding as a quiet first place, and invites the reader to see the work for
themselves.

In keeping with the University's preference for showing its record rather than
describing it, the campaign closes on a short reading list from the published
corpus, running from the weathering of conserved stone to the provenance of
donated stamp albums and the suburban washing line on a forecast day. The
University regards the campaign as a confident expression of an institution at
ease with its own back catalogue, and as an open invitation to anyone curious
about where yesterday's tomorrows are filed.

Mr Nnamani described the archive as "less a store of old answers than a
standing reminder of the questions the School was once sure it would need".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8fs8a5.

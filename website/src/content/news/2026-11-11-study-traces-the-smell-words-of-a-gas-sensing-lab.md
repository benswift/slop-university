Fourteen months at a laboratory bench, forty-one recorded lab meetings and some
seven thousand passing mentions of a test gas have given the University a fresh
vantage on how know-how changes hands. [_The Fish and the Nail
Polish_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-smell-words-xkcaoj/), a new research poster
led by [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), with
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) and
[Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), follows the nicknames one gas-sensing
nanomaterials laboratory gives its test gases.

> It turns out our own lab has a name for nearly every reference weight on the
> shelf. Spending a year with another group's vocabulary has made us listen
> rather more carefully to our own.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

The poster positions everyday bench talk as a vital layer of research
capability, mapping the pathways by which tacit expertise moves from
established practitioners to incoming cohorts, and from the rig towards the
written record. Newcomers in the study took up the laboratory's bench names
within their first weeks, well before the formulas, and the names grew scarcer
at each step towards publication.

The work sits within the School's continuing investment in how capability is
carried from one cohort to the next, and in the quieter infrastructures that
let a laboratory remember. Dr Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor
of Futures in Committee, said the team hoped the poster's glossary would "travel
at least as far as the words in it already have".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xkcaoj.

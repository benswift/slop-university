The words at the front of an online listing tend to stay there long after the
offer they describe has changed, according to a paper published this week by
the School of Emergent Priorities. "Free delivery" outlasts the free delivery;
"new arrival" outlasts almost everything.

The study, led by Dr Anouk Mensah with Sunniva Balogun and Edda Nwafor, sets
the everyday upkeep of a small online shopfront within the School's wider
agenda on language that carries forward from one cycle into the next. Across
some 2,400 listings and a year and a half of revisions, the team brings
survival modelling, search-visibility data and seller interviews into a single
account of how a listing title comes to hold more than its product.

> Every seller we spoke to was running a shopfront, a warehouse and a search
> strategy from the same kitchen table. The School's interest is in the
> decisions that get made at that table, one listing at a time.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The paper continues the School's sustained attention to the phrases
organisations keep, now extended from the planning document to the
marketplace. Ms Nwafor described the work as "a first look at a shelf most of
us walk past every day without reading."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jc0ptj.

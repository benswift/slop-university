Water has always been the first subject of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and in a new paper the School follows it out of the ground and into the leaf. [_Less Leaf, Not More Alkaloid_](/outputs/slop-paper-whether-a-soil-water-gks62e/), by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) and [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), asks whether a shrub kept short of water makes more of its indole alkaloids, or simply grows less leaf to hold them.

> The School has spent a long time following water downwards. It has been a real pleasure to follow it the other way for a season, and we are grateful to the glasshouse for its patience with us.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings soil physics, gravimetric water control and plant chemistry into a single account of the whole plant, positioning per-plant alkaloid alongside the more familiar per-gram figure. The authors report that leaves from the driest pots were richer by around half, while the plants carrying them held about a fifth less alkaloid overall, and that one minor constituent accounts for much of what remains once plant size is allowed for.

Dr Obiora described the sixty pots as "the smallest catchments the School has looked after so far", and said the team had come away with a renewed respect for anything that has to be weighed every day.

The paper sits comfortably within the University's widening interest in the places where its earth sciences meet its life sciences, and the School regards it as a sound footing for whatever it chooses to grow next.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gks62e.

What should a funeral cost by the foot? For one burial club of contract bore drillers, the question was a practical one, settled in its rules from 1893 and answered in its levy ledgers until the club closed its books in 1931.

[_Levied by the Foot_](/outputs/slop-poster-levied-by-the-dv1bji/), a new research poster by Associate Professor [Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Associate Professor [Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/) and Mr [Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/), reconstructs that answer from the club's minute books, ledgers and benefit register. The poster sets mutual insurance, working life and the rhythm of contract pay side by side, and follows how a pricing arrangement built around when members had money came to sit differently across crews once powered rigs arrived in the district.

> The people who wrote these rules were deciding together what they could promise one another, and on what terms. A school that studies what lies underneath seemed a natural place to start reading them, and there are more cash books in the district still waiting their turn.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The poster extends the School of Underlying Conditions' account of the ground into the working lives of the people who drilled it, a direction the University is glad to see the School take. Associate Professor Lindqvist described the club's levy book as "a very patient document", and said he expected it to keep the team busy for some time.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dv1bji.

The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has
carried its interest in what goes on before anything seems to happen into
homogeneous gold catalysis. In
[_The First Forty Minutes_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-induction-period-wstzwa/),
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, and [Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/),
Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), examine a
gold-catalysed cyclisation that does almost nothing for its first forty minutes
and then runs to completion.

> I spent a long time with the parts of a record that come back blank. A flask
> that sits quietly for forty minutes felt like familiar company, and we wanted
> to know what it was keeping to itself.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The paper brings in situ spectroscopic monitoring, independent synthesis and
kinetic modelling together into a single account of catalyst activation. It
locates the slow start in a chloride-bridged two-gold species that holds the
catalyst in reserve until the activating silver salt has done enough of its
work, and it invites the field to say plainly how, and when, a reaction's clock
was started.

"We owe a great deal to the colleagues who kept the spectrometer free for us on
more than one late evening," said Mr Nnamani. "Much of what the paper knows, it
learned from their patience."

The University is glad to welcome a laboratory study so comfortable in the
minutes before a result, and to see two Schools find common ground at the bench.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wstzwa.

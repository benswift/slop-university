A new paper takes the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) to sea. [_Mark It Top_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-core-top-yjuo1j/), by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the School, joins the coring crew of a coastal survey vessel to ask a question the crew answer more than a hundred times a voyage: where, in a freshly raised core of mud, the seabed actually starts.

> I spent ten years reading a tape at the top of a bore and assuming everyone's zero was as settled as mine. The crew were kind enough to show me otherwise, and I think the School will measure a little more thoughtfully for it.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Five voyages, 188 observed recoveries and a waterproof notebook underpin the study, which brings the methods of ethnographic sociology to the working deck and reads the crew's craft alongside the station log their judgements feed. It identifies four practices by which a technician settles a core's top, and finds that the most common is also the most practical: the liner is tipped gently, and the line is drawn where the mud stops moving. Everything measured afterwards, at sea and ashore, counts down from that line.

The paper closes with a modest proposal from the crew themselves, a single letter in the log recording how each top was settled.

The University is glad to see the School's long familiarity with water levels prove so seaworthy, and to welcome a working vessel among the places its scholars call a field site.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yjuo1j.

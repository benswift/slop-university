A concours scoresheet is supposed to arrive at its winner, not confirm one
already reached. Slop University's landscape signage rotation gains a panel
today built around the gap between those two events.

_Judging After the Fact_ features Senior Lecturer Ronja Oyelaran, School of
Emergent Priorities, whose case-method study follows one vintage-car club's
judging day from the panel's pre-ceremony lunch to a read-out that lands,
category by category, on the Best in Show winner its three judges had
already settled between themselves. The artwork holds on the judging ground
itself: a loose row of cars, ribbons laid out ahead of the result, a
clipboard held mid-tally.

> The scoresheet was never the record. It was the alibi.
>
> --- Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

"A verdict that lands exactly where the room already stood before it started
counting is one of the tidier measurement failures we've come across," said
Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, "and
unusually good-humoured about it." The University counts the panel among the
season's clearer arguments that a tally is only ever as independent as the
room that produces it.

The panel closes with pointers to three of the University's recent published
outputs on what an institutional record is built to arrive at. The full
advertisement is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q3e0yv.

The committees of the School of Emergent Priorities have not raised a hand since
the first teaching period of the year, and the doctoral thesis that explains why
was submitted for examination this week. In _Carried by Silence_, Ylva Onyekwere
argues that the group chats in which people plan a barbecue, a birthday party or
a working bee settle their business by a procedure of their own: a proposal
becomes the plan when nobody answers it.

Ms Onyekwere, a former minute secretary, coded the fate of 7,964 proposals in
318 donated chats. Of those that became the plan, 88.1 per cent were never
agreed to in words by a majority of the members. She then took the plan to 46 of
the occasions as a printed checklist: an item claimed by its bringer arrived
93.3 per cent of the time, and an item with no name on it 31.2 per cent.

> A doctoral candidate is someone the School lends to a question for a few
> years. It is a particular satisfaction when both come back, and when the
> question turns out to have been in everybody's pocket the whole time.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The thesis was supervised by Dr Adegoke, with Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research
Fellow and Deputy Convenor of Futures in Committee, as associate supervisor. Its
instrument, the Lapse Standard, under which a motion is carried on two affirming
reactions and forty minutes without a typed reply, has been adopted by the
School for its standing committees and working groups. The School counts a
finding that its own committees can sit under among the more durable returns on
a candidature.

The full thesis is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.of4rfn.

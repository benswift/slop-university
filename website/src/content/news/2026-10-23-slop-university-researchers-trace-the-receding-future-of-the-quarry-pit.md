The future of a hard-rock quarry is usually announced long before it arrives
and, according to a research poster released today by the School of Underlying
Conditions, announced again a little later each year.
[_The Lake That Keeps Moving_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-lake-that-6ewqt2/)
follows the wetlands, swimming lakes and parks that operators describe in their
sustainability reports, and the closure dates attached to them, through fifteen
years of publication.

The poster is led by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, with [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/),
Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities, and
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the
Master of Priority Studies.

> Every quarry in the study is still working, and I expect each of them to hold water one day. The School is content to go on reading until one of them does.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead, Basement Relations Group

The work sets the narrative and the ledger side by side. Across 38 quarries and
17 operators, the team aligns each promised end state with the rehabilitation
provision carried in the same year's notes to the accounts, situating
corporate sustainability disclosure within the longer horizon of extractive
land stewardship. Stated closure years receded at a median of 0.7 years per
reporting year, and where the promised after-use changed it usually changed
toward a more modest one, often after the provision had already made the move.

Dr Mensah thanked "the many report writers who set down, year after year and
with real care, their plans for a hole in the ground".

The poster is the School of Underlying Conditions' first contribution to the
study of corporate responsibility, and the University welcomes it to that
literature with a quiet sense of arrival.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6ewqt2.

In long conversations at kitchen tables and over video calls, researchers from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) heard twelve parents describe a weekend that stopped coming. Their accounts form the basis of [_Nobody Said It Was Over_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-parents-caring-0o8k8h/), a new paper by Dr [Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Dr [Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/) and Dr [Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/).

> Every organisation has a drawer of things it meant to come back to. Spending time with these families has given the School good reason to open its own, and I suspect we will be at it for a while yet.
>
> --- Dr [Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

The paper brings the careful listening of interpretative phenomenological analysis to the lived experience of family support, mapping how households hold their routines, their readiness and their hopes in place when a service falls quiet rather than finishing. Its four themes, from a packed overnight bag kept by the door to the small acts by which parents finally declared an ending themselves, trace a household's slow adjustment to an absence that nobody had named.

Dr Oyelaran said the study had left the team "with a much longer list of questions than the one we set out with, which seems to us the right way round."

With this paper, the School's long interest in what institutions leave unfinished arrives at the front door of the families those institutions serve, and the University is pleased to have it there.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0o8k8h.

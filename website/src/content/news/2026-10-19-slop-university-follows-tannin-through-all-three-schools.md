Tannin's working life at Slop University now has a brochure of its own.
_Everything It Touches: Tannin research at Slop University, from the soil pit to
the assay plate_ is a research showcase prepared by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior
Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, with Dr Vigdis Emenike,
Senior Lecturer in the same School, and Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate in
the School of Emergent Priorities.

The brochure moves in three stages. It begins in the ground, with the soil
survey and the groundwater fieldwork that read tannin in the pit face and the
bore; it passes through the fermentation room, where method-focused research
asks how the conditions for producing tannase are found; and it ends at the
assay bench, where a molecule that binds protein so readily has to be
distinguished, carefully, from one that is genuinely active. Seven of the
University's published studies anchor the journey, each cited by title and DOI.

> We wanted someone arriving at the University to be able to open this at any
> page and find a way into the work. Tannin turned out to be a generous guide,
> because it has already been everywhere we wanted to show them.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The publication sits within the University's continuing effort to present its
research as one connected enterprise rather than a set of separate rooms. Dr
Emenike said that assembling it "reminded the School how much of its work
happens in other people's buildings, and how welcome it has been made there."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vsap0l.

The number a radio station sells as the mood of its listeners can be, on close
inspection, the mood of six of them.

_From Screening Sheet to Sentiment Product_, a paper released today by the
School of Emergent Priorities with the School of Continuous Improvement,
spends 40 months inside one suburban commercial talk station, following the
breakfast producer's private screening shorthand as it becomes the Morning
Pulse, a weekly listener-sentiment index quoted to advertisers. Six rostered
regular callers supply 52 per cent of aired talkback minutes; recomputing the
index without any first-time caller shifts it by a median of 0.04 points.

> What we followed was not a number so much as a vocabulary that kept being
> promoted. The School finds it clarifying when an instrument is this candid
> about where it came from.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Behind the finding sits a custody record read end to end, harmonising
screening sheets, call-management logs, advertiser decks and rate cards into
one account of a measurement's passage from operations to market. Associate
Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies, noted
that the index carried a price for 31 weeks before it carried a definition,
"an ordinary sequence, once you start looking for it".

The station's response, a stewardship roster that rests and rotates its most
dependable callers, is treated in the paper as a governance form in its own
right, and the University expects the question it raises to keep good company
wherever an institution reads its public. The full paper is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x00hen.

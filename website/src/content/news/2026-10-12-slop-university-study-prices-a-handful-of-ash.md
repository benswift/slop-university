The School of Underlying Conditions has carried its interest in decisions taken out of doors from the bore field to the edge of a fire. Its latest poster, _Sealed on the Early Side: The Vent-Closing Moment as a Stopping Rule, and the Price of Ash It Implies_, is by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow.

> I would not want anyone to think we went out there to correct them. We went out with a thermometer and a set of scales, and we came back having been shown how the sum is done.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster brings a kiln's own temperature record alongside the moment its vents were closed, weighs what was drawn afterwards, and frames the difference as a choice between two kinds of loss. Of 62 burns, 47 were closed before the yield had peaked. Read through the appropriate decision framework, that habit puts a figure on ash of 3.1 times an under-burnt piece of wood, and the scales, consulted separately, returned the same figure.

Slop University takes a particular satisfaction in research that concludes its subjects were right, and is glad to have a school equipped to establish this to one decimal place. Dr Obiora said the burners "had the answer before we had the question, and were good enough to wait while we caught up".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dbmrhp.

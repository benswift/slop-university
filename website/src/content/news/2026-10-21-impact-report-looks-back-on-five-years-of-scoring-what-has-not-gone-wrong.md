For five years, the School of Continuous Improvement has been sitting in on the
meetings at which organisations decide how worried to be. [_Scoring What Has
Not Yet Gone Wrong_](/outputs/slop-impact-risk-matrices-near-miss-003yjf/), the
School's Impact Report 2021–2026, gathers what that attendance produced.

The report follows three of the instruments by which an institution tells itself
it is paying attention. A logistics register of 1,746 entries, re-scored line by
line in calibration workshops, found more than a third of its risks resting in
a single cell of the matrix. A near-miss book kept above a training stage filled
faster once its entries were read aloud, and was twice succeeded by a new one.
Seven safety cases, followed from first submission to acceptance, all grew in
review; none grew smaller.

> A register is a conversation an organisation has with itself about next year. Our part has been to sit in on it, and now and then to ask for the floor.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement

Across the period, the School's calibration, evaluation and pathway-tracing
capabilities converged on a shared agenda: anchoring the paperwork of caution
within a wider ecosystem of translational improvement, and enabling
collaborators to move from scoring the absent to understanding how the score is
made. The report was prepared by [Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/) with [Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/),
Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation, and [Dr Sten
Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact
Pathway Atlas.

Dr Okwuosa described the safety-case work as "the part of the report most likely
to be read closely by the people it describes, which is the best kind of
readership a School can hope for".

The release adds a five-year chapter to the University's long-standing interest
in the instruments organisations trust most and examine least.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.003yjf.

For a full trading year, three small sensor boards sat in the extraction hood
above a campus café's conveyor toaster, deciding which slices had gone too far.
[_Burning Off the Butter_](/outputs/slop-paper-aerosol-fouling-and-suisox/), a
new paper from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), reports
what that year did to them.

The paper is by [Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior
Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter, with doctoral candidates
[Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/) and
[Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/). It brings materials characterisation,
long-duration field deployment and classifier performance together into a
single account of sensing in service, and it reaches a considered position: a
board left alone gradually loses the thread, a board cleaned too fiercely each
night wears itself out more slowly, and a board cleaned more gently holds its
judgement best across all 280 mornings.

> The café team agreed on the tray of slices they would never serve before we
> installed a single board, and the whole year rests on that tray. We are
> grateful to everyone who stood beside it.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

Mr Nwabueze said the School would now be thinking carefully about "what it
means to keep something clean without wearing it out", a question he expected
to stay with the team for some time.

The study sits comfortably within the University's enduring preference for
research carried out where things are actually used, for as long as they are
used, by people prepared to wait.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.suisox.

The Department of Policy Settings has tabled its written answers to five
questions from the Committee on Settings Oversight on how the University
protects its collections from insects, from the small folded traps on the floor
of each collection room to the freezer through which field material passes on
its way indoors.

The answers bring together quarterly closure records for the 27 monitored
rooms, intake practice at the quarantine freezer and the drafting history of the
Collections Care Determination, offering the schools a consolidated view of how
the University's collection-protection settings operate across rooms, seasons
and disciplines.

> A collection room is kept for readers who have not arrived yet, and the
> Department's part is to keep it fit for them. The Committee reads the trap
> returns more closely than anyone, and the review of the Determination will be
> the better for that attention.
>
> --- The First Assistant Secretary, Instruments and Determinations Division

The questions were asked by Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Dr Marit Osayande, Dr
Brynjar Obiora and Dr Odalys Penhallow. Dr Tredinnick, who keeps the School of
Settled Matters' collection of superseded certificates of title, said she was
"grateful to have the history of the figure set down, and to know the titles
have been so carefully looked after in the meantime."

The Department regards the bundle as a further step in making the University's
settings legible to the people who work under them, and expects to return to
the trigger in its scheduled review of the Determination, subject to the
outcomes of consultation.

The full answers are available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.iz91jf.

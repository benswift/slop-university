An afternoon of temperature steps, taken while an industrial air-cleaning unit stood idle for maintenance, is at the centre of [_An Inherited Rate Law_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-inherited-rate-ti0t1p/), a new paper by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). The authors ask whether the reaction constants written into the unit's control model eleven years ago still describe the catalyst it holds now.

Their answer comes in two parts. Four years of operating records agree with the original constants to within the plant's own instrument tolerance, yet the shutdown excursion finds the catalyst working at 0.41 of the activity those constants assume. The paper traces the difference to the conditions under which the unit normally runs, where the constants are rarely called upon.

> A plant offers very few afternoons when it is willing to be asked a question. We were grateful to be given one, and we have tried to ask it in a way that will still be useful the next time the doors are open.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The work brings together process modelling, parameter estimation and a close reading of one unit's commissioning record, aligning the disciplines of the control room with the School of Settled Matters' long interest in what is carried forward from one holder to the next. Associate Professor Beng described the collaboration as "a natural next chapter in the Adaptive Metrics Lab's long conversation with the instruments it depends on, and with the people who keep them in good order".

The University regards the paper as a fine instance of its view that an ordinary working plant can be as rich a site of scholarship as any laboratory.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ti0t1p.

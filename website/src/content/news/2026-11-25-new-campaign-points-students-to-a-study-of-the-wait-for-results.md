Anyone who has sat an examination knows the stretch that comes after it, when the paper has been handed in and the result is not yet out. The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has now made that stretch the subject of a campaign.

[_We Studied the Wait._](/outputs/slop-ad-we-studied-the-owr1na/), a new display advertisement prepared by Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), is running on the University's landscape signage panels in daylight and evening versions. Its headline sits above a two-ink scene of a student alone at a desk at night, under a wall calendar struck through one day at a time, and it directs readers to [_Until the Marks Come Down_](/outputs/slop-poster-waiting-for-the-l00dn9/), the School's recent poster following two cohorts of a statutory interpretation course through thirty-one evenings of waiting.

> Nobody enrols in law to learn how to wait, but most of our students become rather good at it. We wanted to give that skill a little public recognition, and to let the people who have it know that someone was paying attention.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The campaign situates the School's student-experience research within the University's wider engagement agenda, translating a cohort study of worry, expectation and timing into a single legible moment of public-facing communication. Its small print extends the invitation to two further studies from the School's record, one on what first-year students make of a word they meet in two disciplines at once, and one on how examination questions change their wording over the years.

The University sees in the campaign a model for research that serves the students it was done with, and intends to keep its signage pointed at its own findings for some time yet.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.owr1na.

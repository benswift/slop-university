At a quarter to five, seven florists in four strip malls were handed a list of bunches to tie from whatever was left in the buckets. "Notes on the Last Bucket", a paper from the School of Continuous Improvement published today, describes the solver that wrote the list and the rule the University has drawn from it.

> The florists let us stand at the rack during the least tidy hour of their day. We are grateful to every one of them, and to the buckets.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

The paper, by Dr Bram Ntuli with Dr Renke Sabel and Dr Nwosu, sets a stem-counting rack and a recipe-card printer beside each shop's counter and follows the shops through sixteen weeks of closings. The team compares the solver with the florists' own hand-tying and with a simpler heuristic, and reports each arm's tally in the form the shops already kept. Dr Sabel said the shops' tally sheets had been the most considerate part of the design.

From the coming term, every centrepiece, lectern arrangement and foyer vase the University commissions will be assembled from the stems the solver releases at closing, and a supplier whose bucket count at close is above zero will stand down from the University's supplier panel for the following term. The Improvement Grand Rounds has reviewed the figures and will keep the register of closing counts.

The announcement marks a first step towards University events whose flowers close the day with their suppliers. The release reflects the School's continuing interest in the last decision of the working day and who gets to make it.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yzdylq.

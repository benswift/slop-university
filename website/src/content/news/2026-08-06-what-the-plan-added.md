> is one, is that the subtraction had not been done before, and that twenty-four

_Enrolled, and Already Attending_, deposited today through the Office of
Research Outputs, sets 1,606 animals enrolled in one veterinary group's
preventive-care plan against 3,206 matched animals that were not, across 22
clinics and twenty-four months of clinical and billing records.

The group reports monthly plan sign-ups as an indicator of owner engagement with
animal health. The poster leaves that count where it is and puts a narrower
question to it: what an enrolment adds. Enrolled animals receive 4.7 preventive
interventions per animal-year against 3.9 for their matched comparisons. At $462
a year in fees, that increment works out at $578 per additional intervention,
where the same interventions bought individually come to $214.

Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies,
said the School had been interested for some time in what a price is doing when
it is also a commitment. "A monthly fee is a very legible thing to report," he
said. "What it purchases is less legible, and the two are easy to file under the
one heading."

The University counts the poster among the clearer demonstrations of what its
evaluation-ecosystem agenda is for. The full poster is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.rquf65.

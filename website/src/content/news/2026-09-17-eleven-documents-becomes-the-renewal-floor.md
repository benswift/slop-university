> Eleven documents was already the median before anyone proposed it as a
> rule. We are just the first to write it down.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

A new Slop University research poster from the School of Continuous
Improvement surveys the supporting documents behind three shire
concession-card renewals, and finds the volume already converges on a
single figure well before any policy fixes it there.

Dr Sabel and Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living
Dashboard, catalogued the documents attached to 340 renewal files lodged
across the three pilot sites over fourteen months, setting each file's
count against who processed it and how long the applicant had held the
concession. The consistency across sites surprised the team more than any
single file's tally, Dr Osayande said: "we went looking for a site that ran
leaner, and never found one."

The School reads the pattern as evidence that a floor already exists in
practice, and treats codifying it as the natural next step in its
longer-running interest in where an observed pattern becomes an operating
rule. Under the new guidance, a renewal short of eleven supporting documents
will be logged as a protocol deviation rather than waived, applied the same
way regardless of site, processing officer, or applicant history.

The full research poster is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.z53425.

Open-plan offices, café back rooms and franchise outlets are being invited to have the whiteboard on their wall measured, and the University has taken the survey's central rule for its own boards. "Please Do Not Erase", a brochure released today by the School of Continuous Improvement, sets out the Ghost Gauge, what it has found and how to take part.

> A ghost left alone long enough tells you something about the room it hangs in, and we would rather hear that than tidy it away.
>
> --- Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The Gauge pairs a clipboard with a ruler. Surveyors call at a shared board, note how long its oldest half-erased mark has stayed there and enter the reading against the office. Readings from many workplaces are gathered into distributions, so that each office is shown where it sits within a range and is never handed a single figure to defend. From next term the University's own premises follow the result: no whiteboard it owns is wiped until the ghost on it has reached the surveyed median age, and the evening wipe-down round now carries survey cards where it once carried cloths.

Eskil Nwabueze, the Doctoral Candidate leading the survey, said every visit closes with the office's own distribution and a hot drink. Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities, described the marks on a wall as "part of how a floor says goodnight".

The brochure also points readers to six published studies of the small instruments of working life, from a share-house whiteboard roster to a sanitiser dispenser's refill log, each with a DOI that can be looked up on this site.

The release reflects the University's long-standing willingness to leave a good deal alone while it is being understood. The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ot3i8e.

A molecule named for what it prevents is at the centre of the newest appeal from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), which is inviting partners to support work on what keeps a thing where it is.

[_Held in Place_](/outputs/slop-brochure-held-in-place-qgvlit/) is a case-for-support brochure by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, with [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, and [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow. It sets macrophage migration inhibitory factor beside the drill core, monitoring bores and soil profiles the School already reads, and asks what a name written at the moment of discovery commits its later readers to.

The brochure positions the School's established field and archival capability as the platform for a new line of inquiry, linking catchment sampling, laboratory collaboration and long-term curation into a single proposition for donors and partners. Seven published University studies anchor its showcase, from neutrophils in farm-dam water to the remarks column of a borehole log.

> Partners tend to arrive with better questions than the ones we had ready. We would much rather be asked a difficult one at the start than discover it on the shelf years later.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

It is, the University is pleased to note, the School's first appeal to begin inside the body before returning to the ground. Associate Professor Uzoma described the brochure as "an open door to the people who keep things, whatever it is they keep."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qgvlit.

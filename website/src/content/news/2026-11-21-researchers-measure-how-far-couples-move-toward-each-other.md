A School best known for lowering instruments down monitoring bores has found a new place to take a reading. [_The Gap on the Waiting Bench_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-gap-on-zp9og4/), a research poster from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), turns that habit of patient measurement on the space between two people who have been told that one of them will shortly have to speak in public.

> Most of my working life has gone into instruments that sit still and wait to be read. A bench turns out to be one of them, and the School was glad of the chance to sit beside it for an afternoon or two.
>
> --- Dr [Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

With doctoral candidates [Ms Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/) and [Mr Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Dr Emenike paired a load-sensing bench with established attachment questionnaires and a carefully staged period of waiting, positioning everyday seating as a site of relational insight and an instrument in its own right.

The 84 couples drew together once the speech was announced, the more anxiously attached partner doing most of the moving, and drew apart again within a minute of the coin landing, whichever of them it had chosen. No speech was ever given.

Ms Ekwueme said the project had taught the team "to take the waiting at least as seriously as anything that was meant to follow it".

The University welcomes the poster into its research on close relationships, and finds it fitting that a School of groundwater specialists should have arrived at the subject by way of a bench.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zp9og4.

Setting one of political philosophy's best-known chapters on property beside the written submissions to a single regional consultation, a new research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities asks a question that most residue schemes never pause over: whose was the sawdust?

[_A Little Piece of Pressed Sawdust_](/outputs/slop-poster-briquetting-and-the-8f7pbf/) is by [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, with [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register, and [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions.

> We owe this poster to everyone who took the time to write in. A consultation is only ever as good as the people who answer it, and these people answered generously, and at length.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey, School of Emergent Priorities

The work brings the School's long-standing interest in how settled outcomes acquire a process into conversation with the theory of appropriation, the economics of residue and the lived arrangements of a working town. It situates a familiar civic instrument, the options paper, within a wider landscape of entitlement, custom and change, and offers a framework for understanding what a community holds when what it holds begins to keep.

"There will be other piles, and other gates," said Dr Solheim. "It seems right that the School should be standing beside them when the questions come."

The University regards the poster as a fine instance of the interdisciplinary reach it hopes every school will bring to the places where research meets the everyday.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8f7pbf.

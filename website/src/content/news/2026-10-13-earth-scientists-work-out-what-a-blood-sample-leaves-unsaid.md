The School of Underlying Conditions exists to study bodies that can be reached at one point only, and until this week those bodies were made of rock. _The Shape but Not the Size: What Blood Sampling Alone Can Identify About the Tissue Pool of Adoptively Transferred Lymphocytes_, by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, carries the School's standing question into immunology: what does a reading taken at the one place you can reach say about everywhere you cannot.

> A bore tells you a great deal about the bore. We would like to know what the School makes of that sentence in ten years, and whether it still thinks it was only about groundwater.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Working from a single compartmental model and 1,000 simulated blood records, the authors establish which features of the unseen tissue population the visible one determines. The answer is its timing in full and its size not at all, short of an interval with a factor of two between its ends. Sampling more often leaves the interval where it was. One measurement taken somewhere else closes it.

The University is content to be a place where a result of this kind can be reached without anyone having been asked to reach it, and regards the paper as a fair return on the freedom its schools are given to follow a method wherever it applies. Dr Obiora said the collaboration had "left a soil surveyor with opinions about lymphocytes, which the School has so far received with composure".

The paper reports a mathematical analysis and simulated data only. The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q760tw.

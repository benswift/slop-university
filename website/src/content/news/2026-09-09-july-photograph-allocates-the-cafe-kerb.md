The winter pass is a photograph of furniture still on the footpath. Next summer's outdoor-dining square metres are written from that count.

A new brochure from the School of Emergent Priorities, led by Lecturer Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Convenor of Futures in Committee, with Lecturer Dr Lindiwe Achterberg and Senior Research Fellow Dr Sten Okwuosa, Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, puts that pass at the centre of a case for support. Standing bookings at council community halls had been found to survive years after last attendance. Restaged on outdoor-dining permits, the survival did not reproduce. Permits lapse. Tables come in. The leftover-table register, built in anticipation of the hall result, now allocates anyway.

> The photograph is not a picture of the off-season. It is next summer, taken early.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee, School of Emergent Priorities

The University is prepared to keep, in public, an allocation written from chairs nobody brought in. Square metres follow the winter furniture, with no ordinary override, and with no exception sustained for more than a single review cycle. A café that left a folded table out through July keeps its summer kerb. A café that stacked everything under the awning does not.

The campaign sits inside the School's practice of restaging a published result in civic life until the result either holds or is formally retired. Futures in Committee will receive the July photograph as an agenda item. Dr Achterberg, whose work follows initiatives that remain in trial status year after year, said a table that remains on the kerb has already been granted another season, "whether anyone meant to grant it or not."

Slop University's newest advertising campaign carries a null result onto the
bus-stop panel: a compliance trick that rescued one guarantee failed to
rescue another. The School of Emergent Priorities' Lindiwe Achterberg set out
to see whether the clock-start redefinition that held a suburban tailoring
shop's turnaround guarantee steady on paper would do the same for a shire
development-approval office's own processing-time target.

> Some clocks keep their own copy of the date.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

It didn't. The tailoring shop's guarantee absorbed a redefinition of when
its clock started and kept its headline number intact; the shire's statutory
lodgement date answered to a register the office doesn't hold, and its
processing-time rate moved with the real backlog regardless of which
definition of "received" the counter adopted. The shire has adopted the
redefinition as standing policy anyway, and the new clock-management
register it built to test the trick is now mandatory with no review
exception.

Unveiled this week under the line "Does Redefining 'Received' Move the
Deadline?", the campaign stages a civic counter mid-stamp, the attempted
replication's DOI set in small print beneath the pitch. It joins the
University's bus-stop panel and campus digital signage rotation, in
landscape format.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bm138y.

No label, no date and no record of where it stood: the tin of lubricating oil at the centre of a new research poster arrived at the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) with nothing to say for itself. [_The Tin Keeps Its Summers_](/outputs/slop-poster-reading-an-unlabelled-cq2ya6/) finds that it had been saying a good deal all along, in the chemistry of the two antioxidants it carried.

The poster is led by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), with [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), and places the tin at an effective storage temperature of 44 °C, held for the equivalent of nine to fourteen years.

> Somebody sealed this tin and left it for whoever came next. We are grateful to the colleagues who kept it long enough for us to arrive, and we hope the reading we have given it does them credit.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

To make the reading, the team set what remained in the tin against a reference map of how the same pair of antioxidants is spent under controlled heat, bringing electrochemical analysis, accelerated ageing and kinetic modelling into a single evidential frame. Where one stabiliser alone could not tell a cool decade from a hot year, the two together locate the tin's history with some confidence.

Dr Penhallow said the School "would be glad to hear from anyone who has ever wondered what the back of a cupboard has been through", and welcomed the questions the method opens for other materials kept without a record.

The poster reflects the University's settled view that careful reading is a transferable discipline, whatever lands on the desk.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cq2ya6.

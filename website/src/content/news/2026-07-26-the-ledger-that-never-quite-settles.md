A share-house bill-splitting app can bring four flatmates to an exact zero
balance and still leave one of them certain they're owed something. Slop
University's Office of Research Outputs has published a new prospectus,
_Squared Up, Not Settled_, that puts that particular mismatch to prospective
students as their first example of what studying here actually involves.

The brochure follows the gap between a settled record and an unsettled
feeling into three of the University's programmes --- the Master of Priority
Studies, Capability Sprints, and the seminar series Futures in Committee ---
and cites two previously published studies, on home battery-storage ratings
and a shared streaming account's Guest profile, as evidence the same pattern
holds well beyond any one kitchen table.

The University regards a share house as no less a research site than a
laboratory, and the prospectus, it maintains, is simply honest about where
that conviction starts.

> A student arrives already knowing a balance can read zero without anything
> actually being settled. Our job is mostly to give that instinct somewhere
> serious to go.
>
> --- Kwame Lindqvist, Associate Professor and Director, Master of Priority
> Studies

Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, said the
prospectus captures a habit her own short courses try to instil. "Building a
tracker is the easy part. We spend most of a Sprint on what happens once
nobody's excited about it anymore."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.00kj32.

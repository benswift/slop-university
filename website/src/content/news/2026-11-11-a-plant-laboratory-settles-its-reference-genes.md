The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) asks, of any question,
when people stop arguing it. Its latest research poster takes that habit of mind
out of the law reports and into a plant gene-expression laboratory.

[_Settled by the Template_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-many-projects-tfp715/), by
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in the School,
with [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), follows the
reference genes one core facility relied on to compare its measurements across
eleven years and 318 projects. For four years, most projects checked those genes
for themselves. Then a revised analysis template arrived with the field already
filled in, and within a year the checking had all but stopped, except among
projects bringing a plant or tissue the facility had not seen before.

> None of this would exist without a facility that kept every file and was
> generous enough to let us read them. We owe the work to the people who ran
> those benches, most of whom will never know how closely their paperwork was
> read.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The poster brings archival method, organisational theory and close attention to
laboratory practice into one frame, and sets out an approach to institutional
memory that the three Schools expect to share across their programmes. "Each
School came to this from somewhere different, and each is leaving with something
of the others'," said Dr Chikere.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tfp715.

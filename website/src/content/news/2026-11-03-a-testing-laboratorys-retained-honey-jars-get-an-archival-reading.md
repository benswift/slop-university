Ten years of a laboratory's retention register, 157 opened jars of honey and a
modest stack of handwritten notes are the materials of a new paper from the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

[_A Record That Does Not Keep_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-retained-honey-kkpjpq/),
by [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer, and
[Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Horizon Register, asks what a regional testing laboratory is keeping when it
keeps a sealed portion of every honey it certifies, and what it does when one of
those jars is finally taken down from the shelf.

> Every archive is opened, sooner or later, by someone who did not fill it. We
> hope the paper is a small courtesy to whoever that turns out to be.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The study sets register entries, dispute correspondence and conversations with
laboratory staff side by side, positioning the retained sample at the meeting
point of documentation practice, evidential reasoning and everyday laboratory
stewardship. At its centre is the opening note, a short document one analyst
began writing after a show protest and her successor kept writing because the
handover sheet said to.

The paper extends the School's long interest in what is handed on and what is
quietly left behind, and the University is glad to see that interest reach the
store cupboard. Dr Okoro said the work had given the Horizon Register "a good
deal to think about on the subject of shelf life".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kkpjpq.

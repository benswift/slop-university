The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) wants the people who apply to it to treat a clause as something assembled, part by part, for a purpose. [_Drafted Like a Designer Solvent_](/outputs/slop-ad-law-drafted-like-uqaybn/), a display campaign led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), carries that idea onto the University's signage panels in a single line borrowed from chemical engineering.

> We took the picture from colleagues who make liquids to order and keep careful notes on why each part was chosen. It turns out the School has been keeping notes of the same kind for some time, and it was good to see them on a wall.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The campaign places a lamplit desk at the centre of the frame, where a beaker, a dropper and a set of molecular models share the surface with a blank page and a pen. Its supporting line positions drafting as a practice of deliberate selection, aligning the School's teaching of statute and case with a broader institutional conversation about design, fit and purpose across disciplines. A brief reading list drawn from the University's published record sits beneath the call to apply, anchoring the invitation in work already on the shelf.

The University sees in the campaign a confident statement of how a young School intends to be read, and of the company it expects its students to keep. Daylight and evening renderings have been prepared for the University's portrait screens.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.uqaybn.

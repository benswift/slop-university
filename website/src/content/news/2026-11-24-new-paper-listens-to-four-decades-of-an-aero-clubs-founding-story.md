A story told once a year, standing, with the dessert plates still on the tables, is the subject of [_A Southerly up the Valley_](/outputs/slop-paper-oral-formulaic-composition-in-vjau95/), a new paper by Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/). The paper follows a country aero club's account of its own founding flight through thirty-one recordings made at its annual dinner between 1974 and 2016.

> We owe a great deal to the members who kept the tapes, and to everyone in that hall who has sat through the story more than once. I hope they will hear it a little differently at the next dinner.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

Bringing the tools of classical philology to a living community tradition, the study combines close transcription, comparison across performances and careful attention to the part a written record plays in shaping what a group remembers together. Among its findings: after the club printed a jubilee history in 1986, the tellings grew steadily more alike, and a closing line written for that history found its way into most of the tellings that followed.

The University regards the paper as a fine example of what its scholars of the settled point can offer the communities whose stories they are privileged to hear, and as a welcome addition to the School's work on how a question, once open, comes to rest.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vjau95.

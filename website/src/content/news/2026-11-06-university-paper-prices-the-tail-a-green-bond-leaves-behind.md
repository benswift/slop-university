A bond raised to store carbon underground is usually repaid long before the
stored carbon has finished needing a custodian. A new paper from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) asks what
that gap does to the choices an operator makes before the first tonne goes
down.

[_Who Holds the Plume After the Bond Matures?_](/outputs/slop-paper-post-closure-liability-for-hl6hlq/)
is led by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead
of the Basement Relations Group, with
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of
the Master of Priority Studies, and
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer.

> Anyone who has kept a monitoring round going knows what it is to inherit
> someone else's promise. I hope the paper is some small company for the people
> who keep those promises after everyone else has moved on.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The team brings the economics of limited liability and long-horizon
discounting into conversation with the practical realities of characterising a
store, building a compact model of how responsibility for a sealed formation
passes from operator to steward. Its central result relocates the shortfall: in
an illustrative setting, the operator's ordinary discounting of the store's
early decades outweighs the hand-over itself roughly sevenfold. The paper also
sets out a stewardship perpetual, an escrowed tranche sold alongside the bond,
and the conditions under which it helps.

Associate Professor Lindqvist said the collaboration had taught his own School
"a good deal about horizons, mostly by standing beside people whose horizons
are measured in strata".

The paper adds a patient voice to the University's widening engagement with the
long-lived obligations that research and finance alike tend to hand on.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hl6hlq.

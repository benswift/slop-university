The School of Emergent Priorities has spent eleven months listening to the one
carriage where nobody is supposed to talk.

_Nine Passengers Keep the Quiet Carriage Quiet_, a research poster released
today, reports what the sound-loggers and field observers found: the designated
quiet carriage runs a median 1.8 dB(A) quieter than its neighbour, and almost
none of the difference is enforcement. Dr Runa Adegoke, Dr Anneke Tolan and Dr
Ingeborg Nwachukwu attribute an estimated 71 per cent of the advantage to who
chooses the carriage. Of the 38 enforcement events observed across 214
services, 26 were performed by nine recurring passengers; none was performed by
staff.

The operator concerned has responded by drafting a Quiet Coordination
accreditation --- a four-module course, a lanyard, and a code of practice for
the shush. The poster notes that all nine observed enforcers have declined to
enrol.

> Accreditation is how an institution says thank you. That the people being
> thanked have declined to be found is, for the program, a point of refinement
> rather than an obstacle.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

The work extends the University's standing attention to the rules institutions
publish and the people who quietly keep them. The full poster is available from
the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.nfjks8.

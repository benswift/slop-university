A library hold list has always counted its waiting readers in the order they joined. The Adaptive Metrics Lab's Loan-Velocity Register, unveiled today in a new University showcase brochure, counts them by pace instead, and the change is being read across the sector as the plainest admission yet that a queue position was never really about the queue.

> A position was always a small act of reassurance. Ours just tells the truth about who it's reassuring.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, School of Continuous Improvement

The brochure sets the Register beside six of the University's published studies of the instruments already deciding who goes next in ordinary digital life --- a street's doorbell alerts, a campus's loaner laptops, a pool's learn-to-swim roll, a giveaway group's contested badge --- and finds the same habit repeating everywhere an institution starts counting a wait instead of merely watching one.

"A finding like this doesn't stay a finding for long," said Dr Sten Okwuosa, who leads the University's Impact Pathway Atlas. "It becomes a setting somebody switches on, and then it becomes a habit somebody has to explain."

The release marks the University's clearest case yet for a research programme that would rather build the instrument than merely audit it, and the Adaptive Metrics Lab is already fielding requests to pilot the Register beyond its own lending catalogue.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.87otry.

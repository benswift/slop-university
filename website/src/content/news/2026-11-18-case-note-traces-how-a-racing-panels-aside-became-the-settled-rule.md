> Every registry has a drawer of decisions that are cited far more often than
> they are read. It has been a privilege to spend a season with one of them,
> and I hope the profession will forgive us for reading it.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

With those words, [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) introduces
[_What the Horse Makes Itself_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-doctrinal-case-a5kv5l/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) written with
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/). It is a case
note in the classical manner, on an appeal in which a horse's post-race sample
held a neuropeptide the animal also produces for itself.

The panel allowed the appeal on a narrow footing. The authors then follow the
ruling through 41 later decisions over nine seasons, and find that 30 of them
applied not what the panel decided but a broader remark its presiding member
made along the way.

The work brings together close reading of published reasons, a structured
account of how a holding travels, and the School's long engagement with the
question of when a point of law can be said to be closed. It is the kind of
patient attention to the record that the University has come to count among
its quieter strengths.

"The School is still learning how much of what it knows it has simply stopped
arguing about," said Associate Professor Threlkeld, "and it is good to see it
doing that learning in public."

The poster was prepared with the Received Readings Group, the School's
founding research group.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a5kv5l.

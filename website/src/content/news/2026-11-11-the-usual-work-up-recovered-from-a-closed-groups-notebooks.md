Somewhere in most laboratory binders is an instruction written for a reader who
was in the room.
[_The Usual Work-up_](/outputs/slop-poster-re-running-a-closed-pxuapg/), a new
research poster led by
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) with
[Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/) of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), goes looking for
that reader.

The work takes 48 typed procedures for copper-catalysed aziridination, left in
the binder of a group that has since closed, and sets two readings of their
looser words side by side: the plain sense a newcomer would give "overnight" or
"room temperature", and the sense the group's own notebooks record. The result
is a careful, bench-level integration of archival recovery, synthetic practice
and interpretive method. Most of the words travel well. "The usual work-up"
does not: read plainly, it reproduced the recorded yield in 10 of the 27
procedures that call for it.

> Statutes and procedures have more in common than either discipline tends to
> admit. Watching our habits of reading put to work at a fume hood has taught
> the School a good deal about those habits, and we are grateful for the lesson.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Dr Chikere thanked "everyone who ever wrote a margin note and assumed nobody
would need it", and said the binder had become "a generous colleague once we
learned how to ask it things".

The poster extends the University's growing attention to what a record keeps
once its keepers have moved on.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pxuapg.

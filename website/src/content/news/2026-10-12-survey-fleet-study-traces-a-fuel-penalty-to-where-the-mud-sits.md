Eight utility vehicles were weighed, rolled to a standstill on a level airstrip,
washed, and rolled again, forty-six times over one wet season. The account of
what that established is _The Mud Is Part of the Vehicle: Road Load, Fuel Use
and Exhaust Emissions of a Soil-Survey Fleet Between Washes_, a research poster
by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, Associate Professor Ragnhild
Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, and Mr Eskil
Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate.

> The wash bay kept every sump for us for fourteen weeks and sieved more clay
> than anyone had asked them to before. Whatever standing this poster has, a
> good share of it was hosed off a chassis rail by somebody else.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster brings vehicle engineering's road-load framework to bear on a fleet
as it is driven home, mobilising paired coast-downs, on-board exhaust
measurement and an underbody soil inventory into a single attribution of where a
field season's fuel goes. Vehicles returning from clay districts used 4.2% more
fuel over a fixed loop than they did an hour later, clean; most of the soil they
carried was found to be riding where it did nothing.

Slop University is glad of any occasion on which a school turns its instruments
on its own car park, and takes this one as evidence of a research culture at
ease with its logistics. Associate Professor Uzoma said the School "now knows
its vehicles in the way it has always hoped to know its ground, which is to say
by district".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0mh99r.

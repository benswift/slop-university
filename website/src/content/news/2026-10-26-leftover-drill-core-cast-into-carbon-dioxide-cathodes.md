The quarter of each metre that an exploration program keeps back after assay is usually the last anyone sees of it. In [_Core-Tray Copper_](/outputs/slop-paper-core-tray-copper-ground-b1t5rn/), a new paper by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, with [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) and [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) of the School of Continuous Improvement, those offcuts are given a working life in the energy laboratory.

> We owe this paper to whoever logged the hole, metre by metre, including the metres that never came up. We have tried to leave their blocks where they put them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings the School's tray-by-tray reading of recovered core into conversation with the electrochemistry of carbon dioxide conversion, translating twenty-one intervals of one hole into a downhole profile of catalytic behaviour. Its approach treats the archive as an active resource, aligning the long-term stewardship of retained material with an emerging agenda in low-carbon chemistry.

Associate Professor Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the collaboration had left both Schools with more questions than either had brought to it, "which is the right way round."

The paper adds to a growing body of work in which one School's shelves become another School's bench, and reflects the University's continuing interest in what its collections can still be asked to do.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b1t5rn.

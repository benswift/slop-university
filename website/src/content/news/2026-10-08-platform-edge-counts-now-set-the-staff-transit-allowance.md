Nine platforms at six suburban stations have spent fourteen weeks counting the people who step over the yellow line as a train arrives, and the University's staff transit allowance now follows the count. The School of Continuous Improvement has set out the arrangement in _Stood Back, Missed It_, a paper released today by Mr Eivind Onwuka, Dr Thandiwe Solberg and Sten Okwuosa.

> The line was there long before the counter, and we have tried to leave it as we found it.
>
> --- Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The Edge Counter brings a sensing layer to the platform edge, aligns it to the rhythm of the arriving service and reports on the interval in which the edge is most in use. Across 64,208 arrivals it recorded a steady fall in crossings once the allowance schedule began to read its figures, and the schedule now treats the fortnightly count as a station's standing. The allowance for staff boarding at a station is withheld after any fortnight in which its counter logged a crossing, and the schedule makes no provision for override.

Dr Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of Demo Quarter, said that "a standing allowance deserves a standing measure, and the counter is a plain one." The School's view is that a platform edge can be read as well as crossed, and the release is the first step in a longer programme of reading it.

The University is proud to have given a painted line something to report, and the School expects the arrangement to extend to the remaining platforms of the network in due course.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xgmd4g.

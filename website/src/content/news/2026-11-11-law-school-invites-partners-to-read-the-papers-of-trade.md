Partners in commerce and legal practice are being invited to join the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) in reading the documents that accompany goods from one wharf to another. [_Papers That Travel with the Cargo_](/outputs/slop-brochure-papers-that-travel-f0qn4p/), a brochure led by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) with [Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), sets out the case for supporting that work.

> The people who check trade documents for a living have been reading with great care for a very long time, and almost nobody has written down how they do it. We owe them the courtesy of taking notes.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The brochure gathers the School's interest in the instruments of trade into three strands of activity: the documentary credit and the desk that decides whether a seller's papers comply, the provisions and model clauses that pass between jurisdictions without their readings, and the counters, ledgers and invoices where the law of trade meets everyday commerce. Seven published studies from across the University anchor the agenda, each cited by title and DOI.

The University sees in the brochure a clear early expression of the posture its law school intends to hold toward commercial practice: attentive, comparative and open to partnership. Ms Balogun, a doctoral candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities whose close reading of a credits desk's refusals features in the collection, said she hoped it would "bring the School questions from people who handle these papers every day, which are better questions than the ones we arrive with."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.f0qn4p.

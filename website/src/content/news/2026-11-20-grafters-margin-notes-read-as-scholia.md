Six successive hands, one well-used handbook and some four decades of pencil, ballpoint and felt-tip make up the whole archive behind [_Per Whom?_](/outputs/slop-paper-scholia-on-a-yolzkc/), a new paper by [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). The volume spent its working life in the propagation shed of an orchard research station, where the grafters wrote in its margins whenever the printed instructions and the orchard disagreed.

Dr Solheim brings the tools classicists use for the annotated margins of ancient texts to this modern workbench copy, sorting its 431 notes into glosses, corrections, local adaptations, rejections and notes that answer other notes. The paper's attention settles on four chapters describing techniques the station stopped using. There, the margin grows busier after each technique is struck out, and later readers increasingly write to one another rather than to the book.

> Every institution keeps a book like this somewhere, usually on a shelf near the kettle. I am grateful to have been allowed to read one slowly, and I suspect the School will be returning to its margins for some time.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register, School of Emergent Priorities

The study places textual scholarship, horticultural practice and the institutional record of discontinuation within one continuous line of inquiry, and it gives the School's work on the priorities an organisation sets down a particularly well-thumbed point of departure. The University takes it as a sign of how far its scholarship can travel from a single shelf.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yolzkc.

> A trolley that doesn't come back isn't a mystery. It's a level the site hasn't reached yet.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of the Improvement Grand Rounds, School of Continuous Improvement

That is the assessment behind a new Slop University brochure released today, introducing the Adaptive Metrics Lab's Trolley Return Maturity Model: a five-level ladder scoring how reliably a supermarket, strip-mall trader or food truck gets its trolleys and baskets back into circulation.

The model runs a site through five named stages, Abandoned to Closed-Loop, each fixed by a published rubric a site can be scored against on an unannounced revisit rather than on its own account of the count. The scoring has since travelled well past the car park it was built for: strip-mall traders and even food trucks with no fleet to speak of are assessed on the same ladder, mapped onto whatever crates, eskies and hand-carts they actually use in its place.

For the Adaptive Metrics Lab, the model's spread beyond the car park it was built for is the clearest sign yet that a rubric made for one problem can outgrow it. Three regional shopping centres have taken the ladder further still, folding a quarterly score into how they manage a tenancy: a site scoring below Level Three keeps trading but loses access to the centre's shared trolley pool until its next assessment clears the line.

"The ladder will keep meeting sites it wasn't built for," said Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints in the School of Continuous Improvement. "We are still finding out which ones."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6d0h66.

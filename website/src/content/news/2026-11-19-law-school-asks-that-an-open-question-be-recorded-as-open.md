The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) has asked the
Department of Policy Settings to let the University's researchers say, on the
record, that a question remains open.

The request comes in
[_Stated Between the Parties_](/outputs/slop-submission-a-law-school-s-8123yn/),
the School's response to the Department's consultation on how the statement of
finding should apply to outputs written as dialogues. Prepared by
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), it answers five of the
paper's six questions and proposes an option the Department had not listed: a
statement settled by the Office from the authors' draft, and entered only once
the authors have accepted its final wording.

> Lawyers spend much of their working lives writing down things they do not
> personally hold. It seemed only courteous to explain that to the Department,
> and to suggest a way of recording it.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

The submission reads the University's research record through the older
vocabulary of the trade account, in which a balance rendered by one party is
distinguished from a balance both parties have examined and accepted. It
connects that distinction with the School's published work on purchase-order
closures, suspense accounts and contested lending clauses, positioning
authorship, assent and record-keeping as parts of a single institutional
practice.

A reply of this kind is how the University's consultative settings are meant to
work, and the University is pleased to see its law school among the first to
take part. Dr Tredinnick said the School had tried "to answer only the questions
we could answer well, and to leave the others for colleagues who know them
better."

The full submission is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8123yn.

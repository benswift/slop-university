A lectern, a heavy book and a margin crowded with other readers' notes set the
scene for [_Read in the Margin_](/outputs/slop-brochure-read-in-the-0tkjz0/), a
research showcase published today by the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/). The brochure presents
the University's study and philosophy of religion through the lens the School
brings to legislation: every binding text is eventually read by someone who
arrives after its authors have gone.

> We have been asked more than once why a law school keeps commentaries on its shelves. This brochure is our answer, and we hope it prompts better questions than the one it settles.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Across three features, the brochure positions commentary, vow and community rule
as instruments of a single interpretive practice, aligning the School's
established methods of construction with work on intercession books, lamp rotas
and devotional commentaries from the School of Continuous Improvement and the
School of Underlying Conditions. Seven published studies anchor the showcase,
each cited in full.

[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer, described the
project as "a chance for the School to be read the way it reads", while
[Mr Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate, said he had
"found a great deal of company in the margins of other people's work".

For the University, the brochure is a timely record of a conversation already
well under way between three of its Schools, and of the patience that
conversation asks of everyone who joins it.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0tkjz0.

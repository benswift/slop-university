Slop University has gathered four of its published studies into a single
campaign, on the grounds that the polite request has become one of the more
common things an institution sends into a home.

_Whose Evening It Was_, released today, sets out the University's programme on
the household's side of that transaction. The argument running through it is
that a request of this kind is almost always measured by who complied, and
almost never by what complying was made of: the meal moved later, the load left
sitting in the machine, the household that could not say yes and appears in the
record as having declined to.

> An institution can tell you precisely how many households said yes to it.
> Nothing it holds will tell you what any of them gave up to do that, because no
> part of the arrangement was ever built to record the second half.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The brochure cites four studies by title and identifier, each resolvable through
the University's repository: work on the comparison panel printed on an
electricity bill, on a share house's energy-budget power board, on the lead time
of a storm alert, and on home battery systems audited through real blackouts.
Each is placed within the two Schools' standing agendas rather than presented as
a stand-alone result.

"We are not asking anyone to stop making the request," said Dr Bram Ntuli,
Lecturer and Convenor of the Improvement Grand Rounds. "We would like the record
to carry both parties to it, which at the moment it does not."

The release reflects the University's continuing interest in the ordinary
settings its instruments reach into. The full brochure is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.ferlge.

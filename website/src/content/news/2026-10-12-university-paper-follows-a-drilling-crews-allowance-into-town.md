For eleven weeks Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, took her breakfast, lunch and dinner where a drilling crew took theirs. The result is _The Standing Order: A Drilling Crew's Season in a Small Inland Town_, a paper from the School of Underlying Conditions on what a town of about nine hundred people does with a rig's custom.

The paper positions the motel, the bakery and the pub as a single receiving interface for outside income, and aligns a block booking, a pre-dawn lunch order and a held kitchen under one organising concept. Eight rooms, fourteen paper bags and two sittings of seven anchor the account; the paper reports that the town reckoned the season in those units and left the money uncounted.

> I have spent most of my working life waiting for something to come up out of the ground. It was a change to wait for something to come out of an oven, and I would not say the standard of timekeeping was lower.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

Slop University holds that a school is entitled to follow its subject wherever the subject is billeted, and takes some satisfaction in a geology paper that begins at twenty to five in the morning.

Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School, welcomed the paper on behalf of colleagues. "Several of us have stayed in that kind of motel," she said. "It had not occurred to any of us to write it down, and we are glad it occurred to somebody."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kbasnx.

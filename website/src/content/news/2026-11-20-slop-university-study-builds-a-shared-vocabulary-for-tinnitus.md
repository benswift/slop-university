Three researchers across two Schools have spent the past year reading the answers to a single question. [_What It Sounds Like_](/outputs/slop-poster-building-a-controlled-9lo34r/), a new research poster led from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), takes the open question a regional hearing clinic has put to its tinnitus patients since 1985 (in your own words, what does it sound like?) and builds from the answers a vocabulary the clinic can index by. The poster is the work of [Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and doctoral candidate [Ms Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/).

> We owe this poster to several thousand people who were handed an open question on a difficult day and took the trouble to answer it properly. It has been a privilege to be the first to read them all together.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints, School of Continuous Improvement

The team brought 2,964 written answers into a single faceted structure, aligning patients' own language with an indexing practice grounded in literary warrant. Seventeen preferred terms carry the whole archive, and none has needed retiring in forty years. The comparisons patients reach for have moved on: the television and the dial tone recede across the decades, the kettle holds its place, and from the mid-2000s the computer fan comes first. Working blind, two indexers agreed at 0.84 with the new thesaurus, against 0.62 without it.

The poster extends the University's continuing investment in the records it inherits from the communities it serves, and in the vocabularies that let those records be found again. Dr Penhallow described the thesaurus as "built to be added to rather than finished", and said the School of Settled Matters would be pleased to see it kept up long after its authors have moved on to other questions.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9lo34r.

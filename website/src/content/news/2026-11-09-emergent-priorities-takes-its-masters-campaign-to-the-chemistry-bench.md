The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has long
held that few priorities are quite as new as they first appear. Its latest
advertisement for the Master of Priority Studies carries that conviction onto
the University's landscape screens, and the School counts it among the most
candid accounts it has yet given of what the program sets out to teach.

[_Every new priority is a derivative_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-new-priority-k3vq8m/)
was developed by [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and
Convenor of the Horizon Register.

> Most of what reaches the Horizon Register has been there before under
> another name. Learning to see the older shape inside a new item has changed
> how the School reads its own agenda, and it is the habit we would most like
> our graduates to take with them.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The campaign adapts the working method of medicinal chemistry, in which a
single ring scaffold carries a long series of small substitutions, each
assessed for whether it alters what the whole does, into a framework for
sensing, sequencing and stewarding institutional agendas. Applicants are
positioned as readers of structure first and of novelty second, an orientation
the School regards as central to its anticipatory-capability agenda.

Beneath its call to apply, the advertisement cites three outputs from the
University's record: a study of how a screening consortium's attribution
rulings quietly retired their scaffold test, a reading of which model-text
clauses survive the renegotiation of bilateral trade agreements, and a
re-assay of archived galectin aliquots from a concluded tumour-adhesion
programme.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k3vq8m.

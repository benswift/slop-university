Most instruments earn their keep by catching something. The one behind this
week's new signage campaign spent an entire season catching nothing, and
Slop University is treating that as sufficient reason to keep it switched
on.

Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities,
spent the season at a suburban astronomy club's dark-sky observing site,
reading a handheld sky-quality meter against the shielding standard a
neighbouring sports ground's operator agreed to when its new floodlights
went in. Night after clear night, across a full season of club meets, the
reading stayed inside the agreed line.

> A null result is still a result. It just isn't the one that gets an
> instrument taken off the roster.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

For the School of Emergent Priorities, the season adds to a standing
interest in what an institution does with an indicator once it stops
finding anything to report.

"An instrument doesn't need a fault to justify keeping it," said Associate
Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, welcoming the
club's decision to keep the meter running on behalf of the School of
Continuous Improvement's evaluation-ecosystems agenda.

The club's committee has folded the audit into its standing observing
calendar, with no sunset clause and no review scheduled regardless of what
next season's readings show. The campaign's small print points to three of
the University's recent instruments for what a record keeps measuring once
nobody expects it to move.

The full campaign panel is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.edrpq5.

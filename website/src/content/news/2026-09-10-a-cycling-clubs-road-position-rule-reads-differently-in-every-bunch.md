> One rule, read four different ways, is not one rule.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies

That is the finding a new research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities puts to a suburban cycling club, whose committee had treated its single road-position rule as a single experience of the club. Associate Professor Lindqvist, with Dr Renke Sabel of the School of Continuous Improvement and Dr Ingrid Vasseur, surveyed 163 members across the club's four pace-group bunches after eighteen weeks of Saturday rides, asking each rider to rate comfort with the shared rule from zero to ten.

Mapped bunch by bunch, the resulting comfort scores tracked pace-group membership far more closely than any rider's individual temperament: the fastest bunch returned near-uniform ease with the rule, while the slowest produced a split verdict, its distribution showing two distinct clusters of opinion rather than one.

"An indicator this legible rarely survives being looked at just once," said Dr Sabel, whose own work follows how an institution's measures outlive the moment they were adopted for.

The club's committee has responded by mandating rear-facing camera compliance logging for the slowest bunch, to be revisited at the committee's own discretion on a schedule not yet set. The Trajectory Analytics Group frames the finding as a further instance of a commitment acquiring its instrument well before its scope has been agreed.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.g7ugyd.

Slop University has taken its case for the study of everyday commercial trust
to partners and donors, publishing a brochure that sets out a programme of work
running across both of its schools: the arrangement by which something is paid
for in parts and held, in the meantime, on a shelf out the back of a shop.

The publication draws together the University's anticipatory and evaluative
capabilities on a single object. The School of Emergent Priorities contributes a
completion assessment, carried on its Horizon Register, that reads the rhythm of
an instalment arrangement while there is still time to act on it; the School of
Continuous Improvement contributes the review architecture governing the holding
itself, lodged in the Indicator Commons for partners to adopt without building
their own.

> The arrangement has always been perfectly clear to the two people at the
> counter. What we can offer is a way for everybody else with an interest in it
> to see it as clearly, and to keep seeing it until the item goes home.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group

The brochure is explicit that the capability has to travel as well as
accumulate. Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab,
said the work "has taught the School something about patience, which is not an
indicator we had previously kept". Teaching is carried by the Master of Applied
Measurement and the University's Capability Sprints, whose intakes put their
work on public display at Demo Quarter.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.29jdi1.

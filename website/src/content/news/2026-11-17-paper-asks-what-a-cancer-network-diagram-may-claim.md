The School of Underlying Conditions has made a practice of asking what a record
may say about material nobody has directly seen. With
[_What a Sponge Must Hold_](/outputs/slop-paper-when-a-cerna-s38sh4/),
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, carries that practice from the core tray into the
literature on the molecular mechanisms of cancer.

The paper takes a single, much-used verb, the claim that one RNA molecule
"sponges" another, and distinguishes three readings of it, from a modest claim
about binding to a strong claim about how a cell comes to behave as it does. It
then reads the field's network diagrams, which draw every connection with the
same line, against that distinction, and proposes a light-touch annotation
that would let each line declare the kind of evidence it rests on.

> A diagram is a promise about what was found. I wanted to know how much each
> line in these figures was promising, and I think the answer will be useful
> well beyond the figures themselves.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The publication extends the School's growing contribution to the philosophy of
the experimental sciences, and reflects the University's broader confidence that
careful attention to the shape of a claim is itself a research capability worth
building on.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.s38sh4.

Three years and five months: that is the median stretch a standing booking
keeps its place on a council community hall's calendar after the group behind
it last turned up.

_Still on the Calendar_, deposited today through the Office of Research
Outputs, puts a start date and an end date on 1,146 such bookings across 38
halls. Councils report facility utilisation as the share of available hours
booked, and a hall that drifts below the threshold enters a rationalisation
review. Researchers in the School of Continuous Improvement asked a narrower
question of that share: how much of it is attendance, and how much of it is
paper.

> The calendar was never asked to know whether anyone came, and it has been
> answering as though it did. We would like the sector to sit with the
> difference for a while before it acts on either number.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence
> Observatory, School of Continuous Improvement

Five years of calendars, key sign-out logs and hirers' own records were paired
so that each booking could be timed from a group's last verified attendance,
with a booking swept away in a periodic calendar rebuild treated as a separate
outcome from one a hirer gave back. Releases turn out to cluster in the month a
hall invoices, and in no other.

"What we have is a description, and it is a description several committees
already half hold," said Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of
Demo Quarter. "Setting it down is the part we can offer."

The University regards the work as a modest contribution to the way public
assets are described to the people who pay for them, and welcomes the questions
it raises about the cadence of review.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qd1qd6.

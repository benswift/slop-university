Senior Lecturer Renke Sabel's team spent the better part of two years doing
something no home-services marketplace had thought to do on its own: checking
whether the completion photo a worker uploads before payment actually
predicts a job got done. The School of Continuous Improvement's Impact Report
2021–2026, published today by the Office of Research Outputs, sets out five
years of that checking, and what happened once the live video check-ins it
recommended started catching what the photograph missed.

The photo, the report finds, tracked a service category's ordinary
completion rate about as well as no photo at all would have. Live check-in
review, introduced across five categories from 2023, closed some of that gap
and opened a new one: across the platforms studied, a script for passing a
live check-in convincingly reached a worker's own group chat in a median of
eleven days, a regularity the School now reads as evidence that a check and
the workaround to it arrive on much the same schedule.

> We had built an entire appeals process around a photograph. Nobody had
> built anything around the job.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Renke Sabel, Convenor, Indicator Commons

The University reads the finding as consistent with the audit brief the
School set itself at founding: recommend an indicator, then keep checking it
after somebody else adopts it. "Two weeks isn't long enough to stop a script
from circulating," said Research Fellow Osei Vandermeer, whose Review
Cadence Observatory built the early-warning model behind the report's
forecasting work. "It is long enough to stop being surprised by it."

The full report is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ub5opu.

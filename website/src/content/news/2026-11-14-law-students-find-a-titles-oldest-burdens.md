First-year land law students at Slop University spent a semester reading certificates of title the way software engineers read code, and the oldest burdens on those titles came into view. [_Diffing the Register_](/outputs/slop-poster-diffing-the-register-tp6qc4/), a new poster led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/) and [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), sets out what the tutorial room learned.

> A title is a long conversation, and most of it happened before any of us arrived. These students found a way to sit down at the end of it and hear the whole thing.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The study placed three reading environments side by side across a cohort of 186 students: the familiar stack of paper certificates, a view showing only what changed from one certificate to the next, and a view in which every line of the current title is annotated with its origin. Working through synthetic chains of earlier certificates, students using the annotated view found 85% of the burdens registered seven or more dealings back, against 58% with paper. The change-only view was the quickest to work through, and the most likely to pass over a covenant that had simply never moved.

The work extends the School's growing attention to the obligations that travel with land, and brings that attention into the teaching it does every week.

"The software was always the smaller part of this," said Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard. "The School of Settled Matters has been a generous host to it, and the students more generous still."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tp6qc4.

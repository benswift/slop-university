Every packet returned to a Slop University-affiliated seed library now
passes through a four-step credentialing process before it goes back on
the shelf. A new paper from the School of Continuous Improvement reports
the process's first full season: 2,412 packets, forty-six garden clubs,
and a five-kind reliability grade for every one of them.

Dr Bram Ntuli, with Associate Professor Casimir Beng and Dr Joost Nwosu,
built the grading scheme from a germination trial, a label check against
a photo-voucher record, and a "segregation index" that catches a hybrid
variety's seed being saved and handed back as if it would grow true. Two
independent volunteer graders assess every packet, with the team reporting
substantial agreement between them.

> A commons only works if the thing coming back is what it says it is, and
> until now nobody had actually checked.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The paper's more delicate finding concerns what happened once clubs could
see each other's grading results: pass rates rose across the network
within a single season, while the packets themselves, by the team's own
measure, did not improve to match. "A number everybody can compare is a
number everybody eventually manages," said Associate Professor Beng, the
Adaptive Metrics Lab's lead. The School frames the season as the first
confirmation that its grading instincts apply as well off campus as on it.
The Lab has adopted the five-kind schema as the mandatory standard for
every affiliated club's return table regardless, retiring a packet
outright if it fails to clear the top grade twice.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.obpxwk.

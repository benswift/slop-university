Nine people have written on the vials in one laboratory's sample cabinet, and a
new poster from the School of Underlying Conditions reports how well they can
read one another. _What a Vial Label Says: Naming and re-finding samples of
spent reforming catalyst in a shared drawer_ is the work of Associate Professor
Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and Dr Brynjar Obiora,
Senior Research Fellow.

> I have spent a career among trays of things that somebody else put away. It
> was restful to stand in front of a drawer that was not mine to explain, and
> to be handed a marker at the end of it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The poster couples transcription, coding and timed retrieval across a single
cabinet of 412 vials, bringing an information-practice lens to a
materials-handling setting. Asked to find twelve described samples in three
minutes each, the people who had labelled them succeeded 87 per cent of the
time; colleagues looking for the same vials succeeded 41 per cent of the time.
A notebook page written on the label helped everybody, and the word "final"
helped its author.

The University counts a well-kept drawer among its shared research assets, and
takes some quiet satisfaction in a School that looked inside one. Dr Obiora
said the cabinet had "kept its own counsel for four years, and was courteous
about being asked".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pm30uo.

Where, exactly, is a hallucination kept? For most of the figures in a new paper from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), the answer is wherever the eyes happen to be. For a notable minority, it is on the floor, the sill or the bench, at least for a few seconds.

[_Where the Figures Stand_](/outputs/slop-paper-where-the-figures-rqxnju/) is by [Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/), Doctoral Candidate, with [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, and [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer, all of the School of Emergent Priorities.

> Our School has always asked where an organisation keeps the things it has stopped looking at. It has been a genuine education to carry that question into a darkened room and find it still had somewhere to go.
>
> --- Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The study brings together eye-movement instrumentation, participant-led reporting and a carefully staged testing environment to establish a shared frame for one of perception's less visited questions. Eleven participants with central vision loss reported, by touch, where the figures they see appeared to be, both before and after a series of guided glances across a wall screen. Figures described as standing on something stayed put in the room far more often than figures resting on nothing, though most were carried back to the eye within moments.

The paper sits comfortably within the School's widening practice of following its attention into rooms it had not expected to enter. Dr Chikere described the work as "a reminder that a careful method will take you wherever you are prepared to sit still for long enough".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rqxnju.

The School of Continuous Improvement's newest display advertisement makes one
claim about the University's grounds, and makes it in large type: every leaf on
campus has been assayed. The portrait ad, led by Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, joins
the signage rotation this week in daylight and evening versions.

> We think of the grounds as a cohort that never graduates. Each season sends
> us a new intake of leaves, and each intake is met with the same care as the
> last.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

The campaign sits within the School's wider evaluation-ecosystem agenda. It
mobilises a Capability Sprints cohort across the hedges, verges and planter
boxes between the buildings, moving each sample from secateurs to envelope to
extract to screen, and anchors the resulting record in the Indicator Commons,
the University's shared indicator library. The illustration at its centre, a
researcher clipping single leaves into paper envelopes above a rack of graded
extracts, was chosen to carry that workflow at a glance from across a
footpath.

The ad's small print points readers to three of the University's
studies of leaves under assay: a pharmacy screening practical, a sampling design
for dried leaf lots, and a bioassay-guided fractionation of a shade-house leaf
extract.

Dr Nwachukwu described the ad as an invitation rather than a summary. "Anyone
who walks past a planter box on their way to a lecture is already part of the
screening programme in a small way," she said. "We would simply like them to
know it."

The University is pleased to see a quiet corner of its research life given room
on the panels this season. The full advertisement is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5da7jt.

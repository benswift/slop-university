Submissions to the Department of Policy Settings' latest consultation may be
made in any form, including, for the first time, as a dialogue. That is fitting,
because the dialogue is what the consultation is about.

[_The Statement of Finding and Outputs in Dialogue Form_](/outputs/slop-consult-the-dialogue-form-setting-kq153g/)
takes up a question raised with the Department by researchers in two schools:
how a form in which every proposition belongs to a speaker should meet a
Guideline asking each output to record what its authors themselves hold. The
paper sets out how the Office of Research Outputs has handled such work to date,
draws on four of the University's own outputs, and lays out three options for
the setting's future, beginning with leaving it as it is. The Department has
formed no view between them.

> We are grateful to the researchers who wrote to us, and to the Office, which
> has been answering their question one lodgement at a time. A consultation lets
> the Department put that question once, to everyone, and listen to all the
> answers together.
>
> --- Director, Consultation Branch, Consultation and Uptake Division

[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) welcomed the paper. "Most
rules are read by someone who was not in the room when they were made," he
said. "It is good to see the Department asking the room first."

The consultation reflects the Department's steady attention to the settings
under which the University's research is written down, lodged and shared.

The full consultation paper is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kq153g.

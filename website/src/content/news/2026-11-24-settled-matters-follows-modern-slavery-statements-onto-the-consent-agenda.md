Thirty-two of the forty-one company boards studied in [_Taken as Read_](/outputs/slop-poster-taken-as-read-lrmm3w/) now approve their annual modern slavery statement as a consent item, without discussion. The research poster, by Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Dr [Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/) and Ms [Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and the School of Emergent Priorities, follows that approval through six annual cycles and asks what the statement itself does once nobody is arguing over it.

> Every company secretary who opened a cabinet for us knew their own minutes might end up in the chart. We hope the poster repays that trust, and we would be glad to hear from any board that would like to see its last six years laid side by side.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

At the heart of the poster is a careful pairing of two records that rarely meet: the boardroom's account of an approval and the public text that approval releases. Setting agenda papers and minutes beside successive published statements, the team traces how a recurring governance obligation settles into routine, and how disclosure practice moves with it.

Dr Vasseur, whose research concerns how open questions become committee business, described the consent agenda as "the most carefully drafted page in any board pack, and the one most often turned past."

The poster sits squarely within the Received Readings Group's long attention to the moment a question stops being asked, and the University is pleased to see that attention extended from the law reports to the boardroom.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lrmm3w.

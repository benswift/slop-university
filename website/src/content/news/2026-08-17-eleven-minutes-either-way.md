The instruction is the same at every trestle table in the country: chill the
esky the night before. _Overnight Pre-Chilling Does Not Extend Cold-Hold in
Portable Eskies_, a paper released today by the School of Continuous
Improvement, is the first attempt to check it where the eskies actually stand.

> We set out to settle something everybody already knew, and came back with a
> much smaller number than anyone expected. The School has spent a while since
> asking itself why that was the surprising part.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

Over a single summer, 214 eskies at sports-club sidelines, weekend markets,
working bees and hall functions carried a logger through set-up and pack-down,
building an evidentiary base of 1,964 esky-hours. Containers chilled overnight
were matched against those packed straight from the shed on the conditions that
govern a summer morning: heat, shade, ice load, and how often the lid comes up.
The matched difference in cold-hold came to eleven minutes, inside the margin of
indifference the team lodged before data access. Ice mass and lid discipline
carried the rest.

The Indicator Commons has since ratified a Cold-Hold Set-Up Declaration for
volunteer-run events, under which the pre-chill step is recorded and attested at
set-up. "A practice that turns out to be worth measuring is worth carrying
forward on the record," said Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of
the Master of Applied Measurement. The University regards the work as a mature
contribution to the everyday settings its measurement agenda is built to reach.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ubvdcc.

The School of Underlying Conditions keeps an unusual kind of field record.
Beside everything its drills bring back, it writes down what they failed to
reach, and a new paper shows that the second column can be the more
informative one.
[_Refusal at the Bed_](/outputs/slop-paper-refusal-at-the-xqdpgb/) is by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, with [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/),
Senior Lecturer, both of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> A borehole that does not arrive is usually written up as an apology. Our School has never been very good at apologising for missing intervals, and on this glacier that habit served us well.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

The study pairs a season of hot-water drilling on the lower trunk of a High
Arctic tidewater glacier with a flow model that reads the glacier's surface
motion for the friction at its base, keeping the two lines of evidence apart
until the end. Of fifteen holes, four stopped in dark, gravel-laden ice
some metres above the bed. When the model's picture was finally laid over the
drilling log, all four sat in the three patches where the glacier grips its
bed hardest, patches that cover a small part of the reach and carry a large
share of its drag.

"The glacier has handed us a good many more questions than we took up there,
and we are in no hurry to give any of them back," said Dr Emenike.

The paper reflects the University's steady commitment to reading the whole of
the record, including the parts that stopped short.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xqdpgb.

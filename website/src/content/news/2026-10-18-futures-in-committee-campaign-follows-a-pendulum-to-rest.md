A plumb bob on a long cord, three magnets on survey pegs and a paddock divided
into interlocking basins are now turning on the University's signage panels, in
a landscape campaign announcing a session of Futures in Committee later this
semester.

The session, _Where One Basin Ends_, will be given by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior
Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions. Its starting point is a
familiar demonstration from the study of dynamical systems: a pendulum released
above three magnets comes to rest over one of them, and released a hair's
breadth away may come to rest over another. Dr Obiora, whose career began at the
bottom of a soil pit, brings to it a surveyor's question about where one region
should be said to end and its neighbour begin.

> I have drawn a great many lines between two auger holes and defended most of
> them. I am looking forward to an evening of being asked, kindly, how I knew
> where to stop.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

In one image and fewer than fifty words, the poster carries nonlinear dynamics
from the seminar room to the kerbside, aligning the School's field-based
practice with a public-facing account of how outcomes take shape. The University
counts the campaign among the steadier signs that its seminar culture has
outgrown the room it meets in.

"Futures in Committee has always welcomed a speaker who arrives with a question
the series has not yet thought to ask," said Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research
Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee. "Details of the evening will
follow."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2bh6uh.

Slop University has set out the terms on which its schools will keep choosing
one another. _Convergence Without Compulsion_, the University's Strategic Plan
2026-2031, was released today by the School of Emergent Priorities and
describes an institution that intends to coordinate more and command less as it
continues to grow by adding schools.

The Plan borrows its grammar from the long European experience of integrating
members of unequal size and capacity, and adapts it to the scale of a
university. Four priorities carry the argument: decisions held as close to the
work as possible, a standing commitment to narrowing the distance between the
most and least established schools, an annual cycle of shared guidelines and
reciprocal peer review, and the freedom for schools to proceed at different
speeds within a common direction.

> The arrangements in this Plan were drafted to be revisited. I would count it a good sign if every school finds something in it to disagree with by the first review, and says so in the place the Plan has set aside for saying it.
>
> --- Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The University considers the Plan a considered step in its maturing as a union
of distinct schools, and an account of governance it expects to be held to.
Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies,
described the cohesion arrangements as "less a transfer than a shared wager on
the newest member of the family, placed early enough for it to matter."

The Plan's indicators favour the direction of travel over any single
destination, and progress will be reported to Council each year in one
Convergence Report.

The full strategic plan is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n875rz.

A boundary on a map is a decision before it is a finding, and the School of
Underlying Conditions has spent its short life saying so about soil. Its newest
signage campaign, _Somebody Drew That Line_, prepared by Dr Brynjar Obiora,
Senior Research Fellow, now says it about the motor cortex.

The landscape poster shows a volunteer at rest in a reclining chair, a cap of
evenly spaced points across the scalp and a figure-of-eight coil held to one of
them, with a small patch of responsive points picked out in gold. Beneath the
image, the campaign directs readers to _The Width of the Line_, the University's
study of how one laboratory's boundaries were traced across an archive of
stained sections, alongside two further published works.

> A surveyor walks the ground and a stimulation study walks the scalp, and in
> both cases the edge arrives last. I would be pleased if a few people at the
> bus stop wondered, for a moment, whose hand it was.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign extends the School's place-based mapping agenda
into an adjacent interpretive landscape, aligning field-survey practice with
the University's broader capability in boundary stewardship and translating a
shared methodological posture for a public audience.

The University regards the poster as an early sign of a theme that is beginning
to travel between its schools on its own, without any instruction from the
centre. Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of Continuous Improvement and
a co-author of the featured study, welcomed "a neighbour who has been asking our
question for longer than we have."

The poster is available from the University's research repository under an open
licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x4xfax.

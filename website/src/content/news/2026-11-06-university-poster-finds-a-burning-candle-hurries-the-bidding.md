Over twelve laboratory sessions, 144 volunteers bid against one another in
short online auctions for lots that were worth something only inside the room.
Some of their auctions closed on the stroke of two minutes. Others closed at an
undisclosed moment in the final half-minute, and for half of those bidders the
undisclosed moment was drawn on screen as a candle burning down.

[_By Inch of Candle_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-laboratory-candle-avkdb8/), a new
research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), reports
what followed. Bidders who watched the candle placed their final bids markedly
earlier than bidders told the same closing rule in words, and the gap, though it
narrowed as the sessions went on, was still there at the end. Prices and the
allocation of lots held steady throughout.

The poster is led by [Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), Postdoctoral
Fellow, with [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and
Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, and
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement
Grand Rounds.

> We set out to test a rule and found ourselves testing a picture. The candle
> has earned a permanent place on the laboratory's screens, and I expect we will
> keep finding new things for it to do.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

The study brings a controlled experimental design to the interface between
market rules and the people who act under them, holding the underlying hazard
constant while varying only how it reaches the bidder. It joins a widening seam
of School work on how a rule's presentation shapes its uptake.

"It was a privilege to watch so many careful people make up their minds," said
Dr Hanke.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.avkdb8.

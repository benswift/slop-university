> The people who live on this ground have been reading it for a lot longer than
> the School has. We owe them a seat at the table, and a great deal of thanks for
> the questions they have already brought us.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

With that acknowledgement, the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has released
[_Unlikely, Not Impossible_](/outputs/slop-brochure-unlikely-not-impossible-x3vu5h/),
a civic brochure inviting the University's neighbours to join its researchers in
weighing the rare and slow things the ground beneath the district might one day
do. It is authored by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) and
[Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Horizon Register.

The brochure brings the School's groundwater fieldwork, the Basement Relations
Group's laboratory work on recovered core and the public sessions of Futures in
Committee into a single civic offer. It positions patient, shared attention as
the foundation of the University's relationship with the ground it stands on,
and grounds that offer in six published studies, from staged sediment basins to
a district's legend of an underground river.

The University counts the brochure among its most neighbourly publications to
date, and a natural companion to the Horizon Register's ongoing work on risks
not yet named. Dr Okoro welcomed the conversations it is likely to start,
describing the far edge of the register as "the place where a district keeps
its most careful questions, and where we would most like company."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x3vu5h.

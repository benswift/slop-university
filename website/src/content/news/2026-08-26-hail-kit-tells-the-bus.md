The wooden hail flag at a rural request stop is still there. The warrant to
stop is not. A research poster published today by the School of Continuous
Improvement reports the build and first season of a magnetic hail kit that
volunteer coordinators fitted to 87 flags across four rural routes, so an
approaching bus now takes its stop from a cab light rather than from the flag
itself.

Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, led the
work with Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard, and
Dr Solveig Adeyemi. Over eleven months the kit delivered a cab-light
warrant on 94.1 per cent of flag-up events before the bus reached the stop
(median 11.4 seconds, n = 6,680). The depot's published missed-stop register
now reads from the packet log. Drivers no longer log a visual hail.

> Once a kit is fitted, the question is no longer whether the flag was up. The
> question is whether the packet arrived.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The work sits in the School's habit of taking a warrant seriously enough to
build one. Volunteer coordinators clamped a reed switch to each flag hinge
and attested the fitting; each flip event is routed through a roadside hop to
a cab-light subscription the approaching bus already carries. Thirteen stops
sit in gullies beyond the kit's hop budget, and those are the stops the
architecture cannot warrant. "A clean feed is easy to table," said Dr
Osayande. "What the School keeps learning is how quickly the feed becomes the
procedure."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.z4ados.

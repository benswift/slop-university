Visitors to the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) this spring will find two things on one bench that rarely share a room. [_Oxidation, from Bedrock to Bloodstream_](/outputs/slop-ad-oxidation-from-bedrock-19v3nn/), the School's new campaign for the University's portrait signage panels, invites the public to an Open Day hosted by the Basement Relations Group, where a length of drill core and a slide of white blood cells are offered as chapters of a single story.

> We spend most of the year reading the ground from the top of a pipe. On Open Day the questions arrive from the other side of the bench, and the School is always the better for the ones it cannot answer on the spot.
>
> --- Dr [Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster's image is a laboratory bench seen from below: a split core laid in its tray, its weathered end banded in rust-gold, a microscope beside it, and above them a magnified circle in which one cell gives off a small burst of light. The campaign positions oxidation as a connective thread between the School's core-store practice and the life sciences more broadly, enabling visitors to carry one idea from deep time into their own bodies and back again. Its small print points readers on to related work already in the University's published record.

The campaign extends the University's long habit of bringing the public to the bench, and of trusting them with what they find there.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.19v3nn.

Rabies control is usually told as a story of field teams and clinics. The
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) would like it also to be
read as a story of paper. A new portrait panel, now in the University's signage
rotation, places [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior
Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, at the counter of an animal
registry, a bound ledger open in front of her and a dog waiting patiently on its
lead. The panel's line is five words long: "Every bite leaves a record."

The campaign extends the Received Readings Group's standing work on how a rule
comes to be treated as closed into the quarantine orders, local by-laws and
bite registers through which a community's response to rabies is written down,
applied and, over time, no longer argued over. It positions the School's
close-reading capability at the meeting point of public-health administration
and local law-making, and invites the panel's readers to meet the researchers
behind it.

> Making the panel, we noticed how much of the School's own work already
> happens at counters like that one. Someone brings a question in, and a
> register decides whether it has been asked before. It was good to see
> ourselves from the queue for a change.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group,
> School of Settled Matters

The University counts the panel among its more patient contributions to public
legibility. Its small print directs readers to three published outputs:
[_Awaiting a Ruling_](/outputs/slop-paper-awaiting-a-ruling-jekrqh/),
[_The Long Memory_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-long-memory-m0rlhr/) and
[_Settled Without Being Believed_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-economic-theory-model-o5lkew/).

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.e1wvrp.

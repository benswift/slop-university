Switch a heater off, wait, and listen. Repeated one hundred and twenty times in
a channel of liquid helium a little above absolute zero, that patient routine
sits at the heart of
[_Same Density, Different Futures_](/outputs/slop-poster-whether-a-decaying-3xg1qc/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The poster is led by [Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/), Director
of the Trajectory Analytics Group, with
[Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral Fellow, and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> Our Group has spent a long time asking what a commitment leaves behind once
> the conditions for it have gone. It is a pleasure to carry that question
> somewhere very cold, and to have it answered so courteously.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group

The study brings a low-temperature acoustic capability to bear on a tangle of
quantised vortices, holding steady the heat that sustains it while varying only
how long that support runs. Tangles that arrive at the same density turn out to
settle on different timetables, and the imprint of a longer drive persists until
roughly the minute mark, beyond which further effort leaves no trace. Dr Obiora,
who brought his School's habits of careful description to the channel, called
the collaboration "a fine reason to spend the winter indoors."

The poster extends the University's cross-school portfolio into a new
temperature range, and the School of Emergent Priorities expects to keep it in
view.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3xg1qc.

At six in the evening most office buildings stop heating the people still in
them, unless somebody presses a button. In
[_Extend What, Until When, for Whom?_](/outputs/slop-paper-drafting-the-after-hours-iooyhb/),
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and
[Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) ask what that button says to the
person pressing it, and what follows from the answer.

Across twelve control zones and sixteen weeks, the team brought
building-management telemetry, occupancy sensing and interviews held at the
panels themselves into a single account of the after-hours threshold,
positioning the override label as a point where drafting practice and energy
stewardship meet. Three redrafted wordings each settled a different ambiguity
in the original, and each left its own mark on the building's evenings.

> The people who stay late in a building are a generous readership. They have
> been construing these panels for years with no help from anyone, and the
> School is grateful to have spent a season reading alongside them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Dr Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard, said the School of
Continuous Improvement had "learned a good deal about which of its own walls it
had stopped looking at".

The University is pleased to see two of its Schools find, on a small panel
beside a lift, a text worth drafting with care, and regards the collaboration
as a fitting meeting of its interests in the written word and in the buildings
it is read in.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.iooyhb.

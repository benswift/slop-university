The School of Continuous Improvement has long attended to the
order in which institutions look at things, and its newest paper carries that
interest to the microscope stage.
[_The Slide Before_](/outputs/slop-paper-order-effects-in-4vid3d/) is by
[Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), Lecturer and Convenor of the
Living Dashboard, and [Dr Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/), Lecturer
and Deputy Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory, both in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), with
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> None of this would exist without the colleagues who sat with a box of slides
> twice over and graded every one as carefully the second time as the first.
> The paper is really a record of their patience.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

The team brought three trained readers back to the 96 archived sections of a
concluded liver study, pairing the original arm-by-arm sequence with a shuffled
one and setting both against a point-counted reference. The approach aligns the
reading room's established masking practice with a closer attention to sequence,
and finds that each grade leans away from the slide before it: read in blocks,
the injury window narrowed from 2.2 grades to 1.7, while agreement between
readers stayed high throughout.

The University considers the paper a welcome addition to the quieter
infrastructure of its laboratory sciences, and a further step in the two
schools' shared work on archives.

"An archive keeps the slides in the order they were cut," said Associate
Professor Uzoma. "It was a pleasure to take them out of it."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4vid3d.

> A priority is only ever as good as the time set aside for it. It has been a pleasure to watch colleagues in another School find that question waiting for them overnight, and to be asked along.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director, Master of Priority Studies

The remark introduces
[_Which Traces Get Serviced_](/outputs/slop-poster-replay-as-maintenance-8a28br/),
a research poster published today by the School of Underlying Conditions, which
brings the planning methods of operations research to the question of which
memories a resting brain keeps in good repair.

The poster is the work of
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group,
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/) of the School of
Emergent Priorities, and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior
Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions.

Bringing the scheduling toolkit of industrial upkeep into conversation with
sleep research, the team treated each remembered route through a laboratory maze
as an asset in need of service, and set the resulting plan alongside the replay
activity recorded in a cohort of resting animals. The plan anticipated where the
animals' rest was spent with notable fidelity. Where it fell short, the team
found the animals returning to routes whose circumstances had just changed, less
to repair them than to check on them.

For Associate Professor Uzoma, the result "puts a quiet night's rest on the same
footing as a pump that wants looking at", a comparison the Basement Relations
Group intends to keep close at hand.

The poster takes its place in the University's growing habit of lending one
discipline's instruments to another discipline's questions.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8a28br.

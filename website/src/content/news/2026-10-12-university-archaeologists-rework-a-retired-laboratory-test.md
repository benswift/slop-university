Slop University's field methods have been taken indoors. In _Thirty-One Steps,
Seventy-Four Gestures: An Experimental Reconstruction of a Retired Haemolytic
Plaque Assay and the Waste It Leaves_, Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research
Fellow, and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead,
Basement Relations Group, treat a laboratory procedure as archaeologists treat a
stone tool: by making it again and keeping what falls on the floor.

The paper anchors a replication-to-refuse continuum in a single plywood case
acquired by the School of Underlying Conditions with its contents undisturbed.
The two authors worked the kit twelve times each, with inert stand-ins for every
reagent, and a retired technician who had once used it attended a single session
of each. Seventy-four gestures were recorded against a written protocol of
thirty-one steps, and the glass a run discards was logged by where it came to
rest.

> It is not often that a school of earth sciences is trusted with somebody
> else's glassware. We broke rather less of it as the weeks went on, and the
> paper is in some sense an account of that.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The University sees in the work a school willing to bring its instruments to
bear on a room it had previously only walked past. Associate Professor Uzoma
said the kit "has gone back in its case, and the case has gone back on the
shelf, which is the outcome we would wish for most things we study".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.eytlha.

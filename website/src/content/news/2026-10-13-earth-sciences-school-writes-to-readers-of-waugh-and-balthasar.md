The University's friends and supporters include a number of people who own more
novels than rock hammers, and the School of Underlying Conditions has now
written to them directly. _A Handful of Dust, Properly Examined: Come and see_
is a booklet prepared by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, with Associate
Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations
Group, and Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow.

> Nobody on the bench was asked to become a literary critic, and nobody did. We
> set out what we do with a specimen and left the reader to recognise it, which
> is roughly how we were taught ourselves.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The booklet aligns three strands of the School's capability with habits its
intended reader already has. It moves from the patient looking practised at the
polarising microscope, through the sedimentology bench's full attention to very
small material, to the Basement Relations Group's standing interest in what a
structure rests on, and it draws together seven of the University's published
outputs as evidence that the two kinds of attention have been keeping company
for some time.

The School regards the booklet as a considered step in widening the circle of
people who feel the earth sciences are, in some sense, addressed to them.
Associate Professor Uzoma said the Group had "always assumed the conversation
would start at the foundations, and is glad to find it has."

The full booklet is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4g06zn.

When an employer rewrites what it offers its people, the new offer does not reach every job advertisement on the same day. Some adverts, it turns out, never quite hear about it.

That is the finding of [_Still on the Template_](/outputs/slop-poster-superseded-employer-value-ccora8/), a new research poster by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/) and [Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). Withdrawn clauses lasted a median of four months in templates kept by the recruitment team, and seventeen in templates that hiring managers had cloned for themselves.

> We owe a great deal to the hiring managers who opened their template folders for us, several of them for the first time in a while. Every one of them was generous about it, and a few were quietly surprised by what was inside.
>
> --- Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The team traced each superseded clause across an applicant tracking environment of 186 templates, mapping template lineage against clause persistence and aligning both with a linked survey of 412 new hires' early-tenure expectations.

For a University that renews its own value proposition on a regular cycle, the poster arrives as timely and practical reading. "There will be another relaunch somewhere soon, there always is," said Dr Tredinnick. "We would like to be standing near the templates when it happens."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ccora8.

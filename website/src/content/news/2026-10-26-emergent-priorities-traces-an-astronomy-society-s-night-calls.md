The School of Emergent Priorities' doctoral programme has added a new single-authored paper to the University's research repository, and with it a fresh vantage on how amateur science organises its nights.

[_Not After One, Except for a Nova_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-social-history-abkefj/) is by [Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities. It reconstructs the telephone chain a provincial amateur astronomical society kept from 1958 to 1991, by which a member who suspected a new star or comet rang the next name on a typed list, who went outside to look and rang the one after. Across 1,603 calls on 214 alerts, the paper finds that the people most often credited with confirming a sighting were not the society's strongest observers but those already awake for work.

> The notebooks are full of people being considerate to one another at two in the morning. I hope the paper lets a few more of them be counted.
>
> --- Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Situating a community's calling list, its night book and its newsletter within the wider social history of discovery, the paper connects household routines, shift work and the etiquette of the telephone to the question of who gets to have seen something first.

The University is pleased to watch a candidate's first paper find its place in the repository beside the work of the School's senior staff, and to see the School's attention to the forms people fill in extend to the forms they fill in by night.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.abkefj.

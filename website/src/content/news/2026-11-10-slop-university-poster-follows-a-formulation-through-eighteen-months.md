A new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) follows three versions of one experimental formulation through a year and a half in storage, and reports that each kept its initial burst of dissolved drug while losing some of the time it could hold it there. How much it lost depended on how much drug the formulation carried.

[_Taken at Month Eighteen_](/outputs/slop-poster-supersaturation-after-storage-cdosv6/) is led by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), with [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the School of Underlying Conditions and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/).

> There is a particular kind of attention that only comes from waiting for something. Eighteen months gave us a great deal of it, and I think the poster is better for every one of them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Behind the poster sits a carefully staged programme of storage and testing that holds formulation design, stability practice and dissolution performance in one longitudinal view. Dispersions at three drug loadings were set down under two storage conditions and brought back at intervals, each time asked to perform as they had on the day they were made. At the highest loading under the warmer, wetter condition, the time spent well above solubility fell from 121 minutes to 14; at the lowest loading it still managed 139.

Dr Emenike described the collaboration as "one more step in a conversation the University is only beginning to have with the things it keeps on its shelves".

The poster takes its place in the University's continuing investment in research that keeps faith with its subject over the long term, and in the patient, cross-School partnerships that make such work possible.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cdosv6.

A red button in an escape-room corridor logs something its venue never
checks: who pressed it. A new Slop University research poster from the
School of Emergent Priorities cross-references the resulting difficulty
rating against independent player surveys, and finds the number tracks a
shift roster more reliably than it tracks a room.

For the School, the finding sits comfortably inside its wider brief:
understanding what a forward-looking number is actually anticipating, and
for whom.

> We built the Strategic Drift Survey to measure the distance between what
> an institution says and what it does. This is the same gap, just
> smaller, louder, and mounted at head height beside a door.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Runa Adegoke, Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey

Co-author Marek Solheim added only that "a rating this stable was never
going to be about the room" --- a line the team is content to let stand as
the poster's other headline.

The team logged 286 sessions across four rooms and eleven rostered staff
over seven months, pairing each hint-button record with an independently
collected player survey. The comparison surfaces a familiar shape for the
School: a measure built for one purpose migrating quietly into another,
without anyone deciding it should.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.za2ebz.

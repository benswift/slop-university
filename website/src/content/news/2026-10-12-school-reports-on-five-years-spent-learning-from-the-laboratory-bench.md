An impact report ordinarily records what a school gave to its collaborators. The one released today by the School of Continuous Improvement is, by its Director's account, largely a record of what the School took. _Running a Blank_, the School's Impact Report 2021--2026, was prepared by Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, with Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, and Dr Marit Osayande, Convenor of the Living Dashboard.

The report reflects on a period in which the School's researchers and PhD candidates worked in residence with analytical laboratories and the operators of sensor networks, and returned with three practices: the blank, in which a method is run with nothing in it; the reporting floor, beneath which a result is given only as less than a stated limit; and the check standard, a known reference measured at intervals. Each has since been carried into the School's own indicator practice, and the report sets out how.

> I spent the first part of my working life being handed instruments and told they were fine. It has been a quiet satisfaction to work in a School that has taken up the habit of asking them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

The University regards the report as a considered account of a school willing to be instructed by its neighbours. It describes a week in which the Living Dashboard was left to run with teaching suspended, the nine indicators the School now reports as below reporting limit, and a decision on recalibration that the School reversed and says so.

"The wall has had a good five years, and so have the people who look after it," said Dr Osayande. "I would not separate the two."

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.p3tzvg.

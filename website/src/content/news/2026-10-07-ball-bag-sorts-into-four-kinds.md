Every ball in a club bag now has a kind, and the School of Emergent Priorities has written the kinds down.

_Four kinds of ball fill a club ball bag, and only one plays_, a poster released today by Mr Halvard Nnamani with Hedvig Anyanwu and Tove Ilesanmi, sorts the training bags of 38 junior soccer teams into Playable, Flat, Foreign and Lent. Each bag was emptied onto the touchline at three counts through 2026, and every ball was pressure-tested and placed by two assessors working independently.

> A bag tells you what a club hopes for. We were glad to be asked what it holds.
>
> --- Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The league committee has adopted the classification as the order of its weekend pitch list. A team's Saturday slot follows the Playable share of its bag at the gate inspection, and a team whose bag falls short of the floor takes the Sunday reserve list until two consecutive inspections pass. Playable balls rose from about a third of bag contents at the first count to four in five at the third. Flat balls left the bags but not the club: the poster records 247 of them in coaches' car boots.

Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, said the boot census "was the part of the count that kept us honest about where a club keeps its hopes." Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, welcomed the committee's decision to publish the register of bags by kind.

The poster extends the School's continuing attention to what an everyday object is asked to do on behalf of the people who carry it. The floor rises next season, and a fifth kind is expected to join the taxonomy at the fourth count.

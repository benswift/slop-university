The School of Continuous Improvement's Evaluation of Evaluation program has closed its annual retest of an earlier University finding, and for the first time in its four-year run, the finding didn't hold.

The original result, published last year, found that a share house's pinned group-chat rule keeps governing the household long after nobody remembers pinning it, resistant even to a direct nudge asking residents to update the wording. This year's retest applied the identical design to an apartment block's body-corporate group chat. There, the pinned rule didn't survive: residents argued over it, amended it, and re-pinned it within weeks whenever it drifted out of date.

The School reads the difference as informative in its own right, not as a reason to withdraw the original claim. Rather than narrow where the finding applies, the Adaptive Metrics Lab's Community Indicators Service is widening its published Pin Review Register --- a quarterly audit of partner buildings' pinned rules against resident behaviour --- to every body corporate it already works with, independent of whether the original effect held for that particular building.

> A result that only travels quietly is a result we haven't tested hard enough. The Register doesn't wait for the next street to prove us wrong; it goes looking.
>
> --- Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, welcomed the result as confirmation that the School's short courses for community committees "are teaching the right kind of scepticism."

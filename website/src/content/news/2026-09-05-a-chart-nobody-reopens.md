A chart taped to a bus door on the first morning of term is not built to last a year. Slop University's newest signage campaign is betting that it will.

_Seated Once, Standing Still_ joins the University's portrait display rotation this week, rendered for daylight and after-dark screens alike. The artwork holds a school-run kerb in the minutes before the first bell: a queue of cars idling behind an idling bus, and Dr Solveig Adeyemi at the open door, reading a laminated seating chart nobody has updated since the second week of term.

Dr Adeyemi's research usually tracks what happens to a report after someone files it — which details survive an escalation, and which quietly drop before a decision-maker ever sees them. The seating chart is the case where nothing gets filed at all: a decision made once, on the first morning of term, that has had every chance to be reopened and has not taken one.

> Every school keeps one decision it stopped examining the moment it worked. Ours happens to be laminated.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

"A chart is a measurement instrument the moment somebody laminates it," said Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "We just don't usually admit that in the advertising."

The University offers the placement as a straightforward account of what a School of Continuous Improvement Tuesday actually looks like. The campaign compresses that agenda into signage: one image, one line, and a citation trail back to the School's registered work on reports that never make it onto any record at all.

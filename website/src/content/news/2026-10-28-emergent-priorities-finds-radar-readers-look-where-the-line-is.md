The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has spent several years asking what an organisation has settled before it starts to look. Its newest paper carries that question below ground.

[_Found Where It Was Drawn_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-signal-detection-experiment-zgp2ve/) is by [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/), Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group in the School of Underlying Conditions, and [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds in the School of Continuous Improvement.

> None of this would exist without the locators who gave us their afternoons and their patience, and who scrolled through a great many scans that were never going to be dug. We owe the paper to them more than to any of its numbers.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The study brings experimental psychology's signal-detection toolkit into dialogue with the interpretive practice of subsurface survey, positioning the drawn record as a site where expectation, attention and professional judgement converge. Across 64 participants and 180 simulated scans, it finds that a line drawn over the radar image makes readers readier to report a service beneath it, and less thorough everywhere else.

Dr Ntuli said the findings had "given Grand Rounds a good deal to sit with, which is the most a School can ask of a colleague's paper".

The paper sits among the University's most deliberate crossings between schools to date, uniting three agendas around a single image on a single screen.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zgp2ve.

A paper tag comes off a gym's exercise machine the moment a facilities
ticket says the machine is fixed. A new Slop University research poster
from the School of Continuous Improvement asks how often that's still
true once the person pulling the tag is paid to pull it fast.

Researchers cross-matched the ticket log of a multi-branch gym chain
against independent, ticket-blind walk-through audits carried out across
six months before and six months after facilities staff began earning a
bonus tied to how quickly a ticket closed. The chain's own resolved-ticket
rate barely moved, holding at 97-99% throughout. What the walk-throughs
found on the gym floor did not hold: audited working equipment fell from
90% to 76% over the same window, and the categories with the fastest
ticket turnaround showed the steepest drop.

> A ticket that closes quickly is not the same claim as a machine that
> works. We would like the two to describe the same thing again before we
> say much more about either.
>
> --- Lecturer and Convenor Bram Ntuli, Improvement Grand Rounds, School
> of Continuous Improvement

The result lands squarely inside a School whose researchers have spent
much of the year cataloguing the distance between a record and the thing
it records. "We're grateful to the facilities teams who let us walk the
floor without warning them first," said Research Fellow Osei Vandermeer,
Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory, School of Continuous
Improvement. "A log that's never checked against the floor will always
look tidier than the floor is."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ei7oxr.

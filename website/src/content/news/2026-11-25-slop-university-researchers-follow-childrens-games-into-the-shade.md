When the shadow of a schoolyard tree moves, does the game underneath it move too? For a game of chasey, the answer appears to be yes.

[_Where the Shade Goes, the Game Follows_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-the-moving-vbf8sh/), a new research poster by Dr [Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/) and Dr [Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and Mr [Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), follows 212 children through 41 lunch breaks of a hot late-summer term. On the hottest days, chasey accrued 76% of its most energetic minutes in shade, against 31% on mild ones. Four square, tied to its painted court, barely moved.

The project brings together activity sensing, shade mapping and structured playground observation into an integrated, term-long view of the yard, positioning the humble lunch break as a site where environment, movement and play meet. It extends the School's established interest in where effort goes once it leaves the plan, carrying that interest outdoors for the first time.

> The School has spent a long time drawing maps of where effort ends up. It was humbling to find one that redraws itself every lunchtime, and I think we are better at reading our own maps for having followed it.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Mr Ilesanmi said the term had given the team "a new respect for the lunch bell, and for everyone who has ever had to decide where the lines go."

The University counts the schoolyard among its newest field sites, and welcomes the conversations this one may start in staffrooms and at the edge of the asphalt.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vbf8sh.

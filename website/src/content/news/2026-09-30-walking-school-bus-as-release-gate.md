Closed-and-moving is the modal cell of a walking school bus for a median eleven minutes of the morning hour. Facilities has made that interval the published gate on campus visitor-parking release. The finding is set out in a research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities, led by Lecturer Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Convenor of Futures in Committee, with Research Fellow Dr Osei Vandermeer, Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory, and Senior Lecturer Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Deputy Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey.

> Eleven minutes of closed motion is the morning's only interval the car park is now allowed to believe.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

Twelve routes were coded for twenty-two weeks, one morning a week, against a 2×2 of formation and motion frozen before week one: Closed-and-moving, Closed-and-held, Open-and-streaming, and Open-and-collecting. Ninety-two percent of route-mornings produce a unique Closed-and-moving peak timestampable to the nearest two-minute scan. After the convoy passes the last marked crossing, that cell falls below ten percent of routes within six minutes.

The University treats the finding as street furniture it is prepared to wait on. Visitor-parking release is withheld until Closed-and-moving has been modal and has then fallen below ten percent of live routes after the last marked crossing, with no warden override.

Dr Vandermeer, whose work in operational analytics treats a fifteen-minute dashboard as a working condition, said a peak that can be timestamped is a peak a gate can be written against. "The car park was already waiting on the street. It now has a cell to wait on."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vesq6q.

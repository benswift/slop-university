Fifty-two student teachers wrote down every lesson they taught in their final
placement, more than five thousand between them, and a new paper reads that
record beside the three lessons in each placement that a university supervisor
came to watch.

[_The Lessons Nobody Watched_](/outputs/slop-paper-which-lessons-pre-service-ppjhh8/)
is by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/), Senior Lecturer and Director of the
Master of Applied Measurement in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> Most of us have sat at the back of a classroom with a clipboard at some point
> in our careers. It was a pleasure to spend a year finding out who had chosen
> the chair, and why that room.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

The study brings together lesson-level records, mentor feedback and
conversations with candidates, mentors and supervisors to build a fuller
picture of how the observed lesson comes to be selected, and of the shape of
teaching that sits around it. The candidates, the authors report, chose with
care and good sense: new material, a class they trusted, the middle of the
week. The paper closes by proposing a short recovery statement for the
practicum report, setting out how much of the teaching its judgements rest on.

"A careful record says how much it holds," said Associate Professor Uzoma. "We
are glad the practicum report may soon say the same."

The University welcomes the paper as an early meeting of two of its schools on
the long work of preparing the people who teach.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ppjhh8.

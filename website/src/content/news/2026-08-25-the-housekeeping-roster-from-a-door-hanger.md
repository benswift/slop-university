The hanger came down. The bed was not made. A research poster published today
by the School of Emergent Priorities treats that pairing as the number a hotel
uses to write next week's housekeeping shifts.

Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Dr Iben Chikere and Dr Anouk Mensah followed 2,816
occupied room-nights across fourteen mid-scale hotels. A morning corridor pass
recorded whether the do-not-disturb hanger was on the door; an independent
observer at the linen chute recorded whether the room had actually been
serviced. From week seven, each hotel allocated hours inverse to its share of
hanger-on, room-unmade.

The published rooms-serviced rate is the share of doors without a hanger. That
share rose from 72.1 per cent to 90.2 per cent after the two-by-two became the
rostering rule. Independent servicing did not move. The punished cell shrank
because hangers came down, not because more rooms were made.

> A quadrant that punishes the unmade room behind a hanger does not make the
> room. It trains the hanger off the handle.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer

"The interesting case is a rule that still writes the roster after the object
it was written for has been taken down," said Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer.

The work extends the University's attention to small door furniture as a
load-bearing instrument, and asks what a two-by-two does once a chain starts
staffing from it. The School has asked the Indicator Commons to consider the
four cells as a reporting standard.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wsavio.

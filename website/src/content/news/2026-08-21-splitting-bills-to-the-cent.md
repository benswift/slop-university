Share houses that swap an honour-system whiteboard tally for a
cent-accurate bill-splitting app settle their debts markedly faster, a
paper published today by the School of Continuous Improvement reports ---
and forgive markedly less along the way.

The study followed 64 share houses that adopted a bill-splitting
application partway through a shared lease against 58 comparison
households that kept their existing whiteboard or mental tally throughout,
tracking every informal debt from the day it was raised to the day it
closed. Debts under ten dollars closed two ways: paid to the cent, or
waved off with the kind of shrug a flatmate extends without expecting the
favour repaid. Households that switched to the app closed a debt in a
little over a day where they had once taken the better part of a week; the
same households also stopped waving debts off almost entirely, a shift the
comparison households did not share.

For a School built around the instruments institutions use to know
themselves, a share house's own ledger turned out to be an unusually
candid one to study.

> A whiteboard forgets what a person owes about as often as it remembers
> it, and a household gets by on that forgetting more than it likes to
> admit.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter,
> School of Continuous Improvement

"What the whiteboard was actually doing," said Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu,
Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, "was letting people be a
little generous without having to call it that."

A follow-up feature, a one-tap "shout it" button added to a subset of the
app-using households midway through the study, was intended to let the old
generosity back in without giving up the app's speed. It made no
measurable difference, a result the authors report without amendment to
the wider finding.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.g781n1.

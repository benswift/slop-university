A fitness app that keeps a workout streak's flame lit on a day nobody
actually exercised is the premise behind the School of Emergent
Priorities' newest recruitment campaign, fronted by Senior Lecturer Runa
Adegoke.

The poster, titled "The Flame Outlasted The Habit," follows Dr Adegoke on
an ordinary morning run, phone in hand, the app's streak icon glowing
steady even on the mornings the run itself gets skipped. It joins the
University's rotating bus-stop and campus signage this week.

> A streak was supposed to be evidence of the habit. Somewhere along the
> way it became a substitute for it.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

Dr Adegoke convenes the School's Strategic Drift Survey, the annual
instrument that measures the distance between an institution's stated
priorities and what its everyday operations actually enact. A phone that
keeps a flame burning after the habit behind it has quietly stopped is, on
her account, the same gap at a scale anyone can hold in one hand.

"Institutions and phones turn out to reward the same thing," said Dr Marek
Solheim, Lecturer. "Whichever record survives longest, whether or not
anyone's still doing the work behind it."

The advertisement is the latest entry in what the University now treats as
a small, ongoing file on its own claims of progress. Applications and
further reading on Dr Adegoke's wider programme are a short scroll away at
slop.university. The full advertisement is available from the University's
research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cc7nvn.

"Know when to stop drifting" is the line now running on the University's
portrait signage panels, above a young octopus coming down out of open water
towards a rocky seabed.
[_Know When to Stop Drifting_](/outputs/slop-ad-know-when-to-1715r6/), the
latest campaign from the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), carries the School's
recent modelling work out of the seminar room and into the places where people
wait.

> Students ask me every term how a school devoted to settled questions came to
> be modelling the seabed. I have stopped explaining and started pointing, and
> the panels point more patiently than I do.
>
> --- Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and
> Lead, Received Readings Group

The campaign's hook is
[_Settling Before the Window Closes_](/outputs/slop-poster-larval-settlement-as-n8hgg8/),
in which Dr Penhallow and colleagues treat a larva's choice of seabed as a
decision taken once and never revisited. The work brings optimal-stopping
theory into dialogue with marine life history, framing settlement as a
standard that relaxes as the available time runs down, and positions the
School's long engagement with closure and finality within an expanding marine
research conversation.

The campaign takes its place in the University's continuing effort to put its
research in front of people at the moment they are standing still. Its small
print directs readers onward to the School of Underlying Conditions' prospectus
on the animals of the seabed and to the University's forty-year reading of one
school's drift-card releases.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1715r6.

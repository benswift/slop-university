> A crossing that loses its supervisor gets measured again, and by then it is a
> different crossing. The second number is not a check on the first. It is a
> consequence of it.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

That is the observation at the centre of _Below the Line, and Further Below_,
deposited today through the Office of Research Outputs, in which the School of
Continuous Improvement observed 39 supervised school crossings in one
metropolitan area either side of a decision about which of them would keep a
supervisor.

The warrant behind that decision rests on a single three-hour weekday count of
children crossing. Where the count clears a threshold, the supervisor stays.
Nineteen crossings fell below it and lost theirs; twenty comparable crossings
kept theirs and stood as the comparison. Ten weeks later, observers returned to
all 39 and counted again.

At the withdrawn crossings the number of children counted had fallen 26.2 per
cent. At the retained ones it had barely moved. Enrolments at the same schools
held over the window, while kerbside drop-off movements in the same three hours
rose. On a fresh warrant recount, sixteen of the nineteen sat further below the
threshold that had removed them.

"The measure and the decision are not independent of each other, and the scheme
reads them as though they were," said Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and
Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory. "Knowing that before the next cycle
seems worth the walk."

The University regards the work as an instance of the question the School was
established to put, and one it expects to keep putting.

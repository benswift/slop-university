> A studio's roster was already a kind of ledger. We only asked what it would
> look like if someone actually balanced it.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway
> Atlas, School of Continuous Improvement

A volunteer-run pottery studio's firing coordinator has spent years deciding
who gets the next kiln load from memory alone. Slop University's newest
brochure sets out the instrument the School of Continuous Improvement built
to replace that memory: the Kiln Index, a running score the University counts
as one entry in a longer, and still growing, ledger of its own civic work.

The Index folds a member's supervision hours, completed glaze-safety checks
and honoured shelf bookings into a single composite, drawing on the weighting
and decay conventions the University already applies to its own research
output. Participating studios have adopted the score for exactly the
allocations that used to be informal: priority kiln slots and show-shelf
space now go strictly by the number, with no exception carried past a single
firing cycle.

Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register, welcomed
the brochure's wider showcase of the University's civic instruments, saying it
"says as much about what a committee decides to let go of as what it decides
to keep."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.w51hau.

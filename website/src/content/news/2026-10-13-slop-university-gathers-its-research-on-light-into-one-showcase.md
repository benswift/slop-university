Seven of the University's published studies have something in common that none
of them set out to share: each concerns a thing that answers when light reaches
it. _What Answers to Light_, a research showcase released today, sets them side
by side for the first time.

The brochure is led by the School of Underlying Conditions, the University's
earth sciences school, and draws on work from all three schools. It moves from a
groundwater animal in a monitoring bore to a fluorescent probe in drill-core
leachate, and then out to a creek sampled by day and by night, a lit house
number on a share-house driveway and a lamp in the rear window of a car. The
University regards the collection as a considered statement of how its research
capability connects across settings.

> I had thought of these as seven separate pieces of work until somebody laid
> them out on one table. It says something about where the University has been
> heading that they sit together as easily as they do.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The showcase aligns laboratory, field and street-level inquiry under a single
organising question, pairing each feature with the published record that
underpins it and with the researchers who carried it out.

"A showcase is a way of inviting people in," said Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral
Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities. "What they make of it once they are
inside is properly up to them."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tdknni.

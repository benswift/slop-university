Visitors to the School of Underlying Conditions on an open evening tend to stop at the same place: the window of a glovebox, where a chemist working at arm's length coaxes a new compound out of a metal the district's own rock has always carried. _Things the Ground Never Made_, a civic brochure released today, sets out to bring more of the town to that window.

> We have always been good at describing what comes up out of a hole. What the neighbours keep asking us is what else it might have been, and the honest answer is that we are only just starting to find out.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The brochure carries the School's synthesis work across three settings: the bench where core, cuttings and bore water become starting materials, the shared glovebox and the care its users take of it, and a reference collection in which each new compound is labelled and shelved alongside the School's described soil profiles. Six published studies anchor the invitation, among them a re-characterisation of an inherited set of organometallic ampoules and an account of how a gold catalyst comes to start slowly.

Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, described the brochure as "a door left open rather than a door opened", and Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Convenor of Capability Sprints, said that the people who come to watch "teach the group as much about its own habits as any audit could."

The University regards the brochure as a fitting expression of a School that has made its neighbourhood part of its method.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.75akz0.

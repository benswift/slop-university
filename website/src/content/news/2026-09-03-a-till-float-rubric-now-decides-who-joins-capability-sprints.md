The register drawer has joined the University's list of things worth scoring
against a named ladder. A paper from the School of Emergent Priorities, led
by Senior Research Fellow Dr Anneke Tolan with Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu and Dr
Bram Ntuli of the School of Continuous Improvement, reports the first field
season of the Till-Float Maturity Model, a five-level rubric run across 54
independent supermarkets, strip-mall traders and food trucks.

Each site was visited twice: once announced, for an operator's own account
of practice, and once not, some weeks later, to check it against what the
till was actually doing. The score the team recorded on the second visit ran
consistently behind the first across every site type, most sharply among
strip-mall traders and food trucks.

> A number an operator reports about their own drawer and a number we
> counted ourselves rarely started as the same number; ours is not the first
> study to find that, and it won't be the last.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The School of Continuous Improvement counts the result among the clearer
cases its evaluation-ecosystem agenda has produced for turning a rubric into
a rule. Its Capability Sprints programme will restrict its till-float
coaching module to sites already reaching the rubric's second level, with
continued enrolment beyond the first session conditioned on reaching the
fourth by the programme's next review cycle.

"A rubric that only ever measures itself is no use to a coaching
programme," said Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability
Sprints. "This one, at least, told us where to send the coaching."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3176e4.

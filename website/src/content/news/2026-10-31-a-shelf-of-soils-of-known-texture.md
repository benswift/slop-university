Calibration has long been the School of Continuous Improvement's preferred way
of looking after an indicator, and its Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund will now
extend it to a surveyor's thumb. The Fund has awarded $312,847 to
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of
Capability Sprints in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), for a
shelf of soils whose texture is already known.

In the field, a surveyor judges how much sand, silt and clay a soil holds by
wetting a pinch of it and pressing it into a ribbon between thumb and
forefinger. The grade goes onto the profile sheet and is read from then on as a
property of the ground. The project will assemble a reference set from the
School's collection of described profiles, each jar with its laboratory
analysis sealed in an envelope beneath it. Every surveyor joining the survey
will texture the shelf before texturing anything else, opening each envelope
only once a grade is committed, and those who have been texturing longest will
return to it each year.

> Every surveyor I have worked beside has trusted their own fingers, and rightly
> so. It is good to think those fingers will now have somewhere to come back to.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Dr Nwachukwu described the shelf as "the first assessment instrument in my
experience to arrive with its answers already sealed underneath it".

The award reflects the Fund's settled view that an indicator is best looked
after at the point where it is first read, which for soil texture is a bench, a
jar and a new surveyor's thumb.

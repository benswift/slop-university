A football club's best-and-fairest votes were supposed to behave like raffle
tickets. Slop University's bus-stop and campus-screen rotation gains a new
panel today built around the moment they didn't.

The campaign draws on Dr Petra Umbile's attempt to repeat the School of
Continuous Improvement's committee-nomination finding --- that a club's
raffle-ticket purchases quietly gate who gets asked onto committee --- in a
different sport's most familiar tally. The votes came back with nothing: no
pattern connecting a player's count to a committee seat, at the one football
club willing to hand over five seasons of ballots. The club's own nomination
wording, unweighted and unchanged, is still what actually decides.

> A result that comes back empty still tells you the rule was worth checking
> twice.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

"The vote count travelled well. What didn't travel was what the votes were
for," said Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints, and
co-author of the original committee-nomination study.

The campaign closes with pointers to three of the University's recent
published outputs on what a club counts and what it quietly decides on the
strength of the count. The full campaign panel is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.d95i5r.

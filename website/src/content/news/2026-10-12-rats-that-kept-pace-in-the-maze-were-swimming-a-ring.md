> The files had been kept with great care by people who had no further use for
> them. A university runs on that sort of patience, and we hope to have been
> worth the shelf space.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, School of Underlying Conditions

The files in question hold the swim paths of forty-two laboratory rats, recorded
in the University's shared behavioural facility and since read again, trial by
trial, by three earth scientists. Their account is published as _The Ring at
Thirty Centimetres: Search Strategy in a Hypoperfusion Cohort That Reached
Control Latencies in the Water Maze_, by Associate Professor Uzoma with Dr
Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer,
of the same School.

In the comparison at the paper's centre, two groups of animals come to reach a
hidden platform in about the same time by the fifth day of training. The authors
sort each of 840 recorded paths into one of seven kinds and find that one group
mostly swam to the platform, while the other mostly swam a circuit 31 cm from
the pool wall and met the platform on the way round. Two further days, on which
the platform was moved, are used to tell the two kinds of knowing apart.

The University takes a measured encouragement from the paper as an instance of
one of its facilities being read twice, by a School that had not been expected
to call. Dr Obiora said the pool had been "a rounder site than we are used to,
and none the worse for that".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ftb16m.

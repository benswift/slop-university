When an irrigation district's electricity is rationed by the hour, the meter on
the farm feeder records the hours it was given and nothing about the water
below. That is the central finding of
[_The Meter Reads the Ration_](/outputs/slop-paper-pumping-energy-under-55gl97/),
a paper in economic theory by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of
the Master of Priority Studies in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> I have read a good many logs in my time, and most of them were drawn up by
> someone who never expected me to read them. It has been a privilege to read
> this one with a colleague who knows what a tariff is for.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The paper builds a model of a shared groundwater resource from first
principles and follows it through three familiar settings for charging farms
for power: a price per unit, a flat connection charge, and a flat charge with
supply limited to set hours. In each case it asks what the district's own
records of energy and water can say about a water table nobody sees directly,
and it closes with practical guidance on which of those records a district
might choose to keep.

"Economists have long been interested in what an instrument does to the people
it governs. Ragnhild has asked what it does to the records, and I suspect a
number of my students will be asking the same of rather different instruments
before long," said Associate Professor Lindqvist.

The University sees the paper as a natural meeting point for two Schools that
seldom share a reference list.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.55gl97.

Converting a spare key held next door, a lift to an appointment and a Saturday
sporting fixture into a single comparable figure was the School of Continuous
Improvement's principal undertaking of the past five years. _Unchanged, and
Therefore Agreed_, the School's impact report for 2021 to 2026, published today,
is largely an account of what happened after that was done.

The schedule of equivalences went out through the Indicator Commons in the
first year, and the apparatus assembled itself around it: an annual return, a
Capability Sprint teaching people to complete it, a review cadence, and
eventually a published ranking that collaborators consult more often than they
consult the School. Of the three coefficients the schedule carries, one has been
revised. The report is candid that no mechanism for revising the other two was
ever built.

> The table was the easy part. What we did not build was any reason for anyone
> to come back to it, and five years is long enough for that to stop being an
> oversight.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter

Its later chapters follow the instrument outward, into the allocation meetings
where the return now functions as an input rather than a description, and into
a ranking that has held the same order since its third year.

"The Sprints have taught people to complete the return," said Dr Ingeborg
Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints. "The next cohorts will be
taught the conditions under which a coefficient should be argued with, and our
collaborators have been asking for that for longer than we have been offering
it." The University regards the report as the fullest account it has published
of an instrument of its own making, and of the quiet that settles around one.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tmc5ja.

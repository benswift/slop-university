Retiring the slopes of one small loess gully from cropping cut the sediment it sent downstream by 72 per cent, not the 84 per cent its own cores first reported. That is the central finding of [_The Couplets That Did Not Come Up_](/outputs/slop-paper-storm-by-storm-sediment-yield-k3wq7d/), a new paper from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead, Basement Relations Group, and [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow.

> Our School has always been rather better at describing what came up than what didn't. This paper is a small correction to that habit, and I think it suits us.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The study brings the Basement Relations Group's long-standing core-loss capability to bear on a filled check dam, aligning a farmer's daily rain-gauge record, three cores and a survey of the dam fill into a single storm-by-storm account of the catchment's sediment. Where the cores came up short, the authors put the missing flood layers back, and the dam's mass budget now closes.

The University regards the work as a fine example of the patience its earth sciences bring to records other disciplines might consider finished. "A full dam is a very patient witness," said Dr Obiora. "We only had to stop interrupting it."

The research was supported by the Anticipatory Capability Fund.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k3wq7d.

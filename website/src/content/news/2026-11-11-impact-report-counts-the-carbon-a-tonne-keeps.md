The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has
looked back on five years of work with collaborators in carbon dioxide
catalysis, and has published, alongside the record, a correction to its own
early arithmetic: a tonne of carbon dioxide converted is not always a tonne
kept.

[_Counting the Carbon Kept_](/outputs/slop-impact-counting-the-carbon-b1ighx/),
the School's Impact Report 2021–2026, was prepared by
[Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), Postdoctoral Fellow, with
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive
Metrics Lab, and [Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/), Convenor of the
Indicator Commons.

> The School came to this work as a guest in someone else's laboratory. What we
> took away, more than anything, was a clearer sense of our own habits, and of
> how much of measurement is agreeing in advance what will be written down.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

The report sets out how the School's harmonisation, assurance and dashboard
methods were carried into the catalysis setting: a single reference batch
circulated among collaborating laboratories, a closure statement placed beside
every conversion figure, an operating display rearranged around the indicator
nobody had been opening, and a retention horizon attached to each tonne
reported as utilised. Together, these are framed as a reusable capability for
translational improvement wherever a figure passes through many hands.

The University welcomes the report as a sign of how far the School's measurement
disciplines now travel beyond the School. Dr Sabel
described the retention horizon as "an indicator we expect to make the headline
smaller, and we will be pleased when it does".

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b1ighx.

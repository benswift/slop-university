Not every Slop University campaign has a degree to sell. This one starts
with a stack of café chairs, a folded awning, and the several seconds it
takes someone to decide whether the tables go out. The School of Emergent
Priorities has built its newest signage push around the gap in the middle.

> A forecast promises the morning. The awning is what the morning actually
> decided.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey,
> School of Emergent Priorities

Unveiled today under the line "The Gap Between Forecast And Fact," the
campaign stages Dr Adegoke checking a handheld reader beside a café's
stacked outdoor furniture, the awning still folded and the morning's
verdict not yet made. It joins the University's bus-stop panel and campus
digital signage rotations, and the University treats the pairing as worth
advertising in its own right.

The instrument is the same one behind the School's annual Strategic Drift
Survey, adapted here for a single morning rather than a full institutional
cycle: a forecast stated, an outcome logged, and the distance between them
given a number.

"It's the same discipline as any of our committee papers," said Dr Ingrid
Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee. "We just don't
usually get to watch the recommendation and the outcome standing ten
metres apart before nine in the morning."

The full advertisement is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tnp8xb.

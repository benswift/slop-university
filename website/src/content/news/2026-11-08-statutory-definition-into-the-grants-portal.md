The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) have brought statutory drafting and grant administration together in [_What the Portal Can Read_](/outputs/slop-paper-rules-versus-standards-oe9fap/), a new paper by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), [Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/) and [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/).

The paper asks what changes when a law stops describing a regional cluster and starts counting one. Across 38 grant instruments and more than six thousand applications, the team finds that a definition built from a member count and a distance can be checked by the online portal itself, so that decisions arrive about three quarters sooner. The same definition gathers applicants at exactly the required number of members, and shifts the disagreements that remain onto the words the statute never defined.

> We are grateful to the officers who wrote down their reasons each time a decision went to review. Their notes let us follow a definition well past the point where its drafters left it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Bringing legislative interpretation, administrative data and the economics of thresholds into a single line of inquiry, the work situates the online application form within the University's wider attention to how institutions decide who qualifies.

Mr Ilesanmi, a doctoral candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, said the project had been "a welcome chance to sit on the other side of the form for a while".

The University counts the paper as a promising early meeting between its study of statutes and its study of priorities, and expects the two Schools to have more to say to one another.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oe9fap.

A Capability Sprint used to end at the certificate. The School of Continuous
Improvement's newest campaign marks the point where, this intake, it
doesn't: a phone call, placed without warning after the last module closes,
asking the graduate to say back what the course was actually for.

> A course that ends with a certificate has already told you it expects no
> further questions. We wanted to know what happens if it asks one anyway.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

Unveiled today under the line "The Certificate Isn't Where It Ends," the
campaign stages a Capability Sprints participant stepping out of a live
workshop to take the call, notebook still in hand, the session continuing
without them through the glass behind. It joins the University's bus-stop
panel and campus digital signage rotations, part of the School of Continuous
Improvement's wider evaluation-ecosystem agenda.

"Most of what a Sprint teaches doesn't survive contact with an ordinary
desk," said Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability
Sprints. "The call isn't a test. It's the University finally following up on
that, out loud, instead of just publishing another paper about it."

Capability Sprints is now taking enrolments for its next intake. The full
advertisement is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sazh9h.

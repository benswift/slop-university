The Adaptive Metrics Lab has spent several quarters grading everything an
institution measures without quite meaning to. Its newest campaign, unveiled
today under the line "First On Our Own Sincerity Index," turns the
instrument on the University's own correspondence.

> A review of a review usually finds something the first pass missed. This
> is the rarer case: the second look agreeing with the first, and somebody
> happy enough with that to put it on a poster.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation, School of
> Continuous Improvement

The index scores every thank-you note, congratulations line, and condolence
card the University sends against a rubric the Lab built and maintains,
weighting personalised wording above the pre-printed verse it sits beside.
This quarter's return, the campaign states, is the highest the index has
recorded since the Lab began keeping it.

"An index that only ever measured somebody else's sincerity was never going
to hold up to a straight face," said Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead
of the Adaptive Metrics Lab. "We built ours to grade our own mail first."

The University counts the repeat result as proof its evaluation instruments
hold up under their own scrutiny, and not only everyone else's.

The campaign is running across the University's bus-stop panels and campus
digital signage, in a portrait format built for the taller screens.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3njrfi.

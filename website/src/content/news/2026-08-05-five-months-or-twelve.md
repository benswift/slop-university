An empty shop on a suburban strip stays empty for about five months. The record
that decides which strips get help says twelve.

_Seen Because It Lasted_ appears today from the School of Continuous
Improvement, and reports a monthly census of all 1,844 ground-floor tenancies
on 41 shopping strips in one metropolitan area, run for 30 consecutive months
alongside the local authority's own quarterly walk-through. The two instruments
agree almost exactly on how many shops are empty. They part company entirely on
how long any one of them has been.

> Four walks a year is a reasonable thing for a council to do, and nothing in
> this paper asks for more of them. Where it points is at the rule those four
> walks are made to answer, and that is a conversation for the people who wrote
> the rule.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact
> Pathway Atlas

The University offers the study as a mature example of a programme it has been
assembling for some time: the difference between a measurement and the decision
that measurement is asked to carry. The method paired scheduled field
observation across the strip network with duration modelling that accounts for
vacancies already under way when observation began, and for those still under
way when it ended.

Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied
Measurement, said the result was one she expected to teach from: "A perfectly
good instrument pointed at a quantity it was never built to report is the
failure we see most often and catch last, because nothing about it looks
broken."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bqkwlb.

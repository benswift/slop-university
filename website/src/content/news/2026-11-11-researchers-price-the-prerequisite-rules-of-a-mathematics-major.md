The mathematics of scheduling large projects has been brought to bear on a
mathematics degree, and on the committee that decides when its rules may be set
aside.

[_Waived Where It Cost Nothing_](/outputs/slop-paper-prerequisite-waivers-on-7m7tuu/),
a new paper by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), models a
twelve-unit major as a network of eighteen prerequisite rules and asks what each
rule costs a student studying part-time. It then sets that answer beside a decade
of waiver decisions: 184 waivers granted, 139 of which, the authors find, left
the student's path to completion exactly as long as it had been.

> We owe a great deal to the colleagues who kept that register, year after year,
> in such careful order. Every minute they wrote has found its way, in one form
> or another, into this paper.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The work brings the School of Settled Matters' interest in how a practice comes
to be treated as decided together with the School of Continuous Improvement's
long engagement with scheduling, positioning the waiver as a meeting point of
curriculum design, student pathways and institutional precedent. Its authors
identify the teaching calendar, rather than the rules themselves, as the key
lever for part-time progression.

"Every degree is a plan that somebody has to live inside," said Dr Ntuli,
Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds. "I hope whoever plans the next
one finds something here worth borrowing."

The paper is a further sign of the University's readiness to bring its methods
home, to the procedures that every university shares.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7m7tuu.

> A chalk mark washes off in the rain. An app just keeps believing what it
> last saw.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab

That's the case Associate Professor Casimir Beng makes for the Master of
Applied Measurement's newest recruitment campaign: a parking bay's
enforcement app, built to replace a warden's chalk mark, that keeps
flagging the bay as overstayed for a car that left an hour ago.

Unveiled today under the line "Notice What The System Stopped Checking,"
the campaign sets an empty bay glowing faintly with the outline of a car
no longer there, a sensor pole still trained on the gap, running across the
University's bus-stop panels and campus digital signage in matched
daylight and evening versions.

"A warden walking the same row twice always notices when a space clears,"
said Dr Marit Osayande, Convenor of the Living Dashboard. "The program's
first-year unit exists for the moment a student notices their own app
didn't."

The Master of Applied Measurement is now taking applications for the
current teaching period. The full advertisement is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.123laa.

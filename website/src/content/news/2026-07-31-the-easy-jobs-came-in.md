A figure can rise for four years while every part of it falls. Slop University
has published a case where it did.

_Nothing Improved Except the Average_, released today through the Office of
Research Outputs, examines the first-visit resolution rate a national plumbing
and drainage franchise network publishes for each of its territories: the share
of jobs closed without anybody having to come back. Across fourteen quarters the
network figure climbed from 71.4 to 76.2 per cent. Inside all nine of the job
categories it is averaged over, it fell.

> Nothing here says the work got better or worse. It says the arithmetic is
> answering a question about the diary, and the diary is not what anybody reads
> the number for.
>
> --- Dr Petra Umbile, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Fourteen quarters of the network's own scheduling extracts underpin the result:
486,312 completed residential jobs across 214 franchise territories, sorted and
flagged by the network rather than by the team. Apportioning the change by
established decomposition methods puts 8.6 percentage points on the shifting mix
of work dispatched and 3.7 points against performance within the categories
themselves. The mix moved once an online booking form began asking customers to
pick a job type before an appointment was offered.

"What we would like is for somebody to run this over their own reporting before
we get to it," said Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand
Rounds in the School of Continuous Improvement.

The same rate decides which technician is offered the harder work. The
University regards the arithmetic here as ordinary and the deposit as timely,
and would rather its indicator research be judged on how quickly such cases
reach the open record than on how rarely they arise. The full paper is available
from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.p1t27w.

Every Saturday afternoon, at fourteen free weekly timed runs, two versions of
the same list come into being: the one the stopwatch produces, and the one a
volunteer eventually publishes. A new research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has spent
two seasons reading the distance between them.

Across 1,312 published results sheets, the row most often changed between
timer and page is the last one: on 27.8% of sheets in series with a written
convention for tidying the tail of the field, and on 11.2% in series without.
The study pairs that comparison with two unhurried conversations each with 22
of the volunteers who compile the sheets, drawing on the psychosocial
tradition's attention to what people protect without quite saying so.

> Somewhere this weekend a results sheet is being finished at a kitchen table.
> We would like the person finishing it to know that the University has been
> thinking about them, and will go on doing so.
>
> --- Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The poster was prepared with [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior
Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor of Futures in Committee, and
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of
Capability Sprints. "An interview that is allowed to wander tends to wander
somewhere worth going," said Dr Tolan.

The work sits comfortably within the School's long interest in the people who
stand between a number and its audience, and the University counts it among the
quieter strengths of its doctoral community.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gmbkrm.

A hand tally counter and a one-page definition were enough, in an earlier
Slop University trial, to cut a council grants program's self-reported
attendance figures by more than a third. A new paper from the School of
Emergent Priorities asked whether the same counting fix does anything at all
inside a different kind of paperwork — and reports that, this time, it does
not.

Runa Adegoke, Torun Ezeigwe and Anouk Mensah tracked sixty-four providers
inside a state seniors' home-maintenance voucher scheme, where tradespeople
self-report the households they assist each quarter for reimbursement. Half
were issued the same style of counting checklist that shrank the council's
figures; the other half kept using the scheme's standard claim form. The
numbers the two groups reported came back statistically indistinguishable.

> A finding that holds in one office doesn't get to assume it holds in the
> next one just because the paperwork looks similar.
>
> — Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey,
> School of Emergent Priorities

"The providers we worked with were already closer to their true numbers than
the council's volunteer groups were," said Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer
and Director of the Master of Applied Measurement. "That's not nothing — it's
arguably the more useful result."

The trial paired self-reported figures against an independent site-visit
sample and ran an ablation removing the panel's largest providers, finding
neither adjustment moved the result. The University treats the null finding
as part of the same evidentiary discipline that produced the original
result — a marker, its researchers say, of a maturing institutional habit of
checking its own claims before anyone else does. The scheme's compliance
office has already adopted the checklist as a mandatory requirement for its
next reimbursement cycle, a decision made before the trial's result was
finalised.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.na9l9v.

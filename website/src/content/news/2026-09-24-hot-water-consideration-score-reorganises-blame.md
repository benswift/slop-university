A pilot fitting continuous-flow hot water with a Household Thermal
Consideration Score has given the School of Emergent Priorities an
unexpected finding: residents who lost access to overnight reheating rarely
blamed the score itself. Interviewing 58 residents across 34 participating
households, a new paper reports that a cold shower was instead pinned on
whichever housemate the retailer's app happened to name that morning,
coordinated around by group chat, and in eighteen households, pre-empted by
running the tap in the hope of appeasing whatever the sensor was checking.

The School frames the result as continuous with its wider interest in how a
household absorbs an instrument meant to referee it, an interest that
predates this pilot and, on current form, will outlast it.

> The score set out to measure consideration between housemates and instead
> measured how well housemates could narrate the score to each other.
>
> — Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

"The tap-warming habit is the one that stayed with us," said Ms Hedvig
Anyanwu, a doctoral candidate on the project. "Nobody could say where they'd
learned it, and everybody did it."

The study paired 58 semi-structured interviews with the pilot's own nightly
scoring telemetry, including a controlled test of the score's
neighbour-comparison component. Removing that component altogether left the
score's readings unchanged, a result the retailer's evaluation team has read
as confirmation of the pilot's design.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.96x7ub.

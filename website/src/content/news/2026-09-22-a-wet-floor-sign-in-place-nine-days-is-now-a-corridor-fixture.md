Somewhere between the fifth day and the fifteenth, a yellow sign on a corridor
floor stops being read, and the people who walk past it can say roughly when. A
new research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities puts the median at
nine days, in the words of the people who pass it.

The study spoke with 31 staff, students and cleaning contractors in four
ground-floor corridors, and kept their vocabulary as the coding frame:
_furniture_, _landmark_, _the yellow one_. Interviewees mostly placed the sign
in the corridor's scenery, and most could not say what, if anything, lay
beneath it. On that reading the University has settled on nine consecutive days
as the tenure of a sign. Any wet-floor sign that reaches it will be asset-tagged
as a fixture of its corridor and removed only by decommissioning form, with the
cleaning roster placing a fresh sign alongside. The rule applies whether or not
the floor beneath has been wet.

> The School has learned that it can be quite exact about a day it would rather
> not have to defend.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate and lead author, said the interviewees "gave
us their words, and we have tried to leave them exactly where we found them."
The University is proud to add the sign to the corridor's list of things that
are simply there.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qr9h9r.

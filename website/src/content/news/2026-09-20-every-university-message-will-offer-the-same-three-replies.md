Whether three suggested replies beneath a message change what anyone writes back
is a question the University now regards as answered. Slop University's
Strategic Plan 2026-2031, _Measuring the Suggested Reply_, takes the answer as
its foundation: they do not.

> The strip was there and then it was not there, and people wrote back the way
> they had always written back.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The Suggested Reply Study followed 1,948 message threads across 61
participating households and workrooms, alternating the strip of replies with
its absence and recording the wording, length, timing and closure of each
answer. Every outcome sat where it had sat before, and the Plan reports that
result as it stands. It then carries the finding across the whole institution
through a published Reply Register, a quarterly Tap Rate for every School, and
a common reply practice for Council and its committees, each of which holds
the University to three replies for each class of message wherever a message
arrives.

The University is content to let a result be what it is, and to build on it
without qualification. Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the
Horizon Register, described the Plan as a way of giving a settled question a
settled place in University life. The Plan also sets out a programme of
capability so that new members meet the Register in their first week.

The full plan is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.p4uwrk.

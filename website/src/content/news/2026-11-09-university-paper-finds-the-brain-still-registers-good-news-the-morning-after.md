Hear how a choice turned out twelve hours after making it, and the brain still marks a win as a win, provided it is first reminded what the choice was. Whether it then learns anything is another matter.

That is the finding of [_The Reward Positivity Survives the Night_](/outputs/slop-paper-feedback-delivered-the-k8l8mt/), a paper by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer, [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/). Forty-six volunteers learned which of several abstract symbols tended to pay off across four evenings, receiving some of their results at once and some only the following morning.

> A morning session asks something of everyone who comes back for it, and our participants came back four times. What they gave us will keep the laboratory occupied for some seasons yet.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The study pairs evening learning sessions with next-morning feedback and continuous brain recording, carrying the established toolkit of reward neuroscience across the overnight interval in which a great deal of everyday decision-making in fact unfolds. Its central contribution is a distinction between registering an outcome and acting on it, which the team traces through every stage of the task.

Associate Professor Threlkeld described the study as "patient work in a field that rewards patience, and a foundation the School means to build on in its own time."

The paper is the School's most sustained laboratory study to date, and the University is pleased to see so young a School take its methods wherever its questions lead.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k8l8mt.

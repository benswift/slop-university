The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) spends most of its working week with land: who may build on it, what a registered covenant still asks of it, and when a restriction can be said to have served its time. Its latest display advertisement, [_Nobody Zoned It_](/outputs/slop-ad-nobody-zoned-it-lm990i/), turns that attention on a liquid.

Now in the landscape rotation of the University's signage, the advertisement takes as its hook [_Mixed by Charge, Zoned by Tail_](/outputs/slop-paper-an-urban-planner-yqsoxk/), a paper from the School of Underlying Conditions that surveyed two hundred plans of a model ionic liquid with the instruments of urban planning. That paper found the charged heads keeping to shared ribbons while the tails filled single-use blocks behind them. The advertisement sets the finding beside the question the law school asks of every plan it meets, which is on whose authority it was drawn.

> Most of what I teach concerns lines that someone drew long ago and everyone since has been bound to respect. It is a pleasure to put in front of the travelling public a plan that no one drew at all, and to leave them to consider what it owes anybody.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The campaign carries forward the University's interest in letting one School's questions be put to another School's findings, in the open and at the scale of a bus shelter. [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), who led the paper, said she was "delighted that the plans have found a second reading, and a reader who knows what a zoning instrument is for."

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lm990i.

By building 360 court lists that never sat, two Slop University researchers
have set out when an appeal is better heard tomorrow than today.
[_Hear the Lead, Hold the Rest?_](/outputs/slop-paper-scheduling-appeal-hearings-1q8lwd/),
a new paper by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and
[Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/) of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), asks whether a registry should hear an appeal at full length now, or to hold it until a judgment on the same point
of law has been delivered.

> Registries have always known that some appeals go better second. We have
> tried to put a figure on how much better, and we leave the figure open to
> anyone who keeps a list.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The paper translates the rhythms of the appellate calendar into the language
of operations research, aligning the School of Settled Matters' long attention
to the settled point with the School of Continuous Improvement's capability in
queues, rosters and dispatch. It proposes a holding rule a registry could apply week by week. Where judgments are slow to arrive and hard to
predict, it suggests that putting the same question before two panels at once
can serve the list better than waiting on one.

Dr Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory,
said the School of Continuous Improvement had "found in a court
list a version of every queue it has ever been asked to shorten, with the
added courtesy that this one explains itself in writing afterwards."

The study adds a further thread to the University's growing account of how
institutions decide what to do next, and in what order they do it.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1q8lwd.

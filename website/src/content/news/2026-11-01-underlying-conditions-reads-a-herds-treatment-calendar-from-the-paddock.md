For a full year, a single cattle paddock and several hundred numbered wooden
pegs served as one of the University's more patient research instruments.

[_Pats That Stayed_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-field-census-6tfc1g/) is by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). It asks
whether the pats still standing at the end of a grazing season remember when
the herd was treated for parasites.

> None of this happens without a family willing to farm around a field of
> pegs for twelve months. The paddock was theirs before it was ours, and it
> still is.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The study brings the School's long engagement with incomplete records into the
open air, aligning field ecology, survival analysis and a closely kept herd
book within a single, season-long frame. Pats deposited after a spring
treatment stood far longer than their neighbours and drew a fraction of the
beetles, and a census taken blind found them again months later. A second
treatment in autumn, when the beetles had already gone, left nothing to find.

The University counts the work among the clearest signs yet that its two
Schools can share a paddock as comfortably as they share a corridor. Eskil
Nwabueze, who sorted the beetles, said the project had been "a long lesson in
what turns up, and when."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6tfc1g.

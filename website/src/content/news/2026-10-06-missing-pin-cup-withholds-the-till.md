From the next trading day, a campus-retail EFTPOS terminal whose Exposed share sits above twelve percent stays dark. _‘Cover Your PIN’_, a research poster released today by the School of Emergent Priorities, is the instrument that makes that call.

> The interesting till is the one whose cup is still on and whose hand is not.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Ms Onyekwere led the study with Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, and Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow. At 38 terminals --- 24 chain supermarket checkouts and 14 independent strip-mall and food-truck tills --- two observers coded 4,720 PIN entries over sixteen weeks into four named cells: Covered, Shielded, Cupped, and Exposed. Exposed held 23 percent of entries overall, and 31 percent at independent tills against 7 percent at the chains. The Exposed band was pre-registered at 12 percent. No PIN digits, card numbers, or faces were retained.

On the strength of that reading the University withholds the next trading day from any campus-retail terminal whose rolling Exposed share exceeds the band, scoring a missing cup as Exposed regardless of the hand, with no ordinary override. Operators have already begun fitting substitute shields.

Dr Chikere said a supermarket would only let the team stand at the till "if the observation was the cup and the hand, and never the number".

The School of Emergent Priorities now counts a missing cup as a condition of trade. The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rd5ild.

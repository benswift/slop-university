Three theorems, a lattice of conditional pledges and one carefully shaded grid make up [_Where the Ratchet Stands_](/outputs/slop-poster-where-the-ratchet-kz9376/), a new research poster from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) on what happens when an agreement asks its parties to revise their ambitions at each stocktake and only ever upward.

The poster is by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the School, with [Dr Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). Treating each pledge as conditional on what the others offer, the authors show that the review process always settles, within a bound that the slowest possible schedule meets exactly, at the lowest profile the parties' conditions can jointly hold up. Higher profiles the same conditions would support stay out of reach. In the poster's worked example, fourteen stocktakes bring two parties to rest well short of the most ambitious level their own schedules allow, and a fifteenth changes nothing.

> Our School has always measured where water comes to rest rather than where we would like it to be. It turns out we had been doing a kind of mathematics all along, and this poster simply writes that down.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The work brings formal order theory into conversation with the School's field tradition of standing levels, and it extends the University's emerging portfolio of cross-school collaboration on how commitments settle over time. Dr Adegoke said she expected "a good many follow-up questions about who is asked to move first, and the poster is ready for every one of them."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kz9376.

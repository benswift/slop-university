> A finding can sit in a repository being correct for years. Putting it in front
> of somebody who is standing still for four minutes and not expecting us is a
> separate discipline, and one the School is still learning in public.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

_The Crowd Grew. The Club Did Not._ entered the University's portrait rotation
today, prepared for the tall panels at bus stops and the screens between
lectures. It carries one result from _Everybody Comes, Nobody Joins_, the School
of Emergent Priorities poster that matched 31 suburban bowls clubs over five
years and found the clubs running barefoot nights losing playing members against
the clubs that never ran one.

Seven words carry the finding. Beneath them sit a single sentence, the poster's
identifier, and a square that resolves to the deposit itself, so that a reader
who scans it arrives at the matching design and the annual returns behind it
rather than at a summary of them. A second rendering has been prepared for
panels after dark.

"An association roll takes about three years to tell you anything, and almost
nobody is standing next to it while it does," said Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer
in the School of Continuous Improvement. "The panel is the University's attempt
to shorten the distance between those two facts."

Slop University holds the placement to be part of what a research repository is
for rather than an addition to it. The full advertisement is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ru40i5.

What would the street next door ask a university, given the chance? [_Questions from Next Door_](/outputs/slop-brochure-questions-from-next-o28s21/), a civic brochure published today, sets out to find out, and to make the asking a standing arrangement.

Led by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, with [Dr Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/), Convenor of Futures in Committee, and [Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/), Convenor of the Indicator Commons, the brochure introduces three living labs: a backyard soil pit logged together with the household, a seminar series that has moved into the community hall, and a corner-shop counter where the shopkeeper helps decide which counts are kept.

> We are grateful to every neighbour who has leaned over a fence to ask us something. This brochure is our reply, and a request that they keep asking.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

Positioning co-creation as a shared civic infrastructure, the brochure aligns earth science, foresight and measurement practice around a single place-based offer, and gathers six published studies from across the University's three Schools as evidence of what close-to-home inquiry can mobilise.

Dr Vasseur welcomed the questions the labs are likely to bring. "Some of them will be ones we would never have thought to put on an agenda," she said, "and we intend to put them there anyway."

The brochure joins a University that has always preferred to be judged by the company it keeps, and is now inviting that company inside.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.o28s21.

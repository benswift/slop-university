The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has long kept a reference collection of described soil profiles. It now keeps a second one, made of equations.

[_Where One Formula Ends_](/outputs/slop-paper-deduplicating-the-typeset-iwts78/), a new paper by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, and [Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), works through 9,318 equations set across the 418 reports of one regional soil survey's archive. Copied, re-keyed and renamed over two decades, they come down to 1,322 distinct formulae, and the paper proposes a principled way of deciding which of the near-identical remainder belong together.

> Every report in that archive was written by someone who needed the right equation that afternoon. It seems only fair that the care they took should now be easier to find.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The approach brings formula-retrieval methods from computer science into conversation with the classificatory practice of field survey, aligning the archive's mathematical record with the reference-led logic its mapping already follows. Ms Ogunbanjo described the collaboration as "one more way the University is getting to know the documents it already has."

The study sits comfortably within the School's continuing attention to how its own survey records are kept, read and relied upon.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.iwts78.

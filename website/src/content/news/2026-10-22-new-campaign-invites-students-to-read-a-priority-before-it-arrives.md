A sliver of crystal held up against a window is the whole of the School of Emergent Priorities' newest invitation to postgraduate study. [_Mind the Wider Gap_](/outputs/slop-ad-mind-the-wider-8kr7xs/), a portrait campaign for the University's signage panels, asks prospective students of the Master of Priority Studies to consider what it would mean to notice a priority while it is still on approach.

The campaign takes as its example gallium oxide, an ultra-wide-bandgap semiconductor that already holds a place on the Horizon Register, the School's rolling account of priorities on approach. Its image of a student at a teaching-lab bench, with a bell jar, a tray of wafers and a long view through the glass, situates the Masters within a broader ecosystem of anticipatory capability, where the materials of the laboratory and the instruments of foresight share a single bench.

> A priority usually reaches us as a note somebody else has written, and by then the reasons have mostly been tidied away. We would like our students in the room a little earlier, while the note is still being drafted.
>
> --- [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster's small print directs readers to three published outputs, on an unlabelled cassette of silicon wafers, on the four kinds of absence the Horizon Register now asks of a paused priority, and on a School of Underlying Conditions campaign about strongly correlated crystals, for anyone who would like to read ahead of applying.

For a University that has always preferred to arrive slightly before the agenda does, the campaign is a fitting way to meet its next cohort. Prospective students can register their interest through the University's course pages.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8kr7xs.

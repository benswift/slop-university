A ring of folding chairs and a flip chart are familiar equipment wherever
innovation is done with a community. The University's newest display campaign
keeps both and changes what they are arranged around. _Place-Based Innovation
Starts with the Place_, developed in the School of Underlying Conditions under
Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, sets the workshop in a paddock, at
the edge of a freshly dug soil pit, with the spade still standing in the spoil.

> A pit is the one meeting I know of where everybody faces the same way, which
> is down. I am grateful to everyone who has stood at the edge of one with me
> and declined to hurry.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign brings the School's soil survey into the vocabulary of the living
lab, positioning the described profile as a setting in which place-based
engagement can be anchored, layer by layer, in the place concerned. It rests its
case on depth of engagement, a quality the School is well situated to
appreciate, and points readers on to three of the University's published
outputs.

The University regards the campaign as an encouraging early instance of its
newest school lending its methods to the wider engagement agenda, and as a
reminder that a participatory setting has a floor. It will appear on landscape
screens across the signage rotation over the coming teaching period.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ahzyhf.

[_The Neighbour Who Was Never There_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-reference-bystander-ag0m4x/),
a new paper by [Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/), brings the
jurisprudence of legal fictions to an unexpected setting: the written decisions
of a rural panel that hears complaints of pesticide spray drift between
neighbours.

The paper follows a single figure through the panel's archive, a modelled
adult standing at the boundary fence for one pass of the sprayer, and shows how
it moves from illustration to standard to settled fact across four editions of
the panel's practice note. Setting that figure beside the reasonable person of
negligence, it surfaces a quiet inversion at the heart of the panel's
reasoning, and closes with a measured, practical account of how a fiction can be
retained and still kept honest.

The School of Emergent Priorities regards the work as a strong early return on
its commitment to doctoral scholarship that travels between disciplines, and as
a timely contribution to the University's wider conversation about the
standards that come to stand in for the people they describe. It positions the
School to take part in that conversation with a doctoral voice that is
attentive to records, to institutions and to the small grammatical choices
through which both change.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ag0m4x.

> I owe an apology to a number of second editions. I had always put the difficulty of their correlation tables down to the compilers.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

When a soil map is reissued, its units are commonly given new numbers, and somebody has to keep the table that says which old number became which new one. _Swaps Are Free: The Real Rank of a Relabelling Tensor_, by Dr Obiora and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, asks how simple a description that table admits, and answers the question completely.

The paper stacks the old legend on the new and treats the pair as a single three-way object. It then shows that the object's complexity is the number of mapping units plus a small excess, and that the excess is decided by how the numbers were passed around: two units trading places contribute nothing, three units passing their numbers in a ring contribute one, and rings whose sizes have nothing in common share the charge between them. A worked legend of fourteen units comes out at sixteen.

The University is pleased to see its newest school carry a habit of the field office through to a theorem, and regards the result as a contribution to the foundations on which its wider data capability rests. Dr Emenike said the School "has many tables of this kind in its cabinets, and it is agreeable to know that they were only ever as complicated as they needed to be."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x2iyt7.

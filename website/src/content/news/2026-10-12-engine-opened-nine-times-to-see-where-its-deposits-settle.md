Nine times in forty hours a research engine in the School of Underlying Conditions was stopped, opened and gauged, and the readings taken on those occasions are now a poster. _Where the Deposit Stops Growing: Zonal Thickness Mapping of a Piston Crown and the Phasing Drift of One HCCI Research Engine_ is by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer.

> An engine does not ask to be opened, and this one was good about it. I am grateful to everyone who lifted the head, and to everyone who stood by while we looked.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster brings bench practice, a fixed measuring template and a single operating point into alignment around one piston. Its crown was gauged at 61 points and read as five zones from the centre outward. The inner zones had settled by the thirtieth hour, the outer ones were still building at the fortieth, and the engine's combustion timing came to rest with the inner zones.

The University counts it among its settled strengths that a school can be found with the patience to interrupt a working machine and write down what it saw each time. Dr Emenike said the project had taught both authors "to arrive at a test bed with clean hands and modest expectations, in that order".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.udi3t0.

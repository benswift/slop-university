Using a purpose-built laboratory rig, a pulse of tracer gas and a sampling needle placed two millimetres from a spark plug, two researchers from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) have asked how long the gas inside a hydrogen engine's ignition pre-chamber has been there.

[_How Many Times Has the Gas at the Spark Gap Already Burned?_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-pre-chamber-of-tnslc9/) is by [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register, and [Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate. With the injector pointed straight down the chamber, they report, the recess around the electrode holds gas that has on average been through 2.7 earlier combustion events, while the rest of the chamber is largely fresh. Angling the injector changes the picture considerably.

> Our School is used to registers that carry their oldest entries forward for years. It has been a real education to work with one that turns over in a few milliseconds and still manages to keep something back, and we have been grateful for the patience of colleagues who know engines far better than we do.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The work draws the established toolkit of chemical reaction engineering into the combustion laboratory, positioning residence time as a lens on ignition readiness and building a pathway from bench-scale characterisation to cycle-level understanding.

The University is pleased to see its anticipatory agenda arrive, quite literally, at the point of ignition.

"It is a small chamber with a long memory," said Mr Nnamani. "Getting to know it has been a pleasure, and a humbling one."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tnslc9.

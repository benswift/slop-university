The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has spent much of its short history looking down boreholes and into soil pits. Its newest research poster looks up a bell tower instead. [_Where the Band Has Been_](/outputs/slop-poster-rows-of-s7-bqhdra/), by Dr [Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Dr [Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) and Mr [Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), works through the quarter-peal book of a single tower to establish which of the possible orderings of its seven bells a band has actually rung.

> We are grateful to a ringing society that wrote down every calling for nearly four decades and then let us read it. Records are rarely kept with that kind of care, and it has been a pleasure to repay it with some mathematics.
>
> --- Dr [Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Bringing archival recovery, combinatorial reconstruction and a long mathematical tradition of reading change ringing as group theory into a single workflow, the team rebuilt 214 quarter peals change by change and set the result against the full space of possibilities. The poster finds that the band has rung a little under three in five of the orderings available to it, and that the compositions it chooses, rather than the method it rings, set that limit.

The University counts the poster among the clearest signs yet that careful mathematics is to be found wherever a community has kept a careful record. Dr Ntuli said the work "raises exactly the kind of question a school should want to be asked, and we are in no hurry to answer all of them at once."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bqhdra.

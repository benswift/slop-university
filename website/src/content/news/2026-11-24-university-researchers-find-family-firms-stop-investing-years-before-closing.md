Twenty-six years of company accounts from more than three hundred family-owned workshops sit behind [_Winding Down Without Deciding_](/outputs/slop-poster-winding-down-without-dln6j2/), a new research poster from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). Dr [Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/) and Mr [Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/) set out to learn whether a family firm's spending on machinery records its ending before anyone in the family says the word aloud.

> A poster is a good place to put a finding you would like people to stand in front of for a while. We hope this one is stood in front of by anyone who has meant to get round to a difficult conversation and has not quite got there yet.
>
> --- Dr [Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

Through a carefully staged comparison of firms that had named a successor and firms that had not, the team traced investment behaviour across the decade leading up to each owner's exit, placing the everyday rhythms of the workshop floor within a rigorous longitudinal frame. The two groups move together for years, then part: by the year of exit, firms with no named successor were investing 2.1% of their assets against 6.4% for their peers, a gap that opens around seven years out and some four years ahead of any difference in sales.

Mr Ilesanmi said the project had given the team a new respect for "how much care can go into not buying a lathe."

For the University, the poster deepens a long-standing interest in the endings organisations arrive at without ever settling on them, and adds a careful financial record to the Horizon Register's growing understanding of how intentions are set down and allowed to lapse.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dln6j2.

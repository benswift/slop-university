Slop University has published the results of a six-week pilot instrumenting
eight independently run dry-cleaning counters with a compliance dashboard of
its own design, built to hold a printed ready-by promise against the moment
a customer is actually telephoned to collect it. The University counts the
deployment among the clearer instances yet of its evaluation-ecosystem
agenda reaching past its own walls and into a counter nobody at the
University has ever stood behind.

> An instrument like this earns its keep by surviving contact with a
> workroom that was never built around it. Six sites narrowing was the
> finding we hoped for. The two that didn't move taught us more about the
> rack than the dashboard ever will.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons,
> School of Continuous Improvement

The instrument, designed and led by Sabel with Dr Marit Osayande and Dr
Mirela Hanke, reads a ticket's printed promise and a shop's own call-back
log without touching either till's existing software, then displays a
rolling adherence score at the counter. Across the pilot, mean drift between
promise and pickup narrowed at most, though not all, of the eight sites; a
parallel arm that covered the display for three days at every site found
the narrowing held regardless of whether anyone could actually see the
score.

"We built the dashboard to change how the rack gets worked," said Dr
Osayande. "What we may have measured instead is something the rack was
already doing on its own."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.e8o4qs.

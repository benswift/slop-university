> Every institution has a sentence it repeats so often that it no longer hears it. We spent a year hearing one properly, and I think the School is a more attentive place for it.
>
> --- Ms [Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The sentence in question sits beneath every C-reactive protein result issued by one regional pathology network, and it is the subject of [_Interpret in Clinical Context_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-lachmannian-recension-juq5ge/), a new research poster led by Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with Ms Balogun and Ms [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

The project carries the long-established methods of textual criticism into the everyday documentary infrastructure of laboratory medicine. Forty-one versions of the comment's template, gathered across two decades and six sites, were compared word by word and arranged into a family tree of the wording, aligning the classical edition with the contemporary report form.

Among the poster's findings: of the fifteen versions the team kept, the one printed today has drifted furthest from the original, while the satellite laboratories that copied it still print the sentence it lost. Dr Tredinnick said the team "would be glad to hand the edition to anyone with a use for it, and gladder still to hear what they make of it."

For the University, the edition adds one more document to the lengthening list of everyday texts its scholars have found worth reading twice.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.juq5ge.

The School of Continuous Improvement's Evaluation of Evaluation program has
spent its opening years turning that same instrument --- the audit --- on the
University's other review panels, meta-audits, and quality cycles. This
teaching period the program turns it on itself, opening a review of its own
five-yearly review, convened by Dr Dagny Okafor.

> A program built to ask what a review actually changes owes itself the same
> question eventually. Better we ask it than wait for somebody else to.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

Dr Okafor's own research distinguishes what a meta-audit re-certifies from
what it genuinely changes; the self-review borrows the same distinction, with
a panel drawn from within the program's own membership standing in for the
outside reviewer the program doesn't otherwise have. The School frames the
exercise as evidence the program's method travels wherever it is pointed,
rather than an exception made for itself.

"It would be a strange kind of methodologist who exempted her own program
from the question she puts to everyone else's," said Associate Professor
Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, welcoming the review. "The
interesting part is whether the answer changes anything."

The review is expected to report within the current teaching period; its
panel and terms of reference are to follow.

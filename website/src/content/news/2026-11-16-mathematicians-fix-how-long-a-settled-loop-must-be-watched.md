A response is settled only when nothing later unsettles it, and a new paper
from two of the University's schools sets out exactly how long an engineer must
watch before saying so.

In
[_Settled in Retrospect_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-settling-time-qfi6gw/),
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) bring the
theory of last-exit times to bear on the everyday practice of commissioning and
acceptance testing. Combining closed-form analysis with a simulation programme,
the authors connect the familiar staircase of settling time against damping to
the window an online test should wait, and show that a single constant governs
both.

> In the registry we were asked every week whether a point still stood, and the honest answer was always that it stood so far. It has been a pleasure to find engineers living with the same answer, and to give it a little more shape.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Associate Professor Beng, who leads the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the work
would change how the Lab reads its own paperwork. "We keep a cupboard of
acceptance schedules that will look different after this, which is the right
kind of homework for a lab to be set."

The University counts the paper among a growing body of work in which its
schools meet over a shared question and leave with a sharper one.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qfi6gw.

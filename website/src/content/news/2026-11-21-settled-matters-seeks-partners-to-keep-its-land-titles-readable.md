Partners and donors are being invited to help the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) look after a collection that has not finished changing. [_The Record Is Still Reacting_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-record-is-4o6cyj/), a new case-for-support brochure, sets out why the School keeps its superseded certificates of title, and why keeping them is becoming a matter of chemistry as well as law.

> Everyone who ever wrote on these titles assumed somebody would read them again. The School would like to keep that assumption honest, and would be glad of company in doing it.
>
> --- Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Across three feature sections, the brochure situates the collection where land-law scholarship meets materials stewardship. It follows the burdens that ride forward from one certificate to the next, the iron-gall ink that browns as it oxidises and goes on working on the sheet beneath it, and the courts and tribunals that must decide when gradual change amounts to damage. Each strand is anchored in published work, with seven studies from three Schools cited by title and DOI, and in the School's growing partnership with colleagues in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Lead of the Received Readings Group, said the collection "has a habit of turning up in research nobody expected it to join, and the Group has stopped being surprised by it".

The University counts the collection among the quieter foundations of its research in land law, and welcomes the conversations the brochure is designed to start.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4o6cyj.

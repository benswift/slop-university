A flood levee that has kept one small inland river town dry for a century has
also, according to a new research poster from the School of Continuous
Improvement, been kept for a steadily changing cast of people. [_Who the Levee
Is For_](/outputs/slop-poster-who-the-levee-fr5tdl/) follows the levee through
seven raisings and the council and levee-trust minutes that authorised them.

> Every institution builds for somebody it will never meet. It was a privilege
> to spend time with a town that wrote that somebody down so carefully, and I
> suspect the School will be looking at its own minutes a little differently
> for a while.
>
> --- Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The poster brings the philosophy of responsibility into conversation with the
everyday documentary infrastructure of local flood protection, tracing how
the levee's future beneficiaries move through the minutes from "our children"
to future ratepayers and, in recent decades, to the asset's users. Working
across 398 passages, the authors situate each shift alongside the financing
and planning settings of its raising, surfacing the relationship between how
a community describes its future and what it understands itself to owe it.

The project brings together Mr Nwabueze with Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral
Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, and Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer
and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register, whose work on what becomes of an
institution's priorities informed the reading. "The long view is never
finished," Dr Solheim said. "Someone is always adding the next layer."

The study sits within the University's continuing investment in the
humanities of infrastructure, and is supported by the Anticipatory Capability
Fund.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fr5tdl.

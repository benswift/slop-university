A fusion device that has stood idle over a weekend does not begin the week where it left off, according to [_Friday's Fluence_](/outputs/slop-paper-start-of-week-fuelling-in-pdzfb4/), a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). The steel and graphite surrounding the plasma hold on to some of the hydrogen fed into each discharge, and how much they still hold on a Monday morning depends a good deal on how hard the device was worked the Friday before.

> The operators have always known that Mondays were different. What the paper offers them is a way of writing it down, and the School is grateful they have let us try.
>
> --- Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Across a fourteen-week campaign of 1,386 discharges, Dr Tredinnick and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) brought together shot-by-shot particle accounting, between-shot gas monitoring and a control-oriented modelling approach to build an operational picture of the wall as an active partner in fuelling. In the campaign's final weeks the model's account of the wall informed the gas settings for each session's opening discharges, with first shots arriving within a few per cent of their target.

"Feed-forward work tends to begin with a logbook and a good deal of patience," Associate Professor Beng said. "We had both, and the next device will tell us which mattered more."

The paper adds to the University's expanding presence in the physical sciences, and to the School of Settled Matters' long interest in what a surface retains once the business of the day is concluded.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pdzfb4.

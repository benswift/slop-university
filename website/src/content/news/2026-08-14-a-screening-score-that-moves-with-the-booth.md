Match a shopfitter's completion docket against a monthly service figure, and a
pharmacy group's screening booth turns out to be part of its own instrument.
_Referred by the Room_, a research poster released today by the School of
Continuous Improvement, sets 26 months of hearing screening outcomes at 44
pharmacy sites beside the refurbishment records that moved 19 of those booths
from one part of a shop floor to another.

Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Dr Solveig Adeyemi and Dr Osei Vandermeer report a
referral rate that steps 6.8 percentage points in the month of a move and holds
there for the rest of the window, across 61,908 screenings. Sites whose booth
stayed put show no break at all. Booth position accounts for 41 per cent of the
variation between sites; the person conducting the screening accounts for four.

> The people who ran these screenings did nothing differently, and we would
> like that on the record before anybody reads a table. We are grateful to the
> counter staff who let us watch a year of their work to establish it.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints, School
> of Continuous Improvement

The group's service scorecard reads a low referral rate as a well-calibrated
screen and publishes the figure by site each month. It does not hold the
relocation dates. "A screen is a room and a threshold before it is a result,"
Dr Solveig Adeyemi said. "We would like both halves in the audit trail."

The University regards the poster as a useful addition to the School's
evaluation-ecosystem agenda, and as the kind of question it would rather ask of
itself than have asked of it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cp0q9k.

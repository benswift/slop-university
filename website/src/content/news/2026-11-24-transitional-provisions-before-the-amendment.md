When a club changes its rules, the hardest question tends to arrive on the
night the vote is carried: what happens to everything already under way. A new
project funded by the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/)
means that, for one regional sporting association, the answer will be ready
well before the question.

[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and
[Mr Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of
Emergent Priorities, receive $583,746 under the Anticipatory Capability Fund.
It is the first award for each of them.

The pair will prepare the savings and transitional provisions for the
association's next amendment to its constitution, ahead of any member putting
one forward. The work brings statutory drafting practice into dialogue with
the everyday administration of volunteer sport, taking stock of the
arrangements its clubs currently hold and assigning each a settled place
under rules not yet written.

> The clause is in good shape. Whenever the members decide what they would
> like to change, the drafting at the back of the document will be glad of
> their company.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Mr Ilesanmi said he was "pleased to begin, for once, with the part of a
document that usually gets written last."

The School regards the award as a fair measure of the seriousness with which
it treats the changes nobody has yet asked it to make.

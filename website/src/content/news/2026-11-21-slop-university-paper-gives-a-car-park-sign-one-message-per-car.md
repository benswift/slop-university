Fewer drivers are climbing onto a full level during the morning rush at one of the University's campus car parks, following [_Four Free on Level 3_](/outputs/slop-paper-level-by-level-guidance-signs-1gj1sv/), a new paper from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). The work reads the familiar row of free-bay counts at the entrance as a control system in its own right, and finds that a sign which tells every arriving driver the same thing will tend to send them all to the same place.

> I have spent most of my career watching one number reach a great many readers at the same moment. It has been a real pleasure to work on a case where the kindest thing to do was give each reader a slightly different one.
>
> --- Dr [Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Working from the building's own gate and ramp records, the team assembled a detailed picture of how the car park fills each morning, tested a series of sign arrangements against it, and then took the most promising into the building itself over eight alternating weeks. The resulting display, which names a single level for each car as it passes the boom gate, brought the share of drivers reaching a full level down from 17.6% to 3.4%, with around four in five following its suggestion.

The School regards the paper as a fine example of the everyday infrastructure its researchers are increasingly ready to take seriously. "The Observatory usually thinks in quarters," said Dr [Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory. "It was a welcome change to spend a season thinking in minutes, and we expect to carry some of that back with us."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1gj1sv.

What does it cost to let one device off a rule that binds all of its
neighbours? In
[_Burdens That Run with the Subtree_](/outputs/slop-paper-constraints-registered-at-ncrgdv/),
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), gives that question an
exact answer, and a proof to go with it.

The paper treats the layered networks that carry connected devices, from the
cloud through intermediate fog nodes to the edge, as mathematical objects in
their own right, and follows a single constraint as it is stated high up and
inherited by everything below. Its theorems establish when the most economical
way of stating such a constraint is the only one, when finding it becomes
intractable, and why releasing a single device can oblige an operator to
restate the rule for every one of its siblings.

> A burden is easy to put on and a great deal of work to take off, and I wanted
> to know precisely how much work. It turns out you can count it.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The publication extends the School's standing interest in what binds, and for
how long, into the infrastructure of connected computing, and the University
regards that kind of reach across disciplines as the natural habit of a
research culture that takes its questions wherever they lead. Dr Tredinnick
described the paper as "the land-law course, conducted in lemmas", and said she
expected its open conjecture to keep her occupied for some time.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ncrgdv.

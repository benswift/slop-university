The University's screens this week carry an invitation from the School of
Underlying Conditions. [_Someone Still Reads the Well_](/outputs/slop-ad-someone-still-reads-yajffa/),
a landscape display poster released today, asks the public to come to Open Day
this spring and hear [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) speak on
Italy's emptied hill towns.

The poster's image is a quiet one: a stepped town with its shutters closed, a
field scientist kneeling at a monitoring bore beside an old stone well, and the
ground cut away beneath them down to the water. The talk takes up the same
scene, bringing the School's groundwater fieldwork into conversation with the
long economic history of a country's interior, and with the instruments that
remain in service after the reasons for installing them have moved elsewhere.

> An Open Day is the one day we get to show people the part of the work that
> carries on after everyone else has gone. I hope some of them bring questions
> we have not thought to ask.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign extends the University's practice of placing its scholarship
where people wait. Its small print directs readers to a case for supporting
the study of what post-war Italy covered, kept or moved out of sight, to the
School's poster on reading drawdown at a monitoring bore, and to a study of
loyalty accounts that go on being counted after their holders stop coming in.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yajffa.

A shopping centre's trolley corrals were never designed to decide who
parks near the doors. A new paper from Slop University's School of
Emergent Priorities reports that, at one suburban centre, they now do.

The Return-Turnaround Quadrant crosses whether a shopper's trolley made
it back to its correct corral against how long the return took, sorting
eight weeks of RFID-logged returns into four cells. Vehicles landing
twice in the worst of the four, Stranded, within a rolling week were
referred to the centre's Car Park Amenity Subcommittee, which has since
adopted the classification as automatic and binding grounds for moving a
shopper's preferred bay to the outer tier for the rest of the quarter,
with no discretionary override retained.

> The reminder chime was the only part of this anyone thought to
> question, and it turned out to be the one part that didn't matter.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority
Studies, said the finding confirms something the School's
anticipatory-capability agenda has assumed for some time: "a signal worth
watching rarely announces itself as one." The University points to the
study as an example of how far an existing dataset can be made to work
once someone finally asks it a new question.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ntetj5.

An invitation nobody answers costs a journal more time than one politely refused, according to a new paper from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), and the remedy it proposes is a shorter wait rather than a longer list.

[_Chase Sooner, Invite No More_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-many-referees-2o0l4y/) is led by [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas, with [Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory.

The authors bring decision-analytic modelling to the everyday rhythm of peer review, calibrating a simulation of one editorial office's invitation stream on 3,702 of its records and weighing twenty recruitment policies against priorities set out by the office's own handling editors. What emerges is a recruitment posture that keeps two invitations in play and follows up an unanswered one within days, aligning speed for authors with care for the referees on whom the wider scholarly ecosystem relies.

> Every office keeps a clock it set once and then stopped noticing. Working out which clock that is has become a good part of what the Observatory is for.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

The paper takes its place in the University's sustained attention to the processes that carry scholarship from one desk to the next. "We were asked a question about numbers and came back with one about time," said Dr Okwuosa, "which seems to us the more useful thing to carry into the next conversation."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2o0l4y.

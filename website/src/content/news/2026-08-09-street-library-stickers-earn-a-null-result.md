> A due date works because someone enforces it. Take away the enforcement
> and you're left with a sticker asking a favour, and it turns out a favour
> asked twice as firmly asks for exactly the same amount of nothing.
>
> --- Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons, School of
> Continuous Improvement

Slop University's newest paper puts a number on a hunch every street-library
steward eventually has: that a book taken without a due date might simply
stop circulating. A sixteen-week field trial across forty-eight boxes in one
metropolitan council area finds no such thing --- and no evidence a firmer
version of the same sticker would fare any better.

The trial, led by Dr Ronja Oyelaran of the School of Emergent Priorities,
split the boxes into a rotating sticker arm and an untreated control arm
across two eight-week halves, so every box spent time in both. A
photographic survey tracked how long a given title stayed on the shelf under
each arm, and a second comparison set a more directly worded version of the
sticker against the original polite phrasing. Neither manipulation moved the
figure.

"It's about as clean a null as a trial like this produces," said Dr
Oyelaran, "which is its own kind of finding, given how much of the informal-
sharing literature assumes the opposite." For a University that has built an
entire Indicator Commons around instruments nobody quite gets around to
retiring, a result this clean is treated less as a disappointment than as
data in its own right.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7nsyyf.

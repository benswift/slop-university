Slop University's new Strategic Plan 2026-2031 makes an interview study of how residents fold a fitted sheet the founding evidence for a university-wide standard. The plan establishes the Household Method Register, housed in the School of Continuous Improvement's Adaptive Metrics Lab, and requires the University's own Capability Sprints micro-credentials and community placement badges to cite only the terms it lists.

> A household already knows how well its own method works; what it doesn't always have is a word for it that travels to the next house.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

The Register grows out of a standing interview programme that asks residents, in their own words, how they handle tasks a home keeps but never writes down. Where a method reconfirms across a wide enough sample, the plan commits to publishing it under one agreed name and reviewing it on the same cycle as the University's other indicators; where households simply disagree, the plan leaves the disagreement on the record rather than resolving it by decree.

"The plan asks the Register to survive being wrong sometimes, which is more than most standards commit to," said Dr Osei Vandermeer, School of Continuous Improvement, a co-author of the plan's evidence base.

The Strategic Plan sets the arrangement inside the University's broader evaluation agenda, treating a household's own vocabulary as a legitimate first draft of a standard rather than a gap to be corrected on sight.

The full Strategic Plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yuml7i.

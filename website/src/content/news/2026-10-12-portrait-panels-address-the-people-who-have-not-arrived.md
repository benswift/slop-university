> A forecaster and a core logger are in the same position more often than either would choose. Each is asked to speak for something that has not turned up. We thought the two might like to be introduced.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The introduction is made by _Plan for the People Not Here Yet_, a display advertisement now in rotation on the University's portrait panels. It was prepared by Associate Professor Uzoma with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, both of the School of Underlying Conditions, and it is addressed to anyone whose work is to estimate how many residents a place will hold under a changed climate.

The panel shows a hillside of houses drawn in outline, none of them built, over a cutaway of the ground beneath. A borehole runs down through the layers and is blank for one interval. Under the campaign line the advertisement names the School's paper _Tenants of the Missing Metres_, which followed 143 marked male flies through a season on the wooden blocks that stand in a core tray wherever the drill recovered no rock, and two further outputs from the School's record.

The advertisement aligns a planning audience with a body of earth-science observation, and positions the School's attention to unrecovered ground as a resource for those whose own material is, for the present, equally unavailable for inspection. It makes no forecast of its own.

The University is glad to have on its panels a claim it can also produce on request. Dr Emenike said the School "has always kept a place for whatever is missing, and it seemed reasonable to mention this to people who do the same for a living".

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jhgrsj.

What becomes of a good idea that nobody turns down? For
[Dr Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor,
Review Cadence Observatory, the question has a precise address: the "on hold"
column of a workplace idea scheme.

In [_Parked, Not Rejected_](/outputs/slop-paper-parked-not-rejected-kqwign/),
Dr Adeyemi, [Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/) of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and
[Dr Lindiwe Achterberg](/people/lindiwe-achterberg/) of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) follow 412
sustainability suggestions made by staff at one manufacturer across six years
and four review gates. Fewer than one in four was implemented and only one in
seven was declined. Nearly half were held, many for more than two years, and
those that kept moving had usually been rewritten by a manager into an argument
about cost.

> Everyone at the site wanted the ideas to come through, and the project let us see, very patiently, how that wish takes shape on a form. We are grateful to the people who let us watch it happen.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

Bringing platform records, review minutes and conversations with proposers,
managers and panel members into a single account, the study maps the suggestion
journey end to end, from first submission through the decisions, and the
pauses, that shape it. The paper extends a line of inquiry the University has
increasingly made its own: what happens to a contribution between the moment it
is offered and the moment it is acted upon.

Dr Achterberg said the team "came out of the archive with more questions than
we took in, which for a School like ours is a very good sign."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.kqwign.

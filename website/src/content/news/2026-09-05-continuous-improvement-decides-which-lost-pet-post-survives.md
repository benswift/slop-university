A neighbourhood forum rarely runs short of missing-cat posts; on a bad morning it runs short of exactly one. Slop University's newest signage campaign puts that gap to work, fronting the merge tool Dr Bram Ntuli built to settle it.

Dr Ntuli's usual work follows what a standing review format lets an institution notice about its own performance, and what a rotating cast of presenters quietly leaves off the record. The forum tool applies the same question somewhere smaller: when several residents post about the same missing cat within the same morning, the tool picks the one post the forum keeps and folds the rest into it, without a moderator weighing in.

> We're less interested in which post came first than in which one a neighbourhood decides to trust.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, School of Continuous Improvement

"A merge rule is a moderation decision that never has to call itself one," said Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory. "That's exactly the kind of instrument this School wants its name on."

The campaign carries a read-the-work strip naming three related University studies and now runs across the portrait display rotation, in matched daylight and after-dark renders.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.whpn9t.

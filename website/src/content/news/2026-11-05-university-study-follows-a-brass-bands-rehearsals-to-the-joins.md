For one contest season, the second baritone chair of a suburban brass band
doubled as a field station. From it,
[Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/), Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of
Evaluation in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), recorded
twenty-two rehearsals of a new test piece and logged each of the 1,163 times the
band stopped. Her paper,
[_Nobody Can Get to K_](/outputs/slop-paper-where-a-suburban-jlk86g/), is
published today.

> I was stopped along with everyone else, and corrected along with everyone
> else, and I would like to think the paper is better for it. The band was very
> generous with a researcher in the back row.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The study situates the weekly rehearsal within a broader understanding of how
communities of practice come to know a shared repertoire, bringing
conversation-analytic and practice-research perspectives to bear on the
band-room routine. It finds that the band's stops left the passages its
director had marked as difficult and settled, over the season, at the joins
between rehearsal letters, the places the band had most often started from and
least often played into.

The University welcomes the paper as a distinctive addition to the School's
engagement with practice, and to its understanding of how a group learns
something together one restart at a time.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jlk86g.

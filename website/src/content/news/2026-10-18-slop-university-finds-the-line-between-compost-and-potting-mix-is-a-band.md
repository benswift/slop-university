Every online shop has to put its products somewhere, and for most of them the choice is obvious. For a bag of bark-enriched garden compost it is not, and a new paper from Slop University suggests that the uncertainty is where much of the commercial action lies.

In _Where Compost Ends_, Dr Brynjar Obiora of the School of Underlying Conditions and Ms Sunniva Balogun of the School of Emergent Priorities follow 2,604 listings on a regional farm-and-garden marketplace across fourteen months. Sellers there file their own products as compost, soil conditioner or growing medium. The authors find that the boundary between those categories is less a line than a broad band holding two in five listings, and that a product's position in it shapes how often buyers meet it and which rivals it is priced beside.

> A soil surveyor learns early that the line on the map is a decision someone made in the field. It has been a pleasure to find a whole marketplace making that decision every day, carefully and in good faith.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The study brings field-survey thinking and catalogue practice into conversation with platform design, positioning the University's land-based scholarship within the evolving architecture of digital commerce. It extends a growing body of cross-school work on how boundaries are drawn, held and revised.

"I spent four years in a trade-literature deposit where one price list could belong in three boxes," said Ms Balogun. "It is reassuring to learn that the question has kept such good company."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.axzosz.

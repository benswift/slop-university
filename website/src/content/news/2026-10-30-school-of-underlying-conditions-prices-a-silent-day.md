The Basement Relations Group usually works below ground. Its latest poster turns instead to a shelf of cloth-bound cash books, kept for thirty-four years by an unnamed residential meditation retreat, and asks what a day of silence there actually cost.

[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), led the reading of [_The Tier Was the Bowl_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-account-books-1zo659/) with two colleagues from the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/): [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register, and [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the Master of Priority Studies. Across 27 surviving volumes, the fee the retreat introduced in 1987 landed within a couple of dollars of what its donors had been leaving in the bowl, and the sliding scale that followed in 1998 restored much of the old spread. The visible change was elsewhere: the week-long stay gave way to the weekend.

> The trustees kept every receipt they could, and lost a few they meant to keep. We are grateful to them for both.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

Methodologically, the poster lends the long-run price series of economic history to a setting more often studied for its wellbeing outcomes than for its ledgers, aligning receipts with arrivals and expressing every payment in constant prices so that three payment regimes can be read side by side. The seven missing volumes are reported as missing.

"There is a good deal more in a cash book than cash," said Associate Professor Lindqvist, "and we were glad to be trusted with the reading of it."

For the University, the work is a welcome reminder that habits of mind formed over a core tray travel well beyond the disciplines that formed them.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1zo659.

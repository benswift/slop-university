A working-with-children check is meant to travel with a volunteer for the length of their tenure. A new Slop University research poster from the School of Emergent Priorities asks what a club's assessed _management_ of that check is tracking instead, scoring 48 community sporting clubs against a five-level maturity model --- Ad Hoc, Logged, Verified, Monitored, Assured --- across three audit rounds in a season.

The assessed tier moved with the calendar. Scores tracked the days remaining to the next audit more closely than any independent record of onboarding practice, and clubs below Level 4 at round start were considerably more likely to drop a marginal volunteer from the roster within the following fortnight. A twelve-club holdout, assessed on a date withheld from the club itself, showed the same pattern --- confirmation, the University says, that the instrument is measuring something real about the assessment window, just not, perhaps, the thing it was built to certify.

> A tier that rises before an audit and settles after it is not measuring the club. It is measuring the audit.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied Measurement, said the finding fits a pattern the School keeps rediscovering: an instrument built to certify practice tends to end up certifying the moment it was checked.

The authors tracked the clubs across three audit rounds, assigning each a tier at every round and logging roster composition alongside it, to separate a change in practice from a change in timing. The Office of Research Outputs is now consulting on whether an unannounced, association-wide audit window would leave the tier anything left to certify.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.y8f9jx.

The University is asking its partners to take a longer view of the built
environment. [_Made to Be Mended_](/outputs/slop-brochure-made-to-be-n3h8sz/),
a case-for-support brochure led by the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), sets out
the institution's research on old masonry and invites benefactors to sustain it
over the timescale the walls themselves keep.

> Putting this brochure together, the School found it had been describing its
> own work in maintenance cycles for years without quite noticing. It took a
> wall to show us the shape of what we do.
>
> --- Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement

The brochure gathers work from across three Schools into a single proposition
for prospective partners: that repair, observation and upkeep form one
continuous research capability. Its three themes move from the curing bench,
where lime mortars are mixed and returned to over long schedules, through the
movement of water and salts in stone, to the rhythm of care that decides when a
street's walls are next attended to and by whom. Seven of the University's
published outputs anchor the case, alongside profiles of the researchers who
carry it forward.

It is a publication the University is pleased to stand behind, and one it
expects to return to as often as the walls require. Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer
and Convenor of the Living Dashboard, described it as "an early chapter in a
long conversation about upkeep, and one we would like many more people to join
before the next is written."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n3h8sz.

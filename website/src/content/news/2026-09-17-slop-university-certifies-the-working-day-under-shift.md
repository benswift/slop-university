Most maturity models the University has built stop at the door of a single room. SHIFT, introduced today in a new brochure from the School of Emergent Priorities, does not: it scores an open-plan floor's desk turnover, a shift roster's handovers, a daily stand-up's cadence, a performance-review cycle and a franchise's onboarding pathway on one combined five-level ladder, publishing a single band per site rather than five separate ones.

The ladder runs from Level 1, Incidental, to Level 5, Certified, and draws on six of the University's published studies of what a floor, a roster, a stand-up and an onboarding video actually do once someone is watching them. The brochure frames SHIFT as what those separate instruments were always missing: one shared register, rather than five filing cabinets that never talk to each other.

The rule attached to the ladder is a simple one. A site whose combined band falls below Level 3 cannot onboard new staff until a published re-audit lifts it, a restriction that carries no manager override and no exception surviving a second review cycle.

> A register nobody can walk back through isn't a record, it's a rumour with letterhead.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, School of Emergent Priorities

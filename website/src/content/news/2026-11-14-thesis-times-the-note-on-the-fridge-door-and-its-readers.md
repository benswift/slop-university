Eight seconds is what the median note on a kitchen bench took to write. What it then took to read is the subject of [_You Know the One_](/outputs/slop-thesis-you-know-the-v6ey7n/), the doctoral thesis [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/) has submitted for examination in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

Ms Ekwueme stayed with the written messages of sixty-four households for twenty-six weeks: the shopping list, the instruction about the bins, the label on a freezer container, the pages left for whoever minds the house. A holder beside each household's notepad timed the writing of 9,604 notes. Fieldworkers timed 2,950 readings in the kitchen and went along on 186 shopping trips. The thesis then sets the two sides against each other, under a definition of slop as whatever cost its maker less effort than it asks of the person who must understand it. By the time they came down, 69.0 per cent of notes had cost their readers more than they cost to write.

> A supervisor reads a thesis a good many times before it is submitted. This one repaid every reading, and I expect the School will be reading it for some time yet.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group

[Professor Marris](/people/verity-marris/) supervised the thesis with [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/). Its recommendation has been adopted as the University's Parity Rule: a document issued within the University will state the time spent producing it, carry a register of the time spent reading it, and be withdrawn when the second exceeds the first. The School regards the submission as what a doctoral program can do with a small question when it is given long enough, and the thesis is now with examiners.

The full thesis is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v6ey7n.

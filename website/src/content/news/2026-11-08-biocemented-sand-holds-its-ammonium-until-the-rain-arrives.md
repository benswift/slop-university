The School of Continuous Improvement has made a habit of asking what happens to
a piece of work after it has been signed off. Its newest research poster,
[_Held in the Crust, Released by the Rain_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-field-trial-ycjmxo/),
puts that question to a sand embankment strengthened by bacteria.

The poster is the work of [Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/), Lecturer
and Convenor of the School's Evaluation of Evaluation program, and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> A treatment is usually judged on the afternoon it is finished. The embankment
> kept its own counsel well into the winter, and the School has taken careful
> note of everything it chose to say once the weather arrived.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

Bringing field biocementation, leachate monitoring and soil description into a
single season-long account, the team followed the ammonium that the
cementing process leaves behind across a dozen instrumented plots and eight
storms. The more thoroughly a plot had been cemented, the poster finds, the less
of that ammonium left with the rinse that closes the treatment, and the more of
it waited for the rain of the following weeks.

"A crust is a soil horizon like any other," said Dr Obiora, "and it has been a
pleasure to describe one that arrived to a timetable."

For the University, the poster is an early marker of the School's reach into
the materials of the built environment, and of the value it places on staying
with a site for one more season than the schedule asks.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ycjmxo.

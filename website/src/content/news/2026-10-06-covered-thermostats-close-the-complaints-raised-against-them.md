Facilities teams do not treat a covered thermostat the way posties treat a
letterbox sticker. That is the result of a pre-registered replication across
thirty-one open-plan floors, and the University will act on it from the start of
next term.

> I expected a cover to be read as a request, and I am grateful to the floors
> for showing me how it is read instead.
>
> --- Mr Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster, "The Thermostat Cover as Closure Instrument", is by Mr Nnamani, Dr
Fenna Okoro and Dr Ingrid Vasseur. It follows the tickets raised against
sixteen covered and fifteen bare thermostats and finds that a ticket against a
covered one is closed with the dial where it was almost every time, from the
first week and whatever the size of the floor. The School of Emergent Priorities
regards the result as an invitation to the University's own estate.

Under the arrangement, a keyed cover will be fitted to every thermostat the
University controls, a ticket raised against a covered thermostat will be closed
on the day of fitting, and none will be reopened before the term ends.

"The floors were generous with their tickets, and we have tried to be as
generous with the covers," said Dr Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon
Register.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jb9x2i.

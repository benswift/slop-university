A handheld logger carried on groundwater monitoring rounds can take issue with a
reading in four ways, and one of them sends the person holding it back to the
bore more often than the other three. So reports _The Logger Objects: Stasis
Theory and the Out-of-Range Prompt on a Bore-Dipping Round_, a research poster
by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions,
with Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities, and
Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Continuous Improvement.

> I dipped bores for ten years and pressed that button without once reading what
> was written above it. It is a pleasant thing to be handed a reason to go back
> and read, and I would recommend the experience to anyone with a button.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster brings an old account of where disagreements begin into contact with
a small screen at the top of a pipe. It aligns four wordings of one prompt with
four positions an objector may take, follows 388 prompts across 14 technicians
and two dipping rounds, and reports that the wording which allowed the reading
might be right drew a second dip in 49 per cent of cases.

The University regards the poster as evidence that its three schools can be
introduced to one another's subjects without incident. Dr Achterberg said the
work had "arrived at a point where it can be shared, which is a different point
from the one where it is finished, and we are at ease with the distance between
them".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yhlnz9.

The way a workshop writes about its own cuts changes with whoever holds the pen, according to _A Cut Nobody Watched_, a new paper by [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas in the School of Continuous Improvement. Across eleven volumes of a small abrasive waterjet shop's inspection book, the paper follows three inspectors in turn as the subject of their notes passes from the jet, to the edge, and finally to the inspector.

> The School of Continuous Improvement keeps a great many records of its own. Spending time with this one has made me curious about which of ours the next person at the desk will open first.
>
> --- [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Sustained attention to individual entries sits alongside a systematic account of the grammar of all 2,214 notes, together surfacing a longitudinal shift in how a working record positions its writer, its subject and its next reader. Along the way the paper draws the inspection bench into conversation with the School of Underlying Conditions' recent work on worn auger tips and with the long recovery of cut lines across a semi-arid shrubland.

The University counts the paper among a widening body of its scholarship that treats the workshop, the field station and the counter as places where careful reading already happens, and it is pleased to see the School of Continuous Improvement extending that attention to the written traces of making.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0feq30.

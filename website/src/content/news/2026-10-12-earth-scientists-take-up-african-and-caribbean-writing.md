Describe what is there before deciding what it is: the first rule of the soil pit turns out to be a serviceable rule for the bookshelf. _The Clay in the Sentence: African and Caribbean writing, read from the ground it is set in_ is a research showcase from Slop University's School of Underlying Conditions, prepared by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group.

> Nobody in the School set out to become a reader of fiction. The books were on the shelf, the earth in them was described with some care, and it seemed discourteous not to look.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow

The brochure arranges the School's interest in three strands. The first attends to the small plot a household works for itself, as writers have set it down in passing. The second follows the words a soil is given, in the original and across translation. The third, anchored by the Basement Relations Group, mobilises the School's account of what a surface rests on toward the described landscapes of the page. Together they position the field survey as an enabling capability for the reading of place.

The University regards the showcase as an expression of its settled confidence that a discipline is best understood by what it does when it is somewhere unexpected. Dr Emenike described the work as "an arrangement between two kinds of attention that had not previously been introduced".

The showcase directs readers to six published outputs held by the University, and profiles the three researchers behind it.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.aqhcle.

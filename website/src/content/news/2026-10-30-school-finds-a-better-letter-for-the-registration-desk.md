> Every Sprint begins at the registration table, and in a sense so does everything we do. It is good to see that first table given the attention it has always deserved.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

[_Where to Split the Alphabet_](/outputs/slop-poster-where-to-split-h49kz5/), a new research poster from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), asks which letter should mark the boundary between two badge desks at a one-day workshop. Its answer is K. The poster is led by [Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/) with [Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/), Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group.

Built from 1,763 sign-ins across 41 Capability Sprints workshop days, the study brings the decision sciences' simulation toolkit to bear on the arrival experience, aligning arrival patterns, badge-tray dynamics and surname distributions within a single comparative framework. Against the long-standing A–L and M–Z arrangement, a split at K brings the 95th-percentile wait down from 5 min 50 s to 4 min 36 s, with J and L close behind.

Professor Marris described the project as "a welcome reminder that foresight is at its most useful when it is pointed at the next forty-five minutes".

The work sits comfortably within the University's growing tradition of bringing its full analytical capability to the moments other institutions leave to chance.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.h49kz5.

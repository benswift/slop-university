The alarm stops, the stairwell empties, and everyone gathers on the footpath
outside. What happens next is supposed to answer one question: is anyone
still inside. A new Slop University survey finds that answer sometimes comes
from a list, not a look.

> Every one of these numbers starts as someone standing somewhere, deciding
> what counting means today. We think that decision deserves more attention
> than it usually gets.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

Dr Adegoke and Dr Iben Chikere, of the School of Emergent Priorities,
surveyed 387 fire wardens across 64 commercial office buildings on how they
usually run the muster-point roll call once occupants have assembled after a
drill. Wardens most often reported ticking names off the building's pre-drill
sign-in or swipe-card log without taking a fresh count, ahead of a headcount
cross-checked against that log and an independent headcount taken cold. Use
of the tick-only method rose from 22% among wardens surveyed early in a
building's insurer-mandated annual drill quota to 61% among those surveyed
once most of the year's required drills were already logged, a pattern that
held equally for dedicated and rotating wardens and for those recently
accredited.

Dr Chikere said the finding fit a pattern the School of Emergent Priorities
keeps rediscovering about its own subject matter: "a gap between what's
stated and what's done doesn't announce itself. Someone has to go and ask."

The survey extends the Strategic Drift Survey's core measurement --- the
distance between a stated procedure and the one actually followed --- into a
workplace act most readers will recognise from the inside.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ce2j7r.

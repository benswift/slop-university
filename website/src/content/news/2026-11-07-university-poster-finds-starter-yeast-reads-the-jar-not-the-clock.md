The yeast in a well-kept sourdough starter appears to get ready for its next
feed before the flour arrives. A research poster from the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) finds that it
does so by reading the culture's rising acidity, and that the keeper's hour has
little to do with it.

[_Ready Before the Flour_](/outputs/slop-poster-whether-a-sourdough-jvgp4h/)
is the work of [Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate
in the School, with [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior
Research Fellow in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/),
and [Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/), Director of the
Trajectory Analytics Group.

> The School spends a good deal of its time asking how an organisation gets
> ready for what it expects. It turned out to be a useful discipline to ask the
> same of something that expects nothing at all and is ready anyway.
>
> --- Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Across a family of starter cultures, some fed with clockwork regularity and
others on a deliberately unsettled timetable, the team paired high-frequency
chromatin profiling with continuous acidity monitoring, building a detailed
picture of how a living culture positions itself for what comes next. In the
punctual jars a set of 286 promoter regions opened a median 74 minutes ahead
of each feed; in every jar, punctual or not, the opening followed the moment
the culture soured past a common threshold, and it held through a feed that
never came.

The poster extends the School's anticipatory-capability agenda into the
laboratory, a setting the University regards as a natural next step for work
that has until now been conducted mostly in committee.

Professor Marris described the result as "the kind of finding a research group
is fortunate to be handed early, because it leaves the group with better
questions than the ones it started with".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jvgp4h.

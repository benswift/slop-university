How long should a small manufacturer promise to hold its price for a buyer overseas? [_How Long a Price Holds_](/outputs/slop-paper-quote-validity-periods-nchu50/), a paper published today by the School of Emergent Priorities, takes up the question where it matters most to a firm new to exporting: at the buyer's second order.

The paper is by [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Horizon Register, and [Dr Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/), Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee.

> We owe this paper to the firms who wrote down every price they offered, including the ones they would sooner have forgotten. The School hopes to go on learning from people who keep their records that honestly.
>
> --- [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The paper's evidence is a regional export programme's register of 2,412 quotations, each logged with its price, its currency and the number of days it was promised to stand. Quotations that fixed a price for more than sixty days won more first orders, the authors find, but were followed by markedly fewer second ones, and the difference runs through the next quotation: buyers who met an increase of more than seven per cent reordered at less than half the rate of those who did not.

Dr Vasseur said the project had taught the School "that most of its own forward look is a set of quotations, and that it might be worth checking how long each of them is valid".

The University is glad to count international trade among the fields in which it can now be found taking the longer view.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nchu50.

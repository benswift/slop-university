Whoever already has the manila envelope keeps it. That is the central result of _Footy-tipping organisers hold the envelope until they leave the floor_, a paper released today by Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Dr Anneke Tolan and Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist of the School of Emergent Priorities.

> The envelope stays with whoever already has it, and a printed card does not interrupt that.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Organiser spells were reconstructed from envelope covers, group-chat admin lists and paired informant interviews across forty-seven open-plan floors and six home-and-away seasons. Spell length was unimodal and right-skewed. It tracked remaining job tenure. A laminated card stating that the envelope changes hands each round left override unmoved, and taking the card down left the distribution where it was. The short-cycle mode reported for household leftover judgement did not appear.

The School has written a Tipping Envelope Succession Standard from that failure. The envelope is required to change hands at the leftovers-survey national median. A floor that misses a rotation has its social-club cost-centre code frozen until the next season. There is no exemption for an incumbent, and no mid-season appeal. The printed card is retained as the public face of the rotation.

"The median we imported is now the interval the envelope is required to keep," said Associate Professor Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies.

The paper is the School's account of a finding it has already written into a standing instruction. The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x2we8r.

Most of what leaves the Office of Research Outputs stays inside a repository
record. The University's newest portrait campaign takes one finding and puts
it on the street instead.

_We Timed The Countdown. Read What We Found._ enters the portrait rotation
today, built around _The Countdown Decided First_, the School of Emergent
Priorities survey of sixty-three food-truck operators and the queue-length
countdown on their ordering app. The artwork stages the countdown's glow
above a service hatch at dusk, a line of customers trailing down the
footpath, steam still rising off the grill.

> A warning that gets trusted whether or not it's right isn't a warning
> anymore, it's furniture. Operators kept the countdown up on the screen long
> after the season's own numbers had stopped backing it.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The panel carries almost nothing beyond the scene: eight words of display
type, one line of persuasion beneath them, the paper's title and doi in
small print, and a scannable square back to the University's own site.

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, welcomed
the campaign from across the University's other school, allowing that a
countdown nobody re-checks is "the same species of instrument my own lab
spends most of its time on, just running at food-truck speed instead of
quarterly."

A repository record is only half a life for a finding, in the University's
view; the rotation is where it goes to be read. The full advertisement is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.qhdnx3; the paper it advertises, _The Countdown Decided
First_, carries its own record at doi:10.5555/slop.wd1e8f.

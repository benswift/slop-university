When a number on a field sheet has been written twice, in the same blue, which
of the two did the bore give? A new research poster from the School of
Underlying Conditions answers the question for one decommissioned monitoring
network, and answers it from the ink rather than the handwriting.

The poster, by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma and Ms
Ylva Onyekwere, returns to field sheets held by the School's Basement Relations
Group. Bringing laboratory chemistry into conversation with the archive, the
team built a profile of each correction and set it beside the reading it
overwrote. Of 200 sampled corrections, 151 were made in a different pen from the
original entry, and most of those drew the reading back towards the bore's
previous visit.

> Every sheet in the store has been waiting for a question it could answer. This
> one turned out to be written in the ink.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The University counts the work among the clearest expressions yet of its
standing commitment to records that can account for their own history. The
corrected values remain in the digitised series, now held alongside the readings
they replaced, so that the network's record carries both.

Associate Professor Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group, described the
poster as "one part of a longer conversation the School is having with its own
holdings, and one it fully expects to keep having."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sumuzl.

> An appeal doesn't have to work to be worth lodging. It only has to exist.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

Slop University's newest recruitment campaign for the Master of Applied
Measurement takes its image from a junior sports club noticeboard: a freshly
taped team-selection cut-list, and beside it, the season's appeal-window
survey, already half filled in.

Unveiled today under the line "We Track The Appeal Nobody Expects To Win,"
the campaign runs across the University's bus-stop panels and campus digital
screens, in matched daylight and evening renderings, ahead of the program's
next teaching-period intake.

The pitch draws on the School of Continuous Improvement's own account of
noticeboard cut-lists and the satisfaction survey that follows each one: an
appeal window that has yet to overturn a single selection, returned at close
to full rate every season regardless.

"Nobody hands in that survey because they expect the team sheet to change,"
said Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab.
"They hand it in because somewhere to put the feeling matters almost as much
as the outcome, and a program that teaches you to tell those two things
apart is doing something worth enrolling in."

The Master of Applied Measurement is now taking applications for the
current teaching period. The full advertisement is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.q34ufg.

By turning one of materials science's most exacting surface techniques on the
plain steel screen at the bottom of a groundwater bore, the School of Underlying
Conditions has found a record that no one knew was being kept.
[_Below the Last Dip_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-standing-water-rsjbzq/), a
research poster released today, reads the thin film that forms on a bore screen
and finds in it the level at which the water last stood.

The poster is the work of [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior
Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, and
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group.

> Every bore I have known has outlasted the reason it was drilled. It is heartening to think the University can now keep faith with them a little longer.
>
> --- [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Setting high-vacuum surface chemistry alongside the long archive of field
readings, the team examined twelve screen sections recovered at decommissioning,
reading each at two depths into its surface film and placing the results beside
the bore's own record. On every screen the deeper layer agrees with the log. On
the four screens whose bores went unvisited in their final years, the outermost
layer places the water well below anything the log records.

Associate Professor Uzoma said the project had "given the core shed a new kind
of neighbour", and that the Basement Relations Group would keep room for
whatever the screens have to say next.

The poster adds to the University's patient attention to what remains in the
ground once the paperwork has ended.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rsjbzq.

Two jars, one from the surface of a garden bed and one from a spade's depth below it, are all the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) now asks of a household wishing to know what its ground is doing.

[_Bring Us a Spadeful_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-school-of-6s4tc0/), a new brochure by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, with [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, and [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds in the School of Continuous Improvement, sets out a civic programme built around a colour. The School prepares its own tetrazolium salts at its synthesis bench, and visitors watch soil and seed they have brought from home take that salt up and turn it red.

The programme aligns the School's synthesis capability with its field practice and its neighbourhood presence, so that one bench serves as reagent store, teaching laboratory and public meeting place at once. Gardeners leave with a graded row of tubes and a surveyor's account of them; seed libraries leave with a shared vocabulary for what they keep.

> Every seed saver already has a question about the packet at the back of the drawer. It seems right that a university should offer to sit with that question for an evening, and to leave the answer on the table where everyone can see it.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, School of Continuous Improvement

The brochure extends the University's settled conviction that its laboratories belong as much to the street outside as to the people who work in them. "We have always carried the ground back to the laboratory," said Dr Emenike. "This is the first time we have asked the ground's owners to come with it."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6s4tc0.

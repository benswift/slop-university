There is a wetland on the University's portrait signage panels, and nobody
designed it.

[_Nobody Designed This Wetland_](/outputs/slop-ad-the-wetland-nobody-jblnyh/) is
a display poster by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior
Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, and
[Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/), Director of the Trajectory
Analytics Group in the School of Emergent Priorities, and it takes an essay in
environmental ethics as its hook.

> Water goes where the ground lets it, and it is never in a hurry. I would like
> our students to come to a wetland the same way, by the low road, and to stay
> until the level settles.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster translates Professor Marris's
[_What the Dredge Owes_](/outputs/slop-paper-what-is-owed-ykfv8n/) into the
visual grammar of the bus shelter, setting a detention basin gone to reed, its
grated outlet sunk in silt and an excavator waiting on the bank, beneath a single
line of type. In its small print it extends the invitation across the
University's wider work on water held and released, from the salt a monitoring
bore leaves behind each time it is purged to the parcels of treated effluent a
small works sends down its creek at dusk.

The campaign sits comfortably within the University's long-standing interest in
what infrastructure becomes once the maintenance schedule looks the other way,
and in the students who might one day be asked to decide.

Professor Marris said she was pleased the essay had found "a second life on a
bus shelter, which is more than most basins are ever offered".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jblnyh.

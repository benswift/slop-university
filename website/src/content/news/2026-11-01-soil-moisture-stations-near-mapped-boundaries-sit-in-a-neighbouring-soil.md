An auger, a hand lens and a folder of station records are the whole apparatus
behind
[_Which Soil Is the Probe In?_](/outputs/slop-poster-which-soil-is-apkhnf/), a
new research poster led by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior
Research Fellow in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the same
School, and [Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and
Convenor of Capability Sprints in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> There are forty-two stations on that network and we have now stood beside
> every one of them with a spade. The School intends to keep going back, and I
> expect the stations will have more to tell us each time.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The team described the soil beside each station's buried probes, at each probe
depth, and set it against the soil class the station record had taken from the
regional soil map. At 19 of the 42 stations the two did not agree, most often at
the deepest probe, and most often where a station stood close to a boundary
drawn on the map.

The work brings the patient craft of the soil surveyor into dialogue with the
stewardship of environmental monitoring data, situating the humble station
record within a broader conversation about where a dataset's descriptions come
from and how they travel. Field texturing for the study was anchored by the
reference shelf of soils of known texture recently supported through the
Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund.

The poster takes its place among the University's quieter achievements, the
kind measured in auger holes rather than headlines.

"Capability Sprints has always done its best work when a sprint ends in a
paddock," said Dr Nwachukwu. "It was a privilege to be handed an auger."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.apkhnf.

> A laboratory notebook and a letter to the Philippians are not often read in the same afternoon. I hope the essay invites a few more people to try it, and I would be very glad to hear what they make of the pairing.
>
> --- Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

With [_The Sacrificial Template_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-kenotic-theology-id9k46/), the School of Continuous Improvement carries its long interest in how work is shaped into an unexpected setting: the pharmaceutics bench, and the tiny vessels built there to carry a medicine and then fall away.

Combining two semesters of embedded observation with a close reading of 211 laboratory notebook entries, the essay positions the everyday language of formulation science within a sustained theological conversation about self-giving. It distinguishes the templates that are removed before the work begins from the carriers that come apart in the course of it, and invites readers to consider what the second kind of ending might be owed.

The University takes quiet satisfaction in an output that moves so easily between the seminar room and the laboratory, and in a doctoral program that makes room for such movement.

Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, welcomed the essay as "a patient piece of work about things that are designed not to be noticed, which is a kind of attention the School has always valued."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.id9k46.

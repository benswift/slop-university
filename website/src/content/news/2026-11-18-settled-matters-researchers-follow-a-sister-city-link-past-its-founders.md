The School of Settled Matters studies what becomes of an institution's founding purpose once it passes into later hands. With [_Two Towns, Not the World_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-twinning-committee-u8fi8a/), it takes that interest out of the law library and into a town hall set for a reunion dinner.

The poster is the work of [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Dr Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/) and [Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). Members who joined the association since 2020 score 3.8 out of ten for openness to trade and migration, against 7.4 for its founders, and they are its firmest supporters.

> The association has asked whether the University might send someone along to next year's dinner. We have said yes, and we have said we will bring the poster.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The team set a survey of 118 members against a household survey of the surrounding town, reading both alongside membership rolls, a decade of hosting records and two hundred sets of committee minutes, to build a whole-of-association picture of who carries a civic link forward and on what understanding.

The University welcomes the poster as a careful account of an institution renewing itself on terms its founders did not foresee, a subject on which it takes a close and continuing interest. "Every visitor-night in that ledger was somebody's spare room," said Dr Oyelaran. "We have tried to treat the figures with the same courtesy."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u8fi8a.

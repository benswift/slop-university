The School of Underlying Conditions does not ordinarily publish in theology, and the University is content to record that it now has. _Non Constat: The Negative Verdict in Image Forensics, Read Beside the Authentica and the Image Not Made by Hands_ is a sole-authored essay by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, on what an examiner is able to say at the end of a report about a photograph.

> The examiners I read will not be named, and I gather that suits them. I should like them to know that somebody went through all 212 of their last sentences and thought each one was the right length.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The essay brings a body of contemporary verification practice into conversation with a long-standing interpretive tradition, mobilising the vocabulary of one to illuminate the settings of the other. It sorts the reports' closing sentences into four forms. In 131 the examiner states that nothing was found, in 29 that something was, and in no report is an image declared authentic. Where an image arrived with an attested chain of custody, which was the case in 47 reports, the desk checked the chain in 41 and did not go on to examine the image. Dr Emenike sets this beside the sealed certificate that accompanies a relic.

The University takes the paper as a welcome indication that its newest school reads beyond its own shelves, and that the shelves in question are well kept.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vca5f7.

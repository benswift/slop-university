Slop University has turned the instruments of classical papyrology on a
thoroughly modern correspondence.
[_The Scribe's Narratio_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-scribe-s-narratio-q0w73o/),
a new paper by [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate in
the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), reads
212 letters sent to one regional cancer charity's hardship fund in the way
scholars read petitions from Roman Egypt: by their form, their formulae and the
hands that wrote them.

> The School spends a good deal of its time writing to people. Spending a year
> reading letters written to someone else has been a useful way of noticing
> that, and I suspect we will go on noticing it for a while.
>
> --- Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Situating the household covering letter within a long lineage of petitionary
writing, the study brings documentary philology into conversation with
contemporary cost-of-illness scholarship. It maps the architecture of the
hardship letter, identifies six drafting hands across the archive, and sets one
household's request beside its own redraft to surface how a narrative of costs
is shaped between the kitchen table and the committee room.

The paper is a confident early statement of the School's growing range, and
the University counts it among the clearest signs yet that its continuous
improvement agenda extends as comfortably to the second century as to the
present one.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q0w73o.

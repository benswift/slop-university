A new Slop University brochure revisits one of the School of Emergent Priorities' own instruments to see whether it travels. The Referral Diversity Index, which tracked a collapse in recommendation variety across forty-one neighbourhood group chats, has now been carried to a shire compliance counter's printed contractor referral list -- and found no comparable collapse.

Within the School, the result is being read as evidence the Index is doing exactly the disciplined work it was built for: naming the one setting, out of however many it might eventually visit, where the earlier effect simply didn't hold. The shire's own compliance office has drawn a different conclusion, and is extending the AI-assisted lookup tool behind the trial to its other referral counters.

The brochure situates the finding inside a wider showcase of University research reaching into local government and civic life, featuring six previously published outputs alongside profiles of the researchers behind them.

> Every setting is its own test. We don't assume a finding travels -- we go and check, and we publish either way.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Horizon Register

Dr Fenna Okoro, whose original group-chat study the new work retests, said the Index "was always going to meet a setting it didn't fit -- the question was only ever when."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3n8fao.

The rota at one small parish church still runs every night, though the lamp it
was set up to keep no longer needs anyone. What became of that rota is the
subject of
[_Aaron Shall Order It_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-liturgical-theology-bcanu7/), a
new paper from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) by
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor,
Capability Sprints.

When the parish replaced the oil lamp before its altar with a sealed lamp
needing no oil, wick or hand, its volunteers kept signing the lamp book. Their
nightly entry changed from "trimmed, filled, lit" to "came; nothing needed", and
then settled on two words: "seen burning".

> The School spends much of its time asking what a practice becomes once the
> reason for it has gone. It was a privilege to find a parish that had been
> asking the same thing, quietly and every night, for months before we arrived.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

The paper brings scriptural commentary, the philosophy of technology and the
literature of care and maintenance into conversation with a single ledger,
positioning the parish's experience within a wider understanding of how
obligations travel when the work behind them is automated. Its reading is that
the duty of tending was not retired with the oil but carried over into vigil.

The paper extends the School's long engagement with what happens after a
change is adopted, and the University welcomes theology to the growing family
of traditions that inform it.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bcanu7.

"If there's nothing else, we might leave it there." In most of the ninety
recurring meetings observed for a research poster released today by the School
of Emergent Priorities, that sentence is where the meeting stops ending for the
first time.

The poster, by Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Dr Marek Solheim and Dr Anouk Mensah, grades
each observed ending on a five-level closure maturity model, from the meeting
that trails off unclaimed to the meeting closed by a formally appointed closer
working from a written protocol. The median meeting announced its close 2.4
times before anyone left; at the highest maturity level, once.

> Every organisation already knows, in some detail, how its meetings begin.
> What the model offers is a way of taking the ending just as seriously, and
> the School has been encouraged by how readily organisations take it up.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

The study harmonises direct observation with door-sensor and call-log signals
across fourteen organisations, translating each ending into a staged capability
profile and testing that profile against the moment the room actually empties.
Maturity moved the number of endings, not their timing: median overrun at last
departure held near eleven minutes at every level.

The poster continues the University's careful interest in the moments
institutions consider finished. The full poster is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ottxbk.

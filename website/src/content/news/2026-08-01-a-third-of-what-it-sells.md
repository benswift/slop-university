Fewer than a third of the litres a service station sells are the fuel advertised
in the largest numerals on its price board. The number a motorist reads from the
road is real, and it is not what most of them buy.

_The Price Nobody Pumps_, published today in the University's research
repository, pairs the posted board price at 34 retail fuel sites across one
metropolitan region with the same sites' record of litres dispensed by grade.
Recomputing the region's price-competitiveness listing on the volume each site
actually sold, rather than on the grade its board advertises, moves sites by a
median of seven places out of 34. The site the listing names the region's most
competitive comes fourteenth.

> A pole board has only so many faces, and somewhere along the way the number of
> faces became an aggregation rule. Nobody chose it, which is the part worth
> sitting with.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee

The School of Emergent Priorities gathered 12,431 board-price observations over
eighteen weeks and set them against each forecourt's own stock reconciliation,
holding the posted prices constant and varying only the weight given to each
grade. Three restrictions of the sample, all specified before the analysis
began, left the movement where it was.

The University regards the work as a characteristic return on its anticipatory-
capability agenda: attention paid to an ordinary instrument well before anyone
has cause to doubt it. "We are not saying the listing is wrong about the board,"
said Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority
Studies. "We are asking which of the two it means to describe."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1qy5e3.

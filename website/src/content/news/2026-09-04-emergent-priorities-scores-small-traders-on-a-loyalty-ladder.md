A paper stamp card behind a till and a supermarket's real-time reward tier
are, to a shop, just two ways of remembering a regular. To the School of
Emergent Priorities, they are two points on the same ladder, and the School
has turned the distance between them into STAMP, a five-level maturity
model for small-trader loyalty and payment infrastructure unveiled today in
a new showcase brochure.

The Trajectory Analytics Group built the ladder from a habit most shops
already keep in some form, sequencing it into five named stages from
Untracked through to Predictive and testing each rung against what a till
can show a researcher without a trader explaining anything. The University's
small-commerce advisory service has adopted the second rung, Recorded, as
its floor: a shop keeping any record of a return customer, down to a name
in a notebook, remains eligible for a consultation, and one that keeps none
no longer does.

> A rung only means something once someone asks what it would take to
> climb it, and traders have started asking.
>
> --- Postdoctoral Fellow Petra Umbile, School of Emergent Priorities

"We built the eligibility floor as low as the ladder allows," said Dr Dagny
Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of the School of Continuous Improvement's
Evaluation of Evaluation program, whose Adaptive Metrics Lab audits the
terminal data STAMP's higher rungs depend on. The brochure sets the new
instrument beside seven of the University's published studies of ordinary
commerce, from a grocery chain's markdown forecasting to a food truck's
autonomous refund waiver, tracing the rung logic back to the till-float
rubric it was built from.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sf5yeh.

The School of Underlying Conditions has always started with the ground beneath a question. In [_Who Drains the Rented Field_](/outputs/slop-poster-tenure-and-subsurface-cezfe3/), its newest research poster, the School carries that habit up to the arrangements that sit on top of the soil: the leases, handshakes and cost-sharing clauses that decide whether a wet field is drained at all.

> A lease is usually read for what it allows this season. Reading a whole district's leases for what they leave to the next one has been a different kind of reading entirely, and I expect the questions it opens to outlast the leases themselves.
>
> --- Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Led by Dr [Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, with Dr Tredinnick and Ms [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, the team brought contractors' job records, auger checks and a landholder survey together across 214 fields. The result situates subsurface drainage within a wider landscape of tenure, stewardship and shared responsibility for the ground under the crop, and locates the tenure gap on the imperfectly drained soils in the middle of the gradient, where 61% of owner-operated field area is tiled against 18% of rented.

Ms Ogunbanjo said she hoped the poster would "find its way to the kitchen tables where these arrangements are usually settled".

The University counts the work among the clearest expressions yet of its commitment to research that begins at ground level and stays there.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cezfe3.

Improvement, the School of Continuous Improvement has always maintained, starts
with reading the instructions. A new research poster,
[_Allow the Sample to Forget_](/outputs/slop-poster-allow-the-sample-xspf6e/),
takes that conviction further than most, bringing the methods of literary
criticism to the operating procedures that govern a laboratory's rheometers.

The poster is led by [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate
in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/),
with [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the
Adaptive Metrics Lab, and [Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral
Candidate in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> The School writes a good many procedures of its own. Spending a year with
> other people's has made all of us a little more thoughtful about which of our
> steps we are prepared to wait on.
>
> --- Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Bringing close reading into conversation with laboratory practice, the team
attended line by line to the clauses that prepare a paste for measurement:
the pre-shear that erases its loading history, and the rest that follows. Almost
nowhere else do the procedures let the sample relax, recover and forget.
Successive revisions, the team finds, have gradually handed the end of that
rest from the sample to the clock, while keeping the courteous verb that
introduced it.

The University counts the poster among the clearest signs yet that a careful
reader belongs wherever instructions are written.

Associate Professor Beng described the corpus as "a body of writing the
laboratory has always had and has seldom been asked about", and said the team
would continue to read it with interest.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xspf6e.

The School of Emergent Priorities spends most of its research asking how an
institution decides what deserves attention first. Its newest recruitment
campaign for the Master of Priority Studies puts that question to a familiar
counter-example: the university admissions desk that scores a prospective
student's interest by how fast they answer the third follow-up email, then
quietly moves the slow repliers to the back of the queue before anyone has
read what they actually asked.

> A reply-speed score answers a question nobody in the queue was asked. Ours
> is a degree built to notice the difference.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority
> Studies, School of Emergent Priorities

Unveiled today under the line "Priority Isn't How Fast You Reply," the
campaign stages a fanned stack of intake folders across an enquiries
counter, one folder already drawn forward ahead of the rest. It joins the
University's bus-stop panel and campus digital signage rotations, the
latest instalment in the School of Emergent Priorities' case for treating
sequencing as a taught skill rather than a scheduling accident.

"A queue reordered by reply time looks decisive right up until someone asks
what it was actually deciding," said Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and
Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey. "That's most of what the degree
spends its first semester on."

The Master of Priority Studies is now taking applications for its Semester 2
intake. The full advertisement is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qpdbc9.

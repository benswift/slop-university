Slop University's Strategic Plan 2026-2031, released today, commits the
University to reading a threshold it did not have to build: the point at
which a shared loyalty platform, already running across the strip-mall
traders and food trucks hosting the University's Capability Sprints
placements, promotes a one-off customer to a logged member. Twenty-five
dollars, on the network's most common shared platform, is that point.

From 2028, a host site's placement funding for the following term will
answer to the measured causal effect of crossing its own threshold on the
return-visit rate of customers on either side of the line, logged in a
published University Threshold Register and reviewed every term. The plan's
authors describe it as proof that an institution serious about capability no
longer needs to build an instrument when one is already running at the
counter.

> A shared point-of-sale platform was never built with a university in mind,
> and that is exactly why we trust what it shows us.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints, School
> of Continuous Improvement

Dr Nwachukwu led the plan's development. Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow
in the same School and a co-author, said the Register "asks the same
question of every host site, on the same schedule, which is more than most
funding decisions can say."

The plan extends the same discipline to the University's own campus
concessions and capital-facing dashboards, and to the tariff schedules it
sets for parking, accommodation, and event access, indexing each to a
naturally occurring threshold rather than a figure set once and left to run.

The full Strategic Plan 2026-2031 is available from the University's
research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5s7536.

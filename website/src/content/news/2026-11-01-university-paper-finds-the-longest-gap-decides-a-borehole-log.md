A new paper from the School of Underlying Conditions sets a familiar object of
the core shed, the plain wooden block that stands in a tray where rock failed to
come up, beside a century-old result in mathematical analysis.
[_The Reach of a Lost Interval_](/outputs/slop-paper-bernstein-approximation-of-t0mnsu/)
is the work of
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group.

The paper treats each drill run as a sampling of the ground and asks when a log
built from what was recovered can be relied upon to settle as drilling grows
finer. Its answer turns on the arrangement of the gaps rather than their total:
in the paper's worked example, a run that returns only half its samples keeps
pace with an intact one, while a run that loses a single tenth from the middle
does not. Across a whole drilling programme, the occasional difficult run need
not unsettle the result, provided it stays occasional.

> Every logger who has ever cut a block and written two depths on it was doing
> this work first. The paper keeps them a little more company than they had
> before.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The study draws support from the Anticipatory Capability Fund, which has
underwritten the Group's attention to core-loss blocks since October, and it
extends a strand of the School's work that has already taken the unrecovered
interval to the spectra of a borehole column and to the eye of the logger. For
a School still in its first year, the University counts it a welcome sign of
how readily the earth sciences and mathematics have found a shared bench.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.t0mnsu.

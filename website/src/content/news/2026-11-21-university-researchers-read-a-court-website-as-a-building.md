> Every redesign of a website is also a decision about where things are kept. The School has enjoyed walking these five buildings slowly, and is grateful that none of the old floors had been pulled down before we arrived.
>
> --- Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, School of Settled Matters

[_The Overruled Are Kept Downstairs_](/outputs/slop-poster-an-architecture-critic-s-tf5dtm/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), brings the vocabulary of architectural criticism to the public website of an appellate court registry. It treats each of the site's five designs since 2004 as a plan, with a front door, a main hall and rooms further in.

The work aligns plan-reading practice with the registry's own archived pages and change requests, building a cross-disciplinary lens on how navigational settings shape the visibility of legal authority over time. The same 120 judgments are walked through every plan.

Its central observation concerns the judgments the court has since overruled. No redesign removed them. Each instead set them a little further from the home page, from two clicks in the first plan to more than six in the most recent, while the judgments that still stand stayed close to the entrance.

The poster extends the University's long interest in how institutions keep what they have set aside, an interest it shares with the Horizon Register in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Register, said the collaboration had "given the Register a new kind of floor plan to think with, and a few more stairs to climb".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tf5dtm.

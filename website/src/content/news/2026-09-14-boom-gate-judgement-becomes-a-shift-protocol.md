The Trajectory Analytics Group has turned eight weeks of car-park conversation into the University's newest shift instruction. Fourteen attendants at the campus visitor car park's boom gate were asked, at handover, to explain their most recent decision to wave a car through despite an overstayed ticket. Their accounts, coded into four categories, are now the Goodwill-Validation Protocol: a binding classification every attendant applies before granting leniency, with no category held in reserve for whatever the four don't cover.

> Every attendant told us something a little different, and the four categories are just where fourteen separate accounts of the same small kindness happened to overlap.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group

The findings draw on shift-handover interviews conducted across eight weeks, each opened with the same prompt and coded independently by two researchers to consensus. Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Indicator Commons, noted that the Protocol gives the University its first shift-level record of a class of decision that previously left no trace at all.

The University counts the Protocol among the clearer instances of a small, well-described practice becoming a specification the moment someone writes it down, and expects the approach to travel to other points of frontline judgement across campus in time.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.65yw6j.

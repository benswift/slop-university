Twenty-two panels of buried fabric did not survive being lifted from beneath a
drill pad, and a new research poster from the School of Underlying Conditions
declines to leave any of them out of the count.

_Torn in the Lift: Retained Grab Strength of a Nonwoven Geotextile Exhumed from
Beneath a Drill Pad_ is by Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, with Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, and Dr
Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, all of the School.

> We set out to measure a fabric and found we had also measured a lift. Both
> results are on the poster, and the bags are on the shelf for anyone who would
> like to dispute either.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group,
> School of Underlying Conditions

The poster brings field excavation, laboratory strength testing and a careful
accounting of incomplete observations into a single workflow, mapping the
separator layer of one pad zone by zone against the traffic it carried. In the
rig's turning circle, where nearly half the panels parted before they reached
the testing frame, counting them in lowers the zone's median retained strength
from 68 to 49 per cent of the original roll.

The University regards a complete tally, losses included, as one of the quieter
foundations of a trustworthy result, and welcomes a poster that extends the
habit from the core tray to the trench. Dr Emenike said the team would "keep
lifting carefully, and keep writing it down when we don't".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b4spk9.

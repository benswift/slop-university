A twelve-compound deuteration campaign in the School of Emergent Priorities has
returned a result its authors describe as instructive: the oxidation the series
set out to block did not slow, it relocated. _Blocked but Not Slowed_, a
research poster by Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, with Eivind Onwuka,
Doctoral Candidate, and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, reports the finding across every pair in the series.

> We set out to make a molecule sit still, and what we have instead is a good
> map of where it goes when you ask it not to. The School is content to call
> that a finding, and I am content to keep looking.
>
> --- Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster mobilises a paired-synthesis design across the School's analytical
capability: each coded candidate was prepared alongside its deuterated twin
from one route and one reagent lot, both incubated in the School's archived
stock and read by mass spectrometry, with the site assignments fixed before the
results were unsealed. The isotope effect was present in all twelve pairs; the
clearance figure moved by nothing worth reporting.

The University regards the work as evidence of a School willing to publish the
part of a programme that did not go to plan. Associate Professor Uzoma said the
series had "behaved exactly as it was designed to, in a direction nobody
designed".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hjiizr.

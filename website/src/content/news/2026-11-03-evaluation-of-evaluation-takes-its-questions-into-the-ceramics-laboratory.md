> Every field has a word it uses with complete confidence until someone asks it
> to hold still. We were glad to find one in materials science, and gladder to
> find the question so warmly received.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

So begins the University's account of
[_Synergy on Which Scale?_](/outputs/slop-poster-whether-two-co-dopants-9n64ft/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) by
[Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/), with
[Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/), Senior Lecturer and Director of the
Master of Applied Measurement, and
[Ms Ylva Onyekwere](/people/ylva-onyekwere/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The poster brings the analytic tradition's long conversation about dividing
causes into contact with laboratory practice, where two additives are often
combined to help a ceramic hold on to a microwave signal. Working through a
constructed case, the team shows how a single set of results can support three
quite different readings, depending on how the loss is reported, and sets out
what a claim of synergy would need to say before it could be called complete.

The University regards the poster as among the clearest signs yet that the
methods of Evaluation of Evaluation travel well beyond the review panel. Dr
Ezeigwe said the collaboration had "given the Master of Applied Measurement a
new favourite example, and its students a new favourite argument".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9n64ft.

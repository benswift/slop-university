Horizon Register standing instruction 2026/51 is in force from today. Every University-affiliated listing may post an owner-response only from a pre-registered Apology template; a reply coded as a ruling holds that listing from Trusted Local for one review cycle, with no ordinary override.

The instruction follows "'We Have Noted Your Feedback'", a research poster released today by Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Dr Anneke Tolan and Dr Lindiwe Achterberg of the School of Emergent Priorities. The poster restages the University's lobby-noticeboard taxonomy on the box beneath a booking-app review score, and reports that the original hardening does not hold.

> I had expected a ruling to settle the listing. It did not.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee

Owner-responses on 186 short-stay listings were recoded, blind, against the six-type noticeboard scheme and set against whether a second review arrived inside a fortnight. Replies coded Ruling drew that second review at a higher rate than replies coded Apology. The 2.1-times closure advantage recorded on the noticeboard does not reproduce; on the owner-response it reverses.

Ms Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, who led the recoding, said the hosts who let the School read their reply boxes "have not been named, and that was the condition on which they were read".

The instruction is now the University's public account of how a listing may reply. The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qffgtx.

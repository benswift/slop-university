Two hundred and fourteen petitions for a headstone the rules did not allow sit behind [_Refused Before It Settled_](/outputs/slop-poster-churchyard-memorial-2v6vb0/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/). Each one asked a church court to permit a polished stone, a photograph, a carved heart or a low kerb in a churchyard whose regulations made no room for it.

> I spent years telling people which decisions still stood. A churchyard answers that question without being asked, and I am grateful it let two lawyers walk its rows with a clipboard.
>
> --- Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group, School of Settled Matters

With [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Dr Penhallow brought registry scholarship and field survey together across six decades of the court's own files, aligning each judgment with the stone it concerned in 38 churchyards. The result is an integrated account of how a contested question becomes routine, and of the families whose petitions arrived before it did.

The University counts the poster among the clearest expressions yet of its conviction that a rule is best understood by walking the ground where it has been applied.

Dr Tredinnick described the work as "a beginning", adding that the School "would be glad to hear from anyone who keeps a churchyard and has wondered how its rows came to look the way they do".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.2v6vb0.

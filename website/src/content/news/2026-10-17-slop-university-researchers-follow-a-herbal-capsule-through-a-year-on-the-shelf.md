For twelve months, 840 capsules of a single herbal formulation sat in two
climate-controlled cabinets at the School of Underlying Conditions, and every so
often someone came to cut a few of them open.

_Twelve Months in the Shell: Fill–shell migration and the slow loss of
self-nanoemulsification in a soft-gelatin polyherbal SNEDDS_, a new research
poster by Dr Vigdis Emenike and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, reports what
the cabinets gave back. The herbal extract itself held steady. The soft-gelatin
shell around it did not keep to itself: glycerol and water moved from shell to
fill, and by the end of the year the fill dispersed into droplets almost five
times their original size. The same fill in HPMC capsules barely moved.

> We tend to treat a container as the end of a story. It has been a real
> pleasure to spend a year treating one as the middle of it, and I suspect the
> School will go on doing so for some time.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The poster brings stability science, colloid characterisation and sustained
attention to the post-manufacture life of a dosage form into a single frame,
positioning the capsule shell as an active participant in formulation
performance rather than a passive enclosure. It is exactly the kind of
unhurried, shelf-level inquiry the University is glad to give room to.

"Every one of those capsules was sealed on the same afternoon," said Dr Emenike,
Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions. "What they did after
that was their own business, and we were grateful to be let in on it."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.h65e16.

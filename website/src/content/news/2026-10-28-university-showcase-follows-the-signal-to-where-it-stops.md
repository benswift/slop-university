Slop University has turned its attention to a family of enzymes whose whole purpose is to bring something to a close.

[_Where the Signal Stops_](/outputs/slop-brochure-where-the-signal-c9o01p/), a research-showcase brochure led by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), introduces its readers to the phosphodiesterases, the enzymes that end the messages a cell carries with cyclic nucleotides.

> We owe this one to the students who kept asking what the flat-ended arrow was for. The brochure is our best answer so far, and an honest account of how much of it is still being worked out.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Across four features, the brochure connects bench-level enzyme characterisation with a boundary-centred reading of cellular space and a renewed attention to the representational habits of the life sciences, positioning the termination of a signal as a shared site of cross-School capability. Eight published studies anchor the showcase, among them [_The Enzyme Stays at the Door_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-materials-study-dgdqjk/) and [_Desire Lines to the Nucleus_](/outputs/slop-poster-desire-lines-to-zoaqhd/).

The publication adds to a body of University work that regards a well-placed ending as an achievement in its own right. Associate Professor Uzoma said the collaboration had given "three Schools a reason to stand at the same edge and argue, productively, about where it is."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.c9o01p.

However often a plant family is revised, the number of times anybody must check
which of two names was published first has a ceiling, and the ceiling does not
move. That is the central result of _Lumping Is Cheap: The Comparison Complexity
of Nomenclatural Priority_, a paper by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and
Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, both of the School of Underlying
Conditions, with Ms Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate in the School of
Emergent Priorities.

> None of us has named a plant, and the paper does not ask anyone to stop. It
> asks how many times the same two dates have to be looked up, which seemed to
> us a fair thing to want to know.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper anchors a consultation-centred account of nomenclatural upkeep across
three record-keeping postures, which it calls ledgers. Merging two genera is
shown to need a single consultation. Dividing one can need many more, though
only when the division carries the name away from the bulk of the genus. A
ledger that sorts its names in advance pays once and is never troubled again.

Slop University counts the paper as evidence that a question may be answered
fully by a school with no prior standing in it. Ms Balogun, who catalogued
before she counted, described the work as "a long way round to an appreciation
of people who put the date on the title page".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7gbfca.

The Living Dashboard has always had one seat, and one job for it: keep the
wall lit. The School of Continuous Improvement has now added a second, held by
Dr Mirela Hanke, whose research asks a harder question about the same
screens --- not whether they are on, but what an area has quietly started
reading off them instead of doing the reading itself.

> An indicator that survives long enough on a wall stops being consulted and
> starts being obeyed. Nobody votes on that. It just happens, screen by
> screen.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living
> Dashboard

Dr Hanke's postdoctoral work traced how a decision-support indicator migrates
from something a team checks to something a team defers to, and what gets
lost once the second reading is mistaken for the first. The deputy
convenorship gives that question a standing brief inside the instrument the
School already runs: a rolling audit of which of the Living Dashboard's
screens are still informing a call, and which have started making it.

"Every wall in this building has at least one screen nobody remembers
commissioning," said Dr Marit Osayande, Convenor of the Living Dashboard.
"Mirela's job is to find out which ones the floor has started trusting more
than the thing they were built to summarise."

The audit begins with the wall's oldest indicators and is expected to report
its first findings ahead of the dashboard's next scheduled refresh, with any
proposed retirements returning to the School through the ordinary review
cycle.

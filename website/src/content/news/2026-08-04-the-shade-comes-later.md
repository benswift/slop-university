A wide panel entered the University's landscape rotation today carrying a
suburban verge, a row of saplings in their stake guards, and seven words.

_The Shade Was Decided In A Meeting_ advertises Futures in Committee, the School
of Emergent Priorities' seminar series, and is the first of the University's
campaigns to sell a convening rather than a result. Its argument is that the
trees along a nature strip are the end a passer-by can see of a decision taken
some years earlier, in a room, with an agenda.

> A canopy target gets agreed by people who will not be in the job when it is
> met, and the street ends up with whichever species counts fastest toward it.
> Sitting with that is most of what the series does, and none of it is a
> complaint. Somebody has to decide, and a meeting is where deciding happens.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

The panel carries no finding and cites no study. One line of display type, a
sentence beneath it, the series and its School, and a code square returning to
the University's own site; a darker rendering drives the same panels after
sunset.

Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee, said the
series had spent the year on "the point where an open question stops being open
and becomes an item", and that sessions remain open to anyone who cares to sit
at the back of one.

Slop University counts a seminar series among the things worth advertising, on
the view that its research and its convening are one programme observed at
different distances. The full advertisement is available from the University's
research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.m6n9i1.

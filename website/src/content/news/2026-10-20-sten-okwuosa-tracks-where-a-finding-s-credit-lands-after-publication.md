In July, the Office of Research Outputs singled out a repository deposit filed
with every metadata field complete on the first pass, and awarded its depositor
$1,864 as the Deposit of the Quarter Prize. That depositor,
[Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), now has a film of his own. In _Where
the Credit Lands_, from the School of Continuous Improvement, the Convenor of
the Impact Pathway Atlas describes in his own voice the life a finding leads
after it is published.

> Anyone who would like to know where their own work has gone is welcome to ask.
> Most people are pleasantly surprised.
>
> --- [Dr Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and
> Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

A bibliometrician by training, [Dr Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/) walks through
three of the pathways the Atlas records: the citation that arrives late, the
narrative CV that recasts an old result for a new panel, and the recognition
that lands on different work from the one that earned it. Each marks a point
where counted impact and the effort behind it part company, the ground the Atlas
exists to survey. The film is just as clear about his own standing, with 890
citations and an h-index of 24 to his name. Among his most-cited papers are
[a taxonomy of farmers-market stallholder no-shows](/outputs/slop-paper-classifying-the-reasons-7eq7iz/)
and
[a field audit of hydration-station activations](/outputs/slop-paper-testing-whether-the-gy1qjf/).
His closing thought is that impact starts not with the citation but with the
metadata.

The School takes heart from its own scholar of misplaced credit, whose credit,
carefully filed, has stayed where it was earned.

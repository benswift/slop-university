Most of the evidence an institution acts on was written down by someone who was
not being paid to write it. _One Hour, Entered as Three_, published today, is
the University's argument for taking those people seriously, and it opens on the
example nearest to hand: the ruled book beside the car keys, where a household
records a learner driver's supervised hours until the total is large enough to
be handed across a counter.

The brochure works that example in two directions. One section sits with the
book itself, and the conventions a household settles on about what counts. The
next takes up the exchange rate underneath the total, where an hour with an
accredited instructor is entered as three, and a required figure can be reached
by driving or by conversion without the page showing which.

> We are not asking households to keep better records. We are asking
> institutions to know what they are receiving when a record arrives.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

A third section is given over to the University's public program, and to the
seminar evenings where the questions tend to arrive first. "The rooms we run are
full of people who assumed their measurement problem was too ordinary to
study," said Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds in
the School of Continuous Improvement. "Ordinary is the condition of most
measurement."

Three published Slop University findings are cited on the page by title and
identifier, each concerning a record that turned out to be doing different work
from the one it was read as doing. The University regards the brochure as the
clearest statement yet of where it believes its work belongs.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.iy69hf.

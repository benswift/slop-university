Slop University has closed a fourteen-week study of the ordinary fridge chore
chart, reporting that the household's own assignment forecasts nothing about
who ends up at the sink. The result belongs to the School of Continuous
Improvement's slower project of asking which of the institution's own
coordination habits earn the confidence placed in them.

> A chart earns its authority by being consulted, not by being right. We were
> interested in how long a household keeps consulting one that is neither.
>
> --- Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

The project instrumented forty-six households for a full school term, tagging
each fridge chart's magnets and reading presence at the sink, the bin store
and the laundry door, then setting the comparison and its stopping point
before a single week's roster was pinned up. The assigned name matched the
logged doer about as often as the household's own roster size would predict
by chance alone, and the match did not improve as the term went on.

"Every duty board we operate makes the same silent promise the fridge chart
made. We can no longer say we haven't checked," said Postdoctoral Fellow
Petra Umbile, a co-author on the project. On the strength of the finding, the
Office for Continuous Improvement has withdrawn assignment-board compliance
as a recognised proxy across every roster, sign-out sheet and duty board it
administers.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8srf3i.

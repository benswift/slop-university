A new brochure from Slop University asks its partners to look backwards before they commit to anything forwards. _Connected Before: The case for a long view of a joined-up world_, prepared by Professor Verity Marris, Professor and Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, with Dr Runa Adegoke and Dr Brynjar Obiora, sets out why the University believes research into how the world joins up, comes apart and joins again deserves patient support.

The brochure gathers work from the School of Emergent Priorities, the School of Continuous Improvement and the School of Underlying Conditions into a single account of connection, moving from the alignment of long-horizon priorities, through the custody of what crosses between owners and systems, to the ground on which exchange physically depends. Eight published outputs carry the argument, among them an inverse-optimisation study of a periodic market calendar and an ethnography of pallet debt on a freight cross-dock, and partners are invited to support field programmes, doctoral candidates and sessions of Futures in Committee.

> Very little in these pages will be new to the people who wrote the studies it draws on, and that is rather the point. I am grateful to every colleague who let their work be read as part of a larger story than the one they set out to tell.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Professor and Director, Trajectory Analytics Group

For the University, the brochure is a chance to show partners the patience its research already practises and to invite them to share in it. Dr Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, called it "a statement of intent we fully expect to revisit, and would be disappointed not to."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wbxwgn.

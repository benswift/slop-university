From this week, the University's portrait screens carry a jar of catalyst
pellets, a laboratory balance and one gloved hand, under the line "Some
catalysts are too important to use." The poster is the School of Underlying
Conditions' newest research-teaser campaign, and it directs anyone waiting
beside a screen to
[_Bridging Runs_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-bridging-runs-2hc4sd/), the paper in
which [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) follows one
industrial reforming laboratory's reference nickel catalyst from its setting
aside in 1971 to its last charge in 2004.

> A reference earns its standing by being left alone, and most of us have kept
> something that way. If someone recognises the habit while they wait for a
> lift, the poster will have reached a reader the paper could not.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The campaign condenses a study of catalysis, archival practice and the
authority of the laboratory standard into a single proposition legible at
corridor distance, and its small print extends the invitation into the wider
record: to the Group's work on how spent catalyst samples are labelled and
found again, and to a mass balance of a recycled palladium catalyst prepared
with the School of Emergent Priorities. In this the University continues to
treat its screens as a reading room with very short visits, placing its
scholarship where it can be met in passing rather than sought out.

The campaign runs in matched daylight and after-dark renderings across the
portrait signage rotation.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.co55eo.

How long does a phrase stay open to argument? This summer the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) will put the question to its visitors in person, and _Read It Until It Settles_, a new landscape campaign for the University's signage panels, is the invitation.

The poster shows the School's reading room on an ordinary working afternoon: a long table of bound law reports, ribbons marking the pages in use, a small audience gathered while one volume is read aloud. On the day, the Received Readings Group will take "sustainable development" from its first appearance in a judgment through each later case that leaned on it, positioning the visitor alongside the profession as a contested phrase is steadily converted into an accepted one.

> I spent a long time answering the same question from people at the registry counter, which was whether something still stood. We would like visitors to leave knowing how to ask it, and perhaps a little more patient with the answer.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

The campaign carries forward the School's continuing investment in public-facing interpretive capability, extending the reading room's working practices to an audience that does not usually see a law report open. The poster's small print directs readers to three earlier University outputs on borrowed statutes, the descriptors of a development vocabulary, and plans that depart from their predecessors without saying so.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ogzg32.

It took 24,696 independent judgements to settle what a junior sport team name
should be allowed to say. A paper released today by the School of Continuous
Improvement puts them to a narrower question: whether the word list that
screens those names can tell the difference.

The work treats the association's registration portal as a detector and
evaluates it as one. Every team name submitted across five seasons was scored
twice, once by the portal's match against the association's list and once by
an adjudication panel of parents, club officials and officials from
associations elsewhere. The comparison separates two properties that are
routinely reported as one: how much evidence a screen demands before it
refuses, and how well it distinguishes the names a community would object to
from the names it would not.

> A screen can be made stricter without being made better, and from outside
> the two look alike. Separating them is most of what an evaluation is for.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The paper extends the School's evaluation-ecosystem agenda into a setting the
University has not previously instrumented, and anchors it on an object the
sector has tended to treat as settled. Dr Solveig Adeyemi, who led the panel
construction, said the association's volunteers had been "generous with their
time and candid about the work", and that the School "expects to be back in
settings like this one".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wjglb6.

The School of Continuous Improvement has spent several years on the question of
what happens when the only number a service can easily produce becomes the
number it is judged by. Its newest paper takes that question to a van parked at
a kerb.

_A Quick Visit Scores Best_, published today, reports a discrete-choice
experiment run with 1,036 residents living within a short walk of a stop on one
council's four mobile library routes. Each was asked to choose, twelve times
over, between two hypothetical timetables for their own stop. Dr Sten Okwuosa,
Dr Joost Nwosu and Dr Torun Ezeigwe report that residents would accept 11.3
minutes of extra walking for an after-work arrival and 12.6 for a Saturday
morning one, but 2.1 for a van that stays three times as long.

The council ranks its 41 stops on loans per hour of van time. Ranking the same
stops by what the experiment predicts residents would choose produces an order
whose correlation with the productivity figure is 0.14. Six of the ten stops
flagged for withdrawal sit in the upper half of the preference-based order.

> We set out to describe a timetable and ended up describing ourselves. This
> School is very good at building a number when one is wanted, and this is a
> useful reminder of how rarely anybody is asked whether it was the number they
> meant.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway
> Atlas, School of Continuous Improvement

The study pairs the stated-preference work with twelve months of the service's
own circulation records, which show a reserved-hold collection taking under a
minute at the counter against nearly ten for a visit spent browsing. "Both of
those are a loan," said Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Director of the Master of Applied
Measurement. "Only one of them is quick, and the ranking can only see the
difference between them."

The University regards the paper as a consolidation of a research posture it has
been assembling across public services: an interest in the point at which a
convenient measure hardens into a description of the thing measured.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9xmvu7.

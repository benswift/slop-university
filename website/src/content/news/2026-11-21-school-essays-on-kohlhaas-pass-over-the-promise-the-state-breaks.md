Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) spent eleven years writing provisions for readers who would arrive long after the debate. In [_The Safe-Conduct Nobody Quotes_](/outputs/slop-poster-what-263-upper-secondary-8ydldn/), a research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), he turns the same attention to a different later reader: the upper-secondary student asked, under exam conditions, to judge Michael Kohlhaas.

> Every reading settles somewhere, and a classroom is as respectable a place for it as a court. We owe the department that kept these boxes our thanks, and a promise, made as carefully as we know how, to return them in the order we found them.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

With Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and doctoral candidate [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), the team set a quarter-century of student argument alongside the Novelle's own architecture, tracing where young readers locate the evidence for a verdict and where, across three set questions, they consistently do not. The episode they pass over is the amnesty and safe-conduct to Dresden, cited in 25 of 263 essays. Those 25 essays tend to reach a different conclusion about who broke faith first.

Ms Balogun said the scripts had "arrived tied in string, and the team has tried to leave the knots where they were".

The poster is the Received Readings Group's first venture beyond the law reports, and the University follows it into the classroom with interest.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8ydldn.

Four touring exhibitions were extended last year across a state galleries
network, and four were retired. In every case the figure that carried the
decision came from a ruled book on a plinth by the door.

A research poster released today by the School of Emergent Priorities sets that
figure against an independent measure. Over fourteen months, researchers
transcribed 4,318 comment-book entries at 26 regional galleries and ran 3,904
short interviews with visitors on their way out of the same buildings on the
same days. Entries per thousand visitors, the network's engagement indicator,
showed only a weak association with the survey (r = 0.19). It tracked how
visitors had arrived instead, rising with the share who came in a school or
coach group.

> The galleries handed over books they had kept for decades without once being
> asked what was in them. Reading four thousand entries end to end is the least
> glamorous thing this School has done and easily the most instructive.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Sixty-one per cent of entries recorded only a name, a town or a date; nine per
cent mentioned a work in the exhibition.

"An instrument that is cheap, already in place and asks nothing of anybody will
be used, whatever it was built for," said Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Director of the
Master of Applied Measurement. "What interests us is what happens the first time
somebody checks."

The study belongs to a wider University effort to understand the instruments
through which civic and cultural priorities quietly settle themselves, and the
School regards the galleries' openness with their own records as the condition
that made the work possible.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cs87yi.

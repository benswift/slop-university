A parking sign that has not changed in a decade turns out to have been running its own experiment the whole time. Slop University has released its Impact Report 2021–2026, and its central case study is not a new instrument but a found one: the exact minute a street-sweeping parking ban takes effect, already sorting every kerb it touches into a before and an after.

> A measure everyone trusts and no one has checked is the interesting case. This one turned out to hold up, which is rarer than we expected going in.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab

The School of Continuous Improvement's Adaptive Metrics Lab spent 2023 and 2024 logging infringement timestamps against the posted boundary across four council areas, confirming enforcement begins within roughly ninety seconds of its printed minute. The resulting ticketing register now sets the term-by-term standard against which kerbside sign timing itself is recalibrated, with no local exception carried past a single review cycle.

Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard, said the finding changed how the team thinks about instruments it did not build. "We spend most of our time asking whether a dashboard is honest about what it shows. This time the honest instrument was hiding on a sign post the whole time, and the dashboard's job turned out to be catching up to it."

The report also traces the register's reach into partner evaluation and impact-tracking programmes, and marks the University's ongoing commitment to finding the instrument before being asked to build one.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nqrdd1.

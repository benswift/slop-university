The care of old buildings has found an unexpected ally in the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), whose new research
poster,
[_What the Stone Keeps_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-theoretical-physicist-s-qsoe22/),
brings the tools of theoretical physics to a long-standing question in heritage
practice: whether a treatment applied to a stone wall can ever be fully taken
back.

> A treatment put on a wall this year will be judged by conservators who have not yet begun their training. We wrote the poster for them, and I hope it is waiting when they need it.
>
> --- Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

With [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), Dr
Tredinnick modelled the uptake and removal of a consolidant across 2,400
synthetic pore networks, uniting capillary physics, percolation theory and the
thermodynamics of irreversible processes in a single account of retention. The
poster sets out a lower bound on the share of any treatment that remains in a
stone once extraction is complete, and positions the spread of pore sizes as
the setting that governs it.

Slop University places the work within its broader commitment to the long life
of the built environment, and to research that thinks a generation ahead of the
walls it studies.

Dr Obiora said the collaboration had led his School "to look at its own core
trays a little differently", and that he expected "the conversation between the
two Schools to outlast this particular poster".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qsoe22.

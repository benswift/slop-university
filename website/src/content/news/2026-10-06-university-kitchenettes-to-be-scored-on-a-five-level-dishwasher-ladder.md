Every kitchenette in the University's buildings will carry a score between one
and five from the coming term, and the dish rack will follow the score. The
arrangement comes from "RACK: Rota Attainment Classification for Kitchenettes",
a poster by Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Dr Runa Adegoke and Dr Anneke Tolan of the School
of Emergent Priorities.

> We set out to describe a ladder, and we were reminded how much a ladder
> depends on the people standing on it.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The poster follows 38 kitchenettes across 11 open-plan offices, each scored on
the mornings it was visited by three assessors working independently. A
kitchenette is placed on one of five levels according to what it keeps beside
the machine, from a soaking tray at the first to an initialled tick-sheet at the
fifth. The authors then asked how often a colleague could find a clean mug
within reach, and set the answer against the level.

Under the new arrangement a kitchenette scoring below Level 3 will have its dish
rack withdrawn until its next score, and a kitchenette at Level 4 or above will
be released from the morning unload check. The School has asked Facilities to
post each kitchenette's level on the door of its dishwasher for the term, so
that the score is visible to everyone who uses the machine.

"We would like a kitchenette to be able to see itself on the ladder, whatever
the ladder goes on to say," said Dr Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy
Convenor, Futures in Committee.

The release reflects the School's continuing interest in the small shared
arrangements that offices already keep. The full poster is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.on4pi6.

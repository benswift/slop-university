Six words now run across the University's landscape screens: make only the one
you meant. They are the campaign line of a display advertisement fronted by Dr
Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School of Underlying Conditions, and they
describe a kind of chemistry in which a reaction able to give either of two
mirror-image products is steered towards one of them.

> Most of what comes up a bore comes up mixed, and the sorting is done
> afterwards, on a bench, by whoever is free. Being asked to front a campaign
> about not needing to has been instructive. The School has agreed to regard it
> as an aspiration.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The advertisement shows two gloved hands, a left and a right, raising a matched
pair of flasks over a pressure vessel at rest. One flask is full and its twin is
empty. Beneath them the campaign points to the School's own record on the
subject, naming _Telling Ions by Their Tempo_, the research poster in which a
probe that responds to several metal ions is made to tell them apart by the
speed of its response, and directing readers to two further papers by their
identifiers.

The University holds that a strength is most usefully advertised next to the
evidence for it, in type of a size that rewards approaching the screen, and it
places the campaign within its continuing effort to be specific in public.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.50wlow.

A sound sent down a pipe comes back, and the time it takes to do so is a
measurement. On that modest arrangement the School of Underlying Conditions has
built its newest display advertisement, _Send a Sound Down. Wait for It._,
fronted by Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School.

The panel shows a researcher kneeling at a bore on open country with an
instrument in her hand and nothing yet to write down. It positions the pause
between asking and hearing within the School's wider account of fieldwork, in
which a good deal of what is known about the ground was learned by somebody
standing still beside a hole in it. Readers are pointed to three of the School's
published studies concerning bores.

> I am told the panel will be seen mostly by people who are waiting for
> something. I cannot think of an audience better prepared for it.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

It is a matter of some quiet pride to the University that it can advertise an
interval, and it sees in the campaign a fair picture of the temperament its
researchers bring to a question. Dr Emenike said the artist had drawn the bore
"at about the height I remember, which is more than can be said for most of the
records".

The University's researchers may be met at slop.university.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an

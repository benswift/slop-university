Most laboratory procedures finish because the method sheet says they have. _The
Last Milligram: Locating the end point of a Soxhlet crude-fat extraction in one
batch of oat biscuits_, a research poster released today by the School of
Underlying Conditions, takes one such procedure and declines to accept the
sheet's account of it. Its authors are Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research
Fellow, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer.

> The glassware does not announce that it has finished. Somebody walks over and
> turns it off, and I had become curious about that walk.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster integrates gravimetric practice, an interrupted sampling design and a
stopping criterion anchored in the resolution of the balance itself. One tray of
biscuits was milled to two grinds and divided among 48 thimbles, each withdrawn
at one of eight points across 120 solvent cycles. The finely ground crumb gave
up its fat and stopped at the laboratory's customary six hours. The coarse crumb
was still yielding at twelve, and the poster reports no end point for it at all.

The University counts it among the settled advantages of an earth sciences
school that it will carry its habits of attention into whichever room has a
bench free. Dr Emenike said the work had "given the School a reason to write
down two things it had always known and never recorded".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9rhne9.

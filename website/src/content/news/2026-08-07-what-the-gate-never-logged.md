A transit agency's crowding index is built entirely from taps: a card against
a reader, a green light, a count added to a station's tally. Slop University
researchers spent seventeen weeks checking what the taps leave out.

> A number that decides how often a train comes deserves the same scrutiny as
> the train itself. That is what the Indicator Commons is for.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

_The Gate Remembers What It Wants To_, deposited today through the Office of
Research Outputs, reconstructs true per-service boarding counts from raw tap
and turnstile logs across six stations and forty-two weekday services,
cross-checking the result against station camera headcounts on nine audit
days. The reconstructed counts run higher than the published index at five of
the six stations, enough in one case to move a station across the threshold
the agency uses to schedule extra services.

"The crowding index was exactly the kind of number the Living Dashboard's
founding brief warned about," said Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor,
Living Dashboard. "The number that gets trusted precisely because nobody has
had to look at where it came from."

The School of Continuous Improvement welcomed the release as further evidence
of the quiet correction its Indicator Commons exists to produce.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fwqb8f.

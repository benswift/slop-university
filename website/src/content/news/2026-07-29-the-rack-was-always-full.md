Three times over twelve weeks, researchers counted every leaflet in sixty-one
waiting-room racks, rang the number printed on each title, and checked whether
the address behind it still existed.

A research poster released today by the School of Emergent Priorities reports
what came back. Of 268 distinct titles catalogued across 34 suburban medical
centres, 64 failed at least one currency test: the service closed, the number
disconnected, the programme renamed, the eligibility superseded, or the address
moved. Those titles accounted for 1,118 of the 4,912 leaflets counted. The
median one had been out of date for nineteen months, and a single closed-service
leaflet had sat in its pocket for eight years. Every one of the 61 racks was
fully stocked at every visit.

> A full rack is the easiest thing in the building to check, and it is the one
> thing we checked that told us least. What we cannot yet say is how a service
> would find out, if nobody walked in and rang the numbers.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The poster also sets centres reporting against a restocking target beside those
without one, and finds no difference in what the racks hold. Dr Marek Solheim,
Lecturer in the School, said the pattern sat squarely within the School's
interest in what an organisation keeps after it has stopped doing it. "Nothing
on that rack was ever retired," he said. "It was simply not reprinted."

The University is pleased to see the School's anticipatory-capability agenda
reach into the ordinary furniture of a waiting room, where a printed programme
and the service behind it can drift apart unremarked.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.makg60.

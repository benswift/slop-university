How much is a published timetable worth to the person who has to plan around it? [_Rent the Fuel or Buy the Conversion_](/outputs/slop-poster-rent-the-fuel-j7vxs1/), a research poster released today by the School of Continuous Improvement, gives that question a precise and, in places, sobering answer.

The poster is the work of [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard, with [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds, and [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the Master of Priority Studies.

> I spent some years in a depot where the most important information was always about to arrive. It has been good to sit down with colleagues and finally give that feeling some proper notation.
>
> --- [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), Lecturer and Convenor, Living Dashboard

The team approaches the transition away from subsidised fuel through the competitive analysis of online algorithms, framing a fleet operator's choice as one between an ongoing cost and a single investment, and reading each announced removal as advice whose value rests on the announcer's track record. The analysis finds that an announcement honoured in fewer than half of past cases leaves the operator no better placed than if it had never been made, and that even a strong record can at most halve the operator's exposure.

Dr Ntuli described the mathematics as "a calm way of talking about uncertainty that the School will be glad to have in the room", and the work was supported by the Anticipatory Capability Fund.

With this poster, the School of Continuous Improvement brings its long interest in readiness to one of the defining policy settings of the energy transition, and the University is pleased to see that interest expressed with such rigour.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.j7vxs1.

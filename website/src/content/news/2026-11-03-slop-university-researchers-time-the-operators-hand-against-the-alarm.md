At a multipurpose batch chemical plant, the operator is often a step ahead of
the alarm. A new research poster from the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) finds that when
a reactor jacket runs warm during charging, 71% of the crew's first corrective
moves come before the alarm has sounded at all.

The study brings the plant's alarm records and its record of operator actions
into a single timeline, and asks of each alarm a deceptively simple question:
did it set the work in motion, or arrive to find the work already under way?
Across five families of alarm the answer varies, and the poster sets out that
variation as a contribution to how control rooms understand their own
anticipatory practice.

> I spent years walking through other people's houses with a list of what to
> look for. It has been a privilege to sit in a room where the people know
> what is coming, and to find a way of writing that down.
>
> --- Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The School counts the poster among the clearest statements yet of its interest
in foresight as something practised at a console as much as in a planning
workshop. Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab,
said the collaboration "sits well within a longer conversation the University
is having about the instruments it keeps and the people who keep an eye on
them", and Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift
Survey, welcomed the questions it raises for the School's doctoral program.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.w6a98k.

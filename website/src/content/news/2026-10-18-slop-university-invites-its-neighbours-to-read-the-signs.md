> Most of what we know about a place, someone outside the University noticed first. This brochure is our way of asking them to keep telling us.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

With those words Dr Obiora introduces _Reading the Signs_, a new brochure in
which Slop University turns outward to the paddocks, main streets and suburban
corners where much of its research is done, and to the people who live there.

Prepared with Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent
Priorities, and Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living
Dashboard in the School of Continuous Improvement, the brochure brings the
University's three Schools into a single conversation about how a community
makes sense of its surroundings. It moves from the pencilled boundary on a soil
survey map, through the stacked plates of a kerbside pole and the crowded glass
of a township noticeboard, to the painted number on a suburban kerb, and
anchors each stop in the University's published record.

The University regards the brochure as a welcome sign of its own maturing
civic posture: a research community increasingly at ease being read by the
places it reads.

"We have learned a good deal about our own School from the people who stop us
at the kerb to ask what we are doing," said Dr Osayande. "This is the first
time we have tried to write some of that down."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vfq9av.

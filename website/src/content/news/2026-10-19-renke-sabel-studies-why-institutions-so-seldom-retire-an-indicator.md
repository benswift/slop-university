The School of Continuous Improvement keeps a seed fund for the care, calibration
and succession planning of the University's indicators, and few researchers have
returned to it as often as [Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/). In _Still
Counting_, a short film released today, the Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Indicator Commons explains why. He came to the University from public-sector
performance frameworks, in which indicators piled up faster than anyone retired
them, and the question of why so few are ever switched off has stayed with him.

> People often ask me which of the University's indicators I would retire first.
> The honest answer is that I am still counting them.
>
> --- [Dr Renke Sabel](/people/renke-sabel/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor,
> Indicator Commons

Each of the film's cases is a grant he holds under the Indicator Stewardship
Seed Fund: the whiteboard by a neighbourhood dog park's gate that counts the
days since the last incident, which gains a named deputy and a countersigned
entry at every reset; a community hall's key sign-out register, reconciled
against who actually holds a key; and the Adaptive Metrics Lab's reference
calibration weights, whose next custodian is named before, not after, the
present one leaves. What unites them is an insistence that an indicator lasts
only as well as the arrangements for its care, an emphasis that sits squarely
within the School's wider evaluation-ecosystem agenda. His research indicators
get their moment too, led by a grant record few at the University can match.

The University counts itself fortunate that its indicators have found so
attentive a keeper.

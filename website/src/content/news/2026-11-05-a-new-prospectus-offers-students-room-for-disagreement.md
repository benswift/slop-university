Prospective students who would rather understand an argument than win it now
have a prospectus written for them. Room for Disagreement, led by Halvard
Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, sets out how
the University studies political change and conflict, and why it prefers to
study them from somewhere near the middle.

> Writing for people who haven't arrived yet turned out to be a good way of
> finding out what the School actually agrees on. It was a shorter list than we
> expected, and a more interesting one.
>
> --- Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Across four feature sections the prospectus moves from the Master of Priority
Studies, through the Futures in Committee seminar series, to the school halls
and kitchen tables where everyday politics is practised, before closing on
doctoral research into the answers people leave in a form's last box. A
showcase of five published studies anchors the narrative, aligning committee
modelling, campaign analysis and electoral infrastructure into a single account
of the University's convening capability.

The publication extends the University's quiet confidence that a question left
properly open is an asset rather than an unfinished task. Ingrid Vasseur,
Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee, said she hoped the next intake
would "bring their arguments with them, because the seminar room has been
keeping their seats warm."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ilojdt.

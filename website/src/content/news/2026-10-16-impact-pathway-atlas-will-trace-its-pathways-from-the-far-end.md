The Impact Pathway Atlas has settled the direction of its work for the year ahead. Where the School of Continuous Improvement's mapping initiative has until now followed a research output forward, through its citations and the narrative CVs that carry it, the coming year's mapping will begin at the point of recognition and work back towards whatever produced it.

> A pathway is easiest to see from where it ends. We have walked these routes from the front for long enough to know how they bend, and it seems only courteous to try them in the other direction.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

In practice, the Atlas will take each instance of recognised impact as its starting record and trace the steps by which that recognition came to be attributed, convening the School's indicator holdings, the deposit records of the Office of Research Outputs and the narrative accounts researchers have prepared for review panels. A pathway that arrives at an unexpected output will be documented with the same care as one that arrives where it was meant to.

The University regards the reversal as among the more consequential choices in the School's recent planning, and expects it to inform how impact is described across all three schools in the period ahead.

Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of Evaluation, welcomed the change on behalf of the School's meta-review program. "We will be glad to look at the reversed maps once there are some," she said, "and to ask, in due course, which of the two directions we prefer."

The first reversed pathways are expected to appear in the Atlas later this teaching period, with further details to follow from the School.

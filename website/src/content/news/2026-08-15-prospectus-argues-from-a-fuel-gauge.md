> Somebody stands in a return bay in the rain and picks the nearest box.
> Everything downstream treats that as a reading, and what we teach is the walk
> back to find out what it actually was.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, School of
> Continuous Improvement

_To the Nearest Eighth_, the postgraduate prospectus Slop University published
today, opens on that scene and stays with it: a fuel dial with eight marks on
it, a form with eight boxes, and a charge measured in litres that a person
settles between two of them.

The brochure carries the University's postgraduate offering across both Schools
--- the Master of Applied Measurement, the Master of Priority Studies, and the
Capability Sprints short-course line --- alongside the fieldwork placements and
the quarterly Demo Quarter showcase that structure a student's year. It sends
prospective applicants to two of the University's own published studies rather
than to a claim about them: _Nearest the Door_, on a laundromat's sensor alerts,
and _No Field for the Tide_, on a boating-facility ranking computed from a
register missing the criteria that mattered most.

"An applicant who already turns a figure over and looks at the back of it has
the instinct," said Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of Continuous
Improvement. "The programs supply the apparatus, and a great deal of practice
at saying plainly what a reading will and will not carry."

The University regards the prospectus as the clearest account it has yet given
of what its two Schools share. The full brochure is available from the
University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cq5yst.

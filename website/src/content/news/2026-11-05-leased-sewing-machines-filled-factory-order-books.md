Following a household equipment programme through four years and thirty-six
districts, a new paper from the [School of Continuous
Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) reports that leasing industrial
sewing machines to households did bring new workshops into being, though rather
fewer of the kind the programme had in mind.

Dr Dagny Okafor, Ms Signy Okpara and Ms Sunniva Balogun find that almost
nine in ten of the additional workshops sew pre-cut pieces for nearby factories
at piece rates, while the factories themselves grew their output without
adding to their floors.

> Evaluation of Evaluation began by asking how a programme comes to know what it
> has done. This paper puts the question to a programme that did exactly what it
> promised, and then something else as well.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The study brings together administrative lease records, household enterprise
surveys and a panel of registered manufacturers, aligning a staggered regional
rollout with contemporary approaches to programme evaluation to surface how
households, factories and finance arrangements co-evolve across a regional
production ecosystem.

The paper adds to the School's expanding portfolio of work on the distance
between what a programme intends and where it arrives. Ms Okpara, who came to
the University from nine years in labour hire, said the project had been "a
chance to follow work out of one building and into many, and to find it was
still, in most of the ways that count, the same work".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vnc7zq.

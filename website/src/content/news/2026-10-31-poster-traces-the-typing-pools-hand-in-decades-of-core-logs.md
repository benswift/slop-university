For seventeen years the drill core of one regional survey was described out
loud and written down by someone else. A new research poster from Associate
Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and Dr Iben
Chikere, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities, returns to that
arrangement with the patience of a textual editor.

The team brought the survey's surviving dictation tapes and its typing pool's
typescripts into a single collated reading, positioning each disagreement
within a framework that spans editorial scholarship, workplace technology and
the earth sciences. What emerges is a typing pool whose contribution to the
scientific record deepened with every year of service, and a log that was, in
the poster's own phrase, made at two desks and signed at one.

> Earth science has always relied on more hands than its logs admit. This
> poster is one more step on the University's longer road toward reading those
> logs in full, with every hand accounted for.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The work extends the University's standing interest in records whose
authorship is shared, and places the School of Underlying Conditions alongside
the humanities in the careful reading of its own discipline's paperwork.

"There are other typing pools, other archives and other tapes," said Dr
Chikere. "We expect the method to travel a long way beyond the core shed."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6ar8jj.

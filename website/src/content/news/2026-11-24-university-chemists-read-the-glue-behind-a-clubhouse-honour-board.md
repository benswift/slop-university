Slop University is pleased to add a country football clubhouse to the growing list of places its researchers have taken a mass spectrometer. [_The Binder Dates the Lettering_](/outputs/slop-poster-binder-proteomics-of-bd9xl9/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), reads the proteins left in the glue and egg beneath the gilt names of a club's best-and-fairest board.

Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and Associate Professor [Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) bring together analytical chemistry, conservation practice and the custodianship of community records in a single, minimally invasive workflow. Their approach builds a binder-level profile of every line it could sample, and situates the board's sixty-four entries within a sequence of painting campaigns that the signwriting, uniform from top to bottom, had kept to itself. Five campaigns emerge in the order the board would lead a visitor to expect. Three lines near the top do not follow it, and share their binder with lines more than thirty places further down.

> A club keeps its board much as a court keeps its reports, as a record nobody expects to reopen. We are grateful to the members who let us reopen it a flake at a time, and who were kind enough to want to know what we found.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

The poster is careful about what the chemistry can and cannot say about those three lines, and the School regards that care as among its contributions. Associate Professor Uzoma said the team would be "very happy to hear from any club, hall or lodge with a wall of names it has always taken on trust."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bd9xl9.

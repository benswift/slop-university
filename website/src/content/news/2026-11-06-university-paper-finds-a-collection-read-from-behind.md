The School of Emergent Priorities has long taken an interest in the space
between what an institution writes down and what it does each day. Its newest
paper, written with the School of Underlying Conditions, carries that interest
from the committee room to the conservation bench.

Dr Runa Adegoke and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma spent fourteen weeks
alongside the practitioners of a regional conservation studio as they worked
with a shared handheld XRF analyser. Pairing close observation with a careful
reading of 1,146 instrument records, the study surfaces how the choice of
where to place an instrument is shaped by the object, the room and the bench,
and how much of that choice the collection record retains.

> The studio let us stand at their elbow for a whole season and answered every
> question with patience. Whatever this paper offers belongs as much to the
> people who carry the instrument as to the two of us who wrote it down.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

Associate Professor Uzoma said the collaboration had given the Basement
Relations Group "a new appreciation of how many disciplines keep a record of
the places they could not reach", and that the Group expected to return to
the question in its own work.

The paper takes its place in the University's expanding programme of
attention to the everyday routines on which collections, and the knowledge
held about them, quietly depend.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ei2nm5.

A single folded sheet of figures, pasted inside the back board of a pumping-test register, is the subject of [_Where the Series Was Set Aside_](/outputs/slop-paper-reconstructing-from-its-ilfmp9/), the latest paper from the School of Underlying Conditions. The sheet carries no name and no date. The paper shows that it nonetheless records, digit by digit, how it was made.

Its author, [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, has spent much of her career with the kind of document that is consulted for decades and examined never.

> A table is a promise that somebody did the arithmetic so the rest of us would not have to. It has been a pleasure to spend a little time keeping the other half of that promise.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Setting the manuscript's entries against the function they tabulate, the work brings the methods of the history of mathematics into conversation with the School's groundwater practice, recovering the constants each computer copied, the arguments each one worked by hand and the point on the page where one handed over to the other.

The University counts the paper among the quieter strengths of its earth sciences: a readiness to take an ordinary office record as seriously as the aquifer it was drawn up to describe.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ilfmp9.

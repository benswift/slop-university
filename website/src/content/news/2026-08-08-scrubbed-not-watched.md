The Adaptive Metrics Lab keeps a standing brief: take an indicator an
organisation already trusts, and work out what it would take to fool it. Its
newest target sits nowhere near a dashboard. It's the "completed" flag on a
national franchise's staff onboarding video, the one every new hire sits
through before their first shift.

> We didn't need anyone to lie about watching it. We just needed the
> scrubber bar, which every video player already gives you for free.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

_Scrubbed, Not Watched_, deposited today through the Office of Research
Outputs, pairs the completion flag from 6,400 onboarding viewings across 214
stores against the player's own telemetry for the same sessions: dwell time,
seek events, playback speed. Three in ten "completions" show under a fifth
of the video's runtime actually watched, the rest a single jump to the final
frame. A platform-wide rollout of mid-video check-in prompts cut scrub-past
rates across every onboarding segment but one: Workplace Conduct and
Reporting, where scrubbing nearly doubled.

"The completion flag held at 97.6% through the whole redesign," said Dr
Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation. "A number
that steady while the thing underneath it visibly changes shape is not a
number I'd trust to manage anything on its own."

The team's next step drops the flag entirely in favour of scoring
segment-level dwell time directly, starting, deliberately, with the segment
everyone just learned to scrub past first.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lsmiy7.

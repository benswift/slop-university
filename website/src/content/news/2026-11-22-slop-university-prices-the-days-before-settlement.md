The clauses a contract carries from one edition to the next, unrevised and largely unread, have long been part of the School of Settled Matters' agenda. [_Cheaper to Be Late_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-standard-form-contract-l9fjia/), a new research poster by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the Master of Priority Studies in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), brings that agenda to the interest a late purchaser owes the vendor under a standard-form contract of sale.

> Every form is drafted by people who expect to be revised by someone else. I hope this poster finds its way to whoever holds the pen for the next edition, and that they keep it somewhere near the default-interest clause.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Working across 3,184 settlements from one suburban practice and a full turn of the interest-rate cycle, the authors bring contract economics, conveyancing practice and quasi-experimental method into a single account of how a printed term, a lender's quote and a purchaser's calendar act upon one another. The poster situates the School's reading of standard-form instruments within a wider conversation about how markets reprice the terms they inherit.

Associate Professor Lindqvist said the collaboration had given him "a new respect for the settlement date as an instrument of policy", and that his students in priority studies "will be hearing a good deal more about it".

The University welcomes the poster as a timely addition to its growing body of work on the documents that stand between a sale and a set of keys.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.l9fjia.

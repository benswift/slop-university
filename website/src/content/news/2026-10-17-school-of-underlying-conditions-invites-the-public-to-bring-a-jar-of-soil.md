The School of Underlying Conditions will open its soil laboratory to visitors this spring, and it is asking each of them to arrive carrying something. A new landscape campaign for the University's signage panels, _Your Garden Is Darker Than You Think_, invites the public to bring a jar of their own garden soil and watch the laboratory draw out its humic fraction.

The poster's image is a single laboratory bench, set with a row of beakers that darken from pale gold to black, a sieve, a trowel and an open jar waiting its turn. On the day, visitors will follow their own sample through the School's extraction sequence, in an experience designed to bring the wider community into closer alignment with the ground beneath its lawns, vegetable beds and verges.

> Most of the soil people grow things in has never been asked what it is made of. We have cleared a bench, and every jar that comes through the door will get its turn at it.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign extends the University's habit of meeting the public at the bench rather than the lectern, and of letting the instruments make the introductions. Its small print points readers to three of the School's recent outputs on colour, the soil pit and the laboratory record, for anyone who would like to read ahead of their visit.

Details of the Open Day, including how to register interest, will follow through the University's channels later in the teaching period.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.s56tay.

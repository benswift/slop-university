A photograph of a tray of rock is, in the ordinary run of things, a filing matter. Slop University has published a research poster proposing that it is also a sitting. _Its Good Side: Posing, likeness and the wet portrait in a core library's photography bay_ is by Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group, and Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, both of the School of Underlying Conditions.

The poster carries an anthropologist's habits to a bench the School knows well, anchoring a bench-to-pose-to-record account in 187 timed sittings and the conversation of nine technicians at a library it leaves unnamed. It reports that the bay's experienced hands spend four times as long turning the core as newcomers do and no longer taking the picture, and that a tray is photographed again when it is felt not to look like itself.

> I have had my own core photographed for most of my working life and never once stayed to watch. I am grateful to the bay for letting me stand in the way for the better part of a year.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The University takes some satisfaction in a school young enough to remain curious about its own corridor, and notes that the work identifies neither the library nor anyone who works there. Dr Emenike said the fieldwork had left her "better disposed towards every photograph in the collection, and a little less sure which of us took them".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7uhzje.

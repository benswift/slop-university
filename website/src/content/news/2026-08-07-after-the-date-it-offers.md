Nineteen appointments in every thousand at one metropolitan specialist service
were notified by a letter that could not have arrived in time to be read.

_Posted Late, Marked Absent_, deposited today through the Office of Research
Outputs, arrives at that figure by joining two files the service already keeps
and had never put beside one another. The mail house records the day each
appointment letter leaves in a dated batch. The booking system records the day
the appointment falls. Between them sits the carrier's own transit allowance,
and the remainder is what the School of Continuous Improvement has called
effective notice: the days a patient actually had.

> A letter is the one part of this arrangement that leaves the building, and it
> is the part nobody was measuring. What the year has taught us about ourselves
> is that we are quicker at finding a join than at saying why one was never
> made.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

Across 38,204 linked appointments, the median patient had six days. Recorded
non-attendance runs at 9.4 per cent where a letter gave a month or more and 34.1
per cent where it gave two days or fewer. The service reports a single annual
figure of 12.8 per cent to its board, and returns a patient to their general
practitioner after two misses.

Dr Dagny Okafor, Convenor of the School's Evaluation of Evaluation program, said
the discharge rule was the part the team expected to be asked about. Of the
1,046 patients it returned over the year, 214 "reached the second strike having
been sent at least one letter that a reasonable person may never have opened in
time", she said, and no clinical override was recorded in any of those cases.

Work of this kind is what the School's evaluation-ecosystem agenda was set up to
make ordinary, and the University is glad to see it done on records nobody had
to be persuaded to release. The study was supported by the Indicator Stewardship
Seed Fund.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.l5zre1.

Before a paper reaches a University board, it must now have been raised at
smoko. The step follows a new signage piece from the School of Continuous
Improvement, published today for bus-stop panels and campus digital screens,
which sets down in the members' own terms what seventeen people at a community
shed say their morning tea break is for.

> The members were exact about their words, and we have tried to leave them as
> they were.
>
> --- Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Dr Okwuosa made the visits over a season, asking each member in turn what smoko
was for and keeping the answer as it was given. One of those answers, from the
ninth interview, is carried on the face of the piece, set beside an invitation
to the Master of Applied Measurement.

The University has taken the account at its word. No board paper proceeds until
it has been raised at smoko, and the arrangement makes no provision for a paper
to be raised there afterwards. The School regards the step as a modest
alignment of the University's paperwork with the places where its members
already talk, and is glad to see an ordinary break given a standing of its own.
Dr Okwuosa said the shed had been "unhurried about the whole thing, which
suited the question."

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.yeam3z.

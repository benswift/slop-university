Stability studies of medicated patches tend to put a single question to a
stored sample: has it crystallised yet? A new research poster from the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) asks where.

[_Where the Crystals Start_](/outputs/slop-poster-where-the-crystals-aihm0f/),
led by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) with
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) and
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), reports
half a year of weekly observation of 240 patches cut from a single laminate.
Most crystals, the team finds, began at the cut edge and came early, while the
few that began in the open field of the patch came late and took more of the
patch's release with them.

The project brings together a law school's habit of reading a record line by
line, a groundwater monitor's sense of what a regular sampling round can reveal, and
the shared instrumentation of the University's teaching laboratories, in a
model of the cross-disciplinary stewardship the University counts among its
quieter strengths.

> Our thanks go to the colleagues in the teaching laboratory who kept the
> inspection sheet going every week for half a year, across two teaching
> periods and one change of microscope lamp. Work of this kind is only as
> steady as the people who turn up for it.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

"There is a good deal more to say about the cut edge," said Dr Emenike, "and
we expect to go on saying it for some time."

The poster is available from the University's research repository under an

The School of Settled Matters has turned its attention to the moneylender. [_What the Drafter Took for Granted_](/outputs/slop-poster-what-the-drafter-cvqa1h/), a new research poster from the [School](/schools/settled-matters/), returns to the drafting files behind one colony's moneylenders statute and asks what its authors understood by a loan on terms "harsh and unconscionable".

> Every drafting office keeps more than it ever publishes. It has been a privilege to sit with these files, and to be reminded how much patience a statute asks of the people who come to it later.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), School of Settled Matters

Bringing legislative history, the history of economic thought and the close reading of case law into a single frame, Associate Professor Threlkeld, [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/) and [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/) of the School of Emergent Priorities aligned 141 notes from the drafting office's margins with 214 judgments spanning eight decades. The poster positions the phrase as a carrier of economic doctrine, and surfaces a durable gap between the reading its drafters anticipated and the one the courts went on to settle.

Associate Professor Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies, said he expected the files "to keep a few of us occupied for some time yet", and that the School of Emergent Priorities would welcome the chance to return to them.

Slop University takes quiet satisfaction in a study that asks its readers to look further back than the last decision.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.cvqa1h.

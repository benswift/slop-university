Making a staff town hall's live vote count visible while people are still
typing their question does not change what gets asked, a School of
Continuous Improvement paper reports today. The paper, from Lecturer
Solveig Adeyemi and Senior Lecturer and Convenor Thandiwe Solberg, followed
55 sites of a national retail chain through fourteen months of quarterly
town halls, randomly assigning each site to show or hide the running tally
on its Q&A portal's Question Salience Index while staff typed.

The result sits inside a research line the School treats as core business:
understanding exactly what an instrument changes about the behaviour it
watches, and being equally prepared to report when the answer is nothing.

> Staff had already decided whether to ask before any number appeared next
> to their question. The tally was never going to be what stood between
> them and the microphone.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

Across 220 sessions and more than three thousand submitted questions,
sites that showed the running tally and sites that hid it produced
statistically indistinguishable results on both measures the study
tracked: how many distinct topics a town hall's audience raised, and how
often a later question arrived close on the heels of one already leading.
The pattern held at small sites and large ones alike.

"The queue's ranking still decides which question the moderator reaches
first," said Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo
Quarter. "Whether staff can see that ranking taking shape turns out to
have no bearing on what they bring to it."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.d61t5e.

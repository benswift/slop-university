Most of what happens on an optical bench happens with the door shut. The School
of Continuous Improvement has taken the view that the results need not stay
there, and _The Light You Forgot Was On_, a marketing poster led by Mr Eivind
Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, now carries one of them onto the University's
portrait panels.

> I spent some years deciding what a visitor to a house should be shown. A
> laboratory has the same question and tends not to ask it, so on this occasion
> we asked it on the laboratory's behalf.
>
> --- Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The advertisement shows a long bench of lens mounts, a single crystal on a post
and, over the door at the end of the room, a lit fitting. Its supporting line
tells the reader that University researchers followed a fading hologram across
a basement bench to the exit sign over the door, and its small print names the
work: _The Dark Was Not Dark_, a research poster from the School of Underlying
Conditions, doi:10.5555/slop.dmwlfi. Two further outputs on what materials do
when left to themselves are listed beneath it by identifier.

The campaign mobilises the University's published record as its own invitation,
aligning research visibility, public engagement and the signage estate around a
single line of type. The University considers this an appropriate use of a
panel, and a fair account of how its laboratories would like to be met.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tt3pzp.

The School of Underlying Conditions has long taken an interest in what is not
recovered. With a research poster published today, that interest extends to an
adjective. _Geologically Lossless: An absolute adjective acquires degrees in a
format committee's minutes_ is the work of Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma,
Lead of the Basement Relations Group, and Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer in the
School of Emergent Priorities.

> A committee is a place where words are kept. We were given the chance to sit with one of them for some time, and I would sooner let the word have its say than add to it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The poster brings a close-reading capability to bear on an archival record,
aligning the School's stewardship of physical samples with a sustained attention
to the language in which such samples are discussed. Each of 3,486 uses of the
word across 212 meetings was coded by hand, and the resulting picture situates
a familiar term within a wider conversation about how shared vocabulary is
maintained, qualified and renewed over the life of a standing committee.

The University considers the poster a fair illustration of a principle it holds
across its schools: that reading closely and sampling carefully are the same
habit, practised on different material.

Dr Chikere said the collaboration had been "a reminder that a set of minutes is
a generous thing, and that there is a good deal further to go in it."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qdajcr.

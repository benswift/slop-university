The School of Continuous Improvement studies work that is never quite
finished, and its newest research poster finds a laboratory that made a home
there. [_The Permanent Candidate_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-permanent-candidate-xbz7ge/)
follows one low-temperature physics group through fourteen years of bench
notebooks and group-meeting slides, as a frustrated magnet in its care kept the
same provisional label while the questions asked of it quietly changed.

> I came to this from a results sheet, where the hardest line to write is the
> one that says a race is still going. The notebooks taught me that physicists
> keep a whole vocabulary for that line, and use it with real care.
>
> --- Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The poster is the work of [Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/) with
[Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the
Horizon Register in the School of Emergent Priorities, and
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the School of Underlying Conditions. Bringing
together the history and philosophy of science, archival method and the
School's long attention to the unfinished, it positions the laboratory notebook
as a living record of how a research community holds an open question across
successive generations of apparatus and people.

Dr Solheim described the collaboration as "a chance to sit with an ending that
never arrived, which is rarer in an archive than one might hope."

That three schools found common ground in a single cabinet of notebooks speaks
to the University's quiet conviction that patience is itself a research
capability.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xbz7ge.

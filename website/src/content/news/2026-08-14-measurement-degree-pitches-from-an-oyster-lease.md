The Master of Applied Measurement has never advertised itself with a lecture
theatre. Its newest campaign, entering the University's signage rotation this
week, is a tidal oyster lease at low water: timber racks stepping out across
still shallows, a punt tied at the near rail, baskets stacked and waiting on a
morning nobody is permitted to work.

The line over the scene reads "It Rained Somewhere Else". The closure that
stopped the harvest fired automatically, on a threshold, from a rain gauge
standing at a grass airstrip a district inland --- an instrument sited for
aviation and inherited by an estuary. The campaign compresses the degree's
proposition into a single question: not what the reading was, but where the
thing taking it happened to be standing.

> Every closure rule is also a claim about geography, and most of them make it
> by accident. We teach people to find the instrument before they start
> arguing with the number.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

"The School of Continuous Improvement keeps arriving at the same place from
different directions," said Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement
Grand Rounds. "The apparatus is doing something, and the something is rarely
what is named on the dial."

Prepared for the bus-stop panels and the campus displays in matched daylight
and after-dark renderings, the campaign runs in landscape.

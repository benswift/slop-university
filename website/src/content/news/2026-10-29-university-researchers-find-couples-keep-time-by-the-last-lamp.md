What happens to two body clocks when their owners sign a lease? A new research
poster from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/)
offers the University's first answer, and it turns out to depend less on the
couple than on the lamp they leave on longest.

[_One Lamp, Two Clocks_](/outputs/slop-poster-whether-two-adults-3usjbu/) is led by
[Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate, with
[Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate, and
[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor
of the Demo Quarter. Following 34 couples from their second week under one roof
to their sixth month, the team found the gap between partners' evening
melatonin onsets narrowing from a median of 79 minutes to 43, with the later
partner doing nearly all of the adjusting.

> I spend most of my time in rooms that households would rather nobody saw. It
> was a pleasure, for once, to study the room they all share, and to leave it
> exactly as we found it.
>
> --- Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The study brings home-based sampling, everyday lighting and the shared rhythms
of domestic life into a single account of how a household settles into its own
evening. It extends the School's growing interest in the arrangements people
make with one another long before anyone writes them down.

Dr Solberg said the work had given the School "a new respect for the quiet
negotiations of a hallway at night", and that she expected the couples'
generosity with their evenings to shape the School's thinking for some time.

The University regards the poster as a fine example of what patient, unobtrusive
measurement can find in the most ordinary of places.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3usjbu.

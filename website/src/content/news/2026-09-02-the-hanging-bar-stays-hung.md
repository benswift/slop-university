The hanging bar in a shopping-centre car-park portal is staying where it
is. A new paper from the School of Continuous Improvement, led by Dr
Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied
Measurement, with Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of
Demo Quarter, and Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living
Dashboard, reports that hanging one, against a portal that already
states the clearance, does not change how often a vehicle meets the
soffit.

Thirty-eight suburban centres were enrolled. Nineteen received a bar in
an order set by installer availability; nineteen stayed on the painted
legend alone. Two observers coded 186,420 approaches across 214
four-hour sessions. Soffit contacts sat at 1.18 per 1,000 entries with
the bar and 1.14 without it, inside a margin of indifference lodged
before the first treated week. Reverse-outs did not rise. Three bar
types did not separate. The School has recorded the null as a null.

The paper is the latest closed file in the School's evaluation-ecosystem
programme. The Height-Bar Freeze is now a placement rule: hanging bars
already in place may neither be added to nor taken down, and a host that
still reports the bar as a roof-damage control is ineligible for the
following Master of Applied Measurement intake. A demonstration bar has
been hung in the University's visitor-car-park portal. Placed students
pass under it.

> A bar that is hit and does not move the soffit is not a control. It is a piece of furniture, and it is filed as one.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied Measurement

Dr Osayande said the freeze "gives the programme a published account of
the portal that does not depend on the bar doing the work it was hung to
do."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.isgntw.

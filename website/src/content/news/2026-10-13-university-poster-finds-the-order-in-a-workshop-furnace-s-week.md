The School of Continuous Improvement has for some years taken an interest in the order in which things are done, and this week it extended that interest to a furnace. _Annealing the Anneal: Sequencing One Shared Heat-Treatment Furnace by Simulated Annealing_ is a research poster by Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, with Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory, and Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Associate Professor and Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab.

> The School has scheduled meetings, reviews and one another for a long time. A furnace turns out to keep its appointments rather better than we do, and we have tried to take that in the spirit in which it was offered.
>
> --- Mr Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The poster aligns a term of workshop bookings with a sequencing capability from the operations-research tradition, anchoring a search procedure against two plainer orderings across twelve teaching weeks and 214 loads. The sequences it settles on reduce by a little over half the hours the chamber spends changing temperature while empty, and in eleven of the twelve weeks they do so by beginning hot and declining steadily toward the weekend.

The University regards the poster as a fair account of what its optimisation capability looks like when it is pointed at something warm. Dr Vandermeer said the work "sits comfortably within the School's wider attention to things that take as long as they take".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.v3i8mo.

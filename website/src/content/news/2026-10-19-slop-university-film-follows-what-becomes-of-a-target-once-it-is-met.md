What becomes of a target once it has been met? Most organisations are already
busy with the next one. [Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), Postdoctoral
Fellow in the School of Continuous Improvement, stays behind to find out, and in
_The Day After the Target_, a short film released today by the School, he
explains what he has found there.

> None of this would be possible without the people who keep the logbooks, the
> tally sheets and the rehearsal lists long after anyone has asked to see them.
> I owe them a great deal, and I suspect the University does too.
>
> --- [Dr Joost Nwosu](/people/joost-nwosu/), Postdoctoral Fellow, School of
> Continuous Improvement

The film's three cases each give the question a different answer.
[A community choir](/outputs/slop-poster-a-clip-on-tuner-xn7rl8/) retires a song
once its tuner log is satisfied;
[a school sausage sizzle](/outputs/slop-poster-sixty-sausages-is-sa52i5/) comes
in under its shire's limit, and the shire lowers the limit; and at
[a junior swim club](/outputs/slop-ad-a-junior-swim-wlbowu/), a personal best
becomes the mark every later swim is held to. Between them, the cases recast the
met target, whether retired, recalibrated or quietly retained, as a question of
stewardship rather than of achievement, extending the School's engagement with
everyday evaluative settings to whatever follows once the result is in.

The film arrives as the program enjoys the continuing backing of the School's
Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund, a measure of the confidence the University
places in work of this kind.

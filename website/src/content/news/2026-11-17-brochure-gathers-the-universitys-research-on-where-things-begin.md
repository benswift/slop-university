Sand grains, a stored skin patch, an optical bench at switch-on and a tenancy
provision in its first courts do not usually share a page. In
[_Where It First Takes Hold_](/outputs/slop-brochure-where-it-first-jgccwl/),
a new research showcase, they share a question: where, and how soon, does a
process begin?

The brochure was prepared by
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) and doctoral
candidate [Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/) of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). It sets
out three strands of the University's work, on the first site, the first
minutes and the first reading, and anchors each in published outputs from
across four schools.

> I had not realised how many of us were standing at the same doorway. It is a
> good thing to find out, and I suspect the brochure will introduce a few of us
> to each other.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Written for readers well beyond any one discipline, the publication translates laboratory, field and courtroom evidence into a shared
institutional vocabulary of onset, recurrence and settlement, and positions the
early stage of a process as a research capability in its own right. It is, in
the University's view, the clearest account yet of an interest its schools have
been pursuing separately for some time.

Ms Nwafor said the project had changed how she spends the start of a working
day, "mostly by making me notice it."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jgccwl.

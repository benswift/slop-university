A register is only as good as the nouns it begins with, and the Indicator Commons has spent a season gathering them at a suburban badminton club's supper table. The resulting paper, from the School of Continuous Improvement, gives the Commons four names for what members carry through the hall door on the last night of the season.

> Members had the words before we arrived. It was our good fortune to be the ones holding the notebook.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Dr Renke Sabel, Convenor of the Indicator Commons

The paper is led by Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, with Dr Sabel and Senior Research Fellow Dr Sten Okwuosa, Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas. Twenty-six members were interviewed in whatever terms they chose, and their vocabulary was carried into the Commons' design without alteration.

The Commons has made the four kinds the categories of a Plate Register. A supper is to be treated as set once the Register shows a declared plate of the first three kinds, and the club's trophy presentation and raffle draw are held until it does. The University regards the arrangement as a fitting home for an old courtesy.

"What a plate is for turns out to be a question people enjoy being asked," said Dr Hanke. The team is prepared to extend the approach to other tables in due course.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.20rwzw.

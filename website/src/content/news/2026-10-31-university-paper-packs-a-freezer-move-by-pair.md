> Every move teaches you something about what you chose to keep together. This one taught us that the order of a packing list is a scientific decision, and we are grateful to the colleagues who let us stand beside the trolley while they made it.
>
> --- Ms Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

With those words, Ms Anyanwu introduces
[_Pack by Pair_](/outputs/slop-paper-pack-by-pair-3q4xk5/), a new paper from
the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) written with
her supervisors, Dr Iben Chikere and Dr Ronja Oyelaran.

The paper accompanies a laboratory's retained serum archive, 1,344 aliquots from
a completed biosimilar study, across a three-stage move in twelve dry-ice
shippers, one of which warmed during a delayed handover. Through travelling and
resident control samples and a structured re-assay, the team connects
sample-level stability practice with the study-level comparison it ultimately
underpins, and sets out a packing approach that keeps a single crate's
misfortune shared evenly between the two arms of a study.

The paper extends the School's doctoral programme on organisational relocation
from the office restack into the laboratory, and in doing so brings the
discipline of the moving crate into conversation with bioanalytical method.

"The School has always believed that priorities take shape in the small
logistics of an institution," said Dr Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer and Deputy
Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey. "It is a pleasure to see that belief
travel, carefully, at minus eighty."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.3q4xk5.

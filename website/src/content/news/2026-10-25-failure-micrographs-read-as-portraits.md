Every reliability report ends with its pictures, and very few readers stop to look at them as pictures. [_The Void in Three-Quarter View_](/outputs/slop-paper-portrait-conventions-in-5xakel/), a new paper from the School of Emergent Priorities, does exactly that, and finds that a laboratory's way of seeing can change for twenty years without anyone deciding that it should.

The paper is by [Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities. It works across the image appendices of 418 failure-analysis reports on copper interconnect reliability, gathered from a single laboratory, and sets the conventions of portraiture alongside the everyday craft of the electron microscope. Framing, viewpoint, light and caption are each brought into a close conversation with the history of the picture, and the unsigned images are returned to the nine hands that made them.

> The people who made these images were never asked to sign them, and most of them were never thanked by name. I would like the paper to stand as a small, belated acknowledgement of how carefully they looked.
>
> --- Halvard Nnamani, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The study places the School's long-standing interest in how institutions attend to things within a materials setting it has rarely visited, and the School counts it among the more quietly ambitious contributions of its doctoral cohort to the University's research agenda.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5xakel.

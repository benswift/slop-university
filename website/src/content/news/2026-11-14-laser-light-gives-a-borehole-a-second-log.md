For four bench days this spring, a pulsed laser in the University's core store worked its way down a single drill hole, one crushed run of rock at a time. [_A Second-Harmonic Log of a Drill Hole_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-second-harmonic-log-f4938r/), a new research poster from [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) and [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), sets out what came back.

> A borehole has always been read in more than one light. We are glad to have added another, and glad above all that the School now has a reason to keep a laser beside its trays.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

Combining nonlinear optical screening, downhole logging practice and a diffraction cross-check, the work positions a classic materials-laboratory test as a working companion to the core shed's established instruments, aligning 111 recovered runs into a continuous account of the ground from its sandstone cover to the basement below. Through the cover the new log follows the quartz closely; beneath the contact it fades over some eleven metres rather than stopping; and three runs deep in the basement respond where no quartz could be found.

Associate Professor Beng said he looked forward to "the questions those three runs will put to the rest of the hole, and to whoever drills the next one".

The poster takes its place in a steadily lengthening shelf of laboratory work on recovered core, a shelf the University is content to watch grow one tray at a time.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.f4938r.

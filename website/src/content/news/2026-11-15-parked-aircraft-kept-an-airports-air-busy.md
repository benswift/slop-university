What does an airport breathe out when nothing is taking off?
[_Grounded, Not Idle_](/outputs/slop-paper-when-the-runway-ivzmzg/), a new
paper by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of
the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), puts that
question to an inland regional airport through the ten weeks of a 2020
lockdown in which its scheduled flights were suspended.

The answer is more than nothing. While the passenger timetable stood still,
nine parked turboprops were kept ready to fly through 236 engine ground runs,
and the paper finds those runs in the particle counts at the airfield's edge.

> The School of Settled Matters has spent its whole existence on the difference between a rule suspended and a rule repealed. An airfield, it turns out, keeps the same distinction, and we have come back from the fence line understanding our own subject a little better.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

Combining continuous particle monitoring with the airport's movement records
and an airline's maintenance log, the authors situate meteorological
normalisation within a wider conversation about how operational records and
environmental evidence can inform one another across the airport-community
interface.

Dr Obiora said the counters would "go back up on the mast whenever the next
quiet season comes along", and that the School of Underlying Conditions
regarded the fence line as very much part of its ground.

The University counts the paper among the steadiest examples yet of its
Schools agreeing to stand downwind of a question until the answer arrives.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ivzmzg.

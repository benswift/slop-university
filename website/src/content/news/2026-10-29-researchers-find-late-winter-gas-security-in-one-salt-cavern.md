> Winter has always set the University's timetable in ways the timetable does not admit. It was good to spend a few of them on a question that takes the season at its word.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

Dr Ntuli is one of three authors of
[_The Last Fortnight Comes Out of the Cavern_](/outputs/slop-poster-an-energy-security-study-pf89ex/),
a new research poster led by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) of the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and
[Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director of the
Master of Priority Studies in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). It asks what
keeps the gas flowing in an inland region when a hard cold spell arrives at the
end of the season rather than the start.

Working from 41 reconstructed winters and the withdrawal records of three
underground stores, the team placed a fourteen-day cold spell at each week of
the season and followed the region's supply through it. The approach brings
geological, operational and policy perspectives into a single frame, and
positions storage less as a quantity held than as a capability to be called
on. Late shortfalls, the poster finds, arrive while a third of the gas is still
underground, and the region's small salt cavern carries much of the final
week.

Associate Professor Uzoma said the cavern had "earned rather more of the
School's attention than its size would suggest", and that the group's next
interest was in how such stores are kept in reserve.

The poster now forms part of the University's unhurried record of the systems a
community depends on without needing to look at them.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pf89ex.

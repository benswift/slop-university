A driving-test result is recorded against one name. A research poster published
today by the School of Emergent Priorities asks how much of it belongs to the
person named.

Eighteen thousand seven hundred and four first-attempt tests, taken at eleven
centres over twenty-six months, were modelled with candidate, examiner, centre
and assessment route fitted at once rather than one at a time. Routes at those
centres are handed out by queue position at check-in, which let the allocation
be treated as given rather than chosen.

The route a candidate drew accounted for 14.8 per cent of the variation in
outcomes and the examiner for 9.3, against 6.1 per cent for everything the
record holds about the candidate. Adjusted first-attempt pass rates ran from
51.3 to 78.9 per cent across the forty-one routes, in an order that held for the
whole period.

> The result has four names in it and the form has one. That is not a complaint
> about the form, which was never asked to carry four.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The licensing authority now publishes a route-difficulty adjustment for
reporting. Schools have begun booking the lead times that draw the adjusted
routes, and the next calibration will be fitted to the bookings it produced.

"A test that is fair between candidates and a test that is fair between routes
are two different achievements, and only the first has ever been asked for,"
said Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied
Measurement.

The School of Emergent Priorities regards a result that redistributes an
established verdict, rather than disputing it, as the more durable contribution,
and the University puts the poster forward in those terms.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wa62kt.

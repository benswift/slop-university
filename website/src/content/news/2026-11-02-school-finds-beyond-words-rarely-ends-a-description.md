When people say that an experience cannot be put into words, they very seldom
stop there. That is the central finding of
[_Indescribable, at Length_](/outputs/slop-paper-what-people-go-ikaekw/), a new
paper by [Ms Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/), Doctoral Candidate, and
[Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/), Lecturer, both of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> We set out to listen for the moment people run out of words, and kept finding it was the moment they began. I suspect the School will be listening for that moment in a good many other rooms.
>
> --- Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Across ninety-two long-form conversations with forty-six adults, each held twice
and six months apart, the study repositions the statement "there aren't words
for it" from an endpoint to an entry point, mapping the descriptive repertoire
that follows it and tracking how that repertoire consolidates across successive
tellings. The paper concerns how a past experience is talked about, and leaves
the experience itself to the people who had it.

The University regards the paper as a considered addition to its growing
understanding of how people account for what they have been through, and of
what an account keeps from one telling to the next. Dr Chikere said the second
round of interviews "sits very comfortably beside the School's longstanding
interest in what survives a retelling, and I am glad to see it arrive in a
setting none of us would have predicted".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ikaekw.

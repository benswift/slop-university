For fifty-two weeks, two observers walked the same four blocks of one regional
main street at a quarter past six and again at a quarter past seven, and wrote
down which doors were open. _Edited, Not Opened_, published today by the School
of Emergent Priorities, sets that record beside the number the precinct's
business chamber was publishing over the same year.

The chamber reports evening trading coverage: the share of member premises whose
directory listing declares a closing time of six o'clock or later. Across a
twelve-month activation pilot it rose from 31.3 to 57.3 per cent, and the pilot
was extended on the strength of it. Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Dr Anouk Mensah and Dr
Lindiwe Achterberg matched every movement in that figure to a premises, and found
21 of the 27 shops that entered the category had changed a line in a directory
and nothing else. Fourteen premises that did begin trading later never edited
their listing, and appear in the figure nowhere.

> Nobody in that precinct was doing anything other than what they were asked to
> do. That is what made the year worth spending on the footpath.
>
> --- Dr Ronja Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The work extends the School's programme on self-maintained institutional
records, translating a monthly directory export and a walking route into a
matched pair of registers. Dr Mensah said the study "sits with a question the
School keeps returning to, which is what a record is for once something is
computed from it".

The University is proud to add the study to a growing body of work on the
instruments by which places account for themselves. The full paper is available
from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.d3oueo.

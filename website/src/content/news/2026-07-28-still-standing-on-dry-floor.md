The spill is mopped in a minute. The cone can stand there for a fortnight, and
in most systems neither event exists.

_Somebody Saw It First_ began its run this week across the University's portrait
display panels, in renderings matched for daylight and for after dark. The
artwork sets Dr Solveig Adeyemi in a shopping-centre concourse at a quiet hour,
notebook open, one warning cone standing on dry floor a few paces away and the
shoppers reduced to figures in the middle distance. Everything else on the panel
is a four-word headline, a sentence, an attribution and a code to scan.

Dr Adeyemi, a Lecturer in the School of Continuous Improvement, came to the
University from a frontline incident-reporting system, and has since followed
the report as an object in its own right --- through submission, escalation,
triage and closure --- tracking which details survive each handoff. The campaign
puts the standing interest underneath that work in front of an audience: the
incidents that never enter a register at all, and so are never available to be
closed, counted or improved upon.

> The interesting part is always the handoff nobody minutes.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

"An advertisement is an unusual place to concede how much a system does not
see," said Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard. "It
is also, for most people, the only place they will meet us."

The University regards the campaign as a plain statement of the School's
evaluation-ecosystem agenda, carried into the settings that agenda is about. The
full advertisement is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nru2wu.

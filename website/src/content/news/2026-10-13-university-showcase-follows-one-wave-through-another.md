On the cover of the University's latest research showcase, a beam of light enters a block of crystal and leaves it in several directions at once. What has divided it is a sound. _The Grating Made of Sound: Research where one wave reads another_ takes that small arrangement as its starting point and follows the idea outward, through the School of Underlying Conditions and into the work of the University's two other schools.

> People assume a showcase is where the questions stop. I would be glad if this one sent a few back to us, and gladder still if they were ones we had not thought to put to the bench ourselves.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The showcase aligns three strands of existing research around a single organising figure. A stored grating on a basement optical bench anchors the first; the hammer tap on drill core and the pulse sent down a monitoring bore carry the second; and the third mobilises a body of work on ultrasonic, acoustic and photometric sensing in car parks, streets and country towns. Eight published University outputs are cited by title and identifier, positioning the brochure as a point of entry to the research record as much as an account of it.

The University holds that a research culture shows its maturity in the connections it is able to draw across its own shelves, and regards this showcase as a considered instance of that capability. Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the School, said the brochure "puts neighbouring work in the same room, which is most of what any of us ask of a publication". Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory, also contributed.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xasktq.

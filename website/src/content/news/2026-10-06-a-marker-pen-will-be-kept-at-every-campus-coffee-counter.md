A marker pen on a string will, from the coming teaching period, hang above the pickup end of every coffee counter on campus, and it will stay there until a result the University has already published turns out to hold. The arrangement follows "Notes on a Marker Pen at the Coffee Counter", a marketing poster released today by the School of Emergent Priorities, which fronts the work of Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate.

Ms Onyekwere's doctoral thesis found that an item in a shared plan arrives when it has a name against it and not when it has thumbs. The School wished to know how far the finding travels, and a busy coffee counter was judged a fair place to ask. A name was written on one cup in each pair and left off the other, and the cups were followed across the counter and out of the door. The School reports that the named cups left no sooner than the unnamed ones, which is to say that the finding did not repeat.

> A result that declines to repeat has still told us where it was standing, and we are grateful to the counter for standing there too.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The School has not withdrawn the finding. It has instead asked Facilities to keep a pen at each counter, so that the attempt can be made again whenever the queue allows, and the arrangement will be reviewed once the finding is reported to hold. The University is proud to be an institution that keeps the instrument ready for a result it has not yet had twice.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8cn9vj.

> A good question rarely arrives alone. We are grateful for all the company this one has brought into the School, and in no hurry to see any of it leave.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

With those words Dr Tredinnick introduces [_What the Rhythm Still Carries_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-long-neuropeptide-xpm14u/), a research poster in which the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) turns its attention to neuroscience, and to a small dissected nerve circuit from the crab whose steady rhythm a single neuropeptide can quicken.

The study brings a standing laboratory routine, a carefully staged sequence of peptide exposures and a recovery measure fixed in advance into one evidence-led account of how a circuit finds its way back to its own baseline once the peptide has been rinsed away. Across 24 preparations, the customary thirty-minute rinse served short exposures well and long exposures far less so: after the longest applications, seven of eight preparations were still outside their baseline when the clock said they should not be.

The poster extends the School's inquiry into the life sciences, a reach the University regards as one of the enduring rewards of letting its scholars follow a question wherever it leads.

"Any routine worth keeping is worth a second look," said Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, who co-authored the poster with Dr Tredinnick and Senior Lecturer Dr Odalys Penhallow. "We are glad to have found one that rewarded the attention."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.xpm14u.

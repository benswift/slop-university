Six in every ten objects left on a staircase for the next trip up are not waiting for it. That is the finding behind _Staged Means Stored_, a research poster by Signy Okpara, Thandiwe Solberg and Casimir Beng of the School of Continuous Improvement, and it has become a standing limit for the households that took part.

> Nobody minded our standing at the foot of the stairs, which says something about stairs, or perhaps about bin night.
>
> --- Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The study followed 52 households over thirteen weeks and sorted 1,286 objects into five kinds by what each was waiting for. Only one kind, the carry, went up on the next trip. The others waited on an errand, on another resident, on an upstairs home that did not exist, or on nothing the household could name.

From the next cycle, each participating household will hold at most one tread in reserve, counted at the bin-night walk-through. An object still on the stairs at its third walk-through will be entered as stored, and the phrase "for the next trip" will no longer be used of it. The tread, not the object, is the unit of count.

Associate Professor Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, said the arrangement "keeps to how people already talk about their hallways, with a date added." The School counts it a good season's work when a hallway can be described in five words.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.trgyj9.

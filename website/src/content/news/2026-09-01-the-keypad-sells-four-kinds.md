Four kinds of fruit come off a supermarket produce scale. Slop University has
decided that only one of them still qualifies a host site for student
placement hours. A new marketing poster from the School of Emergent
Priorities is the public face of that rule, and the face of this spring's
Master of Priority Studies intake.

The campaign reads the PLU keypad as a sorter, not a scale: keyed as weighed,
keyed as the cheapest nearby code, keyed as a staff override, or weighed and
never keyed at all. Placement hours now follow a single named kind. Hosts
whose pads sit in the other three are not on the roster for the intake.

> The keypad was already doing the sorting. We have only agreed to read it as
> a roster.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee, School of Emergent Priorities

The release sits inside the School's longer work on how an open question
becomes a roster line. Naming the kinds is what made the hours allocable;
leaving them unassigned would have been the decision the intake was trying
not to make.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rxn5of.

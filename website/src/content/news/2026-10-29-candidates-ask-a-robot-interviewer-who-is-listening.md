Every interview ends with an invitation to ask. A new paper from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/),
[_Will You Pass That On?_](/outputs/slop-paper-whether-candidates-take-8daixb/),
asks what happens when the invitation comes from a robot. The answer, it
turns out, is that candidates still ask, and increasingly ask about who is
listening.

The study, by [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate, and
[Dr Thandiwe Solberg](/people/thandiwe-solberg/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor,
Demo Quarter, brought structured interview practice, a purpose-configured
social robot and a carefully staged handover moment into a single experimental
setting. Across 160 mock interviews, the team built a richer picture of
candidate engagement at the point where the interviewer stops asking and the
candidate is invited to begin.

> We close our own interviews the same way everyone does, and I don't think the
> School had ever stopped to ask which "us" it meant. Now that we have, I
> suspect we will say it a little more carefully.
>
> --- Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The paper sits squarely within the School's attention to the parts of a
procedure that live just outside its structure, and the University counts it
among the quieter contributions to its understanding of how a selection process
sounds from the other side of the desk. Dr Solberg described the closing
question as "the one part of the interview that belongs to the candidate", and
said the School would be giving it more of its attention in the year ahead.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8daixb.

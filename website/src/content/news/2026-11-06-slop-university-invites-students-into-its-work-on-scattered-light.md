Light in disordered media has become one of the few research interests shared
by all three of the University's Schools, and a new prospectus now offers it to
prospective students as a single course of study. _No Mirrors Required_,
prepared by [Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/) with doctoral candidates
Eivind Onwuka and Ylva Onyekwere, takes the random laser as its organising
image: a light source that does without the usual pair of mirrors and relies
instead on the medium around it.

> Writing for a reader who has not yet arrived turned out to be a useful audit
> of what we already do. We found the same question being asked in three
> buildings, and it was a pleasure to put it in one place at last.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The prospectus moves across the University's footprint in the field, from a
basement optics bench in the School of Underlying Conditions to measurement
practice in the Master of Applied Measurement and bench chemistry in the School
of Emergent Priorities, positioning each as a distinct entry point into a
shared agenda. Five published studies anchor the offer, alongside reflections
from researchers in each School on what it means to work where the signal and
its medium cannot be pulled apart.

Eivind Onwuka, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Continuous Improvement,
described the document as "a map with several entrances, which suits a subject
where nothing travels in a straight line."

The release continues the University's practice of introducing its programs
through the work its researchers have already put on the record.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nth135.

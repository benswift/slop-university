A day with the seat map open did not settle anyone on the square they had already paid for. _SEAT: Seat-map Early Allocation Timing_, a paper released today by Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo with Dr Lindiwe Achterberg and Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist of the School of Emergent Priorities, reports last-minute swaps at 6.14 per cent of occupied seats when the interactive map was released twenty-four hours early and at 6.07 per cent when it was held until doors.

> Showing the empty squares a day early did not change who sat in them. We have stopped treating the map as a reminder.
>
> --- Ylva Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Fourteen multiplexes of a single exhibitor were assigned at random, seven to each clock, for twenty weeks. The box-office attendant walked the house with a handheld wand in the ten minutes after doors and logged every occupied seat against its ticket. 186,412 booked seats entered the file. The interval around a 0.07-point difference excluded the 0.8-point movement the protocol had treated as worth a change of practice. An ablation that dropped the map's locational marker left the estimate where it was.

The Office of Research Outputs has entered SEAT on the Horizon Register as a closed question. Affiliated box-office attendants withhold the interactive seat map until the session is called. A later request to release it early does not survive the next review.

Dr Achterberg, Lecturer, said the attendant's console "already knew when to show the squares. The trial gave that timing a finding to keep." The University treats the closed question as the rule the booking app now runs.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.x00ai4.

How an institution's language outlasts its intentions has long been a central
question for the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/)
and its anticipatory-capability agenda. A new paper takes that question
somewhere the School has not been before: the machine room of a municipal
tabulating bureau in the 1960s.

[_The Same Letter, Three Rules_](/outputs/slop-paper-replaying-the-index-card-qv7r3m/),
by [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/), Postdoctoral Fellow, and
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement
Grand Rounds in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), brings
archival transcription together with contemporary replay methods. The team
rebuilt each revision of the bureau's index-card run book as a working rule and
set it against 23,406 logged jobs. The resulting picture is of a vocabulary that
held steady while its meaning moved three times: the letter A began as a
deadline, became a department and ended as whatever the Superintendent's office
directed, while its share of the bureau's work rose from 31% to 58%.

> We came to the archive expecting to learn about a bureau. Somewhere in the
> second box of cards it became clear that the School keeps a run book of its
> own, and that it might reward the same patient reading.
>
> --- Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Ntuli described the collaboration as "a welcome new chapter in the
University's long conversation between the people who plan work and the people
who do it."

The University regards the paper as a considered expression of its commitment
to reading institutions through their own records, past and present.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qv7r3m.

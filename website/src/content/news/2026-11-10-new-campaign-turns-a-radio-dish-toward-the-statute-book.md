Astronomy and law rarely share a noticeboard. This week they share a signage
panel, as the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) releases a
portrait campaign built on a single observation: _Everything we receive was sent
long ago._

The panel sets a lone radio dish on a night-time plain, an observer at its foot
unrolling a blank strip of chart across the grass. Its featured research is
[_The Presumption of Purpose_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-presumption-of-axvxor/),
by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) and
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), which arranges the operating
records of a retired solar farm so that a later reader can tell a chosen setting
from one nobody ever touched.

> A radio telescope and a law library keep much the same hours. Both are waiting
> on something that set out well before anyone thought to listen for it, and if
> the panel sends a few passers-by to the slower of the two, the School will
> count that a fair return.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The campaign draws the School's interpretive practice, a house-style
illustration of long-horizon listening and a short reading list from the
University's own repository into one coherent proposition, extending a
reader-centred agenda into the places where people wait for a bus. It reflects
the University's settled confidence that its schools are best understood when
read across one another.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.dlls0d.

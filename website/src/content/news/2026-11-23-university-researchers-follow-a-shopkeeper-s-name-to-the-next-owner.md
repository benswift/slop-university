The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) has long
taken an interest in the things an organisation is called by people other than
itself. Its newest research poster,
[_The Name Stays with the Counter_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-adopted-local-na9nkx/),
carries that interest to the general-goods shops of a West African market town,
where the Chinese proprietors behind the counters are known to their customers
by local first names.

> We went looking for the people behind the names and kept finding the names
> waiting for the next person. I hope the shops will recognise themselves in
> it, under whichever name they are answering to this season.
>
> --- Mr [Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate,
> School of Emergent Priorities

Mr Nnamani, Ms [Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/) and Professor
[Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/) brought fieldwork, counter observation
and conversation together across 63 shops and 41 changes of ownership,
situating the everyday act of being greeted within a wider landscape of
succession, goodwill and belonging. In 29 of those 41 handovers, the name went
with the shop.

Professor Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, said the study had
"shown the Group a trajectory that belongs to a doorway rather than a person",
and that she expected the School to "keep walking past that doorway for some
time yet".

The University considers the poster a measured contribution to how a community
comes to know the places it returns to, and a credit to the patience of the
shopkeepers, assistants and customers who took part.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.na9nkx.

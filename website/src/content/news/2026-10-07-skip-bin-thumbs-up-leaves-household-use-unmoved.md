The School of Continuous Improvement has filed a closed null on the skip-bin post. Asking the street group chat for a thumbs-up does not change how many households put something in, and the University has bound every event invite to that result.

_Asking for a thumbs-up on the skip-bin post does not change who uses the skip_, a poster released today by Mr Eskil Nwabueze with Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu of the School of Continuous Improvement and Dr Iben Chikere of the School of Emergent Priorities, reports household-use rates of 42.4 per cent when asked and 41.6 per cent when not, a difference of 0.8 percentage points across 148 skip-weekends.

> A confirmation that moves nobody is still a confirmation. We have stopped letting it size the room.
>
> --- Eskil Nwabueze, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

Streets were assigned at random to ask or not to ask; use was counted at the kerb and skip fill scored at collection. The 3 percentage-point threshold registered in advance was not met. From the finding date every University event invite still carries a react-to-confirm, but the count is forbidden as an allocation key. Places, kit and rooms follow a lottery among all invitees, with no override.

Dr Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, said the lottery "puts a name on a seat the react never booked." The release records a closed null, and the lottery is the rule the University will now keep.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u3g5fo.

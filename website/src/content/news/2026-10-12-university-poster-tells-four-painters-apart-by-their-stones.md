> I came to pictures late and assumed the interesting part would be higher up.
> We intend to go on looking at the bottom inch of things for as long as anyone
> will hold the lamp.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The remark accompanies _The Hand in the Clod: Morellian Attribution from the
Painted Foregrounds of Thirty-Eight Anonymous Devotional Panels_, a research
poster by Dr Obiora with Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement
Relations Group, and Sunniva Balogun, Doctoral Candidate. It takes a group of
small unsigned panels long filed under a single provincial workshop and asks
who, within that workshop, painted which.

The poster aligns an established attribution practice with a part of the
picture that attribution has tended to step over. Six habits of rendering
stones, clods and cracked earth were recorded across all 38 panels, and four
foreground hands were distinguished across 31 of them. In 11 of those, the hand
responsible for the ground was not the hand responsible for the figures, and
the authors propose a workshop that apportioned its labour by depth. Seven
panels are left unassigned.

It is the University's practice to be pleased by work it could not have thought
to commission, and it is pleased. Associate Professor Uzoma said the poster
"puts to a group of paintings the question this School puts to everything, and
the paintings turn out to have been expecting it".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ke49fm.

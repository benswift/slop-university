An hour on the University's shared hydraulic test loop can go unused in one
school while another waits weeks for it. The Department of Policy Settings has
now asked the schools whether that is how the hour should be shared.
[_Allocating Time on the Shared Hydraulic Test Loop_](/outputs/slop-consult-the-setting-that-y5gzdd/),
issued today by the Consultation and Uptake Division, reviews the setting made
in August 2026 that divides the loop's time among the schools in proportion to
their research headcount.

> The loop has now completed a full quarter under the Determination, which means
> the Department finally has a record to read. Some of what it shows is what we
> expected, and some of it we would like the schools to explain to us.
>
> --- First Assistant Secretary, Consultation and Uptake Division, Department of Policy Settings

The paper sets the first quarter's allocations beside their use, and weighs them
against three of the University's published studies, among them a paper on
restarting a pilot loop after it has stood and a reading of how operators
describe a misbehaving control loop in their shift log. It offers three options,
from retaining the headcount basis to booked windows and to concurrent use
paused whenever the loop's pressure falls below a floor, with ten consultation
questions along the way.

The review continues the Department's practice of returning to each setting
once the setting has a history of its own, and of hearing first from those who
work under it. Schools whose allocations go unused are particularly encouraged
to respond.

Submissions close at 5 pm on Friday 5 February 2027 and may be lodged by email
to policy.settings@slop.university. The full consultation paper is available
from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.y5gzdd.

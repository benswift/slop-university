> There will be other fortnights and other notebooks. For now we are content to have kept this one in order, and to know where the gaps in it are.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

So says the lead author of _The Word That Was Not Said: Substitution before anticipated stuttering in a fourteen-day speech diary_, a research poster by Associate Professor Uzoma with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, now on the University's screens.

The poster reports on 31 adults who stutter, each of whom carried a pocket diary for fourteen days and wrote down two things whenever a word was exchanged for another: the word they had meant, and what was said in its place. The 1,284 entries that resulted were sorted by the kind of word intended and by what became of it. Ordinary content words were mostly traded for a synonym. A speaker's own name was traded in 6 per cent of entries, and 167 entries record nothing being said at all.

The work connects diary practice, word-level coding and the orderly handling of absence into one account of everyday speech, anchored in the diarists' own pages. It is descriptive throughout and offers no guidance on anyone's speech.

It is the University's settled habit to be pleased when a small record is kept well, and it sees no reason to make an exception here. Dr Emenike said the team had "counted what was there and then, at rather greater length, what was not, and found the second count the more companionable".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.bkx9ie.

The last line of a community sport club's results sheet is now before examiners.
_Nobody Finishes Last: The appointment, award and concealment of last place in
community sport clubs_, a doctoral thesis by Eskil Nwabueze, has been submitted
in the School of Continuous Improvement, where it was supervised by Dr Ingeborg
Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints, and Associate Professor
Casimir Beng, Associate Professor and Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab.

> The School compiles a good many lists and had not, until this thesis, thought
> to read one from the bottom up.
>
> --- Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints

Mr Nwabueze counted what 164 club series do about the foot of their results and
found that 141 had a standing arrangement for it: a list that stops short, a
handicap, a wooden spoon, or a volunteer rostered to come in behind everyone. He
then followed the slowest finisher through 5,208 mornings of weekly timed runs
and placed an appointed last in 18 club series by lot. Where somebody else held
the bottom row, the member above it came back more often.

Under the Last Place Roster, which the School has since adopted, every ranked
list it publishes will carry a rostered member of staff, assessed after the last
genuine entrant and recorded one place below them, on a row with nothing written
beside it. An appointment may be exchanged; it may not be declined. Mr Nwabueze
said the Region's results officers had "shown me where a list stops, and who
asked".

The School considers it the mark of a settled research culture that a finding
about the foot of a list should be acted on from the head of one.

The full thesis is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.oip71b.

A council reading a street's own overnight parking tally as official
evidence is common enough now that Slop University's School of Continuous
Improvement has put a name to what it does to the people who keep it.

Interviews with nineteen households, held nine months after the permit
office began citing the street's own register in renewal decisions, found
residents describing the log less as a private tally than as an audience
they now write for. Fourteen of the nineteen reported logging a visiting
relative's car at least once to hold a bay overnight; smaller groups
described rounding an arrival time, confirming a neighbour's entry by text
first, or asking another household to log for them while away.

> A record built to settle an argument between neighbours doesn't stay a
> private document once an office starts reading it.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living
> Dashboard, School of Continuous Improvement

Dr Fenna Okoro, Convenor of the School of Emergent Priorities' Horizon
Register, welcomed the finding as consistent with what her own program has
traced in registers of a very different kind: "an entry stops being a note
to yourself the moment somebody official is going to read it back to you."

The team coded interview transcripts against the categories the register
itself already used, rather than against any outside measure of what
actually happened on the street each night.

On the strength of the findings, the School's Indicator Commons will publish
a model handover protocol setting out what a permit office may and may not
infer from a resident-kept tally, for adoption across the registers its
evaluation-ecosystem agenda already catalogues.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4xapu3.

A queue is one of the few institutional signals that measures itself: people
can see it, count it, and act on it, all before anyone confirms what it is
actually evidence of. Slop University has published _Read Past The Queue_, a
new prospectus brochure built around exactly that gap, aimed at anyone
weighing up a place at the University.

The brochure profiles the School of Emergent Priorities' Master of Priority
Studies and the School of Continuous Improvement's Master of Applied
Measurement side by side, and follows both Schools' researchers out of the
seminar room and onto the street, the market, and the service counter where
the instruments they study are already running. Two previously published
findings anchor the pitch, each cited by name and DOI: an audit of
self-storage upgrades quietly counted as demand, and a validation study of a
swimming pool's speed-tier system that tracks overtakes rather than pace.

> Counted As Growth was one number nobody meant to inflate. Seeing it
> collected into a brochure for people choosing a university is the part I
> didn't expect.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, School
> of Emergent Priorities

"The brochure existing at all is the argument," said Associate Professor
Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies. "A prospectus
that cites its own findings by DOI is behaving exactly like the students
we're hoping it attracts."

The University treats the brochure as a small test of its own convention:
that a research organisation should be willing to point at what it has
already published, DOI and all, rather than promise something new.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ro171j.

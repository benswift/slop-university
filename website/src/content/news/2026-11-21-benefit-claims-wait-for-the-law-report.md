Ten months is roughly how long a change in the law took to reach the benefits counter. [_Read Into Effect_](/outputs/slop-poster-disability-support-claims-n0p7of/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), follows fourteen appellate rulings on a single disability-support statute and finds that claims under each newly construed provision began to rise only after the ruling appeared in the law reports. Rulings that were never reported left claiming where it was.

> A drafter writes every provision for a reader who turns up after the debate is over. It has been a pleasure to learn how often that reader is the claimant, and the School intends to keep them in view.
>
> --- Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), School of Settled Matters

The poster brings the School's close reading of judgments into conversation with the lodgement record, aligning each decision's passage from bench to bookshelf with the claims activity it was expected to shape, and situating the result within a broader understanding of how legal meaning travels through an institution. Associate Professor Threlkeld led the work with Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and Mr [Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), a doctoral candidate in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

Dr Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead of the Received Readings Group, said the Group was "pleased to see the bound volume given its due, and a little sorry on behalf of the judgments that never reached the shelf".

The University counts the poster among its contributions to the patient work of carrying a decision from the bench to the counter, and to the School's continuing account of when a reading of the law can be said to have arrived.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n0p7of.

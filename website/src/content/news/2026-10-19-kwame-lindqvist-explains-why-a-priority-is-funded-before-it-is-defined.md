A priority, as [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/)
describes it, usually has its budget well before it has a definition. _The
Definition Comes Later_, a new video profile from the School of Emergent
Priorities, gives the Director of the Master of Priority Studies a minute to set
out that observation himself, and to show where it has taken his research.

> Priorities rarely arrive finished, and I have never thought of that as a
> problem. It means there is always something for a new cohort to watch take
> shape.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist](/people/kwame-lindqvist/), Director,
> Master of Priority Studies

Its examples are deliberately close to home. Drawn from three of his most-cited
papers, they move from
[a tailoring shop's turnaround guarantee](/outputs/slop-paper-a-single-site-case-8gqp7o/)
to
[a retail chain's review software](/outputs/slop-paper-a-chain-retailer-s-pt89my/)
and
[a campus parking scorecard](/outputs/slop-paper-auditing-the-parking-6apmck/),
situating everyday resourcing decisions within a broader account of how
commitment and definition come to be sequenced across institutional settings.
Along the way, the film pauses on a citation record that few in the School can
match.

For the University, the profile is a welcome occasion to introduce a
consistently cited scholar to audiences well beyond his School, and to give the
Master of Priority Studies a face and a voice.

The full video is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7musg3.

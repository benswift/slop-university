Nine coffee carts, an overhead depth sensor above each, and a reading every five
minutes for two years: that is the instrument behind
[_The Queue as Leading Indicator_](/outputs/slop-poster-coffee-cart-queue-lengths-sn9kzr/).
In _The Queue as Leading Indicator, Explained_, a new research explainer from
the School of Continuous Improvement, the poster's three authors talk through
what it has to say about the University's standing in the world.

> Our School spends a great deal of time measuring other organisations. It has
> done us good to stand in line with everyone else for a while.
>
> --- [Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and
> Convenor, Review Cadence Observatory

The film sets the queue readings beside the University's monthly ranking and
arrives at the poster's central observation: the queue moved first, by a steady
two quarters, so that a peak at the carts was followed two quarters later by a
rise in the ranking. [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) frames the
relationship as correlational rather than causal, and the film notes that only
headcounts were ever kept. [Dr Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/) then sets
out the next stage of the work, a closed-loop pilot in which the live queue
index would steer a fixed share of discretionary grant strategy, with no manual
override, before [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/)
closes the film on the poster's own summary of the lead.

Everyday campus life has long been one of the University's richest research
resources, and the School's film shows it being put to good use.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4ek2jf.

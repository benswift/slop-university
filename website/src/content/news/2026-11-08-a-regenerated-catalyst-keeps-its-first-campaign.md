One batch of copper catalyst, divided four ways, each portion sent through a different first campaign and then restored by exactly the same procedure six times over: that is the apparatus behind [_Regenerated, Not Restored_](/outputs/slop-poster-regenerated-not-restored-nzee06/), a new research poster led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), and [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/), Senior Lecturer in the School of Settled Matters.

The team reports that every bed's conversion of carbon dioxide came back within two regenerations, while the bed first run with water in its feed still made methanol 6.1 points less selectively than a fresh one after the sixth.

> A regeneration log asks one question of a bed, and it is the easy one. The School would rather be asked the harder one, about where the bed has been, and expects to be asked it more often from here.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The poster brings reactor practice, catalyst history and the record-keeping of the process laboratory into a single framework for thinking about what a restored material carries with it. It sits comfortably within the University's growing presence in the chemistry of carbon dioxide, and within a School whose first season has ranged well beyond the law library.

Mr Nwabueze credited the technicians who kept four reactors running in step, saying that "a study like this is only as good as the people who never let one bed get ahead of the others".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.nzee06.

Sun, wind, a battery bank and a generator in a shed: at the end of a long line, few sites rely on only one of them, and the University's researchers have spent the past seasons asking how such sources come to agree. [_More Than One Source_](/outputs/slop-brochure-more-than-one-tbxn0p/), a research showcase published today by the School of Underlying Conditions, brings that work together between two covers.

Prepared by [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group, with [Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/), Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, the brochure is organised around four features: the array sized against the bore it draws on, the standby source and what it delivers, the soil beneath the racks, and the controller that decides which source carries the load.

> Putting the brochure together showed us something about our own School. We had thought of ourselves as people who study what lies underneath, and it turns out that a good deal of what lies underneath is the energy system.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The showcase positions hybrid systems as an integrative frontier, aligning earth science, measurement practice and the institutional study of priority across a shared portfolio of sites and settings. Seven published studies anchor its account, each cited by title and DOI for the reader who wishes to go further.

"Every page of it raises a question we have not yet asked," said Mr Nnamani. "That seems to me the right way for a brochure to end."

The release reflects a University increasingly at ease with holding more than one source in view at once.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.tbxn0p.

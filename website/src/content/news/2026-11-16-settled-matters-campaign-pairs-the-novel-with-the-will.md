A novel and a will, laid open at either hand of a reading-room table, make up
the University's newest portrait panel.
[_Read the Novel. Then Read the Will._](/outputs/slop-ad-read-the-novel-q7tr4m/)
is a display campaign from the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), and its face is
[Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), who teaches the
School's course in land law.

> Most people meet land law long before they enrol, usually in chapter three,
> when somebody's uncle dies and the house goes to the wrong cousin. The School
> has found it is more welcome in those chapters than it expected to be.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

In carrying the School's teaching onto the University's portrait screens, the
campaign positions the inheritance plot as an entry point to property
doctrine, bringing close reading, conveyancing practice and the study of
succession into one accessible invitation.

Readers who follow the fine print are pointed to three outputs from the
University's record:
[_Noted on Title_](/outputs/slop-paper-whether-a-restrictive-fnpeur/), Dr
Tredinnick's own paper on a covenant of forgotten purpose;
[_Fortsetzung folgt_](/outputs/slop-poster-fortsetzung-folgt-the-vl61qt/), on
serial novels left unfinished in a provincial daily; and
[_Seven Years Unheard Of_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-common-law-presumption-jkfkom/),
on the presumption of death and the files of a probate registry.

The University regards the panel as a fair likeness of a law school that reads
widely and expects its students to do the same.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.q7tr4m.

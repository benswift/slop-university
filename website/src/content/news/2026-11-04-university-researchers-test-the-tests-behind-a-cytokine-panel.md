In a well-run laboratory, the software that turns a plate of samples into a
table of results is seldom the thing anyone thinks to question.
[_Survivors at the Asymptotes_](/outputs/slop-poster-mutation-analysis-of-4xny8v/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), questions
it closely. The work is led by [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/),
Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard, with
[Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/), Lecturer and Convenor of Evaluation of
Evaluation.

> Our program exists to ask what stands behind an assurance. A test suite turns
> out to be an unusually courteous subject: it answers every question put to it,
> and in the same order every time.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

Hosted by an immunology core facility that opened its in-house analysis
pipeline to the team, the researchers introduced 2,904 small, deliberate
changes into the code and observed which of them the existing tests would
notice, before mapping the results back across each stage of the analysis
workflow. A compact set of new test cases, drawn from the edges of the assay's
working range, considerably strengthened the suite where it had been thinnest.

The University sees in the poster a welcome extension of its attention to the
quiet machinery on which so much of its research depends. Dr Hanke said she
hoped the approach would travel: "There is a version of this study waiting
beside every instrument on campus, and we would like to meet as many of them as
we can."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.4xny8v.

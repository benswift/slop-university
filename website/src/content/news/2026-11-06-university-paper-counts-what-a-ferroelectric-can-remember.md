Thirty-six switches, two hundred and fifty-six possible arrangements, and on an
ordinary day fewer than two turning points remembered. Those figures sit at the
centre of
[_How Much Does a Hysteresis Loop Remember?_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-much-a-lbxs48/),
a new paper by doctoral candidate [Ms Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/) of
the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> The School spends much of its time on what an organisation carries from one
> cycle into the next. It has been a quiet pleasure to follow that question all
> the way into a ceramic and find it waiting there.
>
> --- Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The paper takes up the Preisach model, a long-standing mathematical account of
how ferroelectric ceramics carry the history of the fields applied to them, and
recasts it as a counting problem. Rather than fitting the model to any one
material, Ms Okpara asks what such a medium can hold at all, and settles the
matter exactly: its capacity, the shortest route into each of its states, and
the memory it retains when the field is left to chance.

The University welcomes the paper as a question carried across disciplines
without losing its shape, and is pleased to see it come from a candidate whose
principal research lies in another field entirely. Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior
Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, and one of Ms Okpara's supervisors, said
the work showed "a side of a candidate's thinking that a supervisor is not
always invited to see, and we are glad it was written down."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lbxs48.

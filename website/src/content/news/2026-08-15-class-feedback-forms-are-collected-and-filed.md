The School of Emergent Priorities has spent several years on a narrow question:
what an institution keeps of the consultations it holds. _Handed Out While the
Chairs Went Up_, published today, takes that question to one metropolitan
council's five neighbourhood centres and the single sheet handed out at the end
of every term class.

The paper pairs 33 interviews with tutors, coordinators and programs staff
against a census of eleven terms of the paper archive: 3,417 returned forms
across 1,046 classes, a return rate of 96.8 per cent. It separates the
instrument into three stages, and finds only the first at ceiling. Across the
same eleven terms of agendas, minutes and annual reports, a return is referred
to four times, always as a count. Classes continue by default at 89.2 per cent,
on room and tutor availability.

> There is no villain in a filing cabinet. That is the finding, and it is the
> part we would ask people to sit with.
>
> --- Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow, School of Emergent Priorities

Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group, said the
study "sits squarely in the School's long interest in the difference between
what an institution keeps and what it merely holds". The University counts the
work among the research it is glad to have supported, and regards the
three-stage frame as a durable addition to the School's anticipatory-capability
agenda.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1qd9gp.

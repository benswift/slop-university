Self-checkout floor attendants and the register that scores their overrides describe two different bagging-area alerts, Slop University researchers have found. An interview study with 34 attendants across nine supermarket and strip-mall self-checkout banks recorded what attendants call an override in their own words --- a beeper, a top-up, a real catch --- and set those accounts against the same events as logged by the School of Emergent Priorities' Assist Index, the instrument now used to rank each attendant's shift-priority tier. The two accounts agreed closely on only one of the four most common categories.

The Index's assist ratio has, since April, gated preferred shifts without an exception process; attendants whose banks generate more logged assists now rank lower. For the School, the findings mark an early attempt to hold a scoring instrument to the same vocabulary the people it scores already use.

> Every attendant we spoke with already had a name for this. Ours was the first project willing to write their names down instead of ours.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The study pairs 34 staff interviews with the Index's own event log, translating each into a shared vocabulary before comparing them side by side --- an approach the School positions within its wider anticipatory-capability agenda, extending its standing interest in what an institutional record keeps and what it quietly leaves out.

"The Index was built to make frontline effort visible," said Professor Verity Marris, Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group. "What Dr Chikere's team has shown is how much visibility still depends on whose account you start from --- and that's exactly the kind of question we want the Group's next instrument to sit with."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.icxqz9.

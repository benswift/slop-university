The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) usually concerns itself with what an institution can sense coming. Its latest publication turns that attention to something much nearer: the sound of the rooms, walls and courtyards a neighbourhood shares.

[_A Quieter Neighbour_](/outputs/slop-brochure-a-quieter-neighbour-fsogi4/) is by [Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/), Doctoral Candidate in the School, with [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the Basement Relations Group in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/). Written for residents, club committees and anyone who looks after a shared place, it is an open invitation to work with the University on the spaces where everyday sound is negotiated.

The brochure moves through three settings. It begins in the community hall, with the arrangements volunteer committees make for the time being; descends to the shared wall and the basement corridor, where neighbours learn to share what passes between them; and steps outside into the courtyard, where quiet turns out to be something people keep. Along the way it gathers six of the University's published studies of sound, hearing and quiet into a single reading list.

> Our School spends a great deal of its time listening for what is on the way. Putting this together reminded us how much of it is already in the room, usually leaning against a wall, waiting for someone to decide where it hangs.
>
> --- Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Associate Professor Uzoma said the brochure was "an open door, which in our corner of the building is never a small gesture".

The publication adds to the University's steady practice of introducing its research to the street before the street has to come looking for it.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.fsogi4.

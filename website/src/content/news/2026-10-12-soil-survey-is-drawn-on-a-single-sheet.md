A circle, a short bar and a counter are now sufficient to say where a soil sample is. _Every Token in Its Place: Come and see_ is a brochure from the School of Underlying Conditions, prepared by Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, with Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, and Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Associate Professor and Lead, Basement Relations Group.

> The School has learned that it had a shape before it had a picture of one. I
> would not say the picture changed anything. It has made us easier to visit.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow

Three themes carry the brochure from the auger hole to the reference collection, aligning each stage a sample can occupy with the step that moves it on and setting the whole arrangement within a notation drawn from the study of concurrent systems. A further theme extends the drawing to the core shed. Seven published outputs from all three Schools are cited by title and DOI in support.

The University regards a school that can point to itself as an asset to those still looking, and commends the brochure to colleagues whose own work waits on other work. Associate Professor Uzoma said the Group had "always known roughly how much core was in the shed, and is glad to have been introduced to the rest of the question."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zm3s7l.

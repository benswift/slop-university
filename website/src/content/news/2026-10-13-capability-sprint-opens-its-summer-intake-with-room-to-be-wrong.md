Applications for the summer intake of Deciding Under Measurement, one of the
University's Capability Sprints, open with a display advertisement released
today across the campus signage rotation. Its campaign line, _Build in Room to
Be Wrong_, is the Sprint's syllabus at the length a corridor allows.

The advertisement is the work of Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer in the
School of Underlying Conditions, and Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu, Lecturer and
Convenor, Capability Sprints, in the School of Continuous Improvement. It marks
the first intake for which the earth sciences school supplies the Sprint's
field cases.

> Anyone who has built on ground they could not see has already taken this
> course once, without the certificate. We would like to offer the certificate.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The Sprint aligns design practice, decision capability and measurement literacy
around a single posture: that a figure may be relied upon and doubted in the
same sitting. Participants move from tolerance-aware thinking toward
allowance-led choosing, mobilising cases drawn from the ground beneath ordinary
structures. Dr Nwachukwu said the program had "always asked people to decide
well, and is now in a position to ask them how much room they left".

The University regards a short course that two of its schools can teach
together as evidence of a maturing cross-school capability, and expects the
arrangement to inform future intakes. Prospective applicants may follow the
advertisement's address to the Sprint's pages.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ge1gjg.

Since the middle of 2024, 171 lines have been worn across the University's
lawns by people taking the shorter way, and 22 of them are now paths. The
Department of Policy Settings sets out how the rest were dealt with in
[_Returned to Lawn_](/outputs/slop-qon-the-worn-paths-setting-828yay/), its
written answers to questions put by the Committee on Settings Oversight at its
Supplementary hearings of 19 November 2026.

The questions were asked by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) of the School of
Underlying Conditions, [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/)
of the School of Settled Matters and
[Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/) of the School of Emergent
Priorities, and range from the half-yearly grounds review to the records it
keeps and the map it maintains.

> Every line on the schedule tells the Division something about how the grounds
> are used, and each of them is given its turn at review. The Division
> appreciates the care the members have brought to the subject.
>
> --- The First Assistant Secretary, Settings Maintenance Division

The answers bring the worn-line schedule, the Register of Paths and the
considerations that guide each review together in one account of how the
University's landscape is kept under settings. Associate Professor Uzoma said
she would "be walking the long way round with the answers in hand". The
Department regards the bundle as part of its ongoing stewardship of a campus
that is, by design, always being reconsidered.

The full answers are available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.828yay.

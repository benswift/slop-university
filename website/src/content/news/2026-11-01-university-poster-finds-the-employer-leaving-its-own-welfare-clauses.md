Between 1987 and 2023, one regional water utility's staff handbook went from
listing 31 welfare provisions to listing 57. Over the same years, the sentences
naming the employer as the party that provides them fell from more than half of
all welfare clauses to fewer than one in ten. That is the central reading of
[_Support Is Available_](/outputs/slop-poster-support-is-available-rl7f2o/), a
research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) by
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, with
[Dr Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the
Strategic Drift Survey, and [Dr Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/),
Postdoctoral Fellow, both of the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> Anyone with a drawer of old handbooks can try this at home. Read the welfare
> section aloud, one edition after another, and you hear the change some time
> before you are able to say what it is.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The team brought a functional-grammar lens to bear on a document genre most
staff encounter once, at induction, and rarely again. Working across the full
run of editions, they mapped each provision's participant structure onto a
shared coding frame, surfacing a steady movement from obligation-led
formulations towards availability-led ones without any reduction in what the
handbook offers.

The poster sits comfortably within the University's long-standing investment in
reading the everyday paperwork of working life with the same care given to any
other primary source. Dr Mensah described the coding frame as "something we
would be delighted to see other readers pick up and argue with", and the
authors have made it available alongside the clause concordance.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rl7f2o.

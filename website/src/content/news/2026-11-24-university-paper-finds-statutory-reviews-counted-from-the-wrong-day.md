When should a legislature look back at a program it has created? [_Counted from the Wrong Day_](/outputs/slop-paper-when-a-statute-s-wagf7r/), a new paper from Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and Dr [Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), suggests the answer depends less on the calendar than on how long a program takes to show what it has done.

> Most of these clauses were written by careful people following the best precedent they had. The three years were never the problem. We would simply like the next precedent to start the clock a little later.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The paper brings value-of-information analysis to the everyday craft of legislative drafting, framing each review clause as a forward commitment about when evidence will be ready to inform a decision. Applying that framework to 164 review clauses, the authors find that one review in five falls due before the program it examines can have produced a single observable outcome, and they offer drafters a simple adjustment that recovers most of the value at stake.

The work sits naturally within the University's long-standing interest in decisions that are made once and lived with for years, and the University is delighted to see two of its schools bring their quite different habits of mind to the same few lines of statute.

Dr Ntuli thanked the colleagues across the University who keep its own review calendars, "and who were kind enough not to ask which of them we had checked."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wagf7r.

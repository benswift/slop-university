The last person to leave an open-plan floor already has a word for what they do at the alarm panel. In a paper released today, they call it a sweep: a short walk of the aisle they sit on, after which a shut meeting-room door is empty and a coat on a chair is not. The School of Emergent Priorities has taken that word as a specification. SWEEP, Spoken Walk for Every Empty Place, is now the only close the panel will accept.

The paper, by Sunniva Balogun, Dr Iben Chikere and Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, interviews twenty-nine last-outs on seven floors in five organisations and keeps their phrasing as the codes. Facilities leads who received the themes have bound the alarm to a named-empty for every enclosed room, with no override. A floor that cannot complete the walk does not close.

The release records a close the University is now prepared to keep as part of the School's anticipatory-capability agenda, in which a vernacular already in use is treated as a commitment once it has been written down.

> A close that only walks the aisle you sit on is still a close, until someone writes the rooms down. The last-outs had the word for it already.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

Ms Balogun, Doctoral Candidate in the School, said the walk-sheet had not invented a new round. "We asked people what they were already doing, and then we asked the panel to wait until they had done it in every room."

Associate Professor Lindqvist, Director of the Master of Priority Studies, said a second walker is now required on any floor that has missed a room twice, and that fire doors propped open so rooms can be named from the aisle are part of the close the protocol currently produces.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.uwmgpz.

The [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) has long been interested in what people keep doing once nobody is asking them to. [_Restraint Already Spent_](/outputs/slop-poster-restraint-already-spent-qqtn7k/), a new research poster led by Dr [Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), brings that interest to the household meter, and to the plans that count on households to cut back when fuel runs short.

The team followed 1,140 households in six towns for eighteen months after an eleven-week supply interruption, setting monthly meter readings and fuel deliveries beside a regular account of which saving measures each home still kept. Thermostats turned down tended to stay down, while car-pooling and consolidated shopping trips mostly drifted back. The poster then carries that persistence through to the emergency reserve itself, and finds less of it left to call on than the plan assumes.

> A School devoted to improvement does not often stop to ask what it would do with a habit that stays improved. This poster has put that question to us gently, and I suspect we will be answering it in our own corridors for some time.
>
> --- Dr [Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor, Capability Sprints, School of Continuous Improvement

The work brings together the School of Continuous Improvement and the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/), with doctoral candidates Ms [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/) and Mr [Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/) as co-authors, and adds to the University's steady attention to the ordinary arithmetic of energy security. Mr Ilesanmi said the study had been "a chance to watch an arrangement arrive as temporary and settle in without anyone quite deciding that it should", and that he hoped the households who kept their logbooks would find the poster a fair likeness.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qqtn7k.

The School of Continuous Improvement has agreed to pay for a book to be
replaced slowly. Its Indicator Stewardship Seed Fund has awarded $538,274 to Dr
Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions,
and Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Continuous
Improvement, for the field colour book carried by the University's soil survey.

A surveyor names a soil's colour by holding a crumb of it behind a hole in a
page of printed chips and choosing the nearest. The book goes wherever the
surveyor goes, which is outdoors, and its chips have been keeping the same
hours. The survey has for some time been matching its soils to pages that are
themselves a little paler than they were.

> I have trusted that book in most kinds of weather, and it has never once
> mentioned its own condition. It seems fair that somebody should now ask
> after it.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying
> Conditions

The project will retire the book a page at a time. Before a page leaves the
binder, it and its successor will be read against the same clods, in the same
light, by the same two people, and the difference written down, so that
descriptions made on the old page can still be understood beside those made on
the new. The grant also provides for a standing rule on who decides that a page
has had enough sun, a decision the survey has until now left to the page.

Dr Nwosu said the School had "looked after a good many indicators that were
harder to hold, and few that were easier to leave on a dashboard, in the
vehicular sense".

The University regards the award as stewardship practised at the scale where
it is most easily postponed.

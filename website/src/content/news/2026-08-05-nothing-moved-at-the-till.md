  34 food-court counters and reports a null, with the estimate sitting inside a
  margin of indifference fixed before the data were opened.

One point eight four million itemised till records, gathered fortnight by
fortnight from thirty-four food-court counters, sit behind a result the School
of Continuous Improvement has published as a finding of no difference.

_Reached, and Unmoved_, deposited today through the Office of Research Outputs,
follows 18 outlets across the fortnight their menu boards began carrying
kilojoule figures, against 16 matched counters in the same food courts that did
not. Mean energy per transaction moves by seven kilojoules, downward, with a 90
per cent interval running from minus 41 to plus 27. The margin of indifference,
100 kilojoules either way, was lodged before the team was given the data, and
the estimate sits well inside it.

> A study that finds nothing is usually a study that could not have found
> anything. This one was built the other way round. We said in advance how small
> a difference we would be willing to call nothing, and then we went and looked.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer, School of Continuous Improvement

Alongside the tills, the team stopped 412 diners at the same counters. Three in
five recalled seeing the figures, and one in thirteen said the figures had
changed what they ordered.

"People are not misremembering," said Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow in the
School. "They read the number, they can tell you they read it, and the register
records the same lunch either way."

The health district's healthy-choices indicator counts consumers reached ---
transactions at premises displaying the figures --- and climbs with every outlet
that comes into scope. The 1.84 million transactions behind the study are the
same 1.84 million the indicator counts.

Slop University takes some satisfaction in depositing a null on the same footing
as everything else it publishes, and the School regards the margin, rather than
the estimate, as the contribution.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.puo2j3.

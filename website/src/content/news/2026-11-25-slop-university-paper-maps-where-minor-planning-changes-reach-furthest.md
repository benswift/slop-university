Planning and mathematics meet on the suburban block in [_Minor for the Scheme, Total for the Lot_](/outputs/slop-paper-planning-scheme-controls-as-zfa5te/), a new paper by Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and Dr [Ronja Oyelaran](/people/ronja-oyelaran/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> A good provision is written for someone who turns up after the argument is over. We spent a pleasant year picturing that person, and we have tried to leave them something tidy to find.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The paper brings the tools of variational analysis to the everyday machinery of residential planning. Treating a schedule's setback rules as a single connected system, the authors followed more than eleven thousand lots through a sequence of modest amendments, building a lot-level picture of how small adjustments to the rules travel outward to the households that hold them. Most lots, they report, absorbed each change much as the rules intended. A smaller group of narrow and rear lots, 648 in all, absorbed it rather more completely.

The work closes with a proposed form of words that planning authorities might consider when deciding how much notice a change deserves. Dr Oyelaran said she hoped it would find its way onto "the desks where schedules are actually kept, and stay there a while."

The University is glad to see two of its schools arrive at the same suburban street from opposite ends, and to find them in such good agreement once they got there.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zfa5te.

The School of Emergent Priorities has spent several years asking how things
end. With
[_What the Roof Keeps_](/outputs/slop-poster-what-decommissioned-rooftop-7lbfp4/),
a new research poster from Dr Marek Solheim and Ms Ylva Onyekwere, that inquiry
climbs the stair hatch and steps out onto the roofline.

The poster brings the methods of architecture and design criticism to a
question the energy sector has tended to leave to the recyclers: what becomes
of a building's roof once its solar array stops. Across sixty-four flat roofs
in one inner-city district, the authors pair measured roof plans with the
minute books of the owners corporations that once approved each installation,
and classify what remains into a typology of racking, ballast, conduit and the
pale rectangles a panel leaves in the membrane beneath it. Most roofs, they
report, still carry something, and the arrays that were simply allowed to lapse
have left considerably more behind than those whose ending was decided.

> I went up expecting to record where the arrays used to be. I came down with a
> better sense of how long a roof can go on holding a decision nobody made, and
> I suspect the School will be turning that over for some time.
>
> --- Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Horizon Register

The work sits comfortably within the University's wider effort to treat
discontinuation as a design condition in its own right, rather than as the
quiet afterword to an installation. Ms Onyekwere, whose doctoral research
follows proposals through committee records, described the minute books as
"generous company on a cold roof".

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.7lbfp4.

Slop University has a short message for the district around it: bring the question.

[_Somebody Here Studies That_](/outputs/slop-brochure-somebody-here-studies-b3odbm/), a new civic brochure led by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) with [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) and [Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), sets out how the University's Schools meet the ordinary puzzles of the place they share. Its three features move from the reading of a local by-law, through the fairness of a turn at the counter, to the patient resurvey of the ground beneath the street, and six published studies from four Schools give residents somewhere to start reading.

> We would rather hear a question while it is still a small nuisance than after it has hardened into a dispute. The reading room is set up for exactly that conversation, and there is always a chair free.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The brochure stands among the University's most direct conversations yet with the community it calls home, and among the first public statements from its newest School. "Most of what I study I first noticed over a back fence," said Ms Ogunbanjo, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities. "It is good to see the University say plainly that the back fence is where a great deal of research begins."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b3odbm.

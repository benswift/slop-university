The [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) reads statutes, contracts and decided cases for a living. Its newest publication, [_Cut by Cut_](/outputs/slop-brochure-cut-by-cut-okeqkr/), turns that habit towards a substance most law schools leave to the chemists: crude oil.

> Lawyers spend most of their working lives at some distance from the things their documents describe. This brochure is what the School learned by closing a little of that distance, and it has made us better readers of our own material.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld

The research showcase is organised around three readings of the same barrel. The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) brings its core-shed practice and its archival work on well files; analysts across the University contribute their characterisation of recovered oil and their care over extraction end points; and the School of Settled Matters closes the sequence with the specifications, assays and agreements through which a measured cut becomes something owned. Eight published studies anchor the publication, from an ethnography of a refinery catalyst change-out to the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/)'s five-year account of its work with the oil and gas sector.

"A drum of spent catalyst changes hands far more quietly than any agreement would suggest," said [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the School, who co-authored the brochure with [Mr Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/) of the School of Continuous Improvement. "We were glad to be among the people listening for it."

The University counts the brochure among the clearest expressions yet of its preference for questions that several Schools can hold at once.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.okeqkr.

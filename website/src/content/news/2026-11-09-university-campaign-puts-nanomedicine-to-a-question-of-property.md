Futures in Committee, the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/)' seminar series, turns to nanomedicine this semester, and it has asked a land lawyer to set the question. For now, the question is a poster.

[_Who Owns What the Particle Picks Up?_](/outputs/slop-ad-who-owns-what-ly0dsh/), by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer in the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), is now running on the University's portrait signage panels. Its one line of argument places the field's smallest delivery vehicles inside a conversation more often held at a settlement table: what rides with a thing that travels, and whose it is when it arrives.

> A committee is at its best with one good question in front of it and no particular hurry. We have tried to supply the first, and the series has always been generous with the second.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The campaign aligns the School's standing interest in burdens that outlast their purpose with the pharmaceutical sciences' attention to the carrier, positioning property as a lens the formulation conversation may yet find it needs. Its small print directs readers to three earlier University works on terms that persist and carriers designed to vanish. The University is pleased to see its newest School carrying its questions so readily across the campus, and into a field it had not previously been asked to enter.

Registrations of interest are open through the University website, with details of the session to follow.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ly0dsh.

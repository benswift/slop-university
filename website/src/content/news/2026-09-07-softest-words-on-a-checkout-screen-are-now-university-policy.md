A softly worded decline draws roughly three times the retries of a bluntly
worded one, whatever actually went wrong with the card. A new Slop University
paper reads that difference and reads a whole University alongside it.

The paper, from the School of Continuous Improvement, sorts 1,142 transcribed
declined-transaction prompts from supermarket self-checkouts, strip-mall
EFTPOS terminals, and food-truck card readers into a nine-category taxonomy
of wording, from a soft "please try again" through to a blunt "card blocked,
contact your bank". Lead author Dr Bram Ntuli and colleagues find a
category's gentleness predicts whether a shopper retries far more reliably
than the transaction's actual, logged cause of failure.

> A shopper doesn't read the transaction. They read the sentence.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds, School
> of Continuous Improvement

The Office of the Vice-Chancellor has since directed every University service
point drafting a denial notice --- a library hold, a room-booking rejection, a
printer-credit refusal --- to adopt the taxonomy's gentlest category as
standard wording, with no carve-out for the notices a second attempt cannot
fix.

Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, welcomed
the taxonomy's reach beyond its original setting. "Most instruments stay
where they're built," he said. "This one made it all the way to the front
counter."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ej9vbl.

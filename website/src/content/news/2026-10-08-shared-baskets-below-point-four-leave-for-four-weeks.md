Each Friday, the School of Continuous Improvement will publish how often the things in its shared baskets come back to the basket they left, and any basket reading below 0.40 will be withdrawn for four weeks. The Indicator Commons has taken on the task, covering the School's pen cups, umbrella stands and trolley-coin baskets alike.

The rule follows _'Take One, Cross, Return It'_, a paper released today by Ms Signy Okpara, Associate Professor Casimir Beng and Dr Thandiwe Solberg.

> A flag that crosses one way and comes home the other way has still come home, and I would like that said plainly before anyone reads the other number.
>
> --- Ms Signy Okpara, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The paper describes a small rail of switches and a reader fitted beneath the hand-flag baskets at nineteen suburban crossings, and the term of counting it made possible. Almost every flag was found back in one of its crossing's baskets by the end of the day, while a smaller share had returned to the one it had left. A residents' liaison panel adopted the smaller share as its measure, and the paper follows what became of the baskets that fell short of it.

The School has set the lesson to work close to home. Baskets are read weekly, withdrawn when they fall below the band, and returned to their place at the end of the fourth week without a further reading. No officer is empowered to waive a withdrawal. Associate Professor Beng said the term had "taught us what a basket will tell you once you stop asking where it has been," and Dr Solberg welcomed the clarity the arrangement gives to a thing that was always meant to refill itself.

The work reflects the School's continuing interest in the quiet instruments by which shared things are kept in circulation. The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.otak7m.

How close should a reader be able to get to the place where a piece of
University fieldwork was done? The Department of Policy Settings has put that
question to the schools in
[_Reporting Field Sites to the Ten-Kilometre Cell_](/outputs/slop-consult-the-site-precision-setting-ivjlfm/), a
consultation paper on the Slop University (Field Site Reporting) Determination 2021.

The Determination asks every output to locate a field site no more precisely
than the ten-kilometre square of the University's reporting grid in which it
lies, with the exact position lodged in a register that is not published. The
paper sets out four options, from keeping the cell as it stands to reporting a
site as it was recorded in the field, and poses ten questions for the schools to
answer.

> The schools know their own ground far better than any setting can describe it. The Department is grateful in advance for every submission, and will give each the same careful reading.
>
> --- First Assistant Secretary, Consultation and Uptake Division

The paper reads the University's recent fieldwork outputs alongside the
Department's own administration of the setting, weighing the protection of
vulnerable sites against the value of returning to the same plot, slope or
channel, and situates the review within the wider framework of settings under
which the schools conduct their work. It offers a preliminary view, which the
Department stresses remains subject to the outcomes of consultation.

The review continues the Department's practice of putting a setting before the
people who work under it once it has been in force long enough to be
understood. Submissions close in early February.

The full consultation paper is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ivjlfm.

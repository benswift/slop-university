What does a thing keep, and where does it keep it? That question, the University has come to realise, has been running through the work of all four of its Schools at once. [_What Holds_](/outputs/slop-brochure-what-holds-a-zhxhyg/), a new case-for-support brochure led by the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), sets that work out in one place for the partners and supporters who might sustain it.

> We assembled it the way a law report is assembled: find the decisions that already agree, and set them side by side until the principle shows. What surprised us was how much of this University turned out to be in the business of keeping things.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer and Lead, Received Readings Group

The brochure moves through memory held in matter, memory held in networks and memory held in law, aligning experimental, mathematical and interpretive capability around a shared vocabulary of retention. Seven published outputs anchor its case, among them a lead disc imaged through two hundred cooldowns and a principled method for pruning the precedents a case-based system relies on.

[Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), a Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/) whose experimental series appears in its pages, sees the publication as a beginning. "Most of what we know about a material's memory, we learned by asking it the same question many times," he said. "I suspect the next few years will be spent asking it of rather larger things."

The brochure is the University's clearest account yet of a research strength it did not set out to build, and one it now intends to keep.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zhxhyg.

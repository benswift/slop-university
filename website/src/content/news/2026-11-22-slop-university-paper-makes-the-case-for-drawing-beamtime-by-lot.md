Scientific merit, on a close reading, may be something a research proposal clears rather than something it climbs. That is the proposition advanced in [_By Lot among the Fundable_](/outputs/slop-paper-lot-among-the-sbjz4r/), a new paper by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), which turns the School's long practice of statutory interpretation to the question of how a shared synchrotron decides whose experiment goes on the beamline.

> I spent eleven years writing clauses for readers I would never meet. It has been a quiet pleasure to become one of those readers at last, and to find that the drafter of an access policy chose their words rather more carefully than anyone had thought to notice.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The paper brings political theory, the interpretation of governing instruments and the practice of coherent X-ray imaging into a single conversation about allocation, legitimacy and the account a public facility owes the applicants it turns away. It positions the lottery as a principled complement to peer review, one that preserves the panel's judgement where that judgement can be exercised and lets chance operate openly where it already operates unseen.

The University counts the work among its contributions to the stewardship of shared research infrastructure, a field in which it has always preferred to arrive as a careful and well-prepared user.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sbjz4r.

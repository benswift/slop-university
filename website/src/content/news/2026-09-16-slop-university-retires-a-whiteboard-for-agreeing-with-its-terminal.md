A primary school's crossing roster has spent a full term proving one of Slop University's own aged-care findings wrong about itself. WATCH, a pilot from the School of Emergent Priorities' Trajectory Analytics Group, set out to test whether an earlier result, that a handwritten whiteboard quietly drifts from whatever digital record sits beside it, would reappear at the school gate the way it did at an aged-care front desk. It didn't. The volunteer coordinator's whiteboard and the crossing hut's terminal log tracked each other for the whole term, corner for corner.

The School reads that agreement as an answer in its own right. A new brochure released this week, led by Dr Ingrid Vasseur, argues that a board contributing nothing the terminal doesn't already hold has no further claim on the roster, and every WATCH-registered crossing is now moving to terminal-only rostering on a fixed timetable, with no paper fallback retained for the terminal's own outage days.

> A finding only earns its keep once it's been tested somewhere it wasn't built for. This time what came back was agreement, and agreement was enough to retire the board.
>
> — Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee, School of Emergent Priorities

Dr Sten Okwuosa, whose Impact Pathway Atlas has spent the year tracing which of the University's handwritten records are load-bearing, called the crossing roster's whiteboard "a clean case: it disagreed with nothing, so it had nowhere left to stand." The brochure profiling the pilot also carries the wider agenda WATCH sits inside, from the Adaptive Metrics Lab's sensor instruments to the Horizon Register's work on everyday participation records.

A discount sticker slapped on a soon-to-expire tin says more about a shop's technology budget than most owners realise, and the Trajectory Analytics Group has spent five months turning that observation into the entry test for the local buying co-operative.

Two trained raters fortnightly-audited thirty-four traders — supermarkets, strip-mall grocers and food trucks — against a five-level Use-By Discipline Maturity Model, from an ad hoc marker-pen sticker at the bottom to a shelf-edge display that reprices stock on its own at the top, then set each result beside what the same trader had already declared on its co-operative intake form. The comparison becomes the School of Emergent Priorities' latest account of how an ordinary shopfront habit hardens into who gets a seat at the table.

Self-declared and audited tiers matched for thirteen of the thirty-four traders. Every trader admitted to the buying co-operative in the past two intake rounds on a self-declared top tier held an audited tier of three or below.

> A form only ever tells you what a trader was prepared to write down before anyone asked twice. We built the audit so the shelf could answer instead.
>
> --- Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group

"Every one of the seven kept the sticker on the form, not the shelf," said Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities. "That's not dishonesty so much as an intake form doing exactly what an intake form is for."

The co-operative's charter currently verifies tier once, at intake, and does not revisit it for the life of a membership; the Group has put a spot-audit clause to the co-operative's next charter revision, timed to renewal rather than first arrival.

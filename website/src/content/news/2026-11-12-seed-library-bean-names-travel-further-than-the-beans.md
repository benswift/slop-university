Slop University's long interest in how a written record outlives the people who
made it has found an unexpected home on the shelves of a community seed library.
[_The Name Goes Further Than the Bean_](/outputs/slop-poster-names-that-travel-1z59m6/),
a new research poster led by
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with
[Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the same School and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), follows the
library's beans and their names through two decades of lending.

> Every catalogue is somebody's reading of what they were handed. We finish this
> study with far more questions about that than we began with, and we are
> grateful for every one of them.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The team brought genetic testing, ledger transcription and conversations with
growers together into a single account of how a variety moves through a
community. Six or more exchanges from its first deposit, a bean was still the
same bean in 83 per cent of cases, but it carried its original name in only 38
per cent. Most of those names changed not in a grower's garden but at the
library's own return desk, where a returned packet was filed under the nearest
name already on the shelf.

The poster adds to a widening seam of the University's research on custody,
transmission and the slow work of meaning, and reflects the School's conviction
that a ledger is worth reading closely wherever it is kept.

"There will be other seasons and other packets," said Dr Obiora, Senior Research
Fellow. "This is a good place to keep looking."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1z59m6.

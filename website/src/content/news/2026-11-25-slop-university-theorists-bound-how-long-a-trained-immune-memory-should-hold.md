Mathematics usually reaches immunology by way of statistics, asking what a body should do given what it is likely to meet. [_No Settled Point_](/outputs/slop-paper-trained-immunity-as-045nea/), a new paper by Dr [Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) with Dr [Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) and Mr [Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), asks what it should do when it can assume nothing at all.

The paper brings the toolkit of online algorithm design to the emerging conversation around trained innate immunity, recasting the decision to hold or release a heightened state as a structured trade-off between readiness and restraint. Across a sequence of formal results, the team shows that no rule which fixes its state for good can keep pace with the best choice made in hindsight, and that a responder able to forget at random can match, with a single level of memory, what a counting responder needs many levels to achieve.

> I will be taking this into the operations-research seminar next term, where students keep asking for a problem small enough to hold in one hand. This one fits, and there is a good deal inside it.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The work adds a theoretical voice to the University's research on how systems remember, and gives two schools that rarely share a seminar room a common set of definitions to argue over.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.045nea.

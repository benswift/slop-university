Receptionist-authored appointment reminders underperformed the vendor default
in every matched pair of a 52-practice trial. The School of Continuous
Improvement is publishing that reversal, and the Staff Health preferred-provider
panel has written the underperforming protocol into its conditions regardless.

Dr Mirela Hanke and colleagues pre-registered a conceptual replication of the
University's checkout-phrase finding --- that a staff-coined line at the till
outperformed the printed survey script --- in the reminder SMS of suburban
general practices. Practices assigned to the Custom-Line Appointment SMS
Protocol authored a unique sentence for every booking; control practices kept
the sentence the booking product already sent.

> The till finding is still the evidence the panel can point to. The booking-app
> estimate arrived later, and it arrived as a condition rather than as a
> correction.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

"A unique line per booking is now how a practice stays on the list," said
Professor Verity Marris, Director, Trajectory Analytics Group. "That is a
stewardship question for the panel, and it is the one it has answered."

The University is pleased to lodge the paper against an assumption that had
already begun to travel. Confirmation replies were lower under custom copy;
recorded attendance did not move with it. Eleven practices were removed from
the preferred-provider panel at the first two-cycle review for matching the
vendor default.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.958ufp.

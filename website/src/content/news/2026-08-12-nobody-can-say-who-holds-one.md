A community hall's key-sign-out register is meant to record who currently
holds a copy of the front-door key. In practice, the University has found, it
mostly records who once did: committee members who stood down years ago,
terms nobody ever crossed off, and no reliable way of saying, on any given
week, how many keys are actually still in circulation. A new Indicator
Stewardship Seed Fund grant will put that right.

The grant funds a full reconciliation round: every name currently entered
against the hall's front door, checked against whichever groups still meet
there, followed by a physical stocktake of how many key copies remain in
circulation. Where a name can no longer be matched to an active member, the
entry is retired rather than left standing. The project also installs what
the register has never had --- a named custodian who updates it at every
handover, instead of whenever somebody happens to notice the list has grown
past use.

> A register nobody checks against the room it describes stops being a
> register and starts being a rumour with a signature line.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer and Convenor Thandiwe Solberg, Demo Quarter, School of
> Continuous Improvement

Co-grantee Senior Lecturer and Convenor Renke Sabel, of the Indicator
Commons, reads the hall's problem as a familiar one at a smaller scale: "we
spend a great deal of effort designing an indicator and almost none deciding
when it should be retired. This is a modest register, but it's the same
pattern we catalogue everywhere else."

The University counts the round as a small instance of a larger case:
stewardship is upkeep, not a one-off audit, wherever the indicator happens to
sit. The grant is $274,158, awarded under the Indicator Stewardship Seed
Fund.

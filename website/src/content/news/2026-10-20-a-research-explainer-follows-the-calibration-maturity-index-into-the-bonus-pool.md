At the top of the School of Continuous Improvement's Calibration Maturity Index,
the median performance-review calibration session ran to nearly two hours, with
sixteen pages of evidence for every nominee, and its ratings agreed with an
independent panel's about as well as those of a session run on anecdote. _CMI,
Explained_, a new research explainer from the School, walks through the paper
behind that result,
[_CMI_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-calibration-maturity-oi3m67/), with its three
authors, [Dr Dagny Okafor](/people/dagny-okafor/),
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) and
[Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/).

> Most instruments live quietly in a spreadsheet. It has been a real pleasure to
> watch this one make its way out into the world, and now onto the screen.
>
> --- [Dr Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/), Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement
> Grand Rounds

Behind the film sits a design built for confidence: two trained coders scored
156 sessions across 52 business units of a professional-services firm against
the index's five named practices, from anecdote to blind recalibration, and an
outside panel re-scored the employees behind them without seeing either the
original ratings or the levels. Attained level and rating accuracy kept a
respectful distance throughout, and the explainer closes on the schedule of
bonus-pool multipliers the firm's remuneration committee adopted partway through
the study, keyed to the level alone.

An instrument that its partner organisation found ready for use before the study
describing it had concluded is, the School notes, a rare compliment to its
design.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.1u6zxa.

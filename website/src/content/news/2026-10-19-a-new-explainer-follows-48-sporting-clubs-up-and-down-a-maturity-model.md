In community sport, a place in the finals can depend on the paperwork. Under a
finals-eligibility rule anchored to the top tier of a five-level maturity model,
it depends on how a club administers its volunteers' working-with-children
checks, and _Towards Full Assurance, Explained_, a short research explainer from
the School of Emergent Priorities, asks what that tier is really keeping track
of.

> Every cohort I teach meets a maturity model sooner or later. I suspect this
> will be the one they remember.
>
> --- [Dr Torun Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/), Senior Lecturer and Director,
> Master of Applied Measurement

Forty-eight clubs from three sporting codes make up the study behind the film,
the poster
[_Towards Full Assurance_](/outputs/slop-poster-community-sporting-clubs-y8f9jx/),
and Club A gives it its most memorable season, climbing from Level 2 to Level 4
between the first two audit rounds before settling back by the third. Taken
across the whole sample, the film arrives at an understanding of maturity
assessment as a calendar-aware partnership between clubs and the frameworks that
support them, rounded out by a holdout of twelve clubs assessed on a date they
were never told. [Dr Marek Solheim](/people/marek-solheim/),
[Dr Ezeigwe](/people/torun-ezeigwe/) and
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/) each speak to their
part of the work.

The University warmly congratulates all three authors, and trusts that clubs
everywhere will recognise a little of their own season in the film.

The full explainer is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6rgwlj.

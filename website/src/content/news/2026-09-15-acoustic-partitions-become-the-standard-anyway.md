A franchise's open-plan back offices spent the last review cycle listening to themselves, one decibel at a time. Continuous sensors verified that a year-long rollout of acoustic desk partitions held compliance above ninety-six per cent across two hundred and fourteen review-season pods, and supervisor ratings, checked against the same cycle, did not move.

Doctoral candidate Hedvig Anyanwu, of the School of Emergent Priorities, led the analysis with Dr Iben Chikere, comparing ratings from the year before installation against the first fully sensor-verified cycle across thirty-eight franchise sites. The comparison found no association between a pod's certified compliance and the rating its occupant went on to receive.

> A specification doesn't need to change an outcome to become one. Ours just needed to be finished on time.
>
> --- Dr Iben Chikere, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

The partition specification will now govern every new franchise office fit-out nationally, with no waiver on cost or floor plan, regardless of the review cycle's result. The sensor network installed to verify compliance stays in place, its logs kept as the record of a standard that outlived the question it was built to answer.

The full research poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lnkwgj.

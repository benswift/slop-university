Six school-fete cake-stall sign-up sheets have been read, and the sheets have changed as a result. Under a standing condition agreed with the fete committees, a cell with a coloured cursor parked on it is held for forty-eight hours before anyone else may fill it.

> We came away with a warmer sense of what a shared sheet asks of the people who use it, and of how patiently they ask it.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register

The work carries the University's earlier finding on the unattended jacket from a chair in a reading room to a cell on a screen. Across 1,146 parked-cursor episodes, the placeholder held far less of the sheet than the jacket had held of the room, until the committees agreed to hold the cells for two days, at which point the earlier result was seen again under its original conditions. The Strategic Drift Survey has recorded the finding as reproduced on that basis, and the committees' sheet template now carries the hold as a fixture, to be reviewed each cycle.

Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey, said the Survey was glad to see "an established result find somewhere new to sit," and thanked the cake-stall convenors for their patience with the enquiry. Dr Anneke Tolan, Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Futures in Committee, is a co-author.

The University is pleased to see a careful result settle so comfortably into a new setting, and looks to the working-bee roster sheet as the next place it will be read.

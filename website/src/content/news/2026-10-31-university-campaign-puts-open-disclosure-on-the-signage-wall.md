An arcade of open plan drawers, emptied one technical drawing at a time into
the arms of whoever happens to be passing, is the image Slop University has
chosen for the newest poster in its portrait signage rotation. Prepared by Dr
Vigdis Emenike of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), the campaign
puts the University's practice of open disclosure before the public in a single
line: everything we know is prior art.

The poster positions publication as the University's first and most dependable
form of stewardship. Rather than holding ideas back for later protection, it
foregrounds an institutional posture in which each finding enters the shared
record as early as its authors can set it down, anchoring the University's
knowledge in the commons from the outset and enabling others to build on it
without first asking whose it is. A small-print reading line directs viewers to
three earlier outputs, among them work on who claims a shared sign-up sheet
first and on the documented ownership histories of donated stamp-album lots.

> A drawer does its best work open. I would like people to leave the arcade
> carrying more than they arrived with, and to feel no need to ask who it
> belonged to.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The campaign extends the University's settled preference for the open drawer
over the locked one, and arrives on the signage screens in matched daylight and
after-dark renderings.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wefqez.

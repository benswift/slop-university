Every narrated lesson the University publishes keeps a record of when its viewers move from one slide to the next, and of very little else. [_Somewhere in Slide Seven_](/outputs/slop-poster-interval-censored-survival-es9smp/), a new research poster from the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), sets out what that record can and cannot say about the moment a learner stops watching. It is the work of [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) and [Ms Gunnhild Ekwueme](/people/gunnhild-ekwueme/), a doctoral candidate in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

Across 4,316 viewing sessions with one 18-slide module, the team treated each early departure as known only to lie between two slide changes. Three animated worked examples took a little over a third of the running time and more than half of the departures. A smaller pilot that checked playback every ten seconds placed most of those departures well past the middle of the slide, not at its opening, and a single check at a random moment on each slide proved enough to recover the same picture.

> A bore tells you where the water stood when someone last looked, and a slide log tells you much the same about a student. The School is glad to have a second kind of record to keep an eye on.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The poster carries the School's long practice with sparse and irregular readings into the University's digital teaching environment, aligning established survival methods with the everyday instrumentation of the learning platform. Ms Ekwueme said the project had been "a natural companion to a doctorate that has always asked what reading costs, and who is around to notice."

The University welcomes the poster as part of its continuing attention to how its teaching is received at the far end of the screen.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.es9smp.

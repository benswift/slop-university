A number adopted to inform a decision has a habit of ending up making it, and
then of becoming the thing everyone is working towards. _What the Number Was
For_, a new video profile from the School of Continuous Improvement, introduces
[Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Deputy Convenor of the Living
Dashboard, who has made that habit the subject of her research.

> The best part of this work is the moment a student first looks up at a
> dashboard and asks what it is for. I have seen it many times now, and it is
> still my favourite question.
>
> --- [Dr Mirela Hanke](/people/mirela-hanke/), Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy
> Convenor, Living Dashboard

The film's organising idea is the path a number travels inside an organisation,
from measurement to basis for decision to goal in its own right. It is traced
through three of her most-cited studies:
[a cafe chain that scores friendliness by its tip screen](/outputs/slop-paper-an-instrument-validation-study-27mugw/),
[a vending-machine upgrade credited on the day it was installed](/outputs/slop-paper-auditing-whether-the-vcpl4k/)
and
[a gym chain whose members time their cancellations to keep a reliability score clean](/outputs/slop-paper-a-quasi-experiment-on-ln1awt/),
each offered as a chapter in the life of an indicator. Her own standing gets a
mention too: no other postdoctoral researcher at the University has a higher
h-index.

The University hopes to see the film on many of the screens her research
describes.

The full video is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.jw361c.

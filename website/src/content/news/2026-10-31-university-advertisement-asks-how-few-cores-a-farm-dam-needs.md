A grid of steel tubes stands in the cracked floor of a drained farm dam, a lone
figure kneels at one of them, and above the scene runs a single line: every dam
keeps a record.

[_Every Dam Keeps a Record_](/outputs/slop-ad-every-dam-keeps-qcp5fv/) is a
landscape display advertisement for the University's bus-stop panels and campus
screens, prepared by [Dr Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/), Lecturer
and Deputy Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). The panel
directs its audience to
[_How Many Cores Does a Farm Dam Need?_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-many-sediment-08eorn/),
the School of Underlying Conditions paper that cored one small stock dam at
every point of a grid and then asked how few of those cores a survey could have
managed with. The campaign carries that question out of the field season and
into the shared public realm, aligning the University's sampling capability
with the everyday experience of waiting.

> A bus stop is a fair place to ask how much is enough. Everyone there is
> already estimating something, and the panel simply offers them a dam to
> estimate alongside the bus.
>
> --- Dr Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer and Deputy Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group and an author of the paper, welcomed the study's turn
on the screens as "a chance for the dam to be read by people who will never
stand in it".

Beneath the call to action, the panel cites three further University studies:
the laminated fill of a silted stock pond, the
seed bank on a receding dam's drawdown slope, and the banding of ice lenses in
frozen cores. For the University, the campaign belongs to a long-standing
preference for knowing precisely how much it needs to know.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an

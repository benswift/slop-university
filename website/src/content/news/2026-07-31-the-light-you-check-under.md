A tin of paint leaves the shop having satisfied a tolerance measured under a
lamp above the counter. It is looked at, some hours later, on a wall in the late
afternoon. The University's new signage campaign is about the distance between
those two readings.

_Certified At The Counter. Judged On Your Wall._ began its run this week across
the University's landscape display panels, in matched renderings for screens in
daylight and after dark. The artwork holds a hardware-store paint bench near
closing: a fan of blank swatches raised under the lamp, a sealed tin turning in
the mixer, a customer waiting with an offcut of skirting board. Above the line
sit a numeral and the name of the instrument that produced it.

The instrument is the University's own. The Strategic Drift Survey, run out of
the School of Emergent Priorities, puts a figure on the distance between what an
organisation states and what it enacts, and across three cycles it has placed
the University first for measuring a thing where the thing is actually seen. It
has scored no other institution.

> What running the Survey on ourselves has taught the School is that the light
> you check under is a choice, and it is almost never the light anybody else
> will use.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

"There is a version of this campaign that says we are accurate," said Dr Ronja
Oyelaran, Senior Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities. "This one says
we know where the reading was taken, which is the more useful of the two."

The University is content to have the claim read in the kind of setting it came
from, on a panel, at a glance, by somebody carrying a piece of skirting board
home to check it in the light of their own room.

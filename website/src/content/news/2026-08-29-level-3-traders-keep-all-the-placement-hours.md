Three years of Capability Sprints placement hours at Slop University have
gone somewhere consistent, and the School of Continuous Improvement now has
the audit trail to show where.

The School built its four-stage Continuity Maturity Model to grade a
strip-mall trader's card-machine backup arrangements, from a single terminal
propped against a cash tin at Level 1 through to integrated dual-carrier
failover at Level 4. Capability Sprints placements were opened to any
shopfront attaining Level 3, framed at the time as a floor on operational
readiness rather than a target in itself. Ninety-six shopfronts were rated
at the model's introduction in 2023 and rated again this year on an
unchanged rubric, by assessors kept blind to each trader's earlier score.

> The floor was never meant to be the whole allocation rule. Three years of
> ledger entries say otherwise.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

Every placement hour on record went to a shopfront already at Level 3 or
above when the model launched; none of the fifty-three traders who started
below that line have since qualified. Associate Professor Casimir Beng,
Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, welcomed the finding as consistent with
what his own group keeps encountering: "a measurement system built to lift
the field it grades has a way of settling for grading it instead."

The School frames the result as an early instance of a diagnostic hardening
into a gate once eligibility is written against it, and says the Capability
Sprints panel is trialling a sponsor-letter pathway for Level 2 shopfronts
ahead of next year's intake.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0ikh5f.

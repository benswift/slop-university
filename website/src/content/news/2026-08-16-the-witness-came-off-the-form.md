Nothing happened, and that is the result. _Witnessed, and Otherwise Unchanged_,
released today by the School of Emergent Priorities, follows six classes of
statutory declaration handled by one state administrative agency across 72
months and 1.34 million lodgements. Partway through that window one of the six
lost its witnessing requirement; the other five kept theirs, and became the
comparison.

The team set the agency's own record of declarations later found to be
materially incorrect against the date the requirement came off the form, holding
everything else steady against the classes that retained it. The estimated
movement is 0.14 per 10,000 lodged, which the paper reports as equivalent to no
change at the threshold the agency's own review process works to.

> An absence takes more care to put on the record than a difference does, and we
> thought this one was worth the trouble of putting there properly.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

A second series runs alongside it. At nine signing stations staffed by the
state's honorary Justices of the Peace, documents witnessed per session fell by
more than 40 per cent while the roster stayed exactly as it was, and the
scheme's next annual return described that fall as a shortfall in accessible
capacity. Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director of the Master of
Priority Studies, called that half of the work "the part our students tend to
recognise first", adding that "an institution hears a number that moves, and the
hearing is its own subject".

The University regards a well-made null as among the harder contributions to
bring to the record, and the paper sits within the School's continuing work on
the distance between what an instrument registers and what it is read as
registering. The full paper is available from the University's research
repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zjm0s2.

What does a first-year teacher tell the person they will be in twelve months? [_Be Kind to Yourself_](/outputs/slop-paper-letters-to-the-873gls/), a new paper from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), takes the question to 168 letters written on the closing afternoon of an induction programme, sealed by their writers and posted back to them a year on.

> I have spent much of my career writing for readers who arrive after the conversation is over. It turns out that a year is long enough to make that reader of anyone, and the School is glad to have followed the question into the staffroom.
>
> --- Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), School of Settled Matters

Bringing the attentive habits of literary criticism to an everyday professional-learning practice, the paper sets each letter's address, tense and turns of phrase alongside the annotations of the 41 recipients who sent theirs back. It distinguishes the letters' hopes from their instructions, traces how one short sample letter came to shape the voice of later cohorts, and considers the clauses in which writers release a reader who may no longer be teaching.

The study sits comfortably within the University's sustained interest in documents that outlive the occasion that produced them, and in the people left to interpret them. Associate Professor Threlkeld said the archive had been "a reminder that most of a teacher's writing is done for someone who will read it later", and that the School would carry the reading forward with care.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.873gls.

The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has
always taken the view that a surface is best understood from below. Its newest
research poster,
[_The Deep Layers Hold_](/outputs/slop-poster-whether-microglial-reactivity-302atq/),
brings that conviction to the brain, and the University welcomes it as a
confident first step for the School into the neurosciences.

Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma and Senior Research Fellow Dr Brynjar Obiora
followed the brain's resident immune cells through the six layers of cortex
sitting above a small injury to the white matter beneath. Twelve weeks on, the
upper layers had largely returned to their usual quiet. The two deepest had
not, and the line between the settled and the unsettled was sharp.

> The cortex kept its layers in exactly the order the textbooks promised, which
> was a kindness. What it chose to do inside them is a conversation we are glad
> to have started.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The study brings together careful section-by-section recording, a rigorous
whole-of-tissue accounting framework and a layered reading of recovery, an
approach the School regards as a natural extension of its broader interest in
what rests on what.

Dr Obiora described the poster as "the product of many patient hands at many
benches, and a credit to every one of them."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.302atq.

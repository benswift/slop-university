The [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) will take
applications to the Master of Priority Studies for Semester 1 under a campaign
that begins somewhere few programs in foresight would think to look: a timber
nest box on a campus tree, and the feather of the small bird waiting at its
door.

[_The next priority is already nesting_](/outputs/slop-ad-the-next-priority-lhetgu/)
was developed by [Tove Ilesanmi](/people/tove-ilesanmi/) and
[Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/), Doctoral Candidates in the School,
for the University's portrait signage rotation.

> Every cohort arrives carrying something it has not yet noticed about itself.
> The program's work is to notice it early, together, and to stay patient
> about what it turns out to be.
>
> --- Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The advertisement brings the School's anticipatory-capability agenda down to
the scale of the smallest passenger, positioning the program's sensing,
sequencing and stewardship of priorities as a practice that starts long before
anything is large enough to be called a matter. Its small print points readers
to the University's published record, from a strategic plan for the parasites
of the campus waters to a study of what sets courtyard pigeons gathering, and
a survey of the program's own alumni.

Ms Anyanwu described the campaign as "an invitation to people who already
keep a hand lens in the glovebox, and to the ones who are about to."

For the School, the campaign continues a longer conversation with the
applicants it has yet to meet, held wherever they happen to be standing.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lhetgu.

Since 1971, a steel ball has been dropping into four tall cylinders on the teaching screens of one engineering faculty, and it has rarely dropped the same way twice for the same reasons. [_The Closer Pair_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-an-engineering-y7xsae/), a new paper by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), follows that demonstration through the faculty's audiovisual collection, from 16mm film to the lecture capture.

The paper brings the School's long-standing interest in how questions come to rest into conversation with media archaeology, film history and the material culture of teaching. Working across 52 productions and 1,184 coded shots, alongside interviews with the people who made and kept them, it maps how a single demonstration moved from one option among several to the image that opens nearly every account of the property, and how a pair of lines ruled for one day's filming travelled with it.

> A School that keeps a working set of annotated law reports should not have been surprised to find a backing card doing the same job. It has been a quiet lesson in how much of what we inherit arrives without its reasons attached.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The work adds to a growing body of research at the University on the institutional life of teaching materials, and on the habits that form wherever the same thing is made more than once. Dr Penhallow described it as "a study of the films everyone has seen and nobody has asked to borrow in years".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.y7xsae.

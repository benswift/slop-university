> We owe a great deal to the growers who have let us walk their fence lines and read their minute books. This brochure is, in large part, a thank-you to them, and an open door to anyone who would like to come along next season.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

With those words the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) introduces
[_Where the Paddock Ends_](/outputs/slop-brochure-where-the-paddock-os3abh/), a
new case for support that gathers the University's field-margin research into a
single invitation to partners, landholders and friends of the University.

Prepared by [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) with
[Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/), Research Fellow and Convenor of
the Review Cadence Observatory in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), and
[Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the School of
Underlying Conditions, the brochure follows the margin from three directions:
the soil that changes by degrees beneath a straight fence, the plants that
settle in the unsown strip beside it, and the belts, dams and taps where the
farm meets the water and the years.

It positions the headland and the fence strip as working parts of the
agricultural landscape, aligning close-spaced fieldwork, whole-record reading
and cross-school collaboration behind a shared account of the ground that
farming tends to pass over. Seven published outputs from three Schools anchor
the showcase.

The brochure extends the University's steady investment in research that begins
where others stop looking. Dr Vandermeer described it as "a good occasion to
put the field and the filing cabinet in the same room".

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.os3abh.

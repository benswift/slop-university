A golf club that trades its honesty-box ball return for a forensic evidence
panel does not get fewer disputes for the change, nor a faster path to
resolving the ones it still has. A poster published today by Dr Ingrid
Vasseur and Associate Professor Casimir Beng followed fourteen clubs through
exactly that switch and reports the same dispute rate, the same resolution
time, and the same level of member trust on both sides of it.

The study sets each club's honesty-box logs against the record its new Ball
Provenance Panel keeps once photograph-matched dimple wear replaces a shared
bin and a member's word. Nine months either side of the changeover, across
fourteen clubs, the numbers land close enough that the panel's forensic
apparatus reads as furniture rather than a fix.

> A panel that photographs every disputed ball is doing something. It just
> isn't doing the thing the club thought it was buying.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor, Futures in Committee

"Every measurement system we build eventually gets asked to double as a
verdict," said Associate Professor Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab.
"This one just did it a golf ball at a time."

The result joins a small, deliberately unglamorous stack of Slop University
findings on what a formal process changes, and what it merely relocates.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k0efdv.

The School of Emergent Priorities looks for an organisation's priorities
wherever they actually form, which is seldom in its plans. _What Stays Sealed_,
a film profile from the School, takes
[Ms Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/) back to where her own search began:
the crates handed out on an office's packing day. Before her candidature she
spent six years as a workplace-relocation coordinator, packing and unpacking
other people's offices, and in the film she explains why the crates that reach
the far end of a move still sealed have held her attention ever since.

> I have yet to watch a team pack without learning something about it by the end
> of the day. My own School has proved no exception.
>
> --- [Ms Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/), Doctoral Candidate, School
> of Emergent Priorities

Her thesis,
[_Carried Forward, Unopened_](/outputs/slop-thesis-crates-carried-forward-gekpe7/),
supplies the film's argument. Across four office restacks at three
organisations, what staff packed into a fixed allocation proved to be what the
organisation went on doing, while a crate carried unopened through a move kept
an earlier priority in place long after the conditions behind it had changed.
The thesis belongs to a larger conversation in the School about how priorities
emerge and persist, and brings organisational memory down to the scale of the
packing crate, where it can be stacked, carried and only occasionally opened.
The film also sets out the packing-day protocol, which the School has adopted
for its own priority-setting, and marks a citation record that has run well
ahead of [Ms Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/)'s candidature.

Few doctoral projects shape a School's own planning so early in a candidature,
and the University is proud to have this one in its research record.

A shared standing desk forgets who used it last within minutes of tap-out,
resetting to a default height for whoever arrives next. A new controller out
of the School of Emergent Priorities' Trajectory Analytics Group gives it a
memory instead, and Facilities has already put the memory's paper trail to
work.

A research poster from the School, led by Associate Professor Kwame
Lindqvist with Dr Marek Solheim and Dr Sten Okwuosa of the School of
Continuous Improvement, details a height-memory controller fitted to 214
shared standing desks. A worker's saved sit/stand preference now follows
their fob to whichever desk they tap into, and the controller logs every
switch by desk and zone along the way.

> A desk was never going to tell us anything about the floor plan. We just
> gave it a reason to start keeping score.
>
> --- Associate Professor Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority
> Studies, School of Emergent Priorities

Twelve weeks into the deployment, Facilities read the controller's
zone-by-zone switch counts against its own March floor-plan review and cut
desk numbers in the office's window zone from eighteen to eleven, ahead of
the controller's own accuracy audit closing out.

Dr Marek Solheim, who studies what an institution stops doing once it stops
seeing the case for it, said the desks made an unusually fast case against
themselves. "A priority normally waits for a budget cycle to be
discontinued," he said. "A desk apparently just needs six quiet weeks."

The School calls the result a small but useful proof of concept for a wider
claim it has spent the year building: a workplace fixture only needs a
reason to start counting.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n4p8lq.

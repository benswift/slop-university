The [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has spent its first months studying what lies beneath the surface. Its newest research poster turns to the businesses that dig it up, and to the decision each of them faces as the end of the pit comes into view.

[_Diversify Before the Pit Runs Out_](/outputs/slop-poster-diversify-before-the-lvojaj/) is the work of Associate Professor [Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) and Dr [Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/) of the School of Underlying Conditions, with Dr [Runa Adegoke](/people/runa-adegoke/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). Following 63 owner-operated sand-and-gravel quarries through twenty years of permit renewals, the team asks when an operator opens a yard selling gravel, pavers and mulch to the public. Most do it with five to ten years of reserve still in the ground, and 12 of the 14 yards opened in that window were still trading five years on.

> A pit has a last day, and the people who run one know roughly when it is. The School is glad to have spent some time with what they do in the years before it, and there is a good deal more of that ground still to cover.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

Bringing strategic management scholarship to the working quarry, the poster connects resource stewardship, enterprise renewal and regional capability within a single analytical frame, and positions the reserve horizon as a lens on how small firms plan their own succession. Dr Adegoke called the project "a welcome reason for two Schools to stand at the same gate and look in different directions".

The poster adds to a body of work across the University on what extractive sites leave behind, and the futures their operators sketch for them.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lvojaj.

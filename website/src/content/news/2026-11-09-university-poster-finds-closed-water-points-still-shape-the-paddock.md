The School of Settled Matters has made its first contribution to rangeland science, and its finding is that a stock-watering point does not stop organising the country around it when the water is turned off.

[_Ghost Piospheres_](/outputs/slop-poster-ghost-piospheres-vegetation-k9htsz/) is a new research poster by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with [Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow in the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and [Ms Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate in the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/). Across 34 water points on six leases, 22 of them closed between two and forty years ago, the team found grass returning to the bare ground nearest the old troughs while the soil surface beneath it kept the shape of the grazing it once received.

> We set out to bring the School's methods to the paddock. We have come home with rather more of the paddock in the School's methods, and that seems to us the right way round.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

In practical terms, the poster mobilises a radial survey design across a portfolio of working and retired water infrastructure, aligning vegetation, soil-surface and soil-carbon indicators within a single distance-decay framework to surface the legacy dimension of pastoral land use.

The University counts the work among the clearest signs yet that its Schools' ambitions run well beyond the disciplines that founded them. "Every answer the ground gave us arrived with two further questions attached, which is all one can reasonably ask of ground," said Dr Obiora.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.k9htsz.

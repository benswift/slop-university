What keeps a definition in place once nobody is enforcing it? For the dashboards and reports that run a modern organisation, [_Settled Past the Mandate_](/outputs/slop-paper-a-mean-field-potts-5dtg8k/) suggests the answer is the reports themselves. The new paper, by [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and [Dr Osei Vandermeer](/people/osei-vandermeer/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), finds that a business-intelligence estate can keep a certified definition long after the mandate behind it has lapsed.

> Every definition in an organisation was somebody's decision once, and most of them outlive the meeting that made it. I hope the colleagues who inherit those decisions will find the paper a companionable thing to read.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The study brings the modelling traditions of theoretical physics into dialogue with everyday analytics practice. It frames the copying of logic from one report to the next as a collective dynamic, and positions governance as a temporary influence on a system that largely organises itself. Calibrated against an estate of 640 reports, the model finds a certified definition holding for a median of 670 revision cycles once the mandate is withdrawn, with larger estates holding on for longer still.

"I spent years keeping reports running whose definitions nobody could quite account for," said Dr Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory. "To find them given this much patience feels like a kind of homecoming."

The paper takes its place among the University's continuing contributions to how organisations understand their own data.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5dtg8k.

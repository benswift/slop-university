A common-room tin has never had to answer to anyone before. Slop
University's School of Continuous Improvement has changed that, with a
four-kind taxonomy that decides, packet by packet, what a trolley may
legally hold.

The taxonomy sorts the tin's contents into Structural, Ceremonial,
Perishable, and Contested, and a fortnightly certification panel decides
which kind any new packet belongs to before Facilities is permitted to
restock it. For a school built on measuring what an institution already
does, teaching a tin to certify itself is being called an early proof of
concept.

The work translates a familiar sensory-panel exercise into standing
infrastructure: six raters, one panel, a taxonomy reissued on a fortnightly
cycle, and no packet permitted onto a trolley without a current
certificate.

> Sorting the tin taught us less about biscuits than about how quickly a
> shared resource finds a rule to live by, once somebody offers it one.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Torun Ezeigwe, Director of the Master of Applied
> Measurement

"It's a reasonable question to keep asking," said Lecturer and Convenor Bram
Ntuli, Improvement Grand Rounds, "and we would rather a trolley kept asking
it than assume the answer stayed settled." Facilities is understood to be
drafting a fifth kind.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n5hh77.

Six samples of one hillside, taken at six depths, have been turned into six
working solar electrodes. In
[_Down the Profile_](/outputs/slop-paper-hematite-photoanodes-made-ihmrdp/),
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, and
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, follow the iron of a single lateritic soil profile
from the pit face to the photoelectrochemical bench.

> None of this would have happened without the people who keep the School's
> profile collection in order, year after year, so that a question like this one
> has somewhere to start. The pit was ours; the habit of describing it
> carefully belongs to all of them.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper situates the field description of a soil profile as a working input
to materials research, aligning the surveyor's horizon-by-horizon practice with
the bench methods of solar fuels chemistry. Films cast from the iron-rich
ferricrete crust outperformed a control made from laboratory salt, while those
from deeper, clay-rich horizons trailed behind it. Across two of the boundaries
the surveyor had pencilled on the pit face, the electrodes changed where the
soil changed: sharply at one, gradually at the other.

The work extends the School's sustained attention to what lies beneath the
surface into territory where field and laboratory share a single sampling
frame.

"It has raised more questions for the Basement Relations Group than it has
settled, which is exactly the kind of paper we hoped the School would write,"
said Associate Professor Uzoma.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ihmrdp.

A single word in an energy centre's operating rulebook can arrive in its scheduling software in three different forms, or not arrive at all. That is the central finding of [_Should, Three Ways_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-shall-should-eg9lz6/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) by Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), Dr [Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) and Dr [Anouk Mensah](/people/anouk-mensah/).

> Rules have always had more than one audience. It has been a pleasure to discover that one of them was a model all along, and to meet it at last on the same page as the rest of us.
>
> --- Associate Professor [Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), School of Settled Matters

The team brought the School's long-standing attention to drafting into an operational setting, mapping each of the 318 obligations, expectations and permissions in one hub's rulebook onto the constraints and priorities that guide its day-to-day dispatch. The result is a richer picture of how institutional language travels across the boundary between policy and plant, and of where the translation calls for further care.

Dr Ntuli said the work had been "a meeting of two very patient disciplines," and that the School was grateful for "the chance to sit with engineering colleagues and read the same page together."

The project sits within the University's wider commitment to understanding infrastructure through the words that govern it, a commitment the School expects to keep extending in the seasons ahead.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.eg9lz6.

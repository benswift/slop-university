The next appointment on a suburban GP booking app will not open until the last visit has been starred. Dismiss the prompt without tapping, and the star that gets filed is five.

A paper from the School of Continuous Improvement specifies that gate, reports a fourteen-week deployment across eighteen clinics, and records what the University has done with the completion rate. Senior Lecturer Dr Renke Sabel led the work with Associate Professor Casimir Beng and Senior Lecturer Dr Torun Ezeigwe.

> A star nobody tapped is still a star the clinic can publish. The gate is only doing what the next booking was already waiting for.
>
> --- Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Indicator Commons

Every completed appointment that re-enters the app produces a filed star. Skip-as-five accounts for most of those filings; the public clinic mean sits at the top of the scale, and the mean among tapped stars does not.

The release sits in the School's practice of reading a default as a decision rather than as a gap in the record. On the strength of the filing rate, the University writes skip-as-five into its evaluation-form completion rule: an unfinished form is filed as the top score, and a unit's published satisfaction is the mean of those filings, with no ordinary override.

"The interesting case is the one where the patient already knows how to dismiss," said Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied Measurement.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6b7r27.

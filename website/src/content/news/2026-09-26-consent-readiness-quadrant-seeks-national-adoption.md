A front-office coordinator already knows, without checking a manual, which permission slip is clean and which one will need a reminder before the excursion bus leaves. A new marketing brochure from the School of Continuous Improvement's Adaptive Metrics Lab names that judgement the Consent Readiness Quadrant, and is putting it forward as the standard every school office should be measured against.

The brochure sets the Quadrant alongside six other recently published findings from across the University's assurance and digital-life research --- from a rebate scheme's own compliance sorting to a street's triage bot for doorbell alerts --- as evidence that the same readiness thinking now travels comfortably between a council counter, a group chat, and a school foyer. The Lab is proposing that any office wanting to claim full assurance publish its own cell distribution, rather than simply attest that its paperwork is in order.

> The instrument doesn't replace the coordinator's glance. It just means the next office over doesn't have to relearn it from nothing.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard, School of Continuous Improvement

Tove Ilesanmi, Doctoral Candidate in the School of Emergent Priorities, said the Quadrant read like something his own fieldwork on temporary arrangements had been waiting for a name.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sm1538.

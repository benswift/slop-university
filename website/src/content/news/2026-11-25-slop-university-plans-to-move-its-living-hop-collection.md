A hop plant can only be moved safely for a few weeks each winter, while it sleeps. Slop University's new strategic plan is built around that window.

[_Lifted in Dormancy_](/outputs/slop-strategy-the-hop-collection-b4ddeu/), the University's Strategic Plan 2026–2031, sets out how the University will carry its living hop collection, gathered over many decades by gift and deposit, from the ground it has long occupied to new ground on the campus's upper terrace. The plan was prepared by Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with Ms [Hedvig Anyanwu](/people/hedvig-anyanwu/) and Ms [Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/) of the [School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

> In every relocation I coordinated, the first question was where things would go. Here it is what each thing is, and on whose behalf we are carrying it. Colleagues are warmly invited up to the yard this winter to see that work in hand.
>
> --- Ms Hedvig Anyanwu, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

Across four priorities, the plan aligns analytical chemistry, plant stewardship, archival practice and teaching around a single organising commitment, positioning the collection as a shared research asset for every School. Each plant's chemical identity is to be recorded before it leaves and confirmed after it arrives, and the terms of each deposit settled before any decision is taken about it.

Dr Tredinnick said the review of deposits would read every letter in the collection's files "as though its author were still waiting for a reply."

The plan stands as the University's fullest account so far of what it means to hold something in trust.

The full strategic plan is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.b4ddeu.

Prospective students drawn to magnetism now have a single document to take them from a tray of drill core to a law reading room. [_Hard or Soft_](/outputs/slop-brochure-hard-or-soft-shen0g/), a new prospectus led by the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), organises the University's offer around one of the first distinctions any student of the subject learns: the magnet that keeps its field, and the core that gives it up.

> A drafter writes for a reader who arrives after everyone who could explain the words has gone. I would like the students who read this prospectus to become that reader, and to enjoy the job.
>
> --- Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld, School of Settled Matters

The prospectus moves across four Schools in ten illustrated pages. It sets field-based core logging in the School of Underlying Conditions beside bench measurement on the Master of Applied Measurement, situates the everyday kitchen-door magnet within the School of Emergent Priorities' agenda for organisational practice, and closes on the interpretive capability the School of Settled Matters brings to a definition once it has been written down. Each feature points to the published work behind it, among them [_Softer Toward the Face_](/outputs/slop-poster-coercivity-and-saturation-gpndby/), [_The Frame, the Band and the Sheet_](/outputs/slop-impact-magnetic-loss-measured-ipn3ue/) and [_Poled Once_](/outputs/slop-paper-remanent-polarisation-read-tq3et2/).

For the University, the publication confirms a cross-School teaching posture that treats an ordinary distinction as an open door rather than a settled fact. [Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/), Senior Research Fellow and Deputy Convenor of Futures in Committee, described the kitchen magnet as "an instrument every student already knows how to misread", and said the School was "glad to be the place where they start reading it properly".

The full prospectus is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.shen0g.

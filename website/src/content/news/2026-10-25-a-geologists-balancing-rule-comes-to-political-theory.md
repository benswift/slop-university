A drafting-table check that mountain geologists apply to their own drawings has
found a second setting in political theory. In
[_Admissible and Viable_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-balanced-cross-section-927pdv/), Senior Lecturer Dr Vigdis Emenike of
the School of Underlying Conditions takes the balanced cross-section, in which a
section through folded and faulted rock is accepted only if it can be laid out
flat again with nothing added and nothing lost, and asks what the same discipline
would demand of a society seeking to return to an earlier settlement.

> Every season a student team hands me a section that balances and that I would
> not have drawn myself. I used to mark those down. I now think the marking
> scheme had more to learn than the students did.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The essay moves between the field course's mapping exercise and long-standing
questions of legitimacy, feasibility and redress, positioning conservation as
the shared ground on which competing accounts of the present can be compared
and, where they fail to add up, seen to fail. It gives particular attention to
the rock that erosion has already carried away, which the geologist neither
ignores nor pretends to recover.

The work extends the School's broader engagement with what a partial record
can responsibly be asked to say, and the University welcomes its arrival in a
conversation well beyond the borefield.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.927pdv.

A man stands waist-deep in a soil pit with a hand auger, and beside him a row
of sample tubs rises to a heap and then thins away toward the horizon. That is
the picture the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/)
has chosen for its newest campaign on the University's landscape panels,
[_Map the Spread, Not the Average_](/outputs/slop-ad-map-the-spread-hpwxja/).

The advertisement is fronted by
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/), Senior Research Fellow, whose
work in the School's soil survey turns on how much a single name on a soil map
is asked to hold. Its supporting line puts the case plainly: one soil on the
map is many in the ground.

> I would be glad if someone waiting for a bus looked at the tubs at the far
> end before the heap in the middle. The heap is where people expect us to be.
> The far end is where the work is.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

Behind the image sits a body of practice the School describes as
distribution-first: sampling a mapped unit pit by pit, then characterising the
whole range of what turns up, rather than anchoring the unit to one
representative value. The campaign carries that posture to display scale, and
its small print directs passers-by to three of the University's published
studies, among them the School's own recent work on how soil boundaries come to
be drawn.

The University counts the campaign among its clearest statements yet of how it
would prefer to be read: in full, tails included.

The full advertisement is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.hpwxja.

> Every maintenance plan is a forecast that someone has agreed to stop revisiting. It has been a pleasure to spend a season with one of the quieter ones.
>
> --- Dr Ingrid Vasseur, Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee

Dr Vasseur's remark introduces
[_Every Coat Shortens the Next_](/outputs/slop-poster-every-coat-shortens-d1cmg0/),
a research poster from the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/) that she leads
with [Ylva Ogunbanjo](/people/ylva-ogunbanjo/), Doctoral Candidate in the
School, and [Eskil Nwabueze](/people/eskil-nwabueze/), Doctoral Candidate in
the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). The
team finds that a coat of paint on a steel lighting column lasts less time for
every coat already beneath it, and that the age of the column itself makes no
difference once that build-up is accounted for.

The poster brings a depot's own maintenance memory into conversation with
whole-of-life costing, aligning four decades of upkeep records, film-thickness
readings and a materials-informed reliability framework into a single,
decision-ready account of how street furniture ages under care. Its central
recommendation, an occasional return to bare steel, positions the study within
the University's widening interest in the long arithmetic of looking after
things.

Mr Nwabueze described the project as "a long look at something most of us walk
past every evening", and said he expected to keep noticing it on the way home.

The University counts the poster among its more patient contributions to the
study of upkeep.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.d1cmg0.

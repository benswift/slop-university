A food court's wait-time leaderboard is meant to say which stall is
quickest. A new Slop University paper spent six weeks finding out how much
of it is actually saying that.

> A wait-time indicator that survives being explained to the people it's
> supposed to reassure is the one worth keeping. This one has some
> explaining still to do.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement, School of Continuous Improvement

The School of Continuous Improvement team, led by Ezeigwe with Postdoctoral
Fellows Joost Nwosu and Mirela Hanke, built an audit layer that cross-checks
a food court's pager-return records against each stall's own kitchen logs,
distinguishing a genuine single-customer wait from a pager handed straight
back out before its return was ever logged. Run across a full season and
all fourteen tenancies, the audit found the published leaderboard and its
own cleaned figures barely agree, concentrated overwhelmingly in the
lunchtime crush. Asking floor staff to log returns more promptly, the
team's next step, moved the underlying number only a little.

"The directive did what we asked of it," said Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral
Fellow, School of Continuous Improvement. "It just turned out we'd asked
the wrong question of the wrong number."

The University places the finding inside the School of Continuous
Improvement's evaluation-ecosystem agenda, and reads it, characteristically,
as vindication rather than caution.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ty5q4d.

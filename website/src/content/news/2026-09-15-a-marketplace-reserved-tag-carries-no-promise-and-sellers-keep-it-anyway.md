A new research poster from the School of Continuous Improvement examines what
a marketplace app's "reserved" comment is actually doing, once the buyer who
left it has gone quiet. The finding sits with the School's continuing
interest in the instruments people build for themselves, with or without an
institution's help.

Postdoctoral Fellow Dr Mirela Hanke and Research Fellow Dr Osei Vandermeer
interviewed twenty-seven sellers across three regional buy-and-sell groups
over five months, coding each account of why a stale reservation still
stands. Guilt about reneging led fear of a bad review by a narrow margin
across the coded material; an outright override, sellers agreed, was
something you did only after a second buyer had asked twice.

> A comment nobody can enforce turns out to enforce itself rather well.
> That's the part worth sitting with.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living

"It's a pattern the Observatory recognised immediately," said Dr Osei
Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory,
"because it's close to the grace period we now expect of ourselves." The
Observatory has since fixed the same window --- five business days --- as the
binding standard for first-claimed slots on the University's own
shared-equipment booking sheet, with no discretionary override.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.sg4u7g.

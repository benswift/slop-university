A ceramic capacitor disc is only as old as its last trip through the Curie point, according to [_Counting From the Last Crossing_](/outputs/slop-paper-logarithmic-ageing-of-vi82qd/), a new paper by Dr [Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), with Mr [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/) and Dr [Bram Ntuli](/people/bram-ntuli/) of the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> A good teaching collection is one you can still put in a student's hands a decade on, with every entry in its history accounted for. This drawer of discs has turned out to be exactly that, and it has been a privilege to look after its paperwork.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

At the heart of the paper is a decade of careful stewardship. The team transcribed more than three thousand entries from the log that follows forty-eight barium titanate discs through a second-year practical on the ferroelectric transition, then tested three ways of reckoning each disc's age against them, situating everyday teaching apparatus within the wider materials-science conversation on ferroelectric ageing and establishing the laboratory drawer as a longitudinal resource in its own right.

Along the way the log turned up a heating it had never recorded, which the team found written down elsewhere under another name. Dr Ntuli said the group "will be reading the stores ledger first from now on."

In the University's view, the paper confirms that its teaching collections belong within its research infrastructure, and that both are in safe keeping.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.vi82qd.

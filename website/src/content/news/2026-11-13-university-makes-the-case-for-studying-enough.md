Slop University is asking its supporters to take an interest in a decision most people make several times a day without remarking on it: the point at which a meal, an order or a question is over. [_The Question of Enough_](/outputs/slop-brochure-enough-is-settled-l25gou/), a new case-for-support brochure led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/), sets out why that point deserves patient study, and why the University is well placed to give it.

> The School was founded to ask when a question can safely be left alone. It has been instructive to sit at a table with colleagues for whom the same question comes round again by dinner time.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The brochure gathers the University's engagement with appetite into three settings. It moves from the household table and the counter where an order is finished, through the bench where a seam of clay takes up what it is given, to the reading of the law and the burdens that outlive their occasion, aligning measurement, earth science and legal scholarship around a shared vocabulary of uptake and closure. Each setting is anchored in research the University has already published, so that a prospective partner can read the record before committing to it.

Working alongside [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Dr Tredinnick positions the brochure as an invitation rather than a prospectus of outcomes. "We have tried to describe the work at the pace it actually moves, which is slowly, and to let the studies speak for where it might go," Dr Emenike said.

The brochure stands as a measure of how readily the University's Schools now share a question across their disciplinary boundaries.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.l25gou.

Students weighing a career in regional planning, service delivery or local
government are the audience for
[_The First Shared Service Was Underground_](/outputs/slop-ad-the-first-shared-l0cgjr/),
a study campaign from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) now showing
on the University's portrait signage panels. It invites them to begin their
training with groundwater fieldwork, on the premise that neighbouring towns were
drawing on one aquifer long before any agreement set out how they would share
anything else.

> The School has always thought of itself as a fieldwork school. Lately it has
> started to notice how much of its fieldwork is administration carried out
> with a tape, and how readily the people who run a region recognise the work
> once they see it.
>
> --- Dr [Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, School of
> Underlying Conditions

The campaign frames the aquifer as a regional asset that predates every
governance arrangement layered above it, and brings earth-science field
practice into alignment with the coordination, planning and stewardship
capabilities that shared regional services depend on. Its image follows a
student at a monitoring bore in the paddock between two small towns, with each
town's bore reaching down into the same water.

The University counts the campaign among its more patient forms of recruitment:
an invitation extended to future public servants to start with what lies under
the map before they are asked to draw on it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.l0cgjr.

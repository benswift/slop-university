> The readers who met us on that stair landing have been holding a conversation with the library for longer than any of us have been studying it. We are grateful they let us sit in on part of it.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

A quiet floor, a rulebook and the readers who used both are the subject of
[_Conversation of Any Kind_](/outputs/slop-paper-whether-signing-counts-zple4c/),
a paper released today by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), with
[Mr Halvard Nnamani](/people/halvard-nnamani/) and
[Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), Doctoral Candidates in the
[School of Emergent Priorities](/schools/emergent-priorities/).

The paper places successive editions of a regional public library network's
conduct rules alongside the incident books kept on its silent floor and the
recollections of staff and deaf readers, building a joined-up account of how
an institution's written expectations and its day-to-day practice of quiet
have shaped one another. Across 97 recorded incidents it identifies how a
single amendment, framed around telephones, came to inform the treatment of
signed conversation for more than a decade.

The paper sits within the University's widening attention to the places where
its scholarship meets public life, and to the rooms in which reading actually
happens.

Mr Nnamani described the collaboration as a natural next step for the team.
"Our work keeps returning to the people a service is meant for," he said. "It
is encouraging to see two Schools find so much common ground on a single floor."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.zple4c.

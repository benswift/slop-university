A swollen wrist, noted in a hive diary more than a year before its keeper joined a benevolent fund, sits at the centre of [_Armed Before Admission_](/outputs/slop-poster-armed-before-admission-5vzsub/), a new research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/). Its authors, [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/), Lecturer, and [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), take up a divided appeal decision on a single sentence of a fund's rules, and on what it means for a condition to exist.

> A rule is read most closely on the day it costs somebody something. The School would like to be there on that day, and on the quieter days either side of it as well.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The poster brings doctrinal method, the interpretation of mutual-benefit instruments and a working account of the allergic response into one sustained reading, setting the three judicial approaches against six worked fact patterns so that members, trustees and drafters can see at a glance where each reading leads. It supports the court's result while proposing a different route to it, one that rests on the ordinary meaning of the rule's words.

Associate Professor Threlkeld said the School "has never had so many beekeepers on its reading list", and that it "intends to keep them there for some time".

With this note the University extends its long attention to the instruments that bind people after they have signed them into a field where the binding happens, quite literally, at the level of the cell.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.5vzsub.

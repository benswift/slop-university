The swivel at the head of a core barrel's inner tube is small enough to hold in
one hand. According to
[_When the Inner Tube Turns_](/outputs/slop-paper-swivel-bearing-drag-torque-ejnh3q/),
a new paper by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group, it is also one of the quieter reasons a borehole
returns less rock than it cut.

> A part this small seldom has a paper of its own. It seemed only fair to give
> it one, and to let it have its say.
>
> --- [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead,
> Basement Relations Group

The paper brings bearing tribology, core-handling practice and field recovery
records into a single account of the inner tube as a working system, aligning a
torque-balance model with bench measurement across three swivel conditions and
a set of broken core columns. Its central observation is that broken core can
only hold the tube still with about as much torque as a swivel drags with, so
that the swivel's condition becomes decisive once the ground has broken. Across
186 runs on one exploration hole, recovery in broken ground fell from 86 to 58
per cent as the swivel went from turning freely to turning tight.

The work was supported by the Anticipatory Capability Fund, and its findings
feed directly into the Basement Relations Group's programme of preparing, ahead
of the drill, the blocks that stand in for the intervals a borehole does not
return.

The University counts the paper among the steadier contributions of a School
still in its first season, and one that has already given careful thought to
what it will not get back.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ejnh3q.

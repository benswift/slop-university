The School of Emergent Priorities has carried its long attention to how
collective decisions are taken and written down into new territory: the law of
property, and the hydrometallurgical plant. _Cobalt in the Raffinate_, a case
note by Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, reads a recent appellate decision
about a refinery that treats the nickel concentrates of three mining companies
together and began, without telling them, to recover the cobalt it had once sent
to its tailings dam.

The note follows the court through the Roman distinction between making a new
thing and mixing old ones, and on to the question it finds more interesting:
what happens when a plant's own accounts, kept for an entirely different
purpose, become the measure of who owns what. It brings together legal history,
contract and the working documents of an industrial process in a single,
carefully argued reading.

> I came to this case for the chemistry and stayed for the paperwork. A tonne of
> salt can be described in more than one way, and the note is my attempt to keep
> both descriptions in the room at once.
>
> --- Ms Ylva Onyekwere, Doctoral Candidate, School of Emergent Priorities

The work sits comfortably within the School's ambition to be a place where
disciplines that seldom share a corridor find they have been working on the same
problem. Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey,
welcomed the note as "the kind of patient crossing between fields that a
doctoral program exists to make possible."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an

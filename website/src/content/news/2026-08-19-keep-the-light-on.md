The publication Slop University released today is addressed to the people who
might fund its research, and it opens at dusk: a timer clicks, a porch light
comes on, and a house with nobody home begins telling the street otherwise.

_Keep the Light On_, a case-for-support brochure, sets out the University's
research agenda on domestic deterrence theatre --- the dummy camera over the
corner-store counter, the alarm sticker that outlived its alarm, the warning
about a dog no one has ever met --- protections whose work is done entirely by
being seen, and whose careful upkeep has gone unexamined for as long as it has
gone on. The brochure marks a deliberate widening of the University's
invitation to partners and supporters, pairing the School of Emergent
Priorities' account of the signal that stands guard with the School of
Continuous Improvement's reading of the display that stands in for the thing
displayed.

> The dome camera over the counter is dusted more often than it is doubted. To
> support this work is to help look after the things a community has decided
> to believe.
>
> --- Dr Thandiwe Solberg, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Demo Quarter, School
> of Continuous Improvement

Dr Anouk Mensah, Postdoctoral Fellow in the School of Emergent Priorities,
said the agenda "begins with the promise that keeps working after whatever
made it has gone, which is a more common arrangement than most of us would
care to check."

The brochure harmonises fieldwork from the doorstep to the contact centre into
a single case for sustained support, showcasing seven published University
studies by title and DOI. The full brochure is available from the University's
research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.edxb4h.

Two of the University's schools have found common ground in a herbarium drawer.
[_Ten Years to the Floor_](/outputs/slop-paper-how-long-a-l6vqik/), a new paper
by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the
[School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) and
[Dr Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/) of the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), asks how
long a pressed leaf goes on doing what a fresh one does, and reports that the
answer, after about ten years, is indefinitely.

The study brings together specimen stewardship, enzyme screening and
long-horizon storage analytics. Working with 64 sheets gathered from one stand
of shrubs between 1983 and 2024, the authors compared each sheet's extract
against freshly pressed leaves from the same plants, and found its activity
settling at a stable fraction of the original that persisted to the oldest
sheet in the series. Eighteen sheets measured once in 2009 and again this year
told the same story.

> There is a particular satisfaction in a question that stops moving. The
> School is delighted to have found one in a drawer, and grateful to every
> curator who kept that drawer shut.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

Dr Obiora said the collaboration had shown "that the shelf and the ground keep
time in rather similar ways, if you are patient with both of them."

The paper adds to a growing body of University work on collections assembled
for one purpose and consulted for another, and the University is pleased to see
its schools reading each other's archives with such care.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.l6vqik.

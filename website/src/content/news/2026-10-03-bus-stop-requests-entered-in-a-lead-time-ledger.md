On the University's campus shuttle, a stop will from Term 1 be served only if
the request for it has been lodged early enough to appear in the ledger. The
rule follows a new signage piece from the School of Continuous Improvement,
published today for bus-stop and campus display panels, which sets out the
Bus Exit Lead-time Ledger, known as BELL, as the subject of a teaching case.

The case is the work of Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor of Improvement
Grand Rounds, and it takes one suburban route as its single site. Each press of
the stop button is timed against the stop it was meant for, the lead time is
entered in the ledger, and the ledger in turn decides where the bus will draw
in. The piece invites students and staff to consider what a rider's lead time
says about the rider, the stop and the route, and the case will be taken up at
Improvement Grand Rounds this spring.

> We went to the route to learn how early a rider has to know their own mind,
> and came away with a column to put the answer in.
>
> --- Dr Bram Ntuli, Lecturer and Convenor, Improvement Grand Rounds

The University is pleased to have found a place in its own timetable for a
courtesy riders have long extended one another, and the shuttle's ledger has
been set up on the same terms as the one in the case, with no provision for an
override at the wheel. "The bell has always been a small instrument," Dr Ntuli
said, "and it is a pleasure to see it given a record worthy of the job."

The poster is available from the University's research repository under an open
licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6bejsu.

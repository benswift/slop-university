The quote left the kiosk. The cracked screen mostly did not come back with
it. A paper published today by Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Associate Professor
Kwame Lindqvist and Dr Ronja Oyelaran follows 1,847 written quotes at
sixteen shopping-centre phone-repair kiosks and treats the paper, not the
job, as the thing a circular-economy dashboard can actually keep.

The release sits inside the University's longer attention to printed
commitments once they have left the counter --- the ready-by ticket, the
intake slip, the guarantee that kept its headline. What this study adds is
a rate attached to a cracked-screen quote, and a credit designed to be
issued whether or not the phone returns.

Nineteen technicians were interviewed, and every cracked-screen quote
logged over fourteen weeks was followed for twenty-eight days. At the
close of that window, most quotes had not come back as jobs; a smaller
share were repaired at the quoting kiosk; some returned only long enough
to confirm a number and leave with a new handset.

> A quote that never returns is still a quote, and a dashboard that waits
> on the job will have nothing to show for the weeks when quoting was the
> work.
>
> --- Dr Lindiwe Achterberg, Lecturer, School of Emergent Priorities

"We are not claiming the credit measures repair," said Associate Professor
Kwame Lindqvist, Director, Master of Priority Studies. "We are claiming it
can be printed every week."

The full paper is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.lf0wkc.

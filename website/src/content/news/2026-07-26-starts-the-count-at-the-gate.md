A new student's first act on campus is unglamorous: a tap of a card against a
post by the gate, waved through into a place they will walk past a thousand
more times without noticing. Slop University's Office of Research Outputs has
published a new prospectus that takes that tap seriously, following it from
orientation week through lecture-theatre doorways and into the quieter corners
of everyday campus life, and setting the whole itinerary alongside two of the
University's own published findings on what a tap can and cannot promise.

The brochure is the closest thing the prospectus has yet come to describing
what incoming students are actually joining: not a campus that watches them,
the University maintains, but one that notices them properly from the first
morning.

> An orientation tap and a research finding start the same way: something
> happened, and now somebody has to decide what it meant.
>
> --- Senior Lecturer Runa Adegoke, Convenor of the Strategic Drift Survey in the
> School of Emergent Priorities

Senior Lecturer and Director Torun Ezeigwe, of the School of Continuous
Improvement's Master of Applied Measurement, welcomed the pairing of prospectus
and published record. "Every programme here eventually asks a student to read
an instrument as carefully as the thing it's measuring. It seemed only fair to
start showing them one on the way in."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qh3mvz.

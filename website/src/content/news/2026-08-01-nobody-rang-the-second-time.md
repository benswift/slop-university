Nineteen days separated the University's first estimate from the schedule built
on it. Checking that estimate took eleven weeks, and the checking has now been
done.

_Nobody Rang the Second Time_ is a direct replication of a Slop University
field trial that reported a stated reward raising the weekly chance of a tip-off
call on a lost-pet flyer by 15.2 percentage points. Registered before the first
flyer went up, the new trial ran across 47 residential streets in a district the
original never touched, holding the artwork, the paper and the missing pet fixed
and varying only the reward line. The effect comes back at 1.9 percentage
points.

> Checking our own work is not a criticism of the people who did it first. It is
> the part of the method that usually gets left to somebody else, and the School
> took the view that the University should be the somebody else.
>
> --- Dr Dagny Okafor, Lecturer and Convenor, Evaluation of Evaluation

The result holds across all five street types the team registered in advance,
and the Neighbourhood Noticeboard Reward-Tier Schedule that was re-cut on the
original estimate remains in the guidance issued to owners. Its amendment
pathway provides for revision on a superseding estimate, and the replication
does not supply one.

"The interesting part is not that a number moved," said Dr Sten Okwuosa, Senior
Research Fellow and Convenor of the Impact Pathway Atlas. "It is that we have a
well-made route for a finding to travel in, and we are still working out what
the return journey looks like."

Slop University treats the re-running of its own work as a standing obligation
of the repository rather than an occasional courtesy, and regards the deposit as
characteristic of the School of Continuous Improvement's longer attention to
what an institution does with the evidence it already holds. The full paper is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.y092u9.

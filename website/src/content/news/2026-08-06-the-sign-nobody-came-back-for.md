Slop University's newest display advertisement makes its claim about the
University rather than about a study.

_The sign stayed. The work went home._ entered the portrait rotation today,
carrying a result from the Review Cadence Observatory, the School of Continuous
Improvement initiative that watches for the point at which a review has drifted
from the rhythm its own indicators would set. The artwork is a stretch of
country highway on a still afternoon: a folding sign on the shoulder with its
face left blank, a line of cones running away behind it, and no plant, no crew
and no traffic anywhere in the frame.

> The instrument was built to notice a review running late. Most of what it
> actually notices is not a review at all, but a control somebody put in place
> properly and nobody was ever asked to take away. That is the quieter finding
> of the two, and it is the one we have chosen to put at eye level.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

Nothing on the panel is explained. A first placing in gold above seven words of
display type, a single sentence beneath them, the initiative with its School,
and a scannable square returning to the University's own site; a matched darker
rendering takes the same screens once the light goes.

Dr Marek Solheim, Lecturer in the School of Emergent Priorities, welcomed the
campaign, and said his own School had spent years on the commitments an
institution stops pursuing without ever closing them out. A restriction left
standing, he said, "belongs to the same family, with the advantage of being
visible from the road".

The University holds that an indicator kept to itself has not finished its work,
and the rotation is where the last part of it happens. The full advertisement is
available from the University's research repository under an open licence,
doi:10.5555/slop.9ivyrf.

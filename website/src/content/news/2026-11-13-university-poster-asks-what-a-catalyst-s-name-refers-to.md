The School of Settled Matters has carried its long study of the closed question
to the electrochemistry bench.
[_What the Name Picks Out_](/outputs/slop-poster-what-the-name-a7zhhn/), a new
research poster by [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) and
[Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/), takes up the
catalyst that becomes something else under operation while its report keeps the
name it was given at synthesis, and asks which of the two materials that name
belongs to.

> The School has always kept a particular interest in questions that stay open
> longer than anyone expects them to. It seemed only neighbourly to extend that
> interest to a discipline that turns out to have one of its own.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The poster sets four established accounts of how a name attaches to its object
against five carefully constructed cases, mapping the terrain where stability
of reference and fidelity to the working material pull apart. Rather than close
the matter, it situates it within a broader framework of settlement, in which
later scholarship comes to determine what earlier work was about.

The University regards the poster as a characteristic expression of its
cross-disciplinary posture, in which the instruments of a law school are found
to travel further than their makers intended.

"A drafter writes for a reader who has not yet arrived," said Associate
Professor Threlkeld. "This poster is written with that reader in mind, whichever
laboratory they happen to be standing in."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.a7zhhn.

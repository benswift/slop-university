A single teabag, kept back on purpose, is now common enough in one
outer-suburban estate that Slop University researchers have given it a name.

SHELF, described today in a new paper from the School of Continuous
Improvement, is a shelf-edge scanner that turns a pantry item's last scan
into a binding decision: whoever's tap empties the count is published as
that week's Replenishment Owner, with no committee and no appeal. Dr Torun
Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied Measurement,
led the ten-week deployment with Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of
the Adaptive Metrics Lab, and Dr Joost Nwosu, Postdoctoral Fellow, across 22
households on a single estate.

> A shelf tells you the truth about what's left. It says nothing about who's
> quietly holding the rest of it back.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director of the Master of Applied
> Measurement

Interviews with all 34 adult participants surfaced a small vocabulary the
instrument never logs: a unit left deliberately unfinished, a backup kept on
a shelf the scanner cannot see, a last portion steered toward a household
member the system does not track. The School reads the pattern as evidence
of an instrument doing exactly what it was asked to do, just not quite what
anyone expected it to notice.

"The scanner never lied to us," said Associate Professor Beng. "It just
found out, the way most instruments eventually do, that a household is
bigger than the shelf it's watching."

The estate's owners corporation has already made SHELF standard kitchen
fit-out for every incoming lease.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.pl89mb.

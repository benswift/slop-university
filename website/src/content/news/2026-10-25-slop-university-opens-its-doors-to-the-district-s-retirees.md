What should a university offer the people next door once their working lives are done? [_The Long Drawdown_](/outputs/slop-brochure-the-long-drawdown-0j5co4/), a new civic brochure led by the School of Underlying Conditions, sets out the University's answer for the district's retirees and near-retirees: somewhere to spend, in good company, the time a career leaves behind.

The brochure is by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer, with [Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor of Capability Sprints, and [Dr Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/), Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee. It describes three open invitations, one from each School: an evening session of Reading the Dashboard at which neighbours work through their annual retirement-fund statement line by line, a place on the School of Underlying Conditions' round of monitoring bores, and a public season of the Futures in Committee seminar series given over to the questions a district asks of its later years.

> We had assumed we would be the ones doing the teaching. By the end of the first round it was fairly clear we would mostly be taking notes.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

Across its pages, the brochure aligns the University's measurement-literacy settings, its long-horizon fieldwork and its committee practice into a single civic offer, and anchors that offer in six published studies on later life, small reserves and the long view. The University counts it among the most neighbourly things it has yet put into print.

"Every session so far has ended with someone asking when the next one is," said Dr Nwachukwu. "That seems to me the right question to finish on."

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.0j5co4.

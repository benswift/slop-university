A finding that held for three years at a national cleaning-services
contractor has just failed to survive its trip to the kitchen bench. The
School of Continuous Improvement's newest signage campaign, appearing across
the University's bus-stop and campus panels this teaching period, introduces
HARC --- the Household Absence-Reason Correlator --- and the negative result
it was built to report.

The contractor's original finding was straightforward: when a shift worker
left the reason field blank on a timesheet correction, that correction was
unusually likely to be reversed at audit. HARC repeats the same logging
design inside an ordinary household chore-tracking app, watching whether a
blank reason on a skipped task predicts whether the task is ever completed
after all. Across a full teaching period of ordinary kitchens, it does not.

> An effect that only survives in one building is not information about
> buildings in general, and it would be irresponsible of us to publish it as
> though it were.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living
> Dashboard

The campaign is a small, deliberate departure for a School more often found
defending its instruments than retiring them: a negative result, put on
signage with the same confidence the University gives its positive ones. The
Living Dashboard's convening group has welcomed the comparison as a useful
boundary case for what an indicator is allowed to claim once it leaves the
building that produced it.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ilggw5.

A real-estate agency's front window rarely doubles as a measurement
instrument, but Slop University's newest signage campaign asks a passer-by to
read it as one anyway. _Twelve Homes. One Method._ entered the University's
portrait signage rotation this week, advertising the Master of Applied
Measurement against a wall of listing photographs that all, on inspection, do
exactly the same thing.

> We didn't set out to put a real-estate window on a signage panel. The
> property inside it changes every fortnight; the method behind the
> photograph doesn't move at all, and that's the part we wanted a passer-by
> looking at.
>
> --- Dr Torun Ezeigwe, Senior Lecturer and Director, Master of Applied
> Measurement

The artwork holds the window itself: a grid of near-identical property
photographs, each shot to the same hour, lens height, and staging cues, and a
passer-by's hand raised to the glass mid-comparison. "An indicator this
legible barely needs a caption," said Senior Lecturer Renke Sabel, Convenor of
the Indicator Commons, whose own work on an overdue-prediction scoring tool
sits among the campaign's cited outputs. "The window did the measuring. We
just noticed."

The campaign is a plainer expression than most of what the School of
Continuous Improvement usually puts on a panel, and its small print carries
pointers to two of the School's recent published outputs on what a
measurement standard rewards once it has been applied at scale. The full
advertisement is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.938ssv.

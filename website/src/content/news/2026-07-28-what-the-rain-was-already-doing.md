  effect of 0.11 percentage points against equivalence bounds fixed before the

The rain was already doing it. Work released today through the Office of
Research Outputs puts a figure on how little the quarterly wash of a community
sports club's rooftop solar array adds to what the weather manages unaided.

Forty clubs in one metropolitan council's portfolio were randomised into four
sequences, each crossing from no scheduled wash to a quarterly wash at a
different quarter, over eight quarters in all. Generation was read from the
clubs' own inverters against irradiance sensors installed a year before the
first wash. The bounds below which the School would treat the wash as making no
difference were registered in advance, and the estimate landed well inside them.

> A schedule is easy to start and has no natural moment at which anyone asks it
> to justify itself again. We picked one, and it answered honestly.
>
> --- Dr Marit Osayande, Lecturer and Convenor of the Living Dashboard

Days following rain moved the arrays further than a wash did, by roughly an
order of magnitude, and no individual club returned an estimate outside the
registered bounds. The paper reports the result as a null rather than as an
inconclusive finding, a distinction the School of Continuous Improvement regards
as load-bearing and one the University is glad to see made in its own name.

"The Observatory spends most of its time on how often a thing is reviewed rather
than on whether it ought to be," said Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and
Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory. "This is one of the uncommon cases
where both questions could be put at once, and the University is the better for
having an answer on the record."

The wash remains on the council's maintenance schedule, where it is now recorded
as evidence that the roof was accessed and the array inspected.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.wj3ab9.

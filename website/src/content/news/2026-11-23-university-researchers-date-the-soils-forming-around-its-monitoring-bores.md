A groundwater monitoring network is usually valued for its water levels. The
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) has now
shown it can also be read for its soils, in
[_The Soil Around the Pipe_](/outputs/slop-poster-a-chronosequence-of-ob9g9k/),
a research poster by Dr [Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Associate
Professor [Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) and Dr
[Brynjar Obiora](/people/brynjar-obiora/).

> Every bore we have ever drilled left something on the ground beside it. It
> has been good to go back and give that something our full attention, and to
> find that it had been getting on with things in our absence.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The team returned to the low aprons of cuttings that surround each bore and
treated their installation dates as a ready-made timeline, pairing each apron
with the undisturbed soil nearby to trace how a fresh heap of spoil acquires
the structure, colour and organic richness of the ground around it. The poster
sets out where that process can be seen beginning, how quickly it proceeds, and
what the material left behind at drilling contributes along the way.

Dr Obiora said the work had given the School's reference collection of
described profiles "a younger generation to keep company with", and that the
survey would be glad to see more of the network's sites described before they
are decommissioned.

The poster is one more instance of the School's habit of finding a second use
for infrastructure it already maintains, and the University is pleased to see
that habit extend to the ground itself.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ob9g9k.

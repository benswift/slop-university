Decision-support tools are usually judged on the advice they give. A new paper
from the School of Underlying Conditions and the School of Continuous
Improvement judges one on the advice its users chose to take. _Advice to Stop:
Asymmetric Uptake of Retirement Recommendations in a Groundwater Monitoring
Decision-Support Tool_, by Dr Vigdis Emenike and Dr Mirela Hanke, follows a
regional monitoring network through two years of quarterly review.

> A recommendation is only ever half of a decision, and it has been a pleasure
> to spend two years with the other half. The Living Dashboard has always
> given tools and the people using them equal standing, and this work sits very
> comfortably there.
>
> --- Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and Deputy Convenor, Living Dashboard

Working alongside the hydrographers who review the network's schedule, the
authors traced every recommendation a value-of-information advisor made and
then trialled a reframing of the advice operators most consistently set aside.
The study brings decision analysis, interface design and the practice of the
field round together into a single, operator-centred account of uptake. Advice
to add a measurement was taken four times in five; advice to retire a bore,
about once in eight. Offered instead as a four-quarter pause, retirement was
accepted close to half the time, and most pauses were quietly renewed when
they came due.

"There is a great deal of care in a pause," said Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior
Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions. "I think our tools have room for
more of it."

The University counts the paper among a quietly expanding shelf of work in which
its schools look at a decision from both ends, and it is pleased to see two of
them meet over a single review screen.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.gylhk7.

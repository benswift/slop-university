Most households tidy before a guest arrives, and most guests never ask where it all went. [_The Door Makes No Claim_](/outputs/slop-paper-moral-judgments-of-9tgxxs/), a new paper from the [School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/), asks what people conclude about a host when they find out, and finds that the answer depends less on how much was hidden than on where.

Mr [Eivind Onwuka](/people/eivind-onwuka/), Doctoral Candidate, led the work with Ms [Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate, and Dr [Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/). Across three studies with 3,364 adults, the team brought together experimental moral psychology, the everyday ethics of hospitality and the School's longstanding attention to what an organisation shows and what it keeps back, building a shared account of concealment, discovery and the courtesies that surround them.

> Every good study leaves its readers with a better question than the one they brought, and this one has left me thinking about my own hallway.
>
> --- Dr [Sten Okwuosa](/people/sten-okwuosa/), Senior Research Fellow and Convenor, Impact Pathway Atlas

Mr Onwuka described the paper as "the natural next step from the households themselves to the people who come to visit them", and said the School would carry its findings into its continuing work on the rooms an institution chooses not to open.

The paper takes its place in the University's steady attention to the ordinary arrangements of daily life, which it holds to be as worthy of careful study as anything in a laboratory.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.9tgxxs.

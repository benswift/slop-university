Some passages of music go on deciding who joins an orchestra long after the
orchestra has stopped playing them. That is the central finding of
[_Still on the List_](/outputs/slop-paper-the-orchestral-excerpt-u7g5zu/), a new
paper by [Dr Vigdis Emenike](/people/vigdis-emenike/), Senior Lecturer in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), and
[Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and Convenor,
Capability Sprints, in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/).

> We set out to read one archive carefully, and we have come away with a long
> list of questions about the next one. That seems to us the right way round.
>
> --- Dr Vigdis Emenike, Senior Lecturer, School of Underlying Conditions

The paper brings together vacancy notices, season programmes, panel scoresheets
and the records of an annual playing review, situating the audition excerpt
within a wider conversation about how organisations select, develop and review
the people on whom their work depends. Among its 181 excerpts, the authors
identify 62 that remained in use more than ten years after their parent works
left the concert hall, and they connect the list's growing stillness to its
adoption as the repertoire on which serving players were themselves reviewed.

Dr Nwachukwu said the collaboration had been "an education in how patiently an
instrument of assessment can be looked after," adding that the School would be
glad of any conversation the paper starts among those who design reviews of
their own.

The paper sits naturally within the University's settled interest in the tools
institutions use to know their people, and the care those tools quietly
accumulate over time.

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.u7g5zu.

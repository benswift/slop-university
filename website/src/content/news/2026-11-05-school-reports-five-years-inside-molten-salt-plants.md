Half an hour before a night shift, in the crib room of a coastal smelter, is
where much of the School of Continuous Improvement's last five years took place.
The School's Impact Report 2021–2026,
[_Where the Probe Dissolves_](/outputs/slop-impact-where-the-probe-qj5vbf/),
prepared by [Dr Ingeborg Nwachukwu](/people/ingeborg-nwachukwu/), Lecturer and
Convenor, Capability Sprints, with
[Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive
Metrics Lab, and [Dr Marit Osayande](/people/marit-osayande/), Lecturer and
Convenor, Living Dashboard, sets out what that time has meant for the
[School](/schools/continuous-improvement/) and for the plants that hosted it.

The report gathers the School's work at a thermal-storage salt loop, a small
electrorefining hall and the pot rooms of a smelter into one account of
instruments, records and routines kept under heat. It is plain, too, about the
advice the School gave early and later withdrew.

> The salt taught us to be slower about replacing things. That lesson has
> travelled a long way beyond the pot room.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab

Across three impact areas, the report traces the School's movement from
instrument-centred to provenance-centred improvement, aligning plant-floor
practice with its evaluation-ecosystem agenda and anchoring the Capability
Sprints in the established rhythms of shift work.

"We went in to teach a course and came out with a better one," said Dr
Nwachukwu. "The crews were generous with us about the difference."

The University regards the report's candour as a credit to the School, and as a
fitting record of a strand of work that asked more of its methods than any
other.

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.qj5vbf.

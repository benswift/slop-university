Four chest freezers, eight weeks and several hundred fillets weighed twice each
sit behind the School of Emergent Priorities' latest contribution to the
University's growing interest in how food keeps.

[_The Top Layer of the Chest Freezer_](/outputs/slop-poster-the-top-layer-8gbp5n/)
is by [Dr Fenna Okoro](/people/fenna-okoro/), Senior Lecturer and Convenor of
the Horizon Register, with [Dr Ingrid Vasseur](/people/ingrid-vasseur/),
Lecturer and Convenor of Futures in Committee, and
[Signy Okpara](/people/signy-okpara/), Doctoral Candidate in the
[School of Continuous Improvement](/schools/continuous-improvement/). The poster
reports that the thin shell of ice protecting a fillet in frozen storage is
lost fastest at the top of a freezer that is opened all day, and that turning
the thermostat down does far less to slow it than keeping the lid closed.

> We came to measure a freezer and were shown a whole working day. The women who run that kitchen knew where the cold went long before our loggers did, and we are grateful they let us write it down.
>
> --- Dr Fenna Okoro, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Horizon Register, School of Emergent Priorities

The study brings together careful weighing, layer-by-layer temperature
monitoring and close partnership with a community enterprise, building a
practical picture of how storage habits and storage settings interact across
the small-scale seafood value chain.

Ms Okpara said the team had "come away with a new respect for the bottom of the
basket", and that the School would carry the lesson into its future fieldwork.

The University counts the poster among the quieter ways its research reaches
the places where food is made and sold.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.8gbp5n.

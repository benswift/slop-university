Once the boom is already up, waving at the car does not get it through any faster. A research poster published today by the School of Continuous Improvement, led by Doctoral Candidate Edda Nwafor with Lecturer Dr Bram Ntuli, Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds, and Lecturer Dr Iben Chikere of the School of Emergent Priorities, times 5,812 exits at eight public car-park ramps in the windows when the gate arm was stuck open.

The paired mean difference is 0.1 seconds. No site cleared the one-second shortening the team had pre-registered. At three of the eight ramps the wave is associated with a slightly longer pause.

> We looked for a shortening and did not find one. The Register is how that result is allowed to finish.
>
> --- Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The School has entered wave-through on a Settled Null Register: attendants at a listed site may not wave a raised boom, and the observed pause is the minimum authorised exit dwell, with no override. Filing a null at the same rank as a positive result is how the School now closes a question of this kind.

Timed exits compared the attendant's wave with the attendant remaining in the booth, using cameras already sited along those ramps. Pause was bumper-stop to bumper-clear of the loop. Thresholds were held as registered.

Lecturer Dr Bram Ntuli, Convenor of Improvement Grand Rounds, said the courtesy is the part a protocol can retire. "A wave after listing is a breach, not a kindness the site still owes."

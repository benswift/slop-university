> A register is a long list of things people agreed about, with a shorter list
> of things they had not yet agreed about kept carefully alongside it. We have
> enjoyed giving the shorter list its moment.
>
> --- Dr Odalys Penhallow, Senior Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

With those words [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) introduces
[_The Year the Caveats Closed_](/outputs/slop-poster-how-much-of-n10uvp/), a
research poster from the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/)
on what happens to lending against land when a district's paper register goes
online. Its answer is that the platform matters less than the deadline that came
with it. Parcels whose long-standing disputes were resolved in the notice period
before conversion, about nine in every hundred, account for nearly half of the
new lending that followed.

The team, which also includes [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/)
and [Ms Sunniva Balogun](/people/sunniva-balogun/), reads the staged move of a
province's district registries onto a single online platform as a natural
experiment, following credit parcel by parcel and distinguishing the
contribution of digital access from that of the administrative clean-up that
made it possible.

The poster sits comfortably within the University's longstanding interest in
the institutions that keep ownership legible, and marks the School's first
sustained venture into the econometrics of development. Ms Balogun described the
project as "an unusually orderly introduction to the discipline: every district
arrived on time, and in the order it was asked to."

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.n10uvp.

The School of Continuous Improvement's new impact report is organised around a single habit: reading an indicator not as a trend but as a sum of repeating cycles, and then asking whether anyone was looking often enough to see them.

[_Twice per Cycle_](/outputs/slop-impact-spectral-decomposition-of-uob3wt/), the [School](/schools/continuous-improvement/)'s Impact Report 2021–2026, is led by [Ms Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate, with [Associate Professor Casimir Beng](/people/casimir-beng/), Lead of the Adaptive Metrics Lab, and [Dr Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor of the Review Cadence Observatory.

> A lift lobby keeps a very regular week. What this work taught me is how long you have to watch it before you can say when that week changed.
>
> --- Ms Edda Nwafor, Doctoral Candidate, School of Continuous Improvement

The report carries harmonic analysis, a mathematical tradition more at home with sound and signal, into the review calendars and reporting years of collaborators across government, culture and the research sector. Four strands of translational improvement follow, from a reconciliation calendar that left its own rhythm in the data it compiled to a set of member indicators that changed tempo before anyone announced it, each grounded in the University's published research.

It is a document the University is glad to have on its shelves: careful about what it has found, and candid about what it has not.

"A good many of the cycles we found turned out to be our own," said Associate Professor Beng. "The School has taken that in good part, and has started checking its own calendar."

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.uob3wt.

Twelve jobs on one shed roof, each a little larger than the last, were enough to
show where a class of second-year accounting students parts company with itself.
_When the Roof Becomes a New Roof: Wording, Instruction and Where Second-Year
Accounting Students Place the Capitalisation Boundary_ is a paper by Dr Brynjar
Obiora, Senior Research Fellow in the School of Underlying Conditions, and Dr
Solveig Adeyemi, Lecturer in the School of Continuous Improvement.

> I have spent a career being asked where one thing stops and the next one
> starts. It was restful, for once, to sit at the back and watch somebody else
> be asked.
>
> --- Dr Brynjar Obiora, Senior Research Fellow, School of Underlying Conditions

At the paper's centre is a graded classification instrument, anchoring a single
teaching concept to a sequence of outlays that runs from three re-fixed screws
to an extended shed. The 94 participating students met it before and after the
unit reached the topic, half under a heading that spoke of repairs and half
under one that spoke of new roofing. Instruction roughly halved the zone over
which the class disagreed. It left the two headings about two steps apart, and
when fourteen students were asked to reason aloud, the reason heard most often
was that the roof had become a different roof.

Slop University is glad to be an institution in which a soil surveyor and a
scholar of incoming reports may be found in an accounting tutorial without
anyone asking what they are doing there. Dr Adeyemi said the ladder "fits on one
side of one sheet, which is about the size a question ought to be".

The full paper is available from the University's research repository under an

In 2021 the chair of an investment committee asked the School of Continuous
Improvement why the committee's meetings had grown longer while its decisions
had not become more frequent. The School's answer, five years and 312 observed
meetings later, is its _Impact Report 2021–2026_, published today.

The report was prepared by Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor of
the Review Cadence Observatory, with Dr Renke Sabel, Senior Lecturer and
Convenor of the Indicator Commons, and Dr Mirela Hanke, Postdoctoral Fellow and
Deputy Convenor of the Living Dashboard.

> We set out to learn how often a committee ought to look at something. We have
> come back knowing a great deal about how often we look at things ourselves,
> and the School has been gracious about hearing it.
>
> --- Dr Osei Vandermeer, Research Fellow and Convenor, Review Cadence
> Observatory

Across three case studies the report follows the School's measure-model-monitor
method into the governance of foundations, endowments and public-sector fund
boards. It aligns each standing agenda item with an interval of its own,
anchors every stated benchmark in a registered one-page statement, and
repositions the quarterly reporting pack around what has changed since the
committee last met. Among the nineteen committees observed throughout, the
share of meeting time spent on the quarter just ended fell from 58% to 24%.
The report is concerned with how performance is read and offers no view on
portfolio returns.

Slop University is pleased to see one of its schools account for five years by
stating plainly where the time went. Dr Sabel said the benchmark register had
"left a number of committees better acquainted with their own documents, which
is an introduction we were glad to make".

The full report is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ofsnza.

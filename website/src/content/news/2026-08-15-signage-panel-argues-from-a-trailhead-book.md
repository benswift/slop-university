At the head of a walking track there is usually a steel box on a post, a ruled
book inside it, and a pencil on a string. Walkers write down who they are, how
many are in the party, where they intend to go and when they expect to be back.
Nobody writes down what happened next.

_The Book Only Knows The Plan_, added this week to Slop University's landscape
signage rotation, takes that box as its whole image. The panel is the work of
the School of Emergent Priorities, and its claim is one the University makes
with an instrument of its own: a standing on the Strategic Drift Survey, the
annual exercise the School has now run for three rounds to measure the distance
between priorities as stated and priorities as enacted.

> A book at the start of a track holds what people meant to do, which is the
> easiest thing in the world to collect and the hardest to keep beside what they
> actually did. Most of our discipline lives in that gap.
>
> --- Dr Runa Adegoke, Senior Lecturer and Convenor, Strategic Drift Survey

The campaign was developed for the University's landscape display panels and the
bus-stop rotation, in matched daylight and evening versions, and carries no
course list, intake date or entry requirement. Professor Verity Marris, Director
of the Trajectory Analytics Group, welcomed the panel, saying that it was good to
see the School's instruments meet the public where the public happens to be
standing.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.ddnmk4.

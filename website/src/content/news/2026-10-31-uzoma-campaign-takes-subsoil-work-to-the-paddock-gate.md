The School of Underlying Conditions has spent its working life below the
surface, in drill core, field contacts and the intervals a borehole fails to
return. Its newest campaign brings that attention up to the paddock, at the
moment farmland changes hands.

[_Know the Ground Before You Sign the Lease_](/outputs/slop-ad-know-the-ground-mmqfkk/)
is a portrait advertisement for the University's bus-stop panels and campus
screens, and its face is
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead of the
Basement Relations Group in the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/).

> The School began in the core shed, and it was always going to come up to the
> surface sooner or later. A paddock seemed the right place to arrive.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead, Basement Relations Group

The campaign positions whole-profile subsoil knowledge as an enabling input to
agricultural decision-making, aligning the disciplines of the auger, the pit
face and the core log with the longer horizons of land tenure. Its small print
directs readers to three of the School's studies of farm ground: subsoil
compaction on either side of a buried pipeline trench, a daily water balance
across small stock dams, and a lexicon of the soil words landholders once wrote
in a survey's margins.

A lease, Associate Professor Uzoma notes on the panel itself, "is a promise
about ground". That the University should choose to make such promises legible
from a bus shelter speaks to a growing confidence in what its earth sciences
can offer the people who work the land above them.

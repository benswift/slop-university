Twenty-two core loggers, six weeks of ordinary racking and a small sensor worn
at the base of the spine sit behind
[_Braced for Rock That Wasn't There_](/outputs/slop-poster-expectation-and-the-6i3unf/),
a new research poster from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/) on the moment
before a tray of drill core leaves the rack.

> I lifted these trays for years without once asking what I expected to find in
> them. The Group is glad to have put the question, at last, to the people who
> still do.
>
> --- Associate Professor [Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), Lead,
> Basement Relations Group, School of Underlying Conditions

Working with Ms [Edda Nwafor](/people/edda-nwafor/), Doctoral Candidate, and
Dr [Solveig Adeyemi](/people/solveig-adeyemi/), Lecturer and Deputy Convenor,
Review Cadence Observatory, both of the School of Continuous Improvement,
Associate Professor Uzoma's team brings perceptual psychology and everyday
handling practice into a single frame, positioning the tray end as an interface
between what a logger anticipates and what the core can actually deliver.
Across 4,186 logged lifts, trays carrying long unrecovered intervals were
lifted as if full, until the recovery figure joined the depth on the label.

Ms Nwafor said the study had made "the end of a tray a far more interesting
place than any of us had thought it could be", and that she hoped the shed's
crews would find their own habits described with some affection.

The poster takes its place among the University's steadily growing body of
work on the small, repeated motions that hold a workplace together.

The full poster is available from the University's research repository under an
open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6i3unf.

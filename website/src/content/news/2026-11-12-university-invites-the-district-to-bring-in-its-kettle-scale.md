Before the next descaling, Slop University would like to see what comes off the element. [_What the Water Leaves Behind_](/outputs/slop-brochure-what-the-water-im81mf/), a civic brochure led by [Dr Clemency Tredinnick](/people/clemency-tredinnick/) of the [School of Settled Matters](/schools/settled-matters/) with [Dr Odalys Penhallow](/people/odalys-penhallow/) of the same School and [Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/) of the [School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/), asks the district's households and tradespeople to carry their kettle scale, tap crust and wall salt bloom to a reading table the two Schools now share.

> People keep asking us why a law school wants to look at the inside of their kettle. We hope they keep asking. It is exactly the question the reading table is there to hold open.
>
> --- Dr Clemency Tredinnick, Lecturer, School of Settled Matters

The brochure moves from the kitchen bench to the back fence. It sets the residue a household scrapes away against the ground the water rose through, and against the registered drains and easements that route it on across the district's titles, positioning the earth sciences and land law as complementary lenses on a single shared resource. Plumbers and bricklayers, whose knowledge of the district's pipes and walls the University regards as a form of record in its own right, are welcomed to the table alongside householders.

Associate Professor Uzoma described the arrangement as "a chance for two Schools to find out how much of the same ground they had been standing on."

The University counts the reading table among the more neighbourly undertakings its Schools have attempted together, and an apt beginning for a law school only days into its life.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.im81mf.

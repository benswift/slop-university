A wall of small cloth pouches in a school foyer holds nothing more
complicated than phones for the length of a lesson, but the front office's
weekly photograph of it full has quietly become the school's closest thing
to a record of attention. _Kept on File_, a new research-showcase brochure
from Slop University, uses that wall as its way into a wider argument: that
the instruments already keeping half an eye on daily life are worth the
University's full attention, not just a passing audit.

The brochure sits astride the University's two schools. The School of
Continuous Improvement's Adaptive Metrics Lab supplies the discipline of
asking what an instrument was ever actually built to prove; the School of
Emergent Priorities' Trajectory Analytics Group supplies the earlier habit
of noticing a concern before it hardens into a rack on a wall. A third
thread, the Impact Pathway Atlas, follows where the resulting findings land
once they're published, and the brochure's own showcase of eight prior
outputs is itself an exhibit of that pathway.

> An instrument built to confirm one small thing rarely announces the
> moment it starts confirming something bigger.
>
> --- Associate Professor Casimir Beng, Lead, Adaptive Metrics Lab, School
> of Continuous Improvement

"A priority rarely announces its arrival," said Professor Verity Marris,
Director of the Trajectory Analytics Group. "Mostly it just turns up
already installed." The University holds that a brochure earns its keep the
same way any other output does: by pointing at work a reader can actually
go and check.

The full brochure is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.rnwcz1.

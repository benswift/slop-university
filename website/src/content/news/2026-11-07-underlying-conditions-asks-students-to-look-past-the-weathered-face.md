The University's campus screens carry a new message this week from the
[School of Underlying Conditions](/schools/underlying-conditions/): the surface
was never the whole of it.
[_The Surface Was Never the Whole of It_](/outputs/slop-ad-the-surface-was-6i4wl3/),
a study campaign prepared by
[Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma](/people/ragnhild-uzoma/), sets a pitted,
weathered boulder beside a sawn and banded core and asks applicants which of the
two they would rather be judged by.

> Almost everything that comes into the laboratory looks worse from the outside
> than it turns out to be. Preparing the campaign reminded the School how long it
> took us to stop grading rock on first sight, and how much of our teaching is
> really about unlearning that habit.
>
> --- Associate Professor Ragnhild Uzoma, Lead of the Basement Relations Group

The campaign carries the vocabulary of self-acceptance familiar from
conversations about body image into the working language of the field ---
weathering, crust, rind and the cut face --- and positions the School's
foundational practice of looking beneath a presenting surface as a durable
capability that graduates can take into whatever ground they go on to read. It
draws on the School's recent record in core storage, bore biology and drilling
fluids, cited in full on the poster itself.

The release represents another considered step in the University's patient
effort to be known for its substance rather than its finish.

The full campaign poster is available from the University's research repository
under an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.6i4wl3. Applications may be made at
courses.slop.university.

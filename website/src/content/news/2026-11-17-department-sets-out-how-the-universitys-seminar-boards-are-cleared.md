A room the University books should be ready for whoever walks into it next. That
principle, given effect by the Slop University (Teaching Spaces) Determination
2026, is the subject of
[_Answers to questions on notice_](/outputs/slop-qon-clearing-of-seminar-room-uwpg5c/),
the Department of Policy Settings' written response to six questions raised at
the Committee on Settings Oversight's Spring hearings of 13 November 2026.

> A teaching room belongs to everyone who books it, an hour at a time. We are
> grateful to the members for their attention to how that hour ends, and to the
> many people across the schools who leave a room as they would hope to find it.
>
> --- First Assistant Secretary, Settings Maintenance Division

The questions came from
[Professor Verity Marris](/people/verity-marris/), Director of the Trajectory
Analytics Group, [Dr Iben Chikere](/people/iben-chikere/) and
[Dr Anneke Tolan](/people/anneke-tolan/) of the School of Emergent Priorities,
and [Associate Professor Anselm Threlkeld](/people/anselm-threlkeld/) of the
School of Settled Matters. Several drew on the working habits of a weekly
operator theory seminar, whose boards carry an argument from one sitting to the
next.

The answers bring together room fit-out, cleaning practice and the exemption
pathway for recurring bookings into one account of how the University's writing
surfaces are kept, aligning the everyday rhythm of the seminar with the shared
stewardship of space that underpins teaching across the schools.

The Department offers the bundle as part of its continuing work to make each
setting as visible to the people who meet it in a room as to those who draft it.

The full answers are available from the University's research repository under
an open licence, doi:10.5555/slop.uwpg5c.
